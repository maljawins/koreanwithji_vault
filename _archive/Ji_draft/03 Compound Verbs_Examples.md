**아/어하다 example dialogues**

I have two example dialogues. Let’s go over the first one.

Example 설명

Teacher: Let's look at our first example dialogue to see 아/어하다 in action! Try to understand the meaning from the context.

*(Display Dialogue 1 from image\_ea4b22.png)*

A: 민준이가 새 장난감을 너무 좋아해. 밤에 잠도 못 자더라. *(Minjun LOVES his new toy. I recall he couldn't even sleep last night.)*

B: 그 로봇 몇 달 전부터 갖고 싶어 했잖아. 사주기 잘했네. *(You know, he's been acting like he wanted that robot for months. Good thing I got him. (I realize now).)*

Teacher: Here, you can see how 좋아해 (from 좋아하다) is used for Minjun's feeling, as observed by speaker A. And 갖고 싶어 했잖아 (from 싶어하다) describes how speaker B observed Minjun *acting like* he wanted the robot, rather than directly stating his desire. The ~더라 and ~잖아 also add to the sense of recalling or assuming based on observation.

**Teacher:** Let's try one more example dialogue.

*(Display Dialogue 2 from image\_ea487b.png)*

**A:** 제인이가 어제 파티에 못 와서 많이 아쉬워하는 것 같더라. *(I recall it seemed like Jane acted regretful about not being able to make it to the party yesterday.)*

**B:** 맞아. 엄청 오고 싶어 했는데... 다음에 다시 초대해야겠어. *(That's right. She acted like she really wanted to come so much (though). I'd better invite her again next time.)*

**Teacher:** In this dialogue, 아쉬워하는 것 같더라 (from 아쉬워하다) shows how speaker A observed Jane's *actions* or demeanor, making her seem regretful. And 오고 싶어 했는데 (from 싶어하다) again highlights that speaker B observed Jane *acting like* she wanted to come, rather than directly knowing her internal desire. These are great examples of how 아/어하다 helps you describe others' feelings based on what you see.
