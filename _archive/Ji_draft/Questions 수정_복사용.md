**Question words = Some(?)**

Let me tell you something very important to remember about the question words before we move on to the next one.

Korean question words can also mean “some\_\_\_\_” Let me explain.

We just talked about 누구.

So, “누구 좋아해?”

Now, this sentence can also mean “Do you like someone?”

And the difference is, it all comes down to HOW you say it. I’ll read it twice in each tone so pay close attention to the intonation.

누구 좋아해? – Who do you like?

누구 (pause) 좋아해? – Do you like someone?

Notice the brief pause in the second version and the different tone of 좋아해?

The same logic applies to pretty much all question words we’re going to learn. So, make sure you keep note of this.

Let’s move on.

Both 무슨 and 어떤 can be used to say “Some kind of”

책이라는게 어떻게 생긴건지 모르는 사람이 아닌 이상 이 말을 할 일을 없겠지만 but just bear with me for the purpose of learning.

“이거 (pause) 무슨 책이에요?” or “이거 (pause) 어떤 책이에요?” would mean “Is this some kind of book?”

And in the lesson “How Korean works”, I mentioned that Korean adverbs are typically placed right before the verb. And

And “어떻게” could sometimes mean “Somehow”

Here’s another super common phrase. “어떻게 좀 해봐”

좀 means “some”, or “a little” but in this case, it’s just used like a filler word that softens the tone of requests or commands.

해봐 is a compound verb combination of 하다 and 보다. 보다 when it’s in a compound verb translates to “Try doing” So, 어떻게 좀 해봐 literally means “try doing somehow”. In English, you’d just say “Do something” to convey the same message.

**Context markers for questions**

And there’s one more factor that can change the ending of a question. The context markers. There are many context markers that are specifically used for questions.

For example, 고양이 좋아하시**지**요? means you like cats, right?, where 지 is used to ask for the listener’s confirmation.

I won’t get too deep into the context markers for now because we’ll learn about them in a separate lesson.
