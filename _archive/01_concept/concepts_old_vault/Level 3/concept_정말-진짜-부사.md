# Adverbs for "Really" and "Very"

Korean offers a wide array of adverbs that carry specific nuances, levels of politeness, and emotional registers for the English concepts of "really" or "very".

## Standard and Casual Forms
* **정말 (정말로):** The most standard and polite way to say "really." Safest choice for formal situations. (e.g., `정말요?`, `정말입니다`)
* **진짜:** Very common, but casual. Avoid in formal situations. Can also function as an adjective meaning "real/legitimate" (e.g., `진짜 금` = real gold).

## Emphatic and Descriptive
* **되게:** A casual, emphatic word for "extremely" or "really." (e.g., `되게 더워!` = It's extremely hot!)
* **아주:** A versatile, standard option meaning "very" or "really." Neither strictly casual nor formal. Can also mean "for good/altogether." (e.g., `아주 맛있어요`, `아주 가버렸어요`)
* **매우:** Similar to `아주` but slightly more formal and polite. (e.g., `매우 기뻐요`)

## Nuanced Expressions
* **몹시:** Means "very/really," but typically used in a negative sense, similar to "awfully." (e.g., `몹시 추운 날씨` = awfully cold weather)
* **굉장히:** Means "greatly" or "very" and is generally reserved for positive attributes. (e.g., `굉장히 똑똑해요`)
* **대단히:** Means "extremely," often used for impressive accomplishments or formal social formulas. (e.g., `대단히 감사합니다`)

## Other Common Uses
* **참:** Translates to "truly/really." Polite and common across all ages. (e.g., `참 예뻐요`). Also used as an interjection `아 참!` (Oh, yeah! / Oh, I just realized!).
* **많이:** Literally "a lot," but acts as "really/very" with certain descriptive verbs. (e.g., `많이 취했어요` = really drunk).
* **너무:** In formal writing, it strictly means "too much" (often negative). But in casual speech, it simply means "so" or "really" in a positive sense. (e.g., `너무 맛있어요`).
* **완전:** From `완전히` (completely). In casual speech, means "completely/really." (e.g., `완전 실패였어요`).

## Additional Intensifiers (TTMIK "13 ways to say Very")
* **꽤:** "Quite/rather" — carries a nuance of an *unexpected* result (you thought it would be less so, but it turned out more so). (e.g., `꽤 추워요` = It's quite cold [colder than I expected].)
* **엄청 / 엄청나게:** "Tremendously" — more intense than 진짜. Lengthening 엄 (`엄~청`) adds emphasis. (e.g., `엄청 어려웠어요` = It was tremendously difficult.)
* **어마어마하게:** "Incredibly/monstrously" — even more intense than 엄청나게, implies something unusually massive in scale. (e.g., `눈이 어마어마하게 많이 왔어요` = It snowed an incredible amount.)
* **상당히:** "Significantly/quite" — similar to 꽤 but noticeably more formal (a child would be unlikely to use it). (e.g., `비가 상당히 많이 왔어요` = It rained quite a lot.)

## Formality Groupings (TTMIK)
- Casual: 진짜, 꽤, 정말, 엄청/엄청나게, 되게, 너무, 많이, 어마어마하게
- Formal/written: 아주, 굉장히, 매우, 상당히, 대단히 (대단히 = most formal, mostly in set phrases like `대단히 감사합니다`)
- 정말 note: slightly more formal than 진짜 when modifying a single adjective (`정말 도움이 됩니다`), but when 정말 modifies the *whole sentence* it becomes a casual emphatic "seriously/I mean it" (`정말 이게 더 좋다니까요`).
