# Conversational "Would Have": ~았/었지

In very casual, conversational Korean, people often use the simple past tense combined with the `~지` ending to express a strong "would have". 

## Formation
You simply take the past tense form and attach `~지` (or sometimes `~잖아`).
* 가다 -> 갔 -> **갔지**
* 하다 -> 했 -> **했지**
* 먹다 -> 먹었 -> **먹었지**

## Usage
Literally, you are just saying "I did it" or "It was." But when paired with an "If" context, it acts as a very emphatic "Of course I would have!" It implies that the outcome was so obvious, it's almost as if it already happened.

**Important Note:** This form is not an official grammar structure for "would have" and is strictly conversational. It absolutely requires clear context (like an "if" clause), otherwise it just means a simple past tense statement ("I did it").

## Examples
* 미리 말해줬으면 나도 **갔지**. (If you told me in advance, (of course) I would have gone too.)
  * *Without context, "나도 갔지" just means "I went too, right?".*
* 몰랐어. 냉장고에 넣어야 한다고 말해줬으면 **넣었지**. (I didn't know. If you told me I had to put it in the fridge, (of course) I would have put it in.)
* 그렇게 맛있는 줄 알았으면 나도 **먹었지**. (If I knew it was that good, I would have eaten it too.)
