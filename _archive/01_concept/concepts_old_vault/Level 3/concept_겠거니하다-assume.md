# To Assume / Guess: ~겠거니 하다

The grammar form **~겠거니 하다** is used when the speaker makes an assumption or a guess about a situation. It translates naturally to "I assume," "I thought that," or "I guessed that."

## 1. Logic and Construction
This form is a combination of two elements:
* **~겠다:** The future/guess suffix (meaning "would" or "must be").
* **~거니 하다:** An older expression meaning "to agree/confirm" (`거니`) combined with `하다`, which acts as a substitute for `생각하다` (to think).

Together, `~겠거니 하다` literally means "to think 'yes, it must be [verb].'"
* **Structure:** [Verb Stem] + `겠거니 하다`
* **Past Tense Assumption:** [Verb Stem] + `았/었/했` + `겠거니 하다` (e.g., 했겠거니 하다 - I assumed that [something] had happened).

## 2. Usage Examples
It is commonly followed by connectors like `~하고` (so I thought that and...) or `~했는데` (I assumed that, but...).

* **오늘도 비가 오겠거니 하고 우산을 가지고 왔어요.** 
  (I assumed it would rain today too, so I brought an umbrella.)
* **다시 말을 안 해도 기억하겠거니 했는데, 아니었어요.** 
  (I thought you would remember even if I didn't say it again, but you didn't.)
* **질문 없으면 다들 이해했겠거니 하고 넘어갈게요.**
  (If there are no questions, I'll assume everyone understood and move on.)

## 3. 겠거니 하다 vs. (으)ㄴ/는 줄 알다
Both forms can translate to "I thought that...", but they have different nuances:
* **~(으)ㄴ/는 줄 알다:** Carries a strong expectation. If the outcome is different, the speaker is often surprised ("I thought it would be X... but it wasn't!").
* **~겠거니 하다:** Is neutral and matter-of-fact. It simply states an assumption without implying a strong emotional investment or surprise if the assumption turns out to be wrong.
