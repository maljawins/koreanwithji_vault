# Habitual Action: ~곤 하다

The grammar structure **~곤 하다** is used to express that an action occurs **frequently** or as a **habit**. 

`곤` is a contraction of the verb connector `고` (and) and the topic marker `는`. The full, uncontracted form is **~고는 하다** (or `~고는 했다`), which has the same meaning but is slightly less common in speech.

## Usage

### 1. Past Habit ("Used to frequently...")
While `~곤 하다` can be used in the present tense, it is most commonly conjugated into the past tense as **~곤 했다** (or `~곤 했어요`) to describe a recurring habit in the past that is generally no longer happening. In English, this translates to "used to".

**Structure:** [Action Verb Stem] + `곤 했다`

* **주말에 할머니 집에 가곤 했어요.** (I used to [frequently] go to my grandmother's house on the weekends.)
* **어릴 때 술을 많이 마시곤 했어요.** (I used to [frequently] drink a lot of alcohol when I was young.)
* **지난 학기에 도서관에서 숙제를 하곤 했어요.** (I used to do my homework at the library last semester.)

### 2. Present Habit
It can also be used in the present tense to show a current frequent action.
* **가끔 한국 음식을 만들곤 해요.** (I frequently make Korean food sometimes.)

## Important Restrictions
* **Action Verbs Only:** Because it describes repeated behavior, `~곤 하다` can **only** be used with action verbs. You cannot use it with descriptive verbs (adjectives) or verbs of state.
  * *Incorrect:* 좋아하곤 했어요. (You cannot "frequently like" something; liking is a continuous state, not a repeated action.)
* **Not for One-Time Events:** It cannot be used for things that only happen once (e.g., "I used to buy a computer there").
* **Often paired with Adverbs:** Because it implies frequency, it is often naturally paired with words like **자주** (often) or **가끔** (sometimes).

## Comparison with Double Past Tense (~았/었었다)
If you simply want to state a past fact that is no longer true (e.g., "I used to live there," "I used to work there"), the Double Past Tense (`~았/었었다`) is much more common. Use `~곤 했다` specifically when you want to highlight the *frequent, repetitive, or habitual* nature of the past action.
