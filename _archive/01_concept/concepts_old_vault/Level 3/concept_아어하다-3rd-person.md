# 3rd Person Observation (~아/어 하다)

In Korean, you generally cannot make definitive statements about another person's internal feelings, desires, or physical sensations because you cannot know 100% what they are experiencing. 

To talk about a third person's feelings or state, you must change a **Descriptive Verb (Adjective)** into an **Action Verb** by attaching **~아/어/여 하다**. This changes the meaning from "to be [adjective]" to "to feel/act [adjective]."

## 1. Structure and Transformation
Conjugate the descriptive verb stem as you normally would (using 아/어/여 rules) and attach `하다`.

* **기쁘다** (to be happy) -> **기뻐하다** (to feel/act happy)
* **슬프다** (to be sad) -> **슬퍼하다** (to feel/act sad)
* **무섭다** (to be scared) -> **무서워하다** (to feel/act scared)
* **힘들다** (to be difficult/hard) -> **힘들어하다** (to struggle/feel the difficulty)

**Examples:**
* **저는 슬퍼요.** (I am sad. - Stating your own internal state)
* **철수는 슬퍼해요.** (Chulsoo is sad. - Stating your observation of Chulsoo feeling/acting sad)

## 2. ~고 싶어 하다
This rule is why you cannot use `~고 싶다` (I/You want to) for the third person. Since `싶다` acts like a descriptive verb, you must use the `~어 하다` form to talk about what someone else wants.
* **마크 씨는 김치를 먹고 싶어 해요.** (Mark wants to eat kimchi.)

*(Note: `싶어 하다` is an exception to the spacing rule and includes a space before 하다, whereas other verbs like `슬퍼하다` do not.)*

## 3. Grammatical Benefits
Because `~아/어 하다` turns descriptive verbs into action verbs, it unlocks grammatical structures that are otherwise forbidden for descriptive verbs:

* **Commands:** You cannot command someone to "be" a state (e.g., 슬프지 마세요 - incorrect). But you can command them not to "act/feel" that way.
  * **슬퍼하지 마세요.** (Don't be sad.)
* **Progressive Tense:** You can use `~고 있다` (which is for action verbs only) to describe an ongoing feeling.
  * **지민 씨는 힘들어하고 있어요.** (Jimin is having a hard time / is currently struggling.)
