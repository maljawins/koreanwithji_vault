# Standard Future Tense: ~ㄹ/을 거다 (것이다)

The most standard and common way to express the future tense in Korean is by using **~ㄹ/을 것이다**, commonly shortened and pronounced in conversation as **~ㄹ/을 거다** (pronounced 꺼다).

## Formation (The Future Branch)
It is formed by attaching the "future branch" `ㄹ/을` to the verb stem, which indicates something incomplete or in the future:
* **Vowel stem:** Add `ㄹ 거다` (가다 -> 갈 거다)
* **Consonant stem:** Add `을 거다` (앉다 -> 앉을 거다)
* **ㄹ Irregular:** Drop the original ㄹ and attach `ㄹ 거다` (팔다 -> 팔 거다)

## Usage and Nuances

### 1. First Person ("I / We") -> Willingness & Future Action
When the subject is the speaker (1st person), it simply indicates a future action or intention.
* 저는 파티에 **갈 거예요**. (I will go to the party.)
* 저희는 한국에 **갈 겁니다**. (We will go to Korea.)

### 2. Second Person ("You") -> Questions Only
According to the Rule of Three, you cannot assume someone else's will. Therefore, for the 2nd person, this form is mostly used as a **question** to ask about their future plans.
* (너는) 한국에 **갈 거야?** (Are you going to Korea?)

### 3. Third Person ("He / She / It") -> Guess / "Should"
When used with a 3rd person subject, the meaning shifts from "willingness" to a **prediction or strong guess** (similar to "should" or "must" in English). Because it's a guess, it can also be used with descriptive verbs (adjectives) for 3rd person subjects.
* 지혜가 **알 거야**. (Jihye should know / I guess Jihye knows.)
* 거기가 **아닐 거예요**. (That shouldn't be the place / I don't think that's the place.)

*(Note: To ask a question about a 3rd person's future/guess, the form `~ㄹ/을까요?` is used instead).*
