# "I want something" (원하다 vs 가지고 싶다)

In English, it is common to say "I want [Noun]" (e.g., "I want a new phone," "I want pizza"). However, directly translating this into Korean using the verb **원하다** (to want) sounds unnatural in casual conversation.

## 1. Why not 원하다?
While `원하다` means "to want," it sounds very poetic, dramatic, and formal, akin to saying "I desire" in English. 
* **김치를 원해요.** (I *desire* kimchi.) -> Sounds very unnatural for everyday speech.

`원하다` is mostly used in:
* Song lyrics (e.g., 너를 원해 - I want you)
* Formal writing or translated texts
* Modifying a noun (e.g., **원하는 것** - "The thing that I want")

## 2. Thinking in Actions
To sound natural in Korean, you must express *what you want to do* with the object using `~고 싶다`.
* If it's food: **피자가 먹고 싶어요.** (I want to *eat* pizza.)
* If it's a drink: **물이 마시고 싶어요.** (I want to *drink* water.)

## 3. Wanting to Have (가지다)
If you want to own or possess an object (e.g., a new phone, a car), you combine the verb **가지다** (to have/possess) with `~고 싶다`.
* 가지다 + 고 싶다 = **가지고 싶다** (or the shortened form **갖고 싶다**)

**Examples:**
* **새 휴대폰을 갖고 싶어요.** (I want [to have] a new phone.)
* **차를 가지고 싶어요.** (I want [to have] a car.)

### Note on "Have" in English vs Korean
In English, "have" can also mean "eat" (e.g., "I want to have pizza"). In Korean, `가지다` strictly means to possess or own. Saying "피자를 갖고 싶어요" sounds like you want to own the pizza as a possession, which is very awkward. Always use the appropriate action verb (like 먹다) for the context.
