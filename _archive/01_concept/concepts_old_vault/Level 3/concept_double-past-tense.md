# Double Past Tense (~았/었었다)

The **Double Past Tense** (대과거) is formed by adding an extra past tense marker (`았/었`) to the regular past tense form. It is used to express that an action happened in the past and the resulting state has **ended** or does not continue into the present. It often translates to **"used to"** or **"had done"** in English.

## Structure
* **Regular Past Tense:** Verb Stem + `았/었` + `어요`
* **Double Past Tense:** Verb Stem + `았/었` + `었` + `어요` 

For example, `가다` (to go):
* Past: 갔어요 (가 + 았 + 어요)
* Double Past: **갔었어요** (가 + 았 + 었 + 어요)

## Usage and Meaning

### 1. Discontinued State ("Used To")
The double past tense clearly indicates that a past situation is no longer true in the present. Korean speakers frequently use this form to translate the English "used to".
* **한국에서 살았어요.** (I lived in Korea. - *Could imply you still do, or just stating a past fact.*)
* **한국에서 살았었어요.** (I used to live in Korea. / I had lived in Korea. - *Clearly implies you do not live there anymore.*)

### 2. Completed Action with Reverted State
It shows an action occurred, but the subject returned to their original state.
* **엄마는 미국에 갔다.** (Mom went to America. - *She might still be there.*)
* **엄마는 미국에 갔었다.** (Mom had gone to America. - *She visited, but has since returned.*)

## Comparison with ~곤 하다
While `~았/었었어요` translates to "used to", it simply states a past fact that is no longer true. It does not carry the literal meaning of doing something *frequently*. To explicitly emphasize that a past action was a frequent habit, the `~곤 했다` structure is used instead. However, for general "used to" statements (e.g., "I used to like that", "I used to live there"), the double past tense is the most common and natural choice.
