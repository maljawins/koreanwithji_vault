# Planning & Ready to Do: ~ㄹ/을 계획 / 예정 / 준비

To express that you are "planning to do" something, you combine an action verb with the future attributive marker `~ㄹ/을` and the noun **계획** (plan) or **예정** (schedule/prearranged plan), followed by the copula **이다** (to be).

## Formation
1. Take the verb stem.
2. Attach the future modifier: `~ㄹ` for vowel stems, `~을` for consonant stems.
3. Add `계획` or `예정`, followed by the conjugated form of `이다` (e.g., `이에요/예요`, `입니다`).

* 하다 -> **할 계획이에요**
* 먹다 -> **먹을 계획이에요**

## 계획 vs. 예정
* **계획 (Plan):** The standard word for "plan." 
  * 내일 서울로 **갈 계획이에요**. (I'm planning to go to Seoul tomorrow.)
* **예정 (Schedule / Prearranged Plan):** A slightly more formal alternative to 계획. It translates more closely to a set schedule. 
  * 주말에 뭐 **할 예정이에요?** (What are you planning to do on the weekend?)

## 준비 (Ready to / Preparing to)
Another noun commonly placed after the future attributive modifier `~ㄹ/을` is **준비** (preparation/readiness).
* **~ㄹ/을 준비가 되다** (To be ready to do...): Uses the passive verb `되다` (to become/be done) in the past tense to indicate the preparation is completed.
  * 갈 준비가 됐어요. (I am ready to go.)
  * 아직 결혼할 준비가 안 됐어요. (I'm not ready to get married yet.)
* **~ㄹ/을 준비(를) 하다** (To get ready to do...): Uses the active verb `하다` (to do), often used as a command to tell someone to prepare.
  * 갈 준비를 하세요. (Get ready to go.)
  * 먹을 준비 하세요. (Get ready to eat.)

## Usage
This form is very practical for stating concrete plans, intentions, and readiness. You can use it in statements to describe your own plans, or in questions to ask about someone else's plans.
* **숙제를 할 계획이에요.** (I'm planning to do my homework.)
* **일찍 은퇴할 계획이세요?** (Are you planning to retire early? - using honorific `시`)
* **파티에 갈 준비가 됐어요?** (Are you ready to go to the party?)
