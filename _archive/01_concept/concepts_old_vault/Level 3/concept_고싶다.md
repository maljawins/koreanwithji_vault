# ~고 싶다 (I want to do)

The grammar form **~고 싶다** is used to express the desire to do an action, equivalent to "I want to [verb]" in English.

## 1. Usage (~고 싶다)
This form is strictly used with **Action Verbs** and is only used for the **first person** (I want to) and **second person** (Do you want to?). 
* **Structure:** [Action Verb Stem] + `고 싶다`
* **Examples:**
  * 가다 -> 가고 싶어요 (I want to go)
  * 먹다 -> 먹고 싶어요 (I want to eat)
  * 한국어를 배우고 싶어요? (Do you want to learn Korean?)

### Using 이/가 vs 을/를
When expressing what you want to eat or do, you can use either the object marker (`을/를`) or the subject marker (`이/가`).
* **피자를 먹고 싶어요:** Focuses on the *action* of eating. (Neutral, direct)
* **피자가 먹고 싶어요:** Focuses on the *pizza* itself. `싶다` is technically a descriptive verb ("Pizza is desirable to eat"), so using `이/가` puts the spotlight on the object of desire. This is very natural and commonly used by native speakers.

## 2. Third Person (~고 싶어 하다)
Korean does not allow you to assume you know 100% what another person feels or wants. Therefore, you **cannot** use `~고 싶다` to say "He wants to" or "She wants to".
Instead, you must use **~고 싶어 하다**, which means "They are acting like they want to" or "They seem to want to".
* **Incorrect:** 철수는 가고 싶어요.
* **Correct:** 철수는 가고 싶어 해요. (Chulsoo wants to go.)

## 3. Negative Forms
To say "I don't want to", you have two options:
1. **안 ~고 싶어요** / **~고 싶지 않아요:** This is the literal negation. It means "I do not desire to do it," but it feels relatively weak, just indicating a lack of desire.
2. **~기(가) 싫다:** This is much more commonly used by native speakers. It literally means "Doing ~ is unpleasant," showing a stronger or more emotional reluctance (e.g., "I don't feel like going", "Ugh, I don't want to").
