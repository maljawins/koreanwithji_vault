# Intentional "Want To": ~(으)ㄹ래(요)

The grammar form **~(으)ㄹ래(요)** is an informal or conversational way to express your **intention** to do something, or to ask about someone else's intention (like "Do you want to?"). It is formed by attaching `을래(요)` to a consonant stem, or `ㄹ래(요)` to a vowel stem. 

## Usage and Meaning

While it often translates to "want to", it differs significantly from the basic `~고 싶다` (to desire). 
* `~고 싶다` is a pure desire. You can say it even if you have no actual plan or ability to do it.
* `~(으)ㄹ래(요)` carries the element of **will or intention**. It implies "I want to do this, and I am going to do it."

### 1. First-Person Intention ("I'm going to / I want to")
* **한국에 갈래요.** (I want to go to Korea / I'm going to go to Korea.)
  * *Nuance:* You actually intend to go. You have the means and the plan.
* **조금 더 생각할래요.** (I'm going to think about it a little more / I want to think about it a little more.)

### 2. Second-Person Questions ("Do you want to?")
When used as a question, it functions as a casual invitation or a check on the listener's intention.
* **같이 공부할래요?** (Do you want to study together? / Are you going to study with me?)
* **뭐 먹을래?** (What do you want to eat? / What are you going to eat?)

## Important Restrictions
* **Conversational Only:** This form is strictly for casual or spoken conversation. Even with the polite `요` attached, it is never used in highly formal settings, speeches, essays, or announcements.
* **No Third Person:** Like `~고 싶다`, you cannot use `~(으)ㄹ래(요)` to state the intention or desire of a third person. 
* **Action Verbs Only:** Because it involves intention and action, it cannot be used with descriptive verbs (adjectives).
