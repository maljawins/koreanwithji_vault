"Think in your target language." But this is obviously impossible when you're a beginner. So, what do you do?
You think of something in English first and then you "translate" it into Korean either in your head or with a translator.
But this "translating" approach is absolutely (pause) problematic to say the least for 3 reasons.

• First, Korean is completely different from English—not just the word order but the way the language works as a whole. Here's an example - show "I ate a lot" and "많이 먹었어".
We don't say "I ate much" in English. Instead, we say "I ate a lot". But think about it, which "lot" are we talking about? certainly not a parking lot. right?
But in Korean, we do use the adverb 많이 which means much.

• Second, Korean is a high context language. Take "사랑해." for example.
You probably know this as “I love you".
But technically, it's just “love” because the context makes it clear that it's obviously "I" who loves "YOU".

• Third, there are many Korean words that simply don’t exist in English.
And the reverse is also true.
So, even if you somehow do manage to translate things really fast in your head, you'll likely sound awkward.
This is why translators often spits out something that doesn't make any sense at all.

So, you'll never truly reach a level where you can "think" in Korean unless you break this “Translating” habit early on.
