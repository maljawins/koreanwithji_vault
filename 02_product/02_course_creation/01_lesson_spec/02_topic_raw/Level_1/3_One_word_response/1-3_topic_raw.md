````md
**One word response**

We established that using 반말 to people you’re not close to is a big no-no in Korean society.

Now, I obviously can't speak for everyone, but Koreans tend to be pretty lenient about this politeness stuff with non-native speakers like yourself because we understand that it’s not particularly easy for you.

However, as you know, there’s always an exception. While most people are kind and understanding, there’s always that one person who ruins it for the rest of us every now and then. So, the last thing you want is to sound rude “accidentally”.

So in this lesson, I'm going to talk about a very common mistake that a lot of Korean learners make—even after learning about 반말 and 존댓말.

This happens especially when they're using very short sentences—namely, one-word responses using just one noun.

Let me give you an example.

[Show the image of BTS]

So, let's say somebody asks you “Who are they?” or “누구예요?” in Korean.

누구 means “who” and “예요” is the conjugated form of the verb “이다” which means “To be”.

We’re going to talk about how to use “이다” in detail very soon in lesson 5 so don’t worry about conjugation for now.

And also, note that “누구예요?” doesn’t have a word that corresponds to “they”. So literally, this sentence is just “who is?”

That’s because in Korean, it’s very common to omit the subject when it’s obvious from the context. We’ll also talk about this in another lesson.

But the point is, in English, you can just say, “BTS”. And it’s perfectly okay to do this.

But in Korean, you cannot say just “비티에스.” Because this would be 반말.

**The Polite Version**

So what you have to say is:

**비티에스요** (one-word response)

or

**비티에스예요** (full sentence - "They are BTS")

The first one is just one word response and the second one is a full sentence with “예요” at the end.

The key takeaway is that you cannot just say “비티에스” on its own.

Now, what if you were to use their Korean name “방탄소년단”?

방탄소년단 ends in a 받침. So you'd say:

**방탄소년단이요** (one-word response)

or

**방탄소년단이에요** (full sentence)

**받침-sensitive**

So here's the pattern:

After no 받침 → add **요**

After a 받침 → add **이요**

Now, Korean has a lot of “받침-sensitive” suffixes like this. So I advise you to take note of this pattern as well as the term “받침-sensitive” itself because it will show up time and time again in our future lessons.

**Another Example: "This"**

Let's look at another example.

Somebody asks you, "What is this?" or "이거 뭐예요?"

[Show on screen - 이거 : This 뭐 : What 예요 : Is]

Another thing you might do is ask for clarification. Like "Oh, this?" or "This one?" in English but again, in Korean, you can't say “이거?” on its own.

You have to say “이거요?”

So, even if you're just using one word, as long as you're giving it as a message, you have to use 존댓말 and you can do that by adding 요 or 이요 at the end

**Question Words and Adverbs**

This applies to not only just nouns but also question words or adverbs.

For example “누구” means “who”. But to say “Who?” It has to be “누구요?”

Another example, “어떻게” means "How" and you should say "어떻게요?".

We’re going to talk about different types of question words in detail in lesson 16, so for now, just remember to put “요” or “이요” depending on whether the word that comes before ends in a 받침 or not, namely 받침-sensitive.

And in our next lesson, we’re going to learn how to say “Yes” and “No.”

See you there
````

#### Making Nouns Polite with 요 and 이요

- 원문 줄: 23~30

````md
### Making Nouns Polite with 요 and 이요

The instructor introduces 요 and 이요 as essential tools for making a standalone noun polite without needing a full verb conjugation. This is particularly useful for answering questions briefly. The rule for selection depends on the presence of a final consonant (받침) in the noun. If a noun ends in a vowel (no bottom letter), 요 is added. If a noun ends in a consonant (bottom letter), 이요 is added.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this distinction. When asked "Who is it?" or "Who are you?" (누구예요?), a speaker might want to simply answer "Me." In Korean, the polite word for "I/me" is 저. Since 저 ends in a vowel, the polite short response is 저요? (Me?). For nouns ending in consonants, such as 책 (book) or 컵 (cup), the speaker adds 이요, resulting in 책이요 (A book) or 컵이요 (A cup). This is contrasted with the full sentence forms like 책이에요 (It is a book) or 컵이에요 (It is a cup).
````

#### The Danger of One-Word Noun Responses

- 원문 줄: 23~32

````md
### The Danger of One-Word Noun Responses

The first way learners accidentally sound rude is by using single nouns as complete responses. In English, it is perfectly acceptable to omit "It is..." and simply say "Coffee" when asked what you are drinking. However, in Korean, stating a noun alone like `커피 (coffee)` is grammatically and socially categorized as **반말 (casual language)**. Using this with someone you have just met or someone who requires **존댓말 (polite/formal language)** will immediately sound disrespectful.

To rectify this, the instructor explains that you must add the polite ending **요** to the noun. For example, instead of just saying `커피`, you should say `커피요 (It's coffee)`. Alternatively, you can use the full polite sentence form `커피예요 (It is coffee)`. This rule applies to all nouns used as messages. If someone asks `이거 뭐예요? (What is this?)`, answering with just `이거 (this)` is rude; you must say `이거요 (this one)`. Even when repeating a word for clarification, such as asking "Tomorrow?" in response to `내일 시간 있어요? (Do you have time tomorrow?)`, you cannot simply say `내일?`. You must use the polite form `내일이요?`.

This principle extends beyond nouns to adverbs and interrogative words. For instance, the word `누구 (who)` cannot be used alone without sounding rude to a stranger or superior. It must be phrased as `누구요?`. Similarly, if you are asking "How?" in a polite context, you should say `어떻게요?` rather than just `어떻게`. The instructor emphasizes that as long as a word is delivered as a message in an interaction, it is treated as a sentence and must carry the appropriate politeness marker.
````
