````md
**Irregular verbs**

Welcome.

It’s finally time to learn about “Irregular verbs”. I know. Just the name itself sounds intimidating but I have good news for you.

Korean irregular verbs are actually **more predictable** than English ones because they follow **specific patterns and rules**.

Unlike English, where you have to memorize each irregular verb individually, Korean irregular verbs change according to set rules.

이번 레슨에서 배우게 될 내용을 무작정 외우려고 하는 것은 추천하지 않는다.

불규칙 동사들이 어떻게 변하는지에 대한 개념만 잡으면 된다.

앞으로 배우게 될 많은 grammar form에 대한 레슨에서 이런 exception에 대한 것을 지속적으로 언급해서 remind 해줄 것이니까.

그러다 보면 익숙해 질 것이다.

그리고 대부분의 경우 informal speech를 만들 때만 irregularity가 발생한다. Formal speech는 원래 하던대로 stem뒤에 ending을 붙여주면 된다.

**7 types of Irregular verbs**

**There are 7 types of irregular verbs in Korean.**

Each type is named after **the 받침 of the verb-stem.**

**And unlike regular verbs, there’s a slight change in the verb stem when conjugated**.

And there are only **three key things** to focus on:

1. **What causes the change?** – 뒤에 붙는 Ending의 첫 문자가 모음일 때도 있고 자음일때도 있다. 둘 중에 어떤 게 triggers the change?
2. **What happens to the stem?** – Does only the **받침** get or the entire syllable get **removed or replaced**?
3. **What happens to the first letter of the ending?** – Does the vowel suffix change?
For example, remember how **으** attaches to words ending in 받침, like **을 거예요**, but not to words ending in a vowel, like **ㄹ 거예요**?

Now for these 3, ㅂ, ㄷ, ㄹ irregular verbs, something in the stem is replaced.

And for these 3 ㅎ, ㅅ, ㄹ, something in the stem is removed

**Irregular ㅂ**

Next, let’s look at the ㅂ irregular verbs.

Not all verbs ending in ㅂ are irregular!

For example, **뽑다, 좁다, 접다, 입다** are **regular verbs.**

Unfortunately, there’s no fixed rule to tell irregulars from regulars, but there are **patterns** to help you recognize them.

But let’s first learn how to conjugate them.

The rule is ㅂ 받침 is replaced with 우 when followed by a VOWEL

And the vowel suffix like 으 is also removed because 우 has no 받침.

So, 춥다 means to be cold and you remove ㅂ받침 from 춥 and replace it with 우 and then conjugate it to 추워요.

And since 우 doesn’t have 받침, 으 suffix is also removed along with ㅂ 받침.

And 돕다 and 곱다 are the only two words that take 아 instead of 어 after 우.

So, you should memorize these words.

Although ㅜㅏ doesn’t exist, phonetically if you say 우 and 아 quickly, it sounds like 와 right?

So 돕다 becomes 도와요 and 곱다 becomes 고와요

Let’s look at more examples.

Remember **고맙다**? It means **"thank you,"** right?

Since **고맙다** is an irregular verb, the **ㅂ is replaced with 우** when followed by a vowel.
That’s why **고맙다 → 고마워요**.

**Another example: 반갑다.**

You’ve seen this word before! **반갑다** means **"to be glad."**

I’m sure you’re 반갑다 to see this word again.

This is why **"Nice to meet you."** is 만나서 반가워요

**Now, let’s see what happens with 습니다.**

Since **-습니다 starts with a consonant,** it doesn’t trigger the ㅂ irregular change.
So, we say: **고맙습니다.**

**But what about 을게요?**

**-을게요 starts with a vowel,** so **ㅂ is replaced with 우.**
And since **우 ends in a vowel,** the **으 in -을게요 gets dropped.**

That’s why **돕다 → 도울게요.** (I will help.)

Here’s some tips to recognize irregular ㅂ verbs

Most descriptive verbs that end in ㅏ, ㅓ, ㅕ, ㅗ, or ㅜ + ㅂ다 are likely to be irregular verbs.

The only exception I’ll mention is *좁다* (to be narrow), which is a regular verb.

Now, for most action verbs, except for a few, they are regular.

The exceptions are *눕다* (to lie down), *돕다* (to help), *줍다* (to pick up), and *굽다* (to roast).

These are irregular verbs, but the rest are regular. Remember, this isn't 100% guaranteed. But you couldn’t go wrong 9 times out of 10.

**Irregular ㄷ**

Now, let’s move on to the irregular ㄷ verbs. Like with the ㅂ irregular verbs, not every verb that ends in ㄷ is irregular.

For example, *받다* (to receive), *묻다* (to ask), and *걷다* (to walk) are exceptions.

The tricky part is that there’s no clear pattern like with the ㅂ irregular verbs.

You just have to remember which ones are irregular.

For instance, *묻다* has two meanings: one is "to ask," and the other is "to bury." The "to ask" version is irregular, but "to bury" is regular.

Same with *걷다*. *걷다* meaning "to walk" is irregular, but *걷다* meaning "to collect" or "to fold up" is regular.

When a ㄷ verb is followed by a vowel, all you need to do is replace the ㄷ with ㄹ, and then conjugate like you would with regular verbs. Also, don’t forget, the vowel suffix isn’t dropped because of the ㄹ 받침.

Let’s check out some examples.

걷다 becomes 걸어요. 저는 매일 걸어요

듣다 in past tense becomes 들었어요. 저는 음악을 들었어요

And in honorific, it becomes 들으세요. 제 말을 들으세요

But when a ㄷ verb is followed by a consonant, the ㄷ doesn’t change. So, formal high, it’s 듣습니다.

저는 락 음악을 듣습니다

And in future tense, 걷다 becomes 걸을 거예요

**Irregular 르**

Next one we’re going to learn is Irregular 르.

First of all, There are just two exceptions 이르다 and 푸르다.

And 모르다, which means to not know, happens to be one of this irregular group

What happens with these verbs is that when followed by a vowel, the whole 르 syllable gets replaced by ㄹㄹ.

One ㄹ moves to the stem, and the other moves to the 아/어 ending.

So, *모르다* becomes *몰라요* and *기르다* becomes *길러요*.

The 으 suffix isn’t affected at all because 르 already ends in a vowel.

Let’s look at some examples

In past tense, 르 is replaced by ㄹㄹ and so it becomes 몰랐어요.

예전에는 sino-korean-numbers|이]] 제품을 몰랐어요

What about the future tense?

Since 르 ends in a vowel, it’s followed by ㄹ거, which is a consonant.

So, in this case, 르 isn’t affected.

For example: *내일부터 집에서 토마토를 기를 거예요* (I will grow tomatoes from tomorrow).

And in honorific form, 르 isn’t affected either. So, *모르다* becomes *모르시다*, and when conjugated, it’s *모르세요*.

Example: *아빠는 제 남자친구를 모르세요* (Dad doesn’t know my boyfriend).

**Irregular ㅎ**

Now, let's look at cases where the 받침 (final consonant) in the stem is simply removed, not replaced.
First, we have the irregular ㅎ.

Only descriptive verbs with stems ending in ㅎ are irregular.

But that doesn't mean all descriptive verbs like this are irregular—*좋다* (to be good) is a regular verb.

However, all action verbs with a ㅎ 받침 are regular verbs.

**How to conjugate Irregular ㅎ**

Let’s talk about how to conjugate these verbs.

When followed by a vowel, the ㅎ 받침 is removed, and *아/어* changes to *애*, while *야* changes to *얘*.

**And Vowel suffix** *also* is dropped because the stem no longer has a 받침.

Most color words follow this pattern. For example, *빨갛다* (to be red).

First, *갛* ends in ㅏ, so it should be followed by *아*, but instead, it changes to *애*.

And since *애* is a vowel, the ㅎ 받침 in *빨갛* is dropped. So, it becomes *빨개요*.

Here’s another example: *어떻다* (to be such, to be a certain way).

This is a very important word, so make sure to remember it.

Since *떻* has ㅓ, it should be followed by *어*, but *어* changes to *애*.

*애* is a vowel, so the ㅎ in *어떻* is dropped, and it becomes *어때요* (How is it? What do you think?).
*어때?* (How’s it?) or *어때요?* (What do you think?).

This is a very common phrase and we use it all the time.

We say 이거 어때요? 여기 어때요? 얘 어때요?

What about honorific-speech|honorifics]]?

Since *어떻* ends in ㅎ 받침, it should be followed by *으시*, right?

But *으* is a vowel, which cancels out the ㅎ.

Now that *어떠* has no 받침, *으시* changes to *시*, making it *어떠세요?*

This is also a very common phrase. And you can hear it in places like stores and restaurants.

It can mean how are these but also “what about this?” or what about that?

It’s the same in the future tense.

For example: *그럴게요* (I’ll do that).

This is another common phrase you should definitely know.

Now, when *어떻* is followed by a consonant, nothing really happens.

By the way, *게* is an adverbial suffix, similar to “~ly” in English.

You’ll learn more about this later, but this is the science behind the question word *어떻게* (how). It’s the adverb form of *어떻다*.

Now, while we’re talking about the irregular ㅎ. We have to talk about **이 / 저 / 그 + 렇다**.

First, remember 이 저 and 그?

* **이** means "this" (near the speaker),
* **저** means "that" (far from both the speaker and listener),
* **그** means "that" (near the listener or not visible).

If you add 렇다 after 이/저/그, they become verbs which mean “to be so”

🔹 **렇다** is used to describe a **state or condition**. It means **“to be so”** based on 이, 저, or 그.

And they follow the same irregular rule we just talked about.

In informal speech

* **이래요** – Present
* **이랬어요** – Past
* **이럴 거예요 / 이럴래요 / 이럴게요** - Future

And this is how it conjugates in the formal speech

* **이렇습니다** – Present
* **이랬습니다 – Past**
* 이럴 겁니다 - Future

Alright! Now let’s move on to some common phrases so you can see how these are actually used.

Now, let's look at some common phrases that use this form.

🔹 **왜 그래?**
It means **"What’s wrong?"**
Literally, it means, **"Why are you like that?"** or **"Why are you doing that?"**
You'll hear this all the time when someone is worried, surprised, or even a little annoyed.

🔹 **그래?**
This means **"Is that so?"**
You use it when you're reacting to what someone just said — kind of like saying, **"Really?"** or **"Oh yeah?"** in English.

🔹 **그래!**
This means **"Okay!"**
Literally, it’s like saying, **"It is so!"** or **"It does!"**
You’re agreeing or confirming something positively.

**Irregular ㅅ**

Next we have irregular ㅅ.

Once again, there's no specific rule to know if a verb is regular or irregular with this one.

And unlike other irregulars, the vowel suffix stays intact even after removing the ㅅ 받침.

So 낫다 for example, which means to recover or to get better.

In the present tense, you might think that *나* and *아* are redundant, so you should just drop the *아*, but no! You need to keep that *아*. So, it’s *나아요*, not *나요*.

In the future tense, it’s *나을 거예요*, not *날 거예요*, and in honorific, it’s *나으세요*, not *나세요*.

*나요*, *날 거예요*, *나세요* have completely different meanings and come from the verb *나다*, which is a regular verb.

Let’s look at some more examples.

In the present tense, it’s pretty straightforward.

Just remove the ㅅ 받침 and add the appropriate vowel suffix (*아* or *어*).

For example, *붓다* (to pour) becomes *부어*.

짓다

In the past tense, you might think *지* ends in a vowel, so you should just add ㅆ to make it *짔어요*, but that’s wrong!

It should be *지었어요*.

It’s the same logic in the future tense. You do not drop *으*. It stays in place.
So, *잇다* (to have, to connect) becomes *이을 거예요*.

**Irregular ㄹ**

Now, let’s talk about irregular ㄹ, which is the last type. We briefly touched on this earlier.

All verbs with stems that end in ㄹ are irregular.

This is the only type of irregular verb that’s influenced by the following consonant.

When followed by a vowel, it follows the regular conjugation rule.

But when followed by these 4 consonants ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㄹ, and 으 the ㄹ 받침 is removed, and the vowel suffix is also dropped.

For example, *알다* (to know) is an irregular verb.

When *알다* is followed by 니 which adds a little bit of kind tone to the question, the ㄹ 받침 is dropped.

니 is a context-markers|context marker]] for questions in casual-speech|반말]]. Adds a little bit of kind tone to the question.

And when followed by ㅅ or ㅂ, In either, *알 + 습니다* or *아 + ㅂ니다*, either way, the ㄹ 받침 in the stem disappears.

When followed by *으* or *ㅅ*, the same rule applies.

*알 + 으세요* or *아 + 세요*. The ㄹ 받침 is removed, and the 으 suffix is dropped as well.

For future tense: *알 + 을 거예요* or *아 + ㄹ 거예요*, either way, the ㄹ 받침 is removed and the 으 suffix is also dropped.

When followed by any vowel other than 으, it follows the regular conjugation rule.
So, in the present tense, it’s *알아요* and *벌어요*.

Let’s look at some more examples.

*벌다* means "to earn money."
When adding *니*, you drop the ㄹ 받침 in *벌*, so it becomes *버니*.
Example: *addressing-people-you|너]]는 한 korean-counters|달]]에 돈을 얼마나 버니?* (How much money do you earn in a month?)

Next example, we have formal high ending.

*달다* means "to be sweet."

It ends in ㄹ, so you might think it should be *달습니다*, but that's wrong.

The *스* in *습니다* cancels out the ㄹ 받침, leaving you with *답니다*.

Alternatively, you could say that since *ㅂ니다* starts with ㅂ, it cancels the ㄹ 받침 in *달*.
Either way, you get *답니다*.

In honorifics, the *으시* suffix cancels the ㄹ 받침 and also removes the 으.

So, *살다* (to live) in the future tense becomes *사실 거예요*, not *살으실 거예요*.

You could also say that the *ㅅ* in *시* cancels out the ㄹ. Either way, you end up with *사실 거예요*.

팔다 in regular future tense becomes 팔 거예요, not 팔을 거예요.

In some resources, they say you don’t have to add 을 and just add 거예요 to make a future tense with ㄹ irregular.

But what’s actually happening is 으 cancels ㄹ 받침 and cancels itself, and this new ㄹ 받침 moves over to the stem.

This is why they say that all you have to do is just add 거예요 after the stem but that’s actually not true.

So, 팔다 in future tense is 팔 거예요
````

````md
### Color Descriptive Verbs and the ㅎ Irregular
Some primary colors are not nouns but descriptive verbs. In Korean, a descriptive verb's dictionary definition is "to be [color]." Because they are verbs, they cannot be used as-is; they must be conjugated based on their position in the sentence. These verbs typically end with the consonant ㅎ.

The instructor uses 파랗다 (to be blue) as the primary example. He notes that the ㅎ followed by ㄷ in the dictionary form creates a aspirated ㅌ sound: 파라타. To use this at the end of a sentence (e.g., "The bag is blue"), you must conjugate it. The rule for these ㅎ-ending color verbs is to remove the ㅎ and change the final vowel to 에.

> **가방이 파래요.** / The bag is blue.
````

````md
### Standard vs. Irregular Conjugation

To understand the irregular rule, the instructor first reviews standard conjugation using the polite informal (아/어 요) form. For regular verbs, the verb stem is identified by removing 다. If the final vowel of the stem is ㅏ or ㅗ, the ending 아요 is attached; otherwise, 어요 is attached. Examples of regular verbs include 작다 (to be small), which becomes 작아요, and 먹다 (to eat), which becomes 먹어요.

However, many words ending in ㅂ do not follow this pattern. For an irregular verb like 맵다 (to be spicy), simply adding 어요 to get 맵어요 is incorrect. Instead, the ㅂ is removed from the stem, replaced with the vowel 우, and then conjugated. This results in 매워요. The instructor demonstrates this transformation visually: 맵다 becomes 매 + 우 + 어요, which contracts into 매워요. A similar process applies to 덥다 (to be hot), which becomes 더워요.

---

### The ㅂ to 오 Exception

While most ㅂ irregulars use the vowel 우, there are two primary exceptions where the ㅂ changes to 오 instead. These are the verbs 곱다 (to be pretty/fine) and 돕다 (to help). Because the resulting vowel is 오, the following ending must be 아요 instead of 어요. Therefore, 곱다 becomes 고와요 and 돕다 becomes 도와요. The instructor notes that other verbs ending in the sound 옵다, such as 외롭다 (to be lonely), actually follow the standard irregular rule (외로워요) rather than this specific 오 exception.

---

### Regular ㅂ Verbs (The "Exceptions")

Not every word ending in ㅂ is irregular. The instructor provides a list of common verbs that conjugate regularly, meaning the ㅂ stays in place and the standard 아/어 요 endings are attached. These include:

- **잡다 (to grab):** 잡아요
- **접다 (to fold):** 접어요
- **씹다 (to chew):** 씹어요
- **집다 (to pick up):** 집어요
- **입다 (to wear):** 입어요
- **업다 (to carry on one's back):** 업어요
- **뽑다 (to pull out/choose):** 뽑아요
- **좁다 (to be narrow):** 좁아요
- **넓다 (to be wide):** 넓어요
- **밟다 (to step on):** 밟아요
- **수줍다 (to be shy):** 수줍어요
````

````md
### Verbs Ending in ㅂ (비읍)
The instructor explains that most action verbs ending in ㅂ are actually regular. For these, you simply follow standard conjugation rules: attach 아요 if the last vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, and 어요 for anything else. Examples shown include:
- **잡다 (to catch)** → **잡아요**
- **접다 (to fold)** → **접어요**

However, descriptive verbs (adjectives) ending in ㅂ behave differently. For these, you remove the ㅂ and attach 우, which then combines with 어요 to become 워요. A common example is:
- **덥다 (to be hot)** → **더워요**

There are five common exceptions to these rules that learners should memorize. While 돕다 and 곱다 are rarely used alone, their conjugated forms are frequent:
- **굽다 (to bake)** → **구워요**
- **눕다 (to lie down)** → **누워요**
- **돕다 (to help)** → **도와요** (uses 오 instead of 우)
- **줍다 (to pick up)** → **주워요**
- **곱다 (to be beautiful/nice)** → **고와요** (uses 오 instead of 우)

---

### Verbs Ending in ㅎ (히읗)
For verbs ending in ㅎ, the rule is to remove the ㅎ and then attach the sound 에. This is most common with color words and demonstrative adjectives:
- **그렇다 (to be so)** → **그래요**
- **까맣다 (to be black)** → **까매요**

A slight variation occurs with **하얗다 (to be white)**. Because the stem already contains a 야 sound, it combines with the 에 sound to result in **하얘요**.
````

````md
### Verbs Ending in ㄷ (디귿)
For certain verbs ending in ㄷ, the ㄷ changes to ㄹ when followed by a vowel. Common examples include:
- **듣다 (to listen)** → **들어요**
- **걷다 (to walk)** → **걸어요**
- **깨닫다 (to realize)** → **깨달아요**
- **싣다 (to load)** → **실어요**
- **묻다 (to ask)** → **물어요**

The instructor notes that some verbs ending in ㄷ are regular and do not change, such as **믿다 (to believe) → 믿어요** and **받다 (to receive) → 받아요**.

---

### Verbs Ending in ㅅ (시옷)
When certain verbs ending in ㅅ are followed by a vowel, the ㅅ is removed. 
- **낫다 (to get better/be preferable)** → **나아요**
- **짓다 (to build)** → **지어요**
- **붓다 (to swell)** → **부어요**
- **잇다 (to connect)** → **이어요**
- **젓다 (to stir)** → **저어요**

Note that some verbs are regular and keep the ㅅ, such as **웃다 (to smile) → 웃어요**.

---

### Verbs Ending in 르
To conjugate verbs ending in 르, such as **모르다 (to not know)**, follow these steps:
1. Remove the ㅡ vowel.
2. Copy the ㄹ to the bottom of the previous syllable.
3. Attach 아요 if the previous vowel was ㅏ or ㅗ; otherwise, attach 어요.
- **모르다** → **몰라요**
- **부르다 (to sing/call)** → **불러요**

A common exception is **따르다 (to follow)**, which conjugates simply as **따라요** without doubling the ㄹ.

---

### Verb Stems Ending in 으
For stems ending in 으, you generally remove the 으 and replace it with 아요 or 어요 based on the vowel in the preceding syllable.
- **기쁘다 (to be happy)** → **기뻐요**
- **고프다 (to be hungry)** → **고파요**

The instructor highlights **잠그다 (to lock)** and **담그다 (to soak)** as specific cases. They conjugate as **잠가요** and **담가요**, though they are frequently mispronounced by natives as 잠거요 or 담거요.
````

````md
### The Foundation: 이, 그, and 저
Before diving into the verbs, it is essential to understand the three basic demonstrative adjectives. 이 (this) refers to something close to the speaker. 그 (that) refers to something close to the listener. 저 (that) refers to something far from both the speaker and the listener. These concepts extend beyond physical objects to include abstract concepts like thoughts or ideas. For example, 이 생각 (this thought) refers to the speaker's own idea, while 그 생각 (that thought) refers to the listener's idea.

These adjectives form the basis for several common nouns. Adding the noun 것 (thing) creates 이것 (this thing), 그것 (that thing), and 저것 (that thing over there). In spoken Korean, these are frequently shortened to 이거, 그거, and 저거. Similarly, they form location nouns: 여기 (here), 거기 (there), and 저기 (over there).

---

### Descriptive Verbs: 이렇다, 그렇다, and 저렇다
The verb 이렇다 means "to be this way" or "to be like this." It is used for things close to the speaker. When conjugated into the polite 요 form, these 렇다 verbs follow a special irregular pattern where the bottom ㅎ is dropped and the vowel changes, resulting in 이래요. An example sentence is:
> **문이 왜 이래요?** / Why is the door like this?

The verb 그렇다 means "to be that way" or "to be like that," referring to something near the listener or something just mentioned. It conjugates to 그래요. This is often used to explain a situation:
> **처음이라서 그래요.** / It is like that because it is the first time.

The verb 저렇다 means "to be that way" or "to be like that" for things far from both parties. It conjugates to 저래요.
> **저 꽃은 왜 저래요?** / Why is that flower (over there) like that?
````

#### The ㅡ Irregular (ㅡ 불규칙)

- 원문 줄: 23~36

````md
### The ㅡ Irregular (ㅡ 불규칙)
The first group involves stems ending in the vowel ㅡ. The conjugation depends on whether the stem consists of a single syllable or multiple syllables. For single-syllable stems like `쓰다 (to write/use)` and `끄다 (to turn off)`, the ㅡ is simply replaced by ㅓ in the present and past tenses, resulting in `써요 / 썼어요` and `꺼요 / 껐어요`.

For multi-syllable stems, the rule is more nuanced: you must look at the vowel in the syllable immediately preceding the ㅡ. If that vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, the ㅡ changes to ㅏ. For example, `바쁘다 (to be busy)` becomes `바빠요 / 바빴어요` and `배가 고프다 (to be hungry)` becomes `고파요 / 고팠어요`. If the preceding vowel is anything else, the ㅡ changes to ㅓ. Thus, `예쁘다 (to be pretty)` becomes `예뻐요 / 예뻤어요` and `기쁘다 (to be happy)` becomes `기뻐요 / 기뻤어요`. The instructor notes that future tense conjugations for this group typically follow regular rules, such as `바쁠 거예요`.

---

### The 르 Irregular (르 불규칙)
When a stem ends in 르, such as `다르다 (to be different)`, an extra ㄹ is added to the bottom (받침) of the preceding syllable, and the ㅡ is replaced by either ㅏ or ㅓ. Similar to the ㅡ rule, if the preceding vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, you use ㅏ. For example, `다르다` becomes `달라요 / 달랐어요`, `빠르다 (to be fast)` becomes `빨라요`, and `모르다 (to not know)` becomes `몰라요`.

If the preceding vowel is not ㅏ or ㅗ, you use ㅓ. For instance, `부르다 (to call/sing)` becomes `불러요 / 불렀어요` and `게으르다 (to be lazy)` becomes `게을러요 / 게을렀어요`. In the future tense, these verbs follow the regular pattern without the extra ㄹ, such as `다를 거예요` or `모를 거예요`.

---

````

#### The ㅂ Irregular (ㅂ 불규칙)

- 원문 줄: 44~70

````md
### The ㅂ Irregular (ㅂ 불규칙)
Many descriptive verbs (adjectives) ending in ㅂ fall into this category, such as `쉽다 (to be easy)` and `어렵다 (to be difficult)`. The rule is to drop the ㅂ and add `워` for present/past tenses and `우` for the future. `쉽다` becomes `쉬워요 / 쉬웠어요 / 쉬울 거예요`.

The instructor highlights important distinctions for temperature words: `덥다/춥다` are used for weather or personal feeling, while `뜨겁다/차갑다` are used for things you touch. Crucially, some common verbs like `잡다 (to catch)`, `입다 (to wear)`, and `씹다 (to chew)` are regular and keep their ㅂ. A unique case is `돕다 (to help)`, which changes to `도와요` (using 와 instead of 워).

---

### The ㄷ Irregular (ㄷ 불규칙)
In this group, the ㄷ 받침 changes to ㄹ when followed by a vowel. Common examples include `듣다 (to listen)`, `걷다 (to walk)`, and `묻다 (to ask)`. These become `들어요 / 들었어요`, `걸어요 / 걸었어요`, and `물어요 / 물었어요`. In the future tense, they take the form `들을 거예요` or `걸을 거예요`.

The instructor warns of "fake" irregulars like `닫다 (to close)` and `받다 (to receive)`, which are regular and maintain the ㄷ: `닫아요`, `받았어요`. She also notes the difference between `묻다 (to ask)` which conjugates to `물어요` and `물다 (to bite)` which is an ㄹ irregular and conjugates to `물어요` in the present but `물 거예요` in the future.

---

### The ㅎ Irregular (ㅎ 불규칙)
This group mostly consists of color words like `빨갛다 (red)` and `하얗다 (white)`, as well as demonstrative verbs like `이렇다 (to be like this)`. When conjugated, the ㅎ is dropped. For most, the final vowel changes to ㅐ: `빨개요`, `노래요 (yellow)`, `이래요`. For `하얗다`, it becomes `하얘요` because of the original 야 vowel.

In the past tense, they become `빨갰어요` or `이랬어요`. In the future, the ㅎ is replaced by ㄹ: `빨갈 거예요`, `이럴 거예요`. Regular ㅎ verbs that do not follow this rule include `좋다 (to be good)`, which stays `좋아요`.

---

### The ㅅ Irregular (ㅅ 불규칙)
For verbs like `낫다 (to be better/heal)`, `붓다 (to swell/pour)`, and `짓다 (to build/name)`, the ㅅ 받침 is dropped when meeting a vowel, but the vowels do not merge. `낫다` becomes `나아요 / 나았어요`, and `붓다` becomes `부어요 / 부었어요`. In the future tense, they take `~을 거예요`, such as `나을 거예요` or `지을 거예요`.

Common regular verbs ending in ㅅ that keep the letter include `웃다 (to laugh/smile)` and `씻다 (to wash)`. These conjugate as `웃어요 / 웃었어요` and `씻어요 / 씻었어요`.
````

#### ㅡ Irregular

- 원문 줄: 421~460

````md
ㅡ Irregular
If the final vowel in a stem is ㅡ (for example: 잠그다 = to lock), when adding ~아/어,
you can not determine whether you need to add ~어 or ~아 to the stem by looking at ㅡ.
Instead, you must look at the vowel in the second last syllable. For example, in the word
"잠그다", the second last syllable in the stem is "잠", and the vowel here is ㅏ. Therefore,
as usual, we add ~아  to 잠그.

For example:

잠그다 + ~아/어
= 잠그아

In cases like this where a word ends in "ㅡ" (that is, there is no final consonant after
"ㅡ") and is followed by ~아/어 (or any of its derivatives), the ~아/어~ the "ㅡ" is
eliminated and the addition of ~아/어~ merges to the stem. For example:

잠그다 = to lock
The last vowel in the stem is ㅡ. The vowel in the second last syllable is ㅏ, so we add 아.
For example: 잠그 + 아
Because there is no final consonant after “ㅡ”, ~아 replaces ㅡ.
잠그 + 아 = 잠가

This would be the same in the past tense as well, for example:

잠그 + 았어요 = 잠갔어요
저는 문을 잠갔어요 = I locked the door
````

#### 르 Irregular

- 원문 줄: 619~640

````md
르 Irregular

If the final syllable in a stem is 르 (마르다), it is conjugated differently when adding
~아/어. This irregular only applies when adding ~아/어(or any of its derivatives) to a
stem and not when adding any other grammatical principles that starts with a vowel or
consonant. Up until now, you haven't learned about any of these other grammatical
principles, that can start with anything other than ~아/어~, so don't worry about this
distinction too much.

When adding ~아/어 to these words, an additional ㄹ is created and placed in the syllable
preceding 르 as the last consonant. The 르 also gets changed to either 러 or 라
(depending on if you are adding 어 or 아). This is done to both verbs and adjectives (the
only exception is 따르다 = to follow/to pour). For example:

다르다 = different
다르 + 아요 = 다 + ㄹ + 라요 = 달라요
그것은 달라요 = That thing is different

빠르다 = to be fast
빠르 + 아요 = 빠 + ㄹ + 라요 = 빨라요
그 남자는 빨라요 = That man is fast
````
