````md
**Introduction**

We now know a whole bunch of different pronouns and how to say "A is B". So, this time, let's learn how to say "A is NOT B."

**Sentence Structure**

The structure goes like this:

**[A은/는 B이/가 아니다]**

Example: 이 사람은 제 친구가 아니에요. - "This person is not my friend."

You see, we have this new particle 가 here. This is called the subject marker, which we will talk about a bit later. Let's learn look at how to conjugate 아니다, which means "to not be"

**아니다 Conjugation**

In casual speech, it's 아니야.

In polite speech, it's 아니에요, and 아닙니다 in formal speech.

It follows a similar pattern as 이다. So, this should be fairly easy.

**Introducing 은/는 and 이/가**

Now, the name of this particle 이/가 is "subject marker," whereas 은/는 is called the "topic marker."

And they have different meanings and purposes.

은/는 and 이/가 are actually known to be one of the most difficult concepts—even for advanced learners—because these concepts simply don't exist in English.

Therefore, we need a whole separate lesson on these particles.

So for now, just focus on learning the structure as a whole without thinking too much about these markers because we'll talk about them in detail in Level 2.

**받침-sensitive**

Before we get into the difference between 은/는 and 이/가, I want you to know about this general rule of thumb.

은 and 는 are the same thing, called the topic marker

이 and 가 are the same thing, called the subject marker

And both particles are, as I call it, 받침-sensitive.

Here's the rule

For words ending in a 받침, the particles that come afterwards start with the silent consonant ㅇ like 은 and 이.

사람 person ends in a 받침. So it should be 사람은 or 사람이.

And the words that don’t end in a 받침 are followed with suffixes that don’t start with the silent consonant like 는 and 가.

친구 friend doesn’t end in a 받침. So it should be 친구는 and 친구가.

Knowing this rule could go a long way. And remember the term 받침-sensitive.

**Example Sentences**

**Let's make some example sentences using the "A은/는 B이/가 아니다" structure we just learned, with the pronouns we learned earlier.**

1. 나는 학생이 아니야.
"I'm not a student."

2. 저는 선생님이 아니에요.
"I'm not a teacher."

3. 이건 내 가방이 아니야.
"This is not my bag."

4. 저건 제 핸드폰이 아니에요.
"That is not my phone."

5. 우리는 친구가 아니야.
"We're not friends."

6. 저희는 한국 사람이 아니에요.
"We're not Korean."

7. 너는 바보가 아니야.
"You're not a fool."

8. 얘는 제 동생이 아니에요.
"He or she is not my younger sibling." Yes, 동생 doesn’t have a gender. To be specific, you can use 남동생 for younger brother and 여동생 for younger sister.

9. 이 분은 제 선생님이 아니에요.
"This person is not my teacher."

10. 여러분은 초보자가 아니에요.
"You(plural) are not beginners."

**Wrap-Up**

Make sure to do the exercises in the lesson notes and create 5 sentences of your own. See you in the next lesson.
````

````md
### 아니에요 — The Conjugated Verb
In contrast, `아니에요` is one of many possible conjugations of the descriptive verb `아니다 (to not be)`. It means "is not," "am not," or "are not." Unlike the standalone "No," this form is part of a sentence structure. Because it is a verb conjugation, it changes based on tense and politeness level. The instructor shows several variations on screen: `아니야` (casual present), `아니었어요` (polite past), and `아니네요` (polite spontaneous realization). While the casual form `아니야` contains the sound "ani," it is grammatically distinct from the standalone `아니`.

---

````

#### Subject Markers and Particle Usage

- 원문 줄: 33~37

````md
### Subject Markers and Particle Usage
When using `아니에요` or any conjugation of `아니다`, the noun that "is not" something should be marked with the subject marker `이/가`. The instructor recommends using `이` after a consonant and `가` after a vowel to make the sentence clear, though they are sometimes omitted in casual speech.

> **저는 한국 사람이 아니에요.** / I am not a Korean person.

````

#### A critical grammatical warning is provided: `아니다` is a descriptive verb, not an action verb.

- 원문 줄: 40~45

````md
A critical grammatical warning is provided: `아니다` is a descriptive verb, not an action verb. Therefore, you can never use the object marker `을/를` with it. The instructor visually crosses out the incorrect sentence `김치를 아니에요` and corrects it to:

> **김치가 아니에요.** / It is not kimchi.
````

````md
### Slide 10 — A is NOT B
- **On screen:** Formula: 아니(not) + 이다(be) = 아니다. Sentence structure: ___은/는 ___이/가 아니다.
- **Visual info:** Example: 이 사람은 제 친구가 아니에요 (This person is not my friend). Color coding: 은/는 (yellow), 이/가 (blue), 아니다 (green).
- **Ji's teaching:** Now that we know how to say "A is B" and various pronouns, let’s learn "A is NOT B." Repeat: 이 사람은 제 친구가 아니에요. 아니다 follows the same conjugation rules as 이다 because it’s a combination of 아니 (not) and 이다. You see a new particle here: 이/가. This is also a subject particle, but it works differently from 은/는. The difference between them is notorious for being one of the most difficult concepts because they don't exist in English and context changes everything. It’s completely normal if it takes a long time to fully understand.
````
