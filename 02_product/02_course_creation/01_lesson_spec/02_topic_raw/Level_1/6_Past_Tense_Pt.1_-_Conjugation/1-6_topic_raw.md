````md
**Intro**

In a “conventional” Korean curriculum, they don’t usually teach you the past tense this early on. But I believe the best time to learn it though is right after learning the present tense because it follows a grammar rule that’s almost identical.

All you have to do is just add a ㅆ 받침 to either 아 or 어.

But this time, you’d add it to the “Branch” part where it represents the tense of the verb if you remember the plant analogy from the last lesson.

And the standard context marker that comes in the leaf part is always 어 for informal speech and 습니다 in formal speech.

Let’s get right in.

**When the stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ The branch is 았**

✔ **So, in the present tense**, if the verb stem **ends in ㅏ or ㅗ**, it’s **아** right?
✔ **In the past tense**, it becomes **았** (with the ㅆ받침).

After **았**, it’s either followed by **어(요)** or **습니다**, depending on speech level.

**When to just add 았 after the stem**

When the stem ends in ㅗ or ㅏ and has a 받침, you simply add 았.

**[Show 작다 – Small]**

작다 means small and the stem ends in 받침.

작 has ㅏ so, you add 았 in the branch and 어 afterwards so it becomes 남았어 for informal low speech level.

[Show : 강아지가 정말 작았어요] - The puppy was really small.

In formal speech, the formal context marker 습니다 takes place after 았. So it’s 작았습니다.

[Show: 신발이 너무 작았습니다] – The shoes were too small.

**[Show 놓다 – To put]**

놓다 ends in ㅗ and has 받침. So it becomes 놓았어(요) and 놓았습니다. But you can also shorten them to 놨어요 and 놨습니다. However, 놓다 is the only word that does this.

[Show 좋다 – good]

For example, [좋았어요 – It was good] cannot be shortened to 좠어요.

책상에 연필을 놓았어요 / 놓았습니다. (놨어요 / 놨습니다 O) – I put the pencil on the desk

고객들의 반응이 좋았어요 / 좋았습니다. (좠어요 / 좠습니다 X) – Customers’ reaction was good [We had a good customer review]

**When 았 merges with the stem**

When the stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ with no 받침, 았 merges with the stem.

[Show 가다 – to go and 오다 – to come]

가았 is redundant so 았 merges with 가 and becomes 갔.

So 가다 becomes 갔어 / 갔어요 / 갔습니다.

**어제 친구네 집에 갔어요 / 갔습니다 – I went to my friend’s house yesterday.**

Again, “에” is a location marker.

And similarly, 오다 becomes 왔어 / 왔어요 / 왔습니다.

오다 ends in ㅗ with no 받침 so 았 merges with 오 and becomes 왔 and followed by 어. it’s 왔어.

Remember 왔어? “Did you come?” To greet friends? This is how it’s done.

**어제 비가 왔어요 / 왔습니다 – It rained yesterday. In Korean we say “the rain came” literally. We don’t have a word like “it” in Korean.**

**When the stem doesn’t end in ㅏ or ㅗ**

Now, just like in the present tense, if the verb stem ends in any vowel other than ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 었, which is 어 plus ㅆ.

[Show 입다 – To wear (clothing)]

입 ends in a 받침 so it becomes 입었어 / 입었어요 / 입었습니다.

언니가 드레스를 입었어요 – My older sister wore a dress.

남자가 쟈켓을 입었습니다. – The man wore a jacket.

When the stem DOESN’T end in a 받침, 어 in 었 either cancels out or merges with the stem. However, ㅆ remains intact and then goes right under the original stem. And the rest is the same.

[Show 서다 – To stand]

서었 is redundant so it becomes 섰. And then you get 섰어 / 섰어요 / 섰습니다.

**물병이 똑바로 섰어요 – The water bottle stood upright.**

배우다 means to learn. 배우었 becomes 배웠. Then you get 배웠어 / 배웠어요 / 배웠습니다.

**저는 한국어를 배웠어요 – I learned Korean (language).**

[Show 재다 – To measure / 세다 – To count]

재다 means to measure and it ends in ㅐ with no 받침. And we learned that ㅓ cancel out because it’s already in the letter ㅐ, alphabetically speaking – And the same goes for ㅔ.

So 재다 becomes 쟀. Then you get 쟀어 / 쟀어요 / 쟀습니다.

And 세다 becomes 셌. Then you get 셌어 / 셌어요 / 셌습니다.

**Example sentences with 재다 and 세다 in past tense**

Next, we have 되다 which means to become.

[Show the movement of the letters in the video – 되었 = 되+ㅓ+ㅆ = 됐]

Now, when 되, is combined with 었, ㅆ remains intact as always and ㅓ merges with ㅚ making it ㅙ

This is how 되었 turns into 됐. Then you get 됐어 / 됐어요 / 됐습니다.

[Show 되었어 / 되었어요 / 되었습니다]

Note that 되었 is not “WRONG” but it’s way too formal and almost never used in spoken Korean. So I recommend you just stick with 됐.

**저는 한국어 선생님이 됐어요. – I became a Korean teacher.**

**Past Stem**

So we learned all about 았 and 었 rule. I’m sure it wasn’t too hard to follow because it’s very similar to the present tense we learned in the last lesson.

Now, up to 았 and 었 part, we’re going to call this the past stem. This will become more relevant later on in level 2 when we learn about more advanced concepts.

For now, all you need to know is that you can use a lot of different endings and forms to express different ideas after the past stem.

**Closure**

I’ve organized everything about the past tense conjugation so you can download it as well.

Just remember, it’s basically the same rule as the present tense but plus the ㅆ 받침 – 았 or 었 making the past stem.

In the next lesson, we’re finally talk about the particle 은는 and 이가.
````

````md
### Past and Future Tense Mechanics
To form the past tense, the instructor explains a three-step process: conjugate the verb to its present form (without the 요), attach ㅆ to the bottom of the last syllable, and then add 어요. For example, 가르치다 becomes 가르쳐, then 가르쳤, and finally 가르쳤어요 (taught). The only major irregular is 이다, which becomes 이었어요 after a consonant and 였어요 after a vowel. 아니다 becomes 아니었어요.
````

#### How to Conjugate the Past Tense

- 원문 줄: 35~45

````md
### How to Conjugate the Past Tense
The instructor outlines a three-step process for conjugating any regular verb or adjective into the past tense. First, conjugate the word into the informal present tense as usual, but stop before adding the final **요**. Second, attach a double **ㅅ** (ssang-siot) to the bottom of that conjugated form as a batchim. Third, attach **어요** to the end.

Visual examples on screen demonstrate this transformation:
- **가다 (to go):** The present tense form (minus 요) is **가**. Adding the double **ㅅ** results in **갔**, and adding **어요** results in **갔어요** (went).
- **먹다 (to eat):** The present tense form (minus 요) is **먹어**. Adding the double **ㅅ** results in **먹었**, and adding **어요** results in **먹었어요** (ate).

The instructor also highlights the change in time markers, showing how **오늘 운동을 해요** (I exercise today) becomes **어제 운동을 했어요** (I exercised yesterday) by changing both the adverb and the verb ending. A vocabulary note defines **어제** as "yesterday."
````

````md
### The 요 Form Shortcut

The instructor highlights a simplified method for those already familiar with the polite 요 form. One can take the standard 요 form of a verb, remove the 요, and then attach the double 시옷 (ㅆ) to the remaining syllable. This creates the base for the past tense. From this base, different endings can be attached: adding 어요 (or just 어 for casual speech) creates the polite past tense, while adding 습니다 for statements or 습니까 for questions creates the formal honorific past tense.
````

#### Past Tense Conjugation Rules

- 원문 줄: 23~31

````md
### Past Tense Conjugation Rules
The past tense is formed by adding specific suffixes to the verb stem, which is the part of the verb remaining after removing the dictionary ending `다`. The instructor identifies three primary categories for conjugation. The first category includes stems ending in the vowels `아` or `오`. For these, the suffix `았다` is used for written forms, while `았어요` is used for polite conversational speech. Examples include `알다 (to know)` becoming `알았어요` and `살다 (to live)` becoming `살았어요`. The instructor notes that `오다 (to come)` and `보다 (to see)` are slightly irregular or abbreviated; `오다` becomes `왔어요` (not `오았어요`), and `보다` can be either `보았어요` or the more common abbreviated form `봤어요`.

The second category consists of stems ending in any vowels other than `아` or `오`. These stems take the suffix `었다` for written forms or `었어요` for conversational speech. For instance, `되다 (to become)` becomes `되었어요` (often abbreviated to `됐어요`), and `주다 (to give)` becomes `주었어요` (abbreviated to `줬어요`). Other examples include `울다 (to cry)` becoming `울었어요`, `있다 (to be/have)` becoming `있었어요`, and `없다 (to not be/not have)` becoming `없었어요`. The instructor points out the irregular verb `듣다 (to listen)`, where the `ㄷ` in the stem changes to `ㄹ` before adding the suffix, resulting in `들었어요`.

The third category covers all verbs ending in `하다`. This is the simplest group because it follows a consistent rule without exceptions: `하` is always replaced with `했`. Therefore, `했다` is used for written forms and `했어요` for conversational speech. Examples provided include `공부하다 (to study)` becoming `공부했어요`, `말하다 (to speak)` becoming `말했어요`, and `보답하다 (to repay/give back)` becoming `보답했어요`.
````

#### Descriptive Verbs and Irregularities

- 원문 줄: 32~38

````md
### Descriptive Verbs and Irregularities
In Korean, adjectives function similarly to verbs when they act as the predicate of a sentence, leading the instructor to refer to them as "descriptive verbs." When an adjective like `작다 (to be small)` is used to say "it was small," it follows the standard past tense rules based on its stem vowel. Since `작` contains the vowel `아`, it becomes `작았어요`. The instructor clarifies that while adjectives have different forms when modifying a noun directly (e.g., `작은`), they conjugate exactly like action verbs when ending a sentence.

Several irregular past tense forms are highlighted as essential for memorization. Beyond the `ㄷ` to `ㄹ` change in `듣다`, the instructor introduces `갖다 (to have)`, which becomes `가졌어요` rather than following the standard `아` vowel rule. Another significant irregular is `모르다 (to not know)`, which transforms into `몰랐어요`. The instructor explains that these forms do not follow a strict logic and must be learned through practice.
````

#### Slide 11 — Past Tense Rules

````md
### Slide 11 — Past Tense Rules
- **On screen:** STEM + 았 + ending OR 었 + ending. Rule 1: If stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ, add 았. Otherwise, add 었. Rule 2: Same as present tense, but with ㅆ받침. Rule 3: Add speech level endings (어, 어요, 습니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** Past tense conjugation is actually very simple. The only difference is that past tense adds ㅆ받침 to 았 or 었—everything else follows the same rules as the present tense. By nature, past tense cannot have propositive or imperative sentences because you can’t suggest or command something in the past.
````

#### Slide 12 — Past Tense: ㅏ and ㅗ Stems

- 원문 줄: 70~73

````md
### Slide 12 — Past Tense: ㅏ and ㅗ Stems
- **On screen:** Rules for stems ending in ㅏ or ㅗ. If there is a 받침, add 았 (아 + ㅆ). Examples: 남다 → 남았, 좋다 → 좋았. If it ends in a vowel, 았 merges. Examples: 가다 + 았 = 갔, 오다 + 았 = 왔. Notes: 보다 → 보았/봤 (both OK), 쏘다 → 쏘았/쐈 (both OK), 오다 → 오았 (Incorrect).
- **Ji's teaching:** In the present tense, if the verb stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ, the "branch" is 아, right? In the past tense, it becomes 았 (with the ㅆ받침). After 았, it’s followed by 어 or 습니다. 어 is a context marker for "plain" context, while 습니다 is for formal high speech. Formal high endings are context markers by themselves—they mark the formality of the sentence. That’s why formal speech doesn’t take additional context markers. When 았 is added to 오 or 아, it merges naturally: 가다 becomes 갔어, 오다 becomes 왔어. This merging isn’t always mandatory; for 보다, both 보았어 and 봤어 are correct. However, 오았 is strictly incorrect—it must be 왔어.
````

#### Slide 25 — Past Tense: Other Vowels

- 원문 줄: 90~93

````md
### Slide 25 — Past Tense: Other Vowels
- **On screen:** Rules for stems NOT ending in ㅏ or ㅗ. The branch is 었. If there is a 받침, add 었 (어 + ㅆ). Examples: 먹다 → 먹었, 길다 → 길었. If it ends in a vowel, 어 cancels out or merges. Examples: 서다 → 섰, 두다 → 뒀, 내다 → 냈, 되다 → 됐. Note: 주다 → 주었/줬 (both OK), 배우다 → 배웠 (배우었 is incorrect). Vowel math: ㅚ + ㅓ = ㅙ.
- **Ji's teaching:** Just like in the present tense, if the verb stem ends in any vowel other than ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 었 (어 plus ㅆ). When the stem ends in 받침, you simply add 었, like 먹었 and 길었. When the stem ends in a vowel, the 어 in 었 cancels out or merges. This follows the same rule as the present tense, except the ㅆ받침 is added. For example, 서다 becomes 섰, 두다 becomes 뒀, and 내다 becomes 냈. With 주다, both 주었 and 줬 are correct, but with 배우다, only 배웠 is correct. For 내다, which has the ㅐ vowel, the 어 cancels out but the ㅆ받침 remains, so 내 becomes 냈. Lastly, 되다 (to become) is very important. If you break down 되, it’s ㅗ + ㅣ. When combined with 었, it becomes 됐 because ㅣ + ㅓ equals ㅐ.
````

````md
-

If the last vowel in a stem is ㅏ or ㅗ (this includes rare cases of the last vowel

나는 문을 닫았다 = I closed the door (닫 + 았다)

나는 창문을 열다 = I open the window (note that this sentence is unconjugated)
The last vowel in the stem is ㅕ. This is not ㅏ or ㅗ. So we add 었다 to the stem:
저는 창문을 열었다 = I opened the window (열 + 었다)
````
