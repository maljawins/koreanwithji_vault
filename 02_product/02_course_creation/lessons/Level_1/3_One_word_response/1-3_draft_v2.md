# 1-3 One word response 대사 초안 v2

## Intro

In the last lesson, we learned that your speech level depends on who you're talking to. Now let's look at a place where it's easy to forget that: a reply with just one word.

In English, someone asks a question, you give them a name, and that's enough. In Korean, that short reply still needs to fit your relationship with the other person.

We'll start with a picture of BTS. Then we'll see how the last syllable changes the ending, and use the same idea when we ask something back.

I'll keep the longer questions in English for now, so you can focus on the Korean replies.

## A short reply still has a speech level

Let's say you're looking at a picture of BTS with someone you've just met. They don't recognize the group, so they ask, "Who are they?"

In English, you can just answer, "BTS." You don't have to say, "They are BTS."

Now try that with the Korean name: "비티에스."

The other person knows which group you mean. But a name by itself doesn't keep the polite speech level you're using with this person.

To keep this short reply polite, add 요.

They ask, "Who are they?"

You answer, "비티에스요."

The group hasn't changed. You're still giving them the same name. The 요 at the end tells them you're speaking politely.

Now suppose you're looking at the same picture with a close friend, and you've agreed to use 반말 together. In that relationship, a short "비티에스" can fit.

So the problem isn't that your answer is short. You can give a short answer in 존댓말, too. Keep the ending that fits the person you're talking to.

For our next example, let's stay with the person you've just met.

## The same group, a different name

BTS also has a Korean name: 방탄소년단.

It's the same group, in the same picture. But listen to the two replies.

"비티에스요."

"방탄소년단이요."

This time, you hear 이요 at the end. Look at the last syllable of each name.

비티에스 ends in 스. There's no consonant at the bottom of 스, so there's no 받침. We add 요: 비티에스요.

방탄소년단 ends in 단. There's a ㄴ at the bottom, so it has a 받침. We add 이요: 방탄소년단이요.

Now the conversation goes like this. The other person asks, "Who are they?" You answer, "방탄소년단이요."

You haven't changed how politely you're speaking. You've changed the name, and its final syllable needs a different form.

For the short replies we're learning here, add 요 after a word with no 받침. Add 이요 after a word with a 받침.

I'll call this a "받침-sensitive" pattern. That means the form changes depending on whether there's a final consonant. You'll hear me use that term again as we learn other patterns.

Let's try it with two ordinary words.

커피 means "coffee." Someone you're speaking politely with asks, "What are you drinking?"

You answer, "커피요."

The last syllable is 피. No 받침, so 요.

Now, 책 means "book." The person points to a book and asks, "What is that?"

You answer, "책이요."

책 has a ㄱ 받침, so we use 이요.

You can make the same decision with either name or either object. Look at the end of the word you're actually saying: 비티에스요, 방탄소년단이요, 커피요, 책이요.

That's the pattern for these short replies. When we start using verbs, we'll learn how their endings work separately.

## Using a short reply to check something

So far, we've used these replies to give an answer. You can also use them to check what the other person means.

이거 means "this" or "this one."

Imagine the person you're speaking politely with points toward a few things on a table and asks, "What is this?"

You're not sure which thing they mean. You pick one up and ask, "This one?"

In Korean, you can ask, "이거요?"

이거 ends in 거, with no 받침. So we add 요.

You're checking the object instead of naming it, but you're still speaking to the same person. The polite ending stays.

We do this with something we've heard, too.

내일 means "tomorrow." The person asks, "Do you have time tomorrow?"

Before you answer, you want to make sure you heard the day correctly. In English, you might repeat, "Tomorrow?"

In Korean, "내일이요?"

Look at the last syllable, 일. It has a ㄹ 받침, so you add 이요.

Compare those two little questions: "이거요?" "내일이요?"

One checks an object. The other checks a day. You choose 요 or 이요 by looking at the last syllable, just as you did when you gave an answer.

## Asking who or how

You can use the same idea when your whole question is "Who?" or "How?"

누구 means "who." Suppose you didn't catch the name in our conversation about BTS.

The other person says, "비티에스요."

You ask, "누구요?"

They repeat the answer with the Korean name: "방탄소년단이요."

Here, 누구요? is like "Who?" or "Who did you say?" You can ask them to repeat without building a long question.

누구 ends in 구. There's no 받침, so you add 요.

Now, 어떻게 means "how."

Someone you use 존댓말 with tells you, "I got hurt."

You want to know how it happened. You can ask, "어떻게요?"

어떻게 ends in 게, with no 받침. So again, 요.

We'll learn question words and how to use them in longer sentences in our lesson called "Questions." For now, keep these little questions connected to the situations we just saw.

## Closure

Let's go back to the BTS picture. With the person you've just met, you say 비티에스요. If you use the Korean name, you say 방탄소년단이요.

The relationship stays the same. The final syllable changes, so the form changes.

And when you ask something back, the same choice still helps: 이거요? with no 받침, 내일이요? with a 받침.

For these short replies, keep the other person in mind, look at the end of the word, and choose 요 or 이요.

In our next lesson, we'll learn how to say yes and no. See you there.
