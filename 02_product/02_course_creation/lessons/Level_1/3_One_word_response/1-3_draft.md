# 1-3 One word response 대사 초안

## Intro

In the last lesson, we learned that your speech level depends on who you're talking to.

Unless you're speaking With someone you're close to, you have to use 존댓말.

Now, I obviously can't speak for everyone, but Koreans tend to be pretty lenient about this politeness stuff with non-native speakers like yourself because we understand that it’s not particularly easy for you.

However, as you know, there’s always an exception. So, the last thing you want is to sound rude “accidentally”.

So in this lesson, we're going to address a very common mistake that beginners make even after learning about 반말 and 존댓말.

which is when you give short replies. Even a reply with just one word needs to match the relationship you have with the other person.

This is easy to miss if you speak English. Somebody asks you a question, you give them one word, and the conversation moves on. But if you do the same thing in Korean, you might slip into 반말 without meaning to.

We'll start with a short answer, see how to choose between 요 and 이요, and then use them when we want to ask something back.

I'll give you the longer questions in English for now, so you can focus on the Korean replies.

## A short reply still has a speech level

Imagine you're looking at a picture of BTS with someone you've just met. They don't recognize the group, so they ask, "Who are they?"

In English, it is perfectly acceptable to omit "they are..." and just answer, "BTS."

Now, suppose you do the same in Korean and just say, "비티에스."

In Korean, stating a noun alone like that is grammatically and socially categorized as **반말 (casual language)**



This would be considered 

The other person can understand you. But in this conversation, that reply sounds like 반말. The name by itself doesn't carry the polite ending you want here.

So, keep the name and add 요.

They ask, "Who are they?"

You answer, "비티에스요."

You're giving them the name of the group, with 요 keeping the reply polite.

And remember, 반말 itself isn't automatically rude. As we discussed in the last lesson, you can use it with someone you're close to when you've agreed to speak that way. Here, we're talking about a conversation where you're using 존댓말.

So, you can keep your answer short. You just need to keep the speech level, too.

Now, why did we add 요 here? What happens if the word ends in a 받침?

## Choosing 요 or 이요

BTS also has a Korean name, 방탄소년단. We're still looking at the same group, with the same person asking the same question.

But this time, you answer with that Korean name.

Look at the last syllable of each name. 비티에스 ends in 스. There's no 받침 at the bottom of 스. So we add 요: 비티에스요.

방탄소년단 ends in 단, which has ㄴ at the bottom. So this time, we add 이요: 방탄소년단이요.

The conversation would go like this.

The other person asks, "Who are they?"

You answer, "방탄소년단이요."

For these short replies, if the last syllable has no 받침, add 요. If it has a 받침, add 이요.

I'm going to call this a "받침-sensitive" pattern. That just means the form changes depending on whether there's a 받침. You'll see other patterns like this as we go through the course.

Let's use a couple of ordinary words, too.

커피 means "coffee." Imagine someone you've just met asks, "What are you drinking?"

You answer, "커피요."

The last syllable is 피, with no 받침, so we use 요.

Now, 책 means "book." Someone points to a book and asks, "What is that?"

You answer, "책이요."

책 has a ㄱ 받침, so we use 이요.

The choice comes from the last syllable of the word you're saying. 비티에스요, 커피요. 방탄소년단이요, 책이요.

We've used these to give an answer. You can also use a short reply to check what the other person means.

## Checking what you heard or what someone means

이거 means "this" or "this one."

Imagine someone you've just met points toward a few things on a table and asks, "What is this?"

You're not sure which thing they're pointing to. You pick one up and ask, "This one?"

In Korean, that little question is "이거요?"

You're checking which thing they mean. 이거 ends in 거, with no 받침, so you add 요.

"이거?" would leave off that polite ending. Since you're using 존댓말 with this person, keep 요 in the little question, too.

We do this when we want to check something we heard, too.

내일 means "tomorrow." Someone you use 존댓말 with asks, "Do you have time tomorrow?"

You want to check the day before you answer. In English, you might say, "Tomorrow?"

In Korean, you ask, "내일이요?"

내일 ends in 일, which has a ㄹ 받침. That's why we add 이요.

So, whether you're checking an object with 이거요? or repeating the day with 내일이요?, the same choice applies. No 받침, 요. With a 받침, 이요.

And even though you're asking something back, the reply still matches your relationship with the other person.

## Asking "who?" or "how?"

This also applies when your whole question is just "Who?" or "How?"

누구 means "who." If you didn't catch the name of the group in our earlier conversation, you could ask, "누구요?"

Imagine the other person says, "비티에스요."

You didn't quite hear the name, so you ask, "누구요?"

They say it again using the Korean name: "방탄소년단이요."

Here, 누구요? means "Who?" or "Who did you say?" You don't need a long question to ask them to repeat the name.

누구 ends in 구, with no 받침, so it's 누구요?

어떻게 means "how." Suppose someone you're speaking politely with tells you, "I got hurt."

You want to ask how it happened. You can ask, "어떻게요?"

어떻게 ends in 게, with no 받침. So again, we add 요.

We'll cover question words and how to use them in sentences in our lesson called "Questions." For now, these are short questions you can use in the situations we just saw.

누구요? asks who the other person means. 어떻게요? asks how something happened in that conversation. Both keep the polite ending, just like our short answers did.

## Closure

So, when you're using 존댓말 with someone, keep that speech level even in a short reply.

Think back to the picture of BTS. 비티에스 has no 받침 at the end, so you say 비티에스요. 방탄소년단 has a 받침, so you say 방탄소년단이요.

And it works when you're asking something back, too. "This one?" becomes 이거요? "Tomorrow?" becomes 내일이요?

You don't need a long sentence every time. For the short replies we practiced today, check the last syllable and choose 요 or 이요.

In our next lesson, we'll learn how to say "yes" and "no." See you there.
