## Teaching Points

- **The "So That" Connector (~게) with Action Verbs:**
    - Ji explains that ~게 implies "so that one CAN," even if the word for "can" (~ㄹ/을 수 있다) is not explicitly used.
    - **Contrast with ~려고:** While both express intention, ~려고 requires the subject of both clauses to be the same. In contrast, ~게 is used when the subjects are different. For example, "집에 가게 택시 좀 불러줘" (Call a taxi so that I [can] go home) involves two subjects: "I" (going home) and "You" (calling the taxi).
    - **Usage in Commands/Suggestions:** ~게 is primarily used in suggestions and commands, whereas ~려고 is not.

- **Causative Constructions (~게 하다 / 만들다):**
    - These forms mean "to let" or "to make" someone do something.
    - **~게 만들다** carries a slightly more forcible nuance than **~게 하다**, though they are often interchangeable.
    - **Permission:** To express giving permission, Ji introduces the compound form **~게 해주다**.
    - **Forcing:** To explicitly state that someone was forced, the adverbs **억지로** or **강제로** (forcibly) are added.
    - **Last Resort:** Ji notes that ~게 하다 is the "last resort" for verbs that do not have a standard causative suffix (like -이, -히, -리, -기).

- **The Causative Verb 시키다:**
    - Used as the causative version of 하다. It is most natural when a verb can be separated into [Noun] + 하다.
    - Example: **운동을 시키다** (to make someone exercise) is more natural than 운동하게 하다.
    - **Ordering Food:** Ji clarifies that 시키다 also means "to order" in the context of delivery food (e.g., 배달 음식을 시키다).

- **Natural Outcomes with ~게 되다:**
    - This form means "to end up" or "to become in such a way that." It implies a natural occurrence or a result of circumstances rather than a singular, deliberate choice.
    - **Contrast with ~고 말다:** While both can mean "to end up," ~고 말다 often implies a negative or unwanted outcome that happened despite the speaker's efforts.

## Lesson Flow

- **Grammar Application (~게):** Ji begins by contrasting ~게 with ~려고, explaining the subject-matching rules. He provides examples like clearing the way for an ambulance (**앰뷸런스가 지나가게**) or pulling a chair so someone can sit (**앉을 수 있게**). He also shows how ~게 is used in casual questions about intention, such as "**자게?**" (Are you trying to sleep?).

- **Causative Structures:** Ji introduces **~게 하다** and **~게 만들다**. He explains that these are the foundation for causative verbs. He uses the example of a mother making a child eat vegetables (**야채를 먹게 했어요**) and a busy schedule making someone tired (**피곤하게 만들어요**). He then introduces **시키다** as a more natural alternative for [Noun]+하다 verbs, like making employees work overtime (**야근을 시켰어요**).

- **Resultative Grammar (~게 되다):** The final grammar point covers "ending up" in a situation. Ji explains the nuance of natural progression, such as ending up at a good university after hard study (**가게 됐어요**) or ending up good at Korean after living in Korea (**잘 하게 됐어요**).

Amira suggests talking about her daily life or journals, as it feels more manageable than complex office-related topics. Ji agrees and adds "Shadowing" to her homework routine to help her catch up to natural speaking speeds.
