## Teaching Points

- **Indirect Questions (~는지):** Used to embed a question within a sentence. For action verbs, use ~는지; for descriptive verbs, use ~ㄴ/은지. If no question word (who, what, where) is present, it translates to "whether" or "if."
- **Intention + Reason (~ㄹ/을 테니까):** A combination of expressing intention/promise and providing a reason. Ji clarifies that future forms like ~ㄹ/을래요 or ~ㄹ/을게요 cannot combine with ~니까; instead, ~ㄹ/을 테니까 must be used.
- **Inference (~ㄴ/은가 보다 / ~나 보다):** Used to express a guess based on current observation or evidence.
    - **A.V.S + 나 보다:** e.g., 비가 오나 봐요 (It must be raining—I see wet ground).
    - **D.V.S + ㄴ/은가 보다:** e.g., 목소리가 작은가 봐요 (My voice must be small—no one is responding).
    - **Contrast:** Unlike ~것 같다 (general thought/opinion), these forms require a factual basis observed in the moment.
- **The Bound Noun 줄 (~줄 알다/모르다):**
    - **How-to:** Only when using a future modifier (~ㄹ/을 줄) with present/future 알다/모르다. e.g., 한국말 할 줄 아세요? (Do you know how to speak Korean?).
    - **Expectation:** In almost all other cases, it means "knowing/thinking that." The meaning of 알다 shifts between "knew" (expectation met) and "thought" (expectation not met) based on context.
- **Purpose Connector (~게 / ~도록):** Both mean "so that." ~게 is preferred in daily conversation, while ~도록 is more formal.
    - **Adverbial Usage:** D.V.S + 게 can create adverbs, but Ji notes that Korean adverbs are often irregular (ending in 이, 히, 리) and should be learned as individual vocabulary.
    - **Action Verbs:** A.V.S + 게 implies "so that one CAN." It is frequently used in suggestions and commands where the subjects of the two clauses are different.
