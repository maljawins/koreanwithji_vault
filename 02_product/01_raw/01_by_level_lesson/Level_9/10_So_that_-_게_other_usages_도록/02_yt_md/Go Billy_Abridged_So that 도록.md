## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor presents this lesson from a unique setting—a cable car overlooking Seoul—to explain the versatile grammar marker 도록. The primary function of 도록 is to express "so that" or "in order to," indicating a purpose or a goal for an action. Throughout the video, the instructor compares 도록 with other similar forms like 게 and 위해, noting that while they share a core meaning, 도록 often carries a more formal tone. The lesson covers three main areas: using 도록 to mean "so that," using it to mean "to the extent that" (often used in hyperbolic expressions), and the specific construction 도록 하다, which is used for formal announcements or making sure a task is completed.

---

### Meaning 1: So That / In Order To

The most common usage of 도록 is to connect two clauses where the first clause is the goal and the second is the action taken to achieve it. It translates naturally as "so that" or "in order to." To conjugate, you simply take a verb stem and attach 도록. The instructor notes that in this specific usage, 도록 is interchangeable with the marker 게, though 도록 sounds slightly more formal.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this purpose-driven usage. To avoid getting a cold, one might say:
> **감기에 걸리지 않도록 입과 코를 만지지 마세요.** / Don't touch your mouth and your nose so that you don't get a cold.

If the goal is to study Korean, the sentence structure follows:
> **한국어를 공부할 수 있도록 한국어 책을 샀어요.** / I bought a Korean book so that I can study Korean.

For long-term goals like a trip, the instructor demonstrates:
> **내년에 한국 여행을 충분히 즐길 수 있도록 지금부터 돈을 모으기 시작하세요.** / Start saving up your money from now so that you can fully enjoy your Korea trip next year.

Finally, for a situational request:
> **모두가 다 들을 수 있도록 크게 말해주세요.** / Please speak loudly so that everyone can hear you.

---

### Meaning 2: To the Extent That / Until

The second usage of 도록 describes the degree or extent of an action. In this context, it translates as "to the extent that," "so much that," or sometimes "until." This is frequently used in idiomatic or hyperbolic expressions to emphasize how much someone did something.

One common example involves getting bored or sick of something:
> **그 노래를 질리도록 들었어요.** / I listened to that song so much that I got tired of it.

The instructor highlights a specific Korean idiom involving waiting so long that your eyes feel like they are "coming out" of their sockets:
> **눈이 빠지도록 기다렸어요.** / I waited so much that my eyes came out (I waited forever).

Hyperbole is also common in expressions of love, using the verb 죽다 (to die):
> **BTS를 죽도록 사랑해요.** / I love BTS to death (until I die).

It can also mark the passage of time to emphasize a long duration:
> **집주인을 2년이 지나도록 못 봤어요.** / I haven't seen the homeowner to the extent that two years have passed.

---

### Meaning 3: 도록 하다 — Formal Announcements and "Making Sure"

When 도록 is followed by the verb 하다, it creates the form 도록 하다. This construction has two main nuances. First, it can function like 게 하다 to mean "to make" or "to let" someone do something. Second, and more uniquely, it is used as a formal way to announce an intention or to give a polite but firm instruction to "make sure" something happens.

In a professional setting, a speaker might announce the start of a meeting:
> **시작하도록 하겠습니다.** / I will make it so that I start (Let's get started).

To express a personal resolution or promise:
> **오늘부터 더 열심히 일하도록 할게요.** / I will make sure to work harder from today.

When giving instructions to others, it carries the nuance of "make sure to":
> **내일 시험에 좋은 점수를 받을 수 있도록 단어를 잘 외우도록 하세요.** / Make sure to do a good job of memorizing your vocabulary so that you can get a good score on tomorrow's test.

Another example of a recommendation:
> **이 생선은 맛있고 몸에도 좋으니까 많이 먹도록 해요.** / Because this fish tastes good and is also good for you, make sure to eat a lot.

---

### Comparison: 도록 vs. 위해 vs. 려고

The instructor clarifies the differences between these "in order to" forms. 위해 is the most literal and direct translation of "in order to." It sounds very "bookish" and formal. While 도록 and 위해 are often interchangeable, 도록 focuses more on "making sure" a specific state is reached.

The instructor points out that 도록 cannot be used in every situation where 위해 fits. For instance, asking "What books do you use so that you learn Korean?" sounds awkward in both languages.
- **Wrong:** 한국어를 배우도록 무슨 책들을 써요?
- **Correct:** **한국어를 배우기 위해 무슨 책들을 써요?** / What books do you use in order to learn Korean?

However, for goals like becoming a teacher, both work well:
> **한국어 선생님이 될 수 있도록 대학원을 다니기 시작했어요.** / I started attending graduate school so that I can become a Korean teacher.
> **한국어 선생님이 되기 위해(서) 대학원을 다니기 시작했어요.** / I started attending graduate school in order to become a Korean teacher.

Finally, the instructor mentions that in everyday speech, the 려고 (intending to) form is actually more common than 위해 because it sounds less stiff.

---

### Pro Tip: 토록 with Nouns

The instructor concludes with a "pro tip" regarding the form 토록. While 도록 is used with verbs, 토록 is its counterpart for nouns. However, learners should not attach it to nouns randomly; it is only used with specific, established words.

Common examples include:
- **영원토록** / Until forever (to the extent of eternity)
- **이토록** / This much (equivalent to 이렇게까지)
- **그토록** / That much (equivalent to 그렇게까지)

Example sentence:
> **영원토록 널 사랑할게.** / I'll love you forever.
