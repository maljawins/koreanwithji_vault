## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson introduces the concept of causative verbs in Korean, which are used to describe causing someone or something to perform an action. The instructor begins by explaining the most basic grammatical construction for causatives—using the suffix ~게 followed by the verbs 하다 or 만들다—and then transitions into specific causative verbs that exist as independent vocabulary items.

The learning arc moves from general grammatical rules to specific lexical examples, emphasizing that while the ~게 하다 form is versatile, dedicated causative verbs often sound more natural in daily conversation. The instructor also covers the nuances of particles (subject vs. object markers) and how they shift when a sentence becomes causative, providing a comprehensive guide for intermediate learners to express making, letting, or causing actions.

---

### Basic Causative Form: ~게 하다 and ~게 만들다
The most fundamental way to create a causative meaning is by taking an action verb stem and attaching ~게 followed by the conjugated form of 하다 (to do) or 만들다 (to make). This construction translates to "to make someone do" or "to let someone do" something. While both verbs are often interchangeable, the instructor notes a subtle difference: ~게 만들다 almost always means "to make" (forceful), whereas ~게 하다 can mean either "to make" or "to let" depending on the context.

To illustrate this, the instructor uses the verb 일어나다 (to wake up). By transforming it into 일어나게 했어요 or 일어나게 만들었어요, the sentence changes from "someone woke up" to "I made my friend wake up." Literally, the suffix ~게 means "so that," so the expression functions as "doing something so that the friend wakes up."

---

### Particle Usage in Causative Sentences
When using the ~게 하다/만들다 form, the particles used in the original non-causative sentence generally remain the same. For example, in the sentence 고양이가 쥐를 먹었어요 (The cat ate the mouse), the cat is the subject and the mouse is the object. To say "I made the cat eat the mouse," you simply add the causative ending to the verb: 고양이가 쥐를 먹게 했어요.

With descriptive verbs, there is more flexibility. To say "I made my friend sad," you can use either the object marker 친구를 슬프게 했어요 or the subject marker 친구가 슬프게 했어요. The latter sounds slightly more like "I made it so that my friend is sad," while the former is a more direct "I made my friend sad." However, if a sentence has two potential objects, you must use the subject marker for the person being caused to act to avoid ambiguity.

---

### Softening and Specifying "Let" with ~게 해주다
If a speaker wants to specifically emphasize "letting" or "allowing" someone to do something as a favor, they should use the form ~게 해주다. This combines the causative ~게 with the auxiliary verb ~어/아 주다 (to do as a favor). An example provided is 자게 해주세요 (Please let me sleep), which is softer and more polite than a direct command. Adding the adverb 좀 (a little/please) further softens the request: 좀 자게 해주세요.

---

### The Causative Verb 시키다
The verb 시키다 is a unique causative verb that often replaces the ~게 하다 form for verbs ending in 하다. For instance, instead of saying 청소하게 하다 (to make someone clean), it is more natural to say 청소를 시켰어요. When using 시키다, the person being forced or told to do the action is marked with 에게 or 한테 (to).

The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence 철수에게 청소를 시켰어요 (I made Chul-soo clean). This construction feels more "snappy" and natural than the longer grammatical form. Additionally, 시키다 has a common secondary meaning: to order food for delivery, such as 피자를 시키다 (to order pizza).

---

### Common Lexical Causative Verbs
Many Korean verbs have specific causative counterparts that look similar to the original verb but include an extra syllable (like 이, 히, 리, 기, 우, 구, 추). These are used exactly like regular active verbs and often have more natural English translations.

- **죽다 (to die) → 죽이다 (to kill):** While 죽게 하다 is grammatically possible, 죽이다 is the standard way to say "to kill."
- **먹다 (to eat) → 먹이다 (to feed):** This is used for feeding babies or animals.
- **앉다 (to sit) → 앉히다 (to seat someone):** Used for seating a guest or a child.
- **울다 (to cry) → 울리다 (to make someone cry).**
- **웃다 (to laugh) → 웃기다 (to make someone laugh/to be funny).**
- **낮다 (to be low) → 낮추다 (to lower) / 높다 (to be high) → 높이다 (to raise).**
- **타다 (to ride) → 태우다 (to give someone a ride).**
- **알다 (to know) → 알리다 (to inform/let someone know):** Often used as 알려주다.
- **자다 (to sleep) → 재우다 (to put someone to sleep).**
- **입다 (to wear) → 입히다 (to dress someone) / 벗다 (to take off) → 벗기다 (to undress someone/something).**
- **살다 (to live) → 살리다 (to save someone's life):** Used in the common plea 살려주세요 (Save me/Please let me live).
- **없다 (to not exist) → 없애다 (to get rid of/remove).**
- **남다 (to remain) → 남기다 (to leave something behind):** Used for leaving food or leaving a comment online.
