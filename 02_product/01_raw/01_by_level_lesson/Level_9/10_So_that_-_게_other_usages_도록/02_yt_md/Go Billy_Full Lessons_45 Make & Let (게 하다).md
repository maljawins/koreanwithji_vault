## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces how to express the concepts of "making" or "letting" someone do something in Korean. The lesson centers on the versatile suffix ~게, which attaches to verb stems to create causative meanings. The video demonstrates three primary constructions: a basic causative form that can mean either "make" or "let," a specific "let" construction using the favor-granting verb 주다, and a more forceful "make" construction using the verb 만들다. The instructor emphasizes that these forms are used with action verbs to describe influencing another person's behavior.

---

### The Basic Causative: ~게 하다

The most common way to say "make" or "let" is by taking a verb stem, adding the suffix ~게, and following it with the verb 하다 (to do). On screen, the instructor shows that this single form can translate to both "I let him eat" and "I made him eat," depending entirely on the context of the conversation.

An example of a negative command is shown visually:
> **강아지가 초콜릿을 먹게 하지 마.** / Don't let the puppy eat chocolate.

The instructor notes that while this could technically be translated as "Don't make the dog eat chocolate," common sense and context usually dictate the "let" translation in English. Another example provided is:
> **내가 또 말하게 하지 마.** / Don't make me say it again.

---

### Permissive "Let": ~게 해주다

To specifically express "letting" someone do something as a favor or permission, the instructor introduces the construction: **Verb Stem + ~게 + 해주다**. This combines the causative ~게 with 해주다, which means "to do something as a favor." This form is more polite and clearly indicates granting permission rather than forcing an action.

The instructor provides two request-based examples:
> **저 좀 자게 해주세요.** / Please let me sleep.
> **TV를 볼 수 있게 해주세요.** / Please let me watch TV.

The second example literally translates to "Please let me be able to watch television," showing how ~게 can interact with the ~ㄹ 수 있다 (can/ability) form.

---

### Forceful "Make": ~게 만들다

When the speaker wants to emphasize "making" or "causing" a result—often with a sense of force or direct influence—they can use the construction: **Verb Stem + ~게 + 만들다**. Since 만들다 literally means "to make," this is a very direct causative.

Examples shown on screen include:
> **철수를 화나게 만들었어.** / I made Chulsoo get angry.
> **친구들이 싸우게 만들었어.** / I made my friends fight.

---

### Important Usage Note

The instructor concludes with a critical grammatical rule: all the verb stems used in these causative constructions (~게 하다, ~게 해주다, ~게 만들다) must be action verbs. They cannot be used with descriptive verbs (adjectives) in this specific causative manner.
