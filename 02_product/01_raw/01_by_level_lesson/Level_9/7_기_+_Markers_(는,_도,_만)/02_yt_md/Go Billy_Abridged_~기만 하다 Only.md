## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form ~기만 하다, which is used to express that someone or something "only" or "just" performs a specific action or exists in a specific state. This form allows the particle 만 (only) to be used with verbs rather than just nouns. The lesson covers the construction of the form, its literal and natural translations, and a related conditional variation, ~기만 하면, which often translates to "whenever" or "all it takes is..." in English.

---

### Construction and Nominalization

To create this form, you take a verb stem and attach the suffix 기. This process is called nominalization, which turns a verb into a noun (similar to a gerund in English). For example, the verb 먹다 (to eat) becomes the noun 먹기 (eating), and 가다 (to go) becomes 가기 (going). This works for both action verbs and descriptive verbs, such as 춥다 (to be cold) becoming 춥기 (being cold). There are no exceptions to this rule. Once the verb is nominalized, you add the particle 만 (only/just) followed by the verb 하다.

While 하다 usually means "to do," its function changes slightly depending on the type of verb it follows in this specific grammar structure. When following an action verb, it functions like "to do," resulting in a meaning of "only doing [verb]." When following a descriptive verb, it functions more like "to be," resulting in "only being [adjective]." The instructor notes that translating 만 as "just" often leads to more natural-sounding English sentences than "only."

---

### Usage and Examples of ~기만 하다

The primary purpose of this form is to emphasize that a single action or state is occurring to the exclusion of others. The instructor illustrates this by comparing it to the standard use of 만 with nouns. In the sentence 저는 사과만 먹어요 (I eat only apples), the "only" restricts the object (apples). However, in the sentence 저는 사과를 먹기만 해요, the "only" restricts the action itself. It implies that the speaker does nothing else with the apples—or perhaps nothing else at all—except eat them.

Several examples are provided to show the range of the form. To describe a beautiful but tasteless apple, one might say 그 사과는 예쁘기만 하고 맛이 없어서 안 사요 (That apple is only pretty and doesn't taste good, so I don't buy it). Here, the apple's only quality is its appearance. Regarding a lazy pet, the instructor uses the sentence 우리 고양이는 밥도 안 먹고 잠을 자기만 해요 (Our cat doesn't even eat and only sleeps). Finally, as a command, the form can be used to tell someone to "just" do something, such as 조용히 듣기만 하세요 (Just listen quietly).

---

### The Conditional Form: ~기만 하면

The instructor introduces a second, related form: verb stem + 기만 하면. This is the conditional version of the previous form, literally meaning "if [subject] only does/is..." However, in practice, this form is most naturally translated into English as "whenever" or "all it takes is..." It suggests that a certain result always follows a specific, simple action or condition.

The instructor provides a personal example: 저는 선크림을 바르기만 하면 얼굴이 빨개져요 (Whenever I put on sunblock, my face gets red). This implies that the mere act of applying sunblock is sufficient to cause the reaction. Another example describes a sensitivity to temperature: 저는 에어컨을 틀기만 하면 감기에 걸려요 (Whenever I turn on the air conditioner, I catch a cold). A final example uses the form to give advice against suppressing emotions: 무조건 참기만 하면 안 돼 (You shouldn't just hold everything in). In this context, 무조건 (unconditionally) is used to mean "just" or "blindly."
