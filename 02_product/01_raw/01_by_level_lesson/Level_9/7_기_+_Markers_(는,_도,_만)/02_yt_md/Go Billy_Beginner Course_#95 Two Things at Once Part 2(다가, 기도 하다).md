## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson introduces the grammar form 다가, which is another way to express the concept of "while" in Korean. While the previous lesson covered (으)면서 for simultaneous actions, 다가 focuses on an action that is interrupted by a second event. The instructor explains the construction of this form, its specific nuances regarding interruption and incompletion, and provides several practical examples. Additionally, the lesson covers the use of the 기 form combined with particles like 는 and 도 to add emphasis or the meaning of "also/even" to verbs.

---

### The 다가 Form: Interrupted Actions

The 다가 form is used to describe doing one action while another thing happens. To create this form, you take an action verb stem and simply attach 다가. The instructor provides two introductory examples:

> **학교로 가다가 친구를 만났어요.** / I met a friend while going to school.
> **저녁을 만들다가 전화를 했어요.** / I made a phone call while making dinner.

Similar to the (으)면서 form, 다가 can only be used when the same subject is performing both actions. However, the key difference lies in the timing. While (으)면서 implies two actions happening simultaneously, 다가 implies that something happens after starting the first action, effectively interrupting it so that it may not be finished. In the school example, the speaker started going to school, but meeting the friend interrupted that journey before they arrived. The action that follows the 다가 form is always the one that interrupts the action preceding it.

---

### Examples and Nuances of 다가

The instructor presents several sentences to illustrate how 다가 functions as "while" through interruption. In the sentence 화장품을 사러 가다가 차가 고장 났어요 (While going to buy makeup, the car broke down), the act of going to buy makeup was started but interrupted by the car breaking down, meaning the purchase was never completed. Another example, 결혼식에 가려고 뛰어가다가 넘어졌어요 (I tripped and fell while running in order to go to the wedding), shows the interruption of running. 

The instructor notes a vocabulary distinction here: just as 걸어가다 and 걸어오다 distinguish walking "to somewhere else" versus "to here," 뛰어가다 and 뛰어오다 distinguish running "to somewhere else" versus "to here." Other examples include:

- **책을 읽다가 잠들었어요.** / I fell asleep while reading a book. (Falling asleep interrupted the reading).
- **초아 씨는 국수를 먹다가 갑자기 나갔어요.** / While eating noodles, Choa suddenly went out.
- **예능 프로그램을 보다가 크게 웃었어요.** / I laughed loudly while watching a variety show.
- **자다가 침대에서 떨어졌어요.** / I fell down from the bed while sleeping.

Regarding the variety show example, the instructor explains that 크다 (to be big) and 작다 (to be small) are used for volume, and their adverbial forms 크게 and 작게 mean "in a loud way" and "in a quiet way" respectively.

---

### Using the 기 Form for Emphasis and Addition

The lesson concludes by explaining how the 기 form (turning a verb into a noun) can be used with particles and the verb 하다 to add specific nuances to a sentence. Using 기는 하다 adds emphasis to the verb. For instance, in the conversation practice, the character says 조금 걱정이 되기는 했어요, which translates to "I was a bit worried," but with a literal nuance of "As for worrying a little, I did."

Similarly, using 기도 하다 adds the meaning of "also" or "even" to the verb itself, rather than to a noun. The instructor compares two sentences:

- **저는 한국어를 공부해요.** / I study Korean.
- **저는 한국어를 공부하기도 해요.** / I even/also study Korean.

This structure allows the speaker to apply the "also/even" meaning of the particle 도 directly to the action being performed.
