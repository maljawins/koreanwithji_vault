## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces two essential grammar structures for describing the beginning and end of an action. The lesson is framed around the concepts of "starting to do" and "stopping doing" something. By combining action verbs with specific endings or adverbs, learners can express changes in their activities, such as starting a new hobby or quitting a habit.

The video follows a clear progression: first, it explains the construction for starting an action using the verb 시작하다 (to start). Second, it covers multiple ways to express stopping an action, ranging from using the adverb 그만 (stop/no more) to specific verbs like 그만하다 (to stop doing) and 그만두다 (to quit). Each point is supported by practical example sentences that demonstrate conjugation in various tenses and politeness levels.

### How to Say "To Start Doing"
To express that you have started or will start an action, the instructor demonstrates a specific formula on screen: **Action Verb Stem + 기(를) 시작(을) 하다**. The process begins by taking an action verb and removing the 다 to find the stem. You then attach the nominalizing suffix 기. While the object markers 를 and 을 are shown in parentheses, the instructor notes they are optional and often omitted in natural speech. Finally, you add the verb 시작하다, which means "to start" or "to begin," and conjugate it as needed.

The instructor provides a visual transformation of the verb 먹다 (to eat). By removing 다 and adding the suffix, it becomes **먹기 시작하다 (to start eating)**. This pattern is then applied to several full sentences:
- **이미 저녁을 먹기 시작했어요.** / I already started eating dinner.
- **내일부터 한국어를 배우기 시작할 거예요.** / From tomorrow, I'll start learning Korean.
- **어제부터 다이어트를 하기 시작했어요.** / I started dieting from yesterday.
- **지금 자동차를 만들기 시작하고 싶어요.** / Now I want to start making a car.

---

### How to Say "To Stop Doing"
The instructor explains that the simplest way to say "to stop doing" something is to use the adverb **그만** directly before an action verb. This functions similarly to saying "stop" or "no more" in English. For example, placing it before the verb 먹다 results in **그만 먹다 (to stop eating)**.

A common example of this usage is the sentence:
> **너무 배불러서 그만 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to stop eating because I'm too full.

Beyond using the adverb with other verbs, the instructor introduces the verb **그만하다** (to stop doing it). This can be used as a command, such as **그만하세요 (Stop it / Stop doing it)**, or to describe an action that has ended. Additionally, the verb **그만두다** is introduced as a synonym that specifically carries the nuance of "to quit." Both can be used to describe stopping a long-term activity like studying a language:
- **프랑스어 공부를 그만했어요.** / I stopped studying French.
- **프랑스어 공부를 그만뒀어요.** / I stopped studying French (I quit).

The lesson concludes with examples of using 그만 in social contexts, such as ending a relationship or telling someone to stop a specific behavior:
- **우리 이제 그만 만나요.** / Let's stop meeting now.
- **다른 남자들을 그만 보세요.** / Stop looking at other guys.
