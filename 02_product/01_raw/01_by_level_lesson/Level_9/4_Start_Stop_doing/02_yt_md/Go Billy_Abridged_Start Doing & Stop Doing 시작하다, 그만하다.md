### How to Say "To Start Doing": ~기 시작하다

To express that one has started or begun an action, the instructor explains a simple two-step conjugation process. First, take the stem of an action verb and attach the suffix 기. This transformation turns the verb into a noun, functioning similarly to the "-ing" gerund in English. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하기 (doing). Second, add a space followed by the verb 시작하다, which means "to start" or "to begin." The literal meaning of the resulting phrase is "to start the doing" of an action.

The instructor demonstrates this with several examples on the whiteboard. To say "Please start now," one can simply use the verb 시작하다 alone as 지금 시작하세요. However, when combined with another action, the full form is required. For instance, "I started making breakfast" is expressed as 아침을 만들기 시작했어요. Here, 만들기 (making) is combined with the past tense of 시작하다. Another common usage involves the time-since expression: 한국어를 배우기 시작한 지 2년이 됐어요 (It has been two years since I started learning Korean). The instructor notes that learners can swap 2년 (two years) for other durations like 육 개월 (six months) to suit their own situation. A final example shows the construction used for immediate actions: 아기가 방금 잠을 자기 시작했어요 (The baby just started sleeping).

---

### How to Say "To Stop Doing": 그만하다 and 그만두다

The instructor moves on to stopping an action. The primary method involves using the word 그만, which literally means "only that much and no more." To use this with an action verb, you place 그만 before the verb. If the verb is 하다, it combines into a single word without a space: 그만하다. For all other action verbs, a space is maintained between 그만 and the verb. For example, 그만 하세요 means "Do only that much and no more," effectively translating to "Stop it" or "That's enough."

A common alternative is the verb 그만두다. While it often shares the same meaning as 그만하다, the instructor points out a specific nuance: 그만두다 is frequently used in the context of quitting a job or a long-term commitment. An example provided is 회사를 그만두다 (to quit one's company/job). Other examples of stopping actions include 그만하고 싶어요 (I want to stop), 그만 먹고 싶어요 (I want to stop eating/I don't want to eat anymore), and 운동을 그만뒀어요 (I quit/stopped exercising). The instructor also shares a positive sentiment using the negative form: 한국어 공부를 그만하고 싶지 않아요 (I don't want to stop studying Korean).

---

### Nuances of "Stopping": 멈추다, 서다, 세우다, and 그치다

The instructor concludes the lesson by explaining that "stop" in English can map to several different Korean verbs depending on the context of the motion or the nature of the cessation. He outlines four distinct categories:

First, 멈추다 is used for stopping physical motion or the operation of a machine. It implies that something moving has come to a standstill. An example is 여기에 멈춰 주세요 (Please stop here), used when telling someone who is walking or carrying something to stop at a specific spot.

Second, the pair 서다 and 세우다 are used for "halting" vehicles like buses, trains, or taxis. The instructor explains the grammatical difference: 서다 is an intransitive verb (the subject stops), while 세우다 is a transitive verb (someone stops an object). For example, 버스가 섰다 means "The bus stopped/halted." If you are in a taxi and want the driver to stop, you use the transitive form: 세워 주세요 (Please stop/halt the car here).

Finally, 그치다 is used for "ceasing," specifically in the context of natural phenomena or things that stop on their own. The most common example is weather-related: 비가 그쳤어요 (The rain stopped). The instructor summarizes these by contrasting them: 멈추다 for motion, 서다/세우다 for halting operations/vehicles, and 그치다 for ceasing.
