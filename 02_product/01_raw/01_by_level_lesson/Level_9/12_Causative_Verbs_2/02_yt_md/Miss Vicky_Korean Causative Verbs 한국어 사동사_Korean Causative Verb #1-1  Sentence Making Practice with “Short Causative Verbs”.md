## Teaching Points

### Overview
This live class focuses on practical sentence-making using "short causative verbs" in Korean. The instructor, Miss Vicky, structures the lesson into two main parts: first, a guided quiz using sentences she prepared to illustrate specific verb pairs, and second, a live correction session using sentences submitted by students. The primary goal is to help learners distinguish between intransitive/transitive base verbs and their causative counterparts, ensuring they understand when to use each based on the subject and object of the sentence.

The lesson covers a wide range of causative transformations, including those derived from action verbs (e.g., `앉다` to `앉히다`) and descriptive verbs/adjectives (e.g., `높다` to `높이다`). Throughout the session, the instructor emphasizes the importance of particle usage—specifically how object markers like `을/를` and dative markers like `에게/한테` change depending on whether the verb is in its base form or causative form.

---

### Deception and Reduction: 속이다 and 줄이다
The instructor begins with the verb `속다 (to be deceived/fooled)` and its causative form `속이다 (to deceive/fool someone)`. She explains that if you are the one being fooled, you use the intransitive `속다`. For example, `만우절 거짓말에 속았어요 (I was fooled by an April Fool's Day lie)`. When you want to specify the person who fooled you, the particle `한테` or `에게` is used: `친구한테 속았어요 (I was fooled by a friend)`. Conversely, when the sentence describes the act of deceiving someone else, the causative `속이다` is required, often paired with the object marker `을/를`, as in `다른 사람을 속이는 말을 거짓말이라고 해요 (Words that deceive other people are called lies)`.

Next, the lesson covers `줄다 (to decrease/reduce)` and `줄이다 (to decrease/reduce something)`. The intransitive `줄다` is used when a subject decreases on its own, such as `실력이 많이 줄었어요 (My skill has decreased/gotten rusty)` or `출산율이 줄고 있어요 (The birth rate is decreasing)`. The instructor provides a visual chart showing the declining birth rate in Korea to contextualize this. The causative `줄이다` is used when an agent actively reduces something, such as `사이즈를 줄였어요 (I reduced the size [of the pants])`.

---

### Attachment and Seating: 붙이다 and 앉히다
The verb `붙다 (to get attached/stuck)` and its causative `붙이다 (to attach/stick something)` are demonstrated through common scenarios. If gum gets stuck in your hair, you say `머리에 껌이 붙었어요`. However, if you are attaching a "beginner driver" sticker to a car, you use the causative: `초보 운전 스티커를 차 뒤에 붙였어요`. The instructor shows various cute Korean "beginner driver" stickers to illustrate this point.

For the pair `앉다 (to sit)` and `앉히다 (to seat someone)`, the distinction depends on who is performing the action of sitting. Passengers sitting in their seats use `앉았어요`, but a flight attendant seating passengers uses the causative `앉혔어요`. The instructor breaks down the word `승객 (passenger)` as `승 (to ride)` + `객 (guest/customer)`.

---

### Cooking and Cooling: 익히다 and 식히다
The instructor explains `익다 (to be cooked/heated)` and `익히다 (to cook/heat something)`. `익다` refers to food becoming edible through heat. If potatoes are not yet ready, you say `감자가 아직 안 익었어요`. If you are the one cooking the meat, you use the causative: `닭고기를 익히지 않으면 배탈이 날 거예요 (If you don't cook the chicken, you will get an upset stomach)`.

Regarding temperature, `식다 (to cool down)` and `식히다 (to cool something down)` are used for food, drinks, and even emotions. If coffee gets cold, it's `커피가 식으면`. Figuratively, if love fades, you say `사랑이 식어서`. The causative `식히다` is most commonly used in the idiom `머리를 식히다 (to cool one's head/clear one's mind)`. Students practiced this with sentences like `머리를 식히려고 밖에 잠깐 나갔어요 (I went outside for a bit to let my head cool off)`.

---

### Wetting, Transferring, and Remaining: 적시다, 옮기다, and 남기다
A unique exception is noted with `젖다 (to be wet)`, which becomes `적시다 (to wet something)`. The stem changes significantly, which the instructor advises simply memorizing. If clothes get wet in the rain, they `젖었어요`. If you wet a towel to put on a hurting stomach, you `수건을 적셔서`.

For `옮다 (to be transferred/infected)` and `옮기다 (to transfer/infect)`, the instructor clarifies the direction of the action. Catching a cold from a friend is `감기가 옮았어요`. Transferring photos from a phone to a computer is `사진을 옮기고 싶어요`.

The pair `남다 (to remain/be left)` and `남기다 (to leave behind)` often deals with food or memories. `밥이 남았어요` means rice is left over. `사진을 많이 남기고 싶어요` means you want to leave behind (take) many photos to preserve memories.

---

### Removal and Height: 없애다, 높이다, and 낮추다
The instructor contrasts `없다 (to not be there/not have)` with `없애다 (to remove/get rid of)`. While `없다` describes a state of non-existence (e.g., `차별이 없는 세상` - a world without discrimination), `없애다` describes the action of removing something, like a stain (`얼룩을 없애다`).

When discussing height, `높다 (high)` becomes `높이다 (to raise/heighten)` and `낮다 (low)` becomes `낮추다 (to lower)`. These are used literally for buildings or voices, and figuratively for standards. The idiom `눈이 높다` means to have high standards. The instructor introduces the slang `모솔 (single since birth)` to create the sentence: `우리 누나는 남자 보는 눈이 높아서 모솔인 것 같아요 (I think my sister is single since birth because she has high standards for men)`. For voices, she teaches `목소리를 낮춰야 돼요 (You should lower your voice)`.

---

### Entrusting, Wearing, and Inserting: 맡기다, 신기다, and 끼우다
The verb `맞다 (to take on/undertake)` and its causative `맡기다 (to entrust)` are often used in professional contexts. Taking on a project is `프로젝트를 맡았어요`. A CEO entrusting a task to an employee is `중요한 일을 맡겼어요`.

For footwear, `신다 (to wear)` becomes `신기다 (to help someone wear/put shoes on someone)`. If a mother helps her young daughter change from sandals to sneakers, she uses `샌들을 벗기고 운동화를 신겼어요`.

Finally, `끼다 (to wear/insert on fingers, eyes, or ears)` becomes `끼우다 (to put on for someone/insert something)`. Wearing your own earphones is `이어폰을 끼다`, but putting a ring on a girlfriend's finger during a proposal is `반지를 끼워줬어요`.

---

### Student Sentence Corrections
In the second half, the instructor reviews student-submitted sentences, providing the following key corrections:
- **Feeding animals:** Instead of just `먹이다`, it is more natural to specify the food: `강아지에게 사료를 먹였어요`.
- **Boiling water:** For making ramen, Koreans typically say `라면을 끓이다` rather than `만들다`.
- **Saving money:** `돈을 살리다` is incorrect; use `돈을 모으다` (to collect/save money) or `돈을 아끼다` (to be frugal/save).
- **Figurative attachment:** `붙다` is for physical sticking. To say you are "attached" to a K-pop group, use `~에 빠지다` (to fall into/be obsessed with).
