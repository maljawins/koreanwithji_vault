## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces intermediate Korean grammar forms used to express that one action occurs after another. The lesson begins by reviewing the basic beginner-level connector `~고`, which can mean "and" or "and then." Building on this foundation, the instructor introduces more nuanced forms: `~고 나서`, `~고 나니(까)`, and `~고 나면`. 

The core of the lesson focuses on how these forms emphasize the completion of an initial action before a second action or a realization occurs. The instructor explains the specific nuances of each form—such as the "realization" aspect of `~고 나니까` and the "conditional/future" aspect of `~고 나면`—while providing practical example sentences and comparing them to simpler structures like `~어서`.

---

### The Basic Connector ~고
Before diving into intermediate forms, the instructor reviews the action verb stem plus `~고`. While often translated as "and," in many contexts it specifically means "and then," indicating a sequence of events. For example, in the sentence `친구랑 밥 먹고 집으로 갔어 (I ate with a friend and went home)`, the use of `~고` implies that the speaker went home after eating. The instructor notes that when using this form, the tense is not reflected in the first verb; instead, the tense of the entire sentence is determined by the final verb.

---

### Emphasizing Completion with ~고 나서
The form `~고 나서` is used with action verb stems to mean "after doing" or "upon finishing." It is derived from the auxiliary verb `나다 (to happen/to result)`. While it shares the same basic purpose as `~고`, it provides a strong emphasis that the first action was completely finished or completed before the next one began. A common translation is "after finishing [verb]" or "upon doing [verb]."

> **밥 먹고 나서 집으로 갔어.** / I went home after I finished eating.
> **숙제를 하고 나서 TV를 보고 싶어요.** / I want to watch TV after I've finished my homework.
> **수업이 끝나고 나서 점심을 먹었어요.** / I ate lunch after class had finished.
> **한국에 도착하고 나서 바로 이삭토스트를 먹었어요.** / I ate Isaac toast right after arriving in Korea.

The instructor adds a usage note regarding motion verbs like `가다 (to go)` or `오다 (to come)`. When the subject remains the same, using the `~어서` form (e.g., `가서`, `와서`) often sounds more natural than `~고 나서` to describe sequential movement.

---

### Realization After an Action: ~고 나니(까)
The form `~고 나니(까)` combines the completion emphasis of `나다` with the discovery/reasoning connector `~(으)니까`. This form is used to express that after finishing an action, the speaker realized something or a certain result became apparent. The instructor notes that `~고 나니까` is much more common and casual, while `~고 나니` sounds more formal.

> **밥을 먹고 나니까 목이 말라졌어요.** / After eating, I realized I got thirsty.
> **일을 다 하고 나니까 너무 피곤했어요.** / After I finished all my work, I realized I was exhausted.
> **시험을 통과하고 나니까 기분이 좋아요.** / I feel good after passing the test (realizing the good mood after the result).
> **요리를 하고 나니까 입맛이 없어졌어요.** / I lost my appetite after I finished cooking.

---

### Future Conditions: ~고 나면
The final form, `~고 나면`, combines the completion of an action with the conditional `~(으)면 (if/when)`. This is used for hypothetical or future situations, meaning "if/when I finish doing [verb], then [result]." Because it deals with conditions or future events, the sentence must end in the present or future tense.

> **이 약을 먹고 나면 배가 더 이상 아프지 않을 거예요.** / Once you've taken this medicine, your stomach won't hurt anymore.
> **시험을 통과하고 나면 기분이 좋아질 거예요.** / You will feel good after you've passed the test.
> **한국어를 배우고 나면 한국인 친구를 많이 사귈 수 있을 거예요.** / After you learn Korean, you'll be able to make a lot of Korean friends.
> **돈을 많이 벌고 나면 이 일을 그만둘 거예요.** / I'll quit this job after I make a lot of money.
