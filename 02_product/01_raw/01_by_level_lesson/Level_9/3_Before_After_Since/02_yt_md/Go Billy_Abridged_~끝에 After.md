## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the grammar structure ~은/ㄴ 끝에, which translates to "after" or "at the end of." While Korean has several ways to express "after," this specific form carries a unique nuance that distinguishes it from simpler structures like ~후에 or ~고 나서. The instructor explains that this form is used to describe a result that comes after a long, arduous, or effort-intensive process. It is primarily found in narrative contexts, such as storytelling or reporting, rather than casual daily conversation.

---

### Conjugating Action Verbs with ~은/ㄴ 끝에

To use this form with an action verb, you must first convert the verb into its past tense adjective form. The instructor demonstrates the conjugation rules on screen: if the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 은; if it ends in a vowel, you attach ㄴ. For verb stems ending in the letter ㄹ, the ㄹ is removed before attaching ㄴ. Once the verb is conjugated, you add the word 끝 (meaning "end" or "finish") followed by the particle 에 (meaning "at").

The literal translation of this structure is "at the end of having done [action]." For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 한 끝에 (after doing), and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹은 끝에 (after eating). The instructor emphasizes that this form focuses on the final result of an action that has been completely finished.

---

### Nuance: Effort, Time, and Positive Results

The defining characteristic of ~은/ㄴ 끝에 is the emphasis on time and effort. It is not used for simple or easy tasks. Instead, it implies that the preceding action was a long and difficult process. Because of this, it can often be translated as "after managing to" or "after finally."

While not strictly required, the result following this structure is frequently something positive—the successful conclusion of a struggle. The instructor notes that using this form with negative verb constructions (like 지 않다) is rare because it is logically difficult to describe "the end of not doing something" as a process requiring great effort. If a speaker wants to convey a personal experience or a sense of "having been doing" something, they may occasionally use the ~던 form to create the past tense adjective, resulting in ~던 끝에.

---

### Using ~끝에 with Nouns

A very common variation of this grammar is attaching 끝에 directly to a noun without any verb conjugation. This is particularly frequent with 한자 (Sino-Korean) nouns that typically pair with 하다. For instance, instead of the verbal form 연구한 끝에 (after researching), one can simply say 연구 끝에 (at the end of research).

The instructor highlights that this noun-based version is actually more common than the verb-based version. It maintains the same nuance of a result following a period of effort or a specific process.

---

### Example Sentences and Idioms

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the "effort and result" nuance:

> **이건 제가 20년을 연구한 끝에 만든 음료예요.** / This is a drink I made after 20 years of research.
> (Nuance: The 20-year research process was long and arduous.)

> **5번을 전화한 끝에 연결이 됐어요.** / After calling five times, I was [finally] connected.
> (Nuance: It took multiple attempts and effort to get through.)

> **30켤레의 신발을 신어본 끝에 딱 맞는 신발을 찾았어요.** / After trying on 30 pairs of shoes, I found the perfect fit.
> (Nuance: The search was exhaustive, but resulted in finding exactly what was wanted.)

The instructor also introduces a famous Korean proverb that utilizes this structure:
> **고생 끝에 낙이 온다.** / At the end of hardship comes pleasure.
> (Equivalent to: "Every cloud has a silver lining" or "No pain, no gain.")

Further examples using nouns:
> **10번의 실패 끝에 드디어 성공했어요.** / After ten failures, I finally succeeded.
> **저희는 상의 끝에 함께 유학을 가기로 했어요.** / After [much] discussion, we decided to go study abroad together.
