## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the essential Korean words for expressing temporal order: **전** meaning "before" and **후** meaning "after." The lesson focuses on how to attach these concepts to both verbs and nouns to describe sequences of events. The instructor emphasizes that the Korean sentence structure for these expressions often mirrors the logical order of the actions, placing the "before" or "after" clause at the beginning of the thought.

---

### Using "Before" with Verbs: ~기 전에

To express that an action happens before another action, the instructor demonstrates the construction: **Action Verb Stem + 기 전에**. This form is consistent regardless of whether the verb stem ends in a vowel or a consonant. The instructor explains that in Korean, the "before" clause typically precedes the main action in the sentence. For example, to say "I met my friend before seeing a movie," the Korean structure literally says "Before seeing a movie, I met my friend."

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this pattern:
- **영화를 보기 전에 친구를 만났어요.** / I met my friend before seeing a movie.
- **밥을 먹기 전에 손을 씻으세요.** / Please wash your hands before eating.
- **자기 전에 한국어를 더 공부하고 싶어요.** / I want to study Korean more before I sleep.
- **집을 나가기 전에 설거지를 했어요.** / I did the dishes before I left the house.

---

### Using "After" with Verbs: ~(으)ㄴ 후에

The grammar for "after" is slightly more complex as it involves a conjugation change based on the verb stem. The instructor outlines the formula: **Verb Stem + 은 후에** (if the stem ends in a consonant) or **Verb Stem + ㄴ 후에** (if the stem ends in a vowel). A specific irregular rule is noted: verb stems ending in **ㄹ** (such as **살다**) will drop the **ㄹ** before attaching **ㄴ 후에**.

Similar to the "before" construction, the "after" clause comes first in the sentence. The instructor demonstrates this with the following examples:
- **선생님을 만난 후에 친구를 만났어요.** / I met my friend after I met my teacher.
- **영화를 본 후에 숙제를 했어요.** / I did the homework after seeing a movie.
- **도착한 후에 전화하세요.** / Please call after you arrive.
- **밥을 먹은 후에 설거지를 했어요.** / I did the dishes after I ate.

---

### Using "Before" and "After" with Nouns

The instructor explains that using these terms with nouns is much simpler. You simply take a noun and attach **전에** for "before" or **후에** for "after." This is particularly useful for specifying times, dates, or general events.

Examples shown on screen include:
- **우리는 다음 주 전에 만날 수 있어요.** / We can meet before next week.
- **세 시 후에 사장님을 만났어요.** / I met my boss after three o'clock.
