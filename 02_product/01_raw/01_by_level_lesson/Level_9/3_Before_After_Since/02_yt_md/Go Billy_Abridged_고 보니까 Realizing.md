## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form **고 보니까**, which is used to express a realization that occurs after completing a specific action. The lesson breaks down the individual components of the form to explain its logic: the connector **고** (and then), the verb **보다** (to see/look), and the ending **(으)니까** (realizing). Together, they create a meaning equivalent to "after doing [verb], I looked around and realized [something new]." This form is essential for narrating personal discoveries or shifts in perspective that happen as a direct result of an experience.

---

### Structure and Components

The form is constructed by taking an action verb stem and attaching **고 보니까**. The instructor emphasizes that this form is strictly for action verbs. He breaks the grammar down into three logical parts on the whiteboard. First, **고** functions as "and then" or "after doing." Second, **보니** comes from the verb **보다**, meaning "to see" or "to look." Third, the **(으)니까** ending, while often meaning "because," here carries the nuance of "realizing." 

Visually, the instructor demonstrates the conjugation using **하다 (to do)**, which transforms into **하고 보니까**. He notes that the final syllable **까** is technically optional, meaning you can say **하고 보니**. However, he strongly recommends using the full **고 보니까** in regular spoken Korean, as it sounds more natural and common. The shortened version **고 보니** is typically reserved for formal writing or when the speaker wants to sound particularly "fancy" or official.

---

### Usage Rules and Nuances

A key characteristic of this grammar is its relationship with time. Because the realization happens after an action is finished, the sentence often ends in the past tense to describe what was discovered. While it is possible to use other tenses depending on the context—such as describing a general truth realized upon reaching a certain state—the past tense is the most frequent partner for this form.

The instructor provides a critical grammatical warning: this form cannot be attached directly to descriptive verbs (adjectives). To use it with a descriptive verb, you must first convert it into an action verb using the **~어/아지다** (to become) or **되다** (to become) constructions. For example, you cannot attach it to **친하다 (to be close)** directly; you must use **친해지다 (to become close)** to form **친해지고 보니**.

Additionally, the instructor mentions a related form, **고 나니까**. While **고 보니까** focuses on the "looking and realizing" aspect, **고 나니까** emphasizes the completion of the action ("after having finished..."). In most conversational contexts, however, they are used interchangeably to express a post-action realization.

---

### Common Set Phrases: 알고 보니 and 그러고 보니

The lesson highlights two specific expressions that are extremely common in daily Korean and have idiomatic English translations.

The first is **알고 보니** (or **알고 보니까**). While it literally translates to "after knowing, I realized," it is best understood as "it turns out." It is used when the speaker discovers a fact that contradicts or adds to their previous knowledge. The instructor provides the example:
> **알고 보니 그 사람은 이미 여자친구가 있대요.** / It turns out (I heard) that person already has a girlfriend.

The second is **그러고 보니**, which comes from the verb **그러다** (to do so/that). This is the standard Korean equivalent for the English phrase "come to think of it." It is used when a previous statement or a thought triggers a new realization in the speaker's mind.
> **그러고 보니 철수 씨가 거기서 일하네요.** / Come to think of it, Chul-su works there.

### Example Sentences and Analysis

The instructor walks through several examples to show how the realization is framed. In the first example, the speaker describes eating too much without noticing:
> **정신없이 먹고 보니 저 혼자 3인분을 먹었네요.** / After eating frantically, I realized I ate three portions by myself.

The second example demonstrates the use of **되다** to allow the grammar to work with a state of being:
> **어른이 되고 보니 책임져야 할 일이 참 많아요.** / After becoming an adult, I realized there are really a lot of things I have to be responsible for.

Other examples include realizing a loss of an item or a change in opinion about a person:
> **버스를 타서 자리에 앉고 보니 핸드폰이 손에 없었어요.** / After getting on the bus and sitting in my seat, I realized my phone wasn't in my hand.

> **친해지고 보니 좋은 사람이었어요.** / After getting close (to them), I realized they were a good person.

Finally, the instructor uses **정신을 차리다** (to come to one's senses) to show a physical realization of location:
> **정신을 차리고 보니 너무 멀리까지 걸어왔어요.** / After coming to my senses, I realized I had walked too far.
