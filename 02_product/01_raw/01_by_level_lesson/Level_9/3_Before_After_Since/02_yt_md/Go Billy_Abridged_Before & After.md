### Overview
The instructor introduces the fundamental ways to express "before" and "after" in Korean using the nouns 전 and 후. The lesson emphasizes that these are not complex grammar structures or particles, but everyday nouns that function similarly to words like 사과 (apple). Because they are nouns, they can be modified by other nouns or verbs to create time-based expressions.

The learning arc moves from simple noun-to-noun combinations to more complex verbal nominalizations. ### Basic Noun Usage: 전 and 후
The most basic way to use these words is to place them directly after a noun that represents a time or an event. 전 means "before" and 후 means "after." For example, 2시 전 means "before 2 o'clock." When these are used to describe when an action happens, the time particle 에 is typically added, though it is technically optional. The instructor notes that in most natural speech, you will include the particle.

Examples of noun usage:
- **2시 전에 오세요.** / Please come before 2 o'clock.
- **2시간 전이었어요.** / It was 2 hours ago (literally: It was 2 hours before).
- **3일 후에 철수를 만날 거예요.** / I will meet Chul-soo in 3 days (literally: after 3 days).

To refer back to a previously mentioned event, you can use 그 (that) to say 그 전 (before that) or 그 후 (after that). The instructor demonstrates this with the question:
> **그 후에는 뭐 했어요?** / What did you do after that?

---

### Using "Before" with Verbs: ~기 전
To say "before doing" an action, you must turn the action verb into a noun. This is done by taking the verb stem (the verb minus 다) and attaching the suffix 기. This is a nominalization form with zero exceptions. Once the verb is nominalized, you simply add 전 (or 전에).

The instructor writes the formula on the board: **Action Verb Stem + 기 전**.
- **점심 먹기 전에 청소를 했어요.** / I cleaned before eating lunch.
- **여행 가기 전에 돈을 조금 벌고 싶어요.** / I want to earn a little money before going on a trip.

---

### Using "After" with Verbs: ~(으)ㄴ 후
Saying "after doing" an action requires a different conjugation than the "before" form. Instead of the ~기 form, you must change the action verb into a past-tense adjective form. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 은; if it ends in a vowel, you attach ㄴ. This is then followed by 후.

The instructor provides examples of this transformation:
- **숙제를 끝낸 후에 드라마를 봤어요.** / I watched a drama after finishing my homework.
- **주유를 한 후에 세차를 할 거예요.** / I will wash the car after getting gas.

---

### Inclusive Time: 이전 and 이후
The instructor introduces 이전 and 이후 as variations of "before" and "after." While they follow the same usage rules as 전 and 후, they carry a specific nuance: the "이" prefix can be thought of as "including." 

On the whiteboard, the instructor draws a timeline to illustrate the difference. While 전 and 후 refer to the space before or after a point, 이전 and 이후 include the reference point itself. This is often used when saying "starting from" a certain time or "up to and including" a certain date.
- **5시간 이후부터 만날 수 있어요.** / I can meet you from 5 hours from now (including the 5-hour mark).
- **코로나 이전에는 그랬을지 몰라도 지금은 달라요.** / It might have been that way before COVID (including the COVID period), but it's different now.

The instructor notes that for very short durations (like "10 minutes"), it is more common to stick to the simpler 전 and 후.

---

### Immediate Time: 직전 and 직후
To express that something happened "right before" or "immediately after," the prefix 직 is added. The usage remains identical to the basic forms, but the meaning becomes much more specific regarding the proximity of the two events.
- **수업 직전에 도착했어요.** / I arrived right before class.

---

### Informal and Alternative Forms: 뒤 and 다음
Finally, the instructor covers two words that are often used interchangeably with 후 in certain contexts: 뒤 (behind) and 다음 (next).

**뒤** is very common in spoken Korean and feels slightly less formal than 후. It is frequently used with time durations. However, you cannot say "이뒤" to mean "including"; you must use 이후 for that specific nuance.
- **10분 뒤에 봐요.** / See you in 10 minutes (after 10 minutes).

**다음** can also mean "after," but it is specifically used with the verbal adjective form (~ㄴ/은 다음). It is not used with simple time nouns like "10 minutes."
- **양치를 한 다음에 세수를 할 거예요.** / After I brush my teeth, I will wash my face.
