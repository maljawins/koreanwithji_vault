### Basic Nouns for Before and After: 전 and 후
The words 전 (before) and 후 (after) are nouns that can be used directly after other nouns, such as amounts of time or specific events. For example, 1시간 전 means "one hour before" or "one hour ago," while 1시간 후 means "after one hour." When describing when an action happens, the particle 에 is optionally but commonly added after these nouns to mark the time.

> **1시간 전이었어요.** / It was one hour before (ago).
> **1시간 후가 더 좋아요.** / After one hour is better.
> **그 전/후에 만날 수 있어요.** / I can meet (before/after) that.

Other examples include using these with life events:
> **결혼 전에 여행을 많이 했어요.** / Before marriage, I traveled around a lot.
> **졸업 후에 다시 오세요.** / Come here again after graduation.

---

### Using Before and After with Action Verbs
To express "before doing" an action, the form ~기 전 is attached directly to an action verb stem. To express "after doing," the form ~(으)ㄴ 후 is used. For ~(으)ㄴ 후, the suffix 은 is used after a consonant-ending stem, and ㄴ is used after a vowel-ending stem. As with the noun forms, the particle 에 is frequently added for natural flow.

> **학교에 가기 전에 밥을 먹었어요.** / Before going to school, I ate a meal.
> **운동을 한 후에 샤워를 할 거예요.** / After exercising, I'll take a shower.

The instructor provides several examples for ~(으)ㄴ 후:
- **밥을 먹은 후에 약을 먹었어요.** / After eating, I took medicine.
- **한 번 더 쓴 후에 바꿀 거예요.** / I'll change it after I use it one more time.
- **더 알아본 후에 예약하세요.** / Make a reservation after finding out more.

---

### Synonyms for After: 뒤 and 다음
The word 뒤 (behind/after) can be used interchangeably with 후 to mean "after," though 후 carries a slightly more formal tone. Additionally, 다음 (next) can be used in the same way as 후 and 뒤, but it is specifically restricted to use after an action verb.

> **한국에 도착한 뒤에 알았어요.** / I realized it after arriving in Korea.
> **설거지를 한 다음 청소를 할 거예요.** / After doing the dishes, I'll clean.

---

### Precise Temporal Boundaries: 이전 and 이후
이전 (before) and 이후 (after) function similarly to 전 and 후 but with a specific inclusive nuance. 이전 means "before and up to" the point mentioned, while 이후 means "after and starting from" that point. The instructor uses a visual timeline to show that these forms include the reference point itself.

> **사고 이전 기억이 없어요.** / I have no memories before (and up to) the accident.
> **시험에 통과한 이후부터 연락이 많이 와요.** / Since (from after) passing the test, I get contacted a lot.
> **수술 이후에는 물이 닿지 않게 조심했어요.** / After the surgery, I was being careful so that water didn't touch it.

---

### Immediate Sequence: 직전 and 직후
To express that something happened "immediately" or "right" before or after, the prefix 직 is added to 전 and 후, resulting in 직전 and 직후.

> **영화가 시작하기 직전에 화장실에 다녀왔어요.** / Right before the movie started, I went to the bathroom.
> **전화를 끊은 직후에 소방관이 나타났어요.** / Right after hanging up the phone, the firefighters arrived.

---

### Emphasizing Effort: 끝에
The connector 끝에 (at the end) is used with nouns or action verbs to mean "after," but it specifically emphasizes that the preceding action took a significant amount of time or effort. It is often translated as "after managing to" and usually leads to a positive result.

> **두 시간 회의 끝에 결정이 났어요.** / After the two-hour meeting, a decision was made.
> **백 번을 시도한 끝에 성공했어요.** / After trying a hundred times, I succeeded.
> **열다섯 번의 전화 끝에 예약을 할 수 있었어요.** / After calling fifteen times, I was able to make a reservation.

---

### Completion and Realization: ~고 나서 and ~고 나니까
The connector ~고 나서 combines the "and then" connector ~고 with the verb 나다 (to happen/finish). This form emphasizes that the first action is completely finished before the second begins. It can be shortened to ~고서, though this is less common.

> **차를 바꾸고 나서 집을 살 거예요.** / After changing my car, I'll buy a house.
> **취직하고 나서 제일 먼저 뭘 샀어요?** / After getting a job, what did you buy first?

When 나다 is combined with the ~(으)니까 form, it creates ~고 나니까, which expresses that the speaker realized something only after completing an action.

> **회사를 그만두고 나니까 할 게 없어요.** / After quitting the company, (I realized) there's nothing to do.
> **포기하고 나니까 후련하네요.** / After giving up, I feel better.
> **한 번 성공하고 나니 요령이 생겼어요.** / After succeeding once, I got the hang of it.

---

### Conditional Sequence: ~고 나면
The form ~고 나면 combines ~고 나다 with the conditional ~(으)면. It translates to "if/when someone finishes doing something, then [result]." It describes a sequence that hasn't happened yet or a general rule.

> **강아지와 산책을 하고 나면 기분이 좋아져요.** / After taking a walk with my dog, I feel good.
> **저는 아이스크림을 먹고 나면 양치를 해요.** / After I eat ice cream, I brush my teeth.
> **강의를 듣고 나면 이해할 수 있을 거예요.** / After listening to the lecture, you will be able to understand.
