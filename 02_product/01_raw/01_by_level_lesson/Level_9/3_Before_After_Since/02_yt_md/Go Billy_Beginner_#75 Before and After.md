### Expressing "Before" with 전

To say that something happens before something else, Korean uses the word **전 (before)**. When used with a noun, the structure is **Noun + 전에**. The particle **에** is attached to indicate the time when the subsequent action occurs. For example, to say "I studied before the test," you use the noun **시험 (test)** to create the phrase **시험 전에 공부를 했어요**.

When you want to say "before doing" a specific action, you must first change the action verb into a noun form. This is done by taking the verb stem and attaching **~기**. The full structure then becomes **Verb Stem + 기 전에**. For instance, the verb **시험을 보다 (to take a test)** becomes **시험을 보기 전에 공부를 했어요 (I studied before taking the test)**. The instructor provides several examples of this pattern: **그 전에 (before that)**, **세 시 전에 (before 3 o'clock)**, **점심을 먹기 전에 (before eating lunch)**, and **출발하기 전에 (before departing)**.

---

### Expressing "After" with 후

The word for "after" is **후**. Similar to "before," when used with a simple noun, the structure is **Noun + 후에**. An example provided is **수업 후에 공부를 할 거예요 (I will study after class)**. However, using **후** with action verbs requires a different conjugation pattern compared to the **~기 전에** form.

To use **후** with an action verb, you must use a modifier form. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach **~은 후**. For example, **들다 (to take/listen to)** becomes **들은 후에**. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach a **ㄴ** bottom consonant to the stem followed by **후**. For example, **보다 (to see)** becomes **본 후에**. There is a special rule for verb stems ending in the consonant **ㄹ**: you must first remove the **ㄹ** and then attach **ㄴ 후에**.

The instructor illustrates these rules with various sentences: **다섯 시 후에 (after 5 o'clock)**, **영화를 본 후에 (after seeing the movie)**, **도착한 후에 (after arriving)**, and **알바를 시작한 후에 (after starting a part-time job)**. Note that **알바** is a common shortened version of **아르바이트 (part-time job)**.

---

### Alternative Ways to Say "After"

The instructor highlights that the **~고** form, which means "and" or "and then," is a very common and simple way to express that one action happens after another. For example, **저는 밥을 먹고 운동을 할 거예요** can be translated as "I will eat a meal and then exercise" or "I'll exercise after eating a meal."

Additionally, two other words can substitute for **후** when used with action verbs: **뒤 (behind/after)** and **다음 (next/after)**. These follow the same conjugation rules as **후** (using **~은/ㄴ**). Examples include **아침을 먹은 뒤에 (after eating breakfast)** and **아침을 먹은 다음에 (after eating breakfast)**. These forms are interchangeable with **후에** in the context of action verbs.
