## Lesson Overview
This session marks the 17th meeting between Ji and Amira, focusing on the complex mechanics of changing Korean verbs into other parts of speech. Part 1 begins with an introduction to **modifiers**—turning verbs into adjectives to describe nouns (e.g., "the studying person"). Ji explains the present tense rules for both descriptive and action verbs, including several irregular patterns. The lesson then transitions into **nominalization**, specifically comparing the three ways to turn a verb into a noun (~음/ㅁ, ~는 것, and ~기). Ji provides a detailed breakdown of the subtle nuances between these forms, helping Amira understand how to choose between a general concept (~는 것) and a specific task (~기). This part concludes with a discussion on how these forms change meaning in sentences involving emotions like fear or difficulty.

## Teaching Points
*   **Modifiers (Present Tense):**
    *   **Descriptive Verbs:** Add **-은** after a consonant (작은 컵) and **-ㄴ** after a vowel (큰 박스).
    *   **Action Verbs:** Always add **-는** regardless of the final consonant (좋아하는 노래).
    *   **Irregular ㄹ:** Remove **ㄹ** and add **-ㄴ** for descriptive (멀다 → 먼) or **-는** for action (알다 → 아는).
    *   **Irregular ㅂ:** Replace **ㅂ** with **우** and add **-ㄴ** (춥다 → 추운).
    *   **Irregular ㅎ:** Remove **ㅎ** and add **-ㄴ** (어떻다 → 어떤).
    *   **있다/없다:** These are exceptions and always take **-는** (있는, 없는).
*   **곳 vs. 데:**
    *   **곳:** Refers specifically to a physical location and works with 이/그/저.
    *   **데:** Much broader; refers to locations but also to **situations** or purposes (e.g., "medicine for when your head hurts"). It does not work with 이/그/저.
*   **Nominalization Forms:**
    *   **~음/ㅁ:** Creates fixed, independent nouns (삶, 꿈). Used in "음슴체" (online slang) to sound factual and cold.
    *   **~는 것 (거):** Refers to the idea, thought, or concept of an action.
    *   **~기:** Refers to the activity or task at hand; often used in lists or tests.
*   **Nuance Comparison:**
    *   **전화하는 게 무서워요:** The *idea* of calling is scary (subjective/general).
    *   **전화하기(가) 무서워요:** The *task* of calling is scary (objective/specific activity).

## Lesson Flow
Ji starts by defining a modifier as a verb acting as an adjective. He uses a table to show the conjugation rules, noting that they are identical to the connector **~는데** but without the **데**. He walks through descriptive verb rules (은/ㄴ) and action verb rules (는), highlighting the lack of a specific word for "favorite" in Korean, which is instead expressed as **가장 좋아하는** (the thing I like the most).

Amira expresses difficulty with creating long sentences. Ji uses a visual slide to break down a complex sentence—"The song that I like the most these days is BTS's Butter"—into its components (Subject, Time, How, Verb) to show how a whole clause modifies a single noun.

The lesson moves to the difference between **곳** and **데**. Ji explains that while both mean "place," **데** is used for situations, such as "food for losing weight" (**살 빼는 데 좋은 음식**). Finally, Ji introduces nominalization. He explains the **~음/ㅁ** form as a way to create fixed nouns and mentions its use in online slang (**음슴체**). He then spends significant time on the subtle differences between **~는 것** and **~기**, using examples like "scared of calling" vs. "scared to call" to illustrate the shift from concept to task.

## Student Notes
*   **Sentence Construction Tip:** When building long sentences with modifiers, think of the modifier clause as a "sentence within a sentence." Identify the main noun first, then place the descriptive clause (S-T-H-V) before it.
*   **Vocabulary:**
    *   **가장 / 제일:** Most (used to express "favorite").
    *   **~는 중이다:** In the middle of [doing].
    *   **무섭다:** Can mean both "scared" and "scary" depending on context.
    *   **지루하다:** Can mean both "bored" and "boring."
*   **Grammar Nuance:** Use **~기 좋다** or **~기 어렵다** when describing the objective quality of an activity (e.g., "This cafe is good for reading"). Use **~는 게 좋다/어렵다** for personal feelings about the act.
