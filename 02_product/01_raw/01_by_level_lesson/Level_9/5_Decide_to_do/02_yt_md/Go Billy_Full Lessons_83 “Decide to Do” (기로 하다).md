### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar structure used to express making a decision or deciding to perform a specific action. The lesson focuses on the construction ~기로 하다, which allows speakers to move beyond simple statements of fact to describing their intentions and resolutions.

The learning arc begins with a comparison between simple past tense and the "decided to" form. It then moves into the mechanical rules of conjugation, provides several practical examples in various contexts (including negative forms), and concludes with a more formal alternative using the verb 결정하다 (to decide).

---

### The ~기로 하다 Structure
To express that you have decided to do something, you must use an action verb. The instructor emphasizes that this form is specifically for action verbs, such as 먹다 (to eat) or 가다 (to go). The construction is formed by taking the verb stem (removing 다) and attaching 기로, followed by the verb 하다.

In almost all conversational contexts, the final verb 하다 is conjugated into the past tense because the decision-making process is viewed as a completed event. Therefore, the structure typically ends in 했어요 or 했습니다. The instructor provides a visual comparison to illustrate the difference:
- **저는 가게에 갔어요.** / I went to the store.
- **저는 가게에 가기로 했어요.** / I decided to go to the store.

The instructor highlights a common mistake for learners: only the final 하다 should be conjugated. The main action verb must remain in its base stem form before 기로. For example, 가다 becomes 가기로 했어요, not 갔기로 했어요.

---

### Examples and Conjugation Practice
The video demonstrates the transformation of several common verbs into this "decided to" form. The verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하기로 하다, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹기로 하다, and 가다 (to go) becomes 가기로 하다. 

Further sentence-level examples are provided to show the grammar in use:
> **오늘은 제가 설거지를 하기로 했어요.** / Today I decided to do the dishes.
> **새로운 TV를 사기로 했어요.** / I decided to buy a new TV.

---

### Negative Decisions
When deciding *not* to do something, there are two ways to apply the negative. First, one can use the ~지 않다 form attached to the verb stem before the grammar point.
> **공부하지 않기로 했어요.** / I decided not to study.

Alternatively, the short negative 안 can be placed before the verb.
> **공부를 안 하기로 했어요.** / I decided not to study.

In both cases, the final verb remains 하기로 했어요 to indicate that a decision was made, while the negation applies to the specific action being avoided.

---

### Formal Alternative: 결정하다
While ~기로 하다 is the standard way to express a decision, the instructor introduces an optional, more direct alternative. The verb 하다 can be replaced with 결정하다, which literally means "to make a decision" or "to decide." This version carries the same basic meaning but feels slightly more formal or emphatic about the act of deciding.

The instructor provides an example of this more formal usage:
> **소방관이 되기로 결정했어요.** / I decided to become a firefighter.
