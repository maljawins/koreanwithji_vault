## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form 기로 하다, which is used to express that a speaker has decided or promised to perform a specific action. This lesson focuses on the construction, common translations, and practical usage of the form in various conversational contexts. The instructor emphasizes that this form is almost exclusively used with action verbs, as it describes a choice or commitment made by an individual.

The learning arc begins with the basic mechanical rules of attaching the suffix to a verb stem and moves into the nuances of tense. While the literal meaning is "to decide to," the instructor demonstrates how it can naturally translate into English as "promised," "planned," "agreed," or even "supposed to" depending on the situation. The lesson concludes with a series of example sentences that illustrate these different shades of meaning, including how to use the form with negative verbs and alternative verbs like 결정하다.

---

### Basic Construction and Tense
To use this form, you take an action verb stem and attach 기로 followed by a conjugated form of the verb 하다. The instructor demonstrates this on screen by taking the verb 하다 (to do), removing the dictionary ending 다 to get the stem 하, and then adding the components to create 하기로 하다. It is critical to note that this form only applies to action verbs; it cannot be used with descriptive verbs (adjectives) because one cannot "decide" to be a certain state, such as being tall or green.

While the form can be used in the present tense, it is most commonly used in the past tense as 기로 했어요 or 기로 했습니다. This is because the act of deciding or promising usually occurs before the moment of speaking. When conjugated in the past, the focus is on the decision already made. If used in the present tense, such as 하기로 해요, it often functions as a way to say "let's" or to establish a mutual agreement in the moment.

---

### Nuances in Translation
The instructor provides a list of natural English translations for 기로 하다 to help learners understand its pragmatic use. Beyond "decided to," it can mean "resolved to," "planned to," "agreed to," or "promised to." A particularly useful translation is "supposed to." This is often used when a decision was made in the past but the action did not actually happen. For example, if someone says they decided to go to the park but ended up staying home, the translation "I was supposed to go" captures the nuance of a failed plan.

---

### Expanded Usage and Negation
The verb 하다 in this pattern can be replaced by more specific verbs to emphasize the nature of the decision. The instructor writes out 결정(을) 하다 (to decide) and 약속(을) 하다 (to promise) as common substitutes. Using 결정하다 makes the sentence sound more formal or deliberate, while 약속하다 explicitly frames the decision as a commitment made to another person. Another alternative mentioned is 결심하다 (to resolve).

To express a decision *not* to do something, the instructor explains two methods of negation. One can place the negative adverb 안 before the action verb stem or use the ~지 않다 form. The resulting structure would be [안 + Verb Stem + 기로 하다] or [Verb Stem + 지 않기로 하다]. This allows the speaker to express a promise or decision to avoid a certain action.

---

### Practical Examples and "Let's" Usage
The instructor walks through several sentences to show the form in action. In the sentence 오늘은 친구와 만나기로 했어요 (Today I decided to meet with a friend), the past tense 했어요 indicates the plan was already set. In contrast, the sentence 나머지는 다음에 이야기 하기로 해요 (Let's talk about the rest next time) uses the present tense 해요 to propose a mutual agreement, effectively functioning as a polite "let's."

Other examples highlight the "supposed to" nuance and specific life events:
- **어제 가기로 했는데 못 갔어요.** / I was supposed to go yesterday, but I couldn't.
- **저녁으로 닭가슴살을 먹기로 했어요.** / I decided to eat chicken breast for dinner.
- **다음 달에 결혼식을 올리기로 했어요.** / We decided to hold our wedding ceremony next month.
- **너무 힘드니까 청소는 내일 하기로 해요.** / Since it's so hard, let's do the cleaning tomorrow.
- **우리는 둘만 낳아서 잘 키우기로 했어요.** / We decided to only have two [children] and raise them well.
- **너무 바빠서 신혼여행은 내년에 가기로 결정했어요.** / Because we are so busy, we decided to go on our honeymoon next year.
