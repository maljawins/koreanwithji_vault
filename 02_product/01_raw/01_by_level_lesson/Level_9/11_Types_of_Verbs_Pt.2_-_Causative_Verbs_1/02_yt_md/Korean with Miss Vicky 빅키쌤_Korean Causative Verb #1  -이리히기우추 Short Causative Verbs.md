## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the concept of causative verbs and sentences in Korean, comparing them to English structures where one person (the causer) makes or helps another person (the causee) perform an action. In English, this is often achieved through verbs like "make," "have," "get," or "help." In Korean, the grammar is more specific, requiring the use of dedicated causative verb forms rather than just adding an auxiliary verb to an active sentence.

The lesson focuses on "short causative verbs" (단형사동사), which are formed by adding specific suffixes to the stem of an active verb or adjective. The instructor emphasizes that causative verbs are always transitive because they inherently require both a subject (the causer) and an object (the causee). The teaching arc moves from basic sentence structure to detailed categorization based on whether the original verb was intransitive, an adjective, or transitive, as each category dictates which particles are used to mark the causee.

---

### Basic Structure of Causative Sentences

A causative sentence identifies who caused an action to happen. The instructor uses the example of "waking up." In an active sentence like "The child woke up" (아이가 깼어요), the child is the subject performing the action alone. In a causative sentence like "Mom woke up the child" (엄마가 아이를 깨웠어요), Mom is the causer and the subject, while the child is the causee and the object.

The fundamental word order for a Korean causative sentence is: Causer (Subject) + Causee (Object) + Causative Verb. The instructor notes that you cannot simply use the active verb 깨다 (to wake up) to mean "to wake someone up"; you must use the specific causative form 깨우다.

---

### Three Ways to Form Causative Verbs

The instructor outlines three primary methods for creating causative verbs in Korean. First, adding suffixes like 이, 리, 히, 기, 우, 추 (and rarely 구, 시, 애) to a verb or adjective stem. These are the "short causative verbs" focused on in this lesson. Second, changing the 하다 ending of a verb to 시키다. Third, using the "long form" causative construction by adding -게 하다, -게 만들다, -게 시키다, -게 해주다, or -게 도와주다 to the verb stem. The latter two methods are reserved for a future lesson.

---

### Suffix Groups and Categorization

While there are some patterns—such as ㄱ-ending stems often taking 이 and ㄹ-ending stems taking 리—there is no absolute rule for which suffix applies to which word. Therefore, the instructor categorizes the verbs by their original type to help with particle usage.

**Group 1: From Intransitive Verbs**
Intransitive verbs like 울다 (to cry), 웃다 (to laugh), and 자다 (to sleep) do not require an object in their active form. When transformed into causative verbs, the causee is typically marked with the object marker 을/를.

- **늘다 (to increase/improve) → 늘리다 (to make something increase/improve)**
  Active: 한국어 실력이 늘었어요. (My Korean skill improved.)
  Causative: 저는 한국어 실력을 늘리고 싶어요. (I want to improve my Korean skill.)
- **웃다 (to laugh) → 웃기다 (to make someone laugh / to be funny)**
  Active: 엄마가 자주 웃어요. (Mom laughs often.)
  Causative: 아빠는 엄마를 자주 웃겨요. (Dad often makes Mom laugh.)
- **앉다 (to sit) → 앉히다 (to seat someone / make someone sit)**
  Active: 손님들이 소파에 앉았어요. (The guests sat on the couch.)
  Causative: 저는 손님들을 소파에 앉혔어요. (I seated the guests on the couch.)
- **살다 (to live/survive) → 살리다 (to save/rescue/make live)**
  Active: 친구가 살았어요. (My friend survived.)
  Causative: 의사 선생님께서 저를 살렸어요. (The doctor saved me/made me live.)

**Group 2: From Adjectives (Descriptive Verbs)**
Adjectives describe a state. When made causative, they describe making something reach that state. The causee is marked with 을/를.

- **넓다 (to be wide/spacious) → 넓히다 (to widen)**
  Example: 외국어 공부는 제 시야를 넓혀 줬어요. (Learning a foreign language widened my horizon.)
  Note: 속이 넓다 or 마음이 넓다 means to be generous.
- **좁다 (to be narrow) → 좁히다 (to narrow something down)**
  Example: 의견 차이를 좁히려면 대화가 필요해요. (If you want to close the gap of opinions, conversation is needed.)
- **비다 (to be empty) → 비우다 (to empty something)**
  Example: 쓰레기통을 오늘 비워야겠어요. (I'd better empty the trash can today.)
- **차다 (to be full) → 채우다 (to fill something)**
  Example: 빈칸을 채우세요. (Please fill in the blank.)
- **늦다 (to be late) → 늦추다 (to postpone/push back)**
  Example: 회의 시간을 한 시간 늦출 수 있을까요? (Can we push back our meeting time by an hour?)

**Group 3: From Transitive Verbs**
Transitive verbs already have an object in their active form. When made causative, the sentence ends up with two objects: the original object and the causee. To avoid redundancy, the causee (if a person or animal) is usually marked with 에게 or 한테, while the direct object keeps 을/를.

- **먹다 (to eat) → 먹이다 (to feed)**
  Causative: 저는 고양이한테 생선을 먹여요. (I feed fish to my cat.)
- **입다 (to wear) → 입히다 (to dress someone)**
  Causative: 제가 제 딸에게 예쁜 드레스를 입혔어요. (I dressed my daughter in a pretty dress.)
- **풀다 (to solve) → 풀리다 (to make someone solve)**
  Causative: 선생님이 학생들한테 단어 퀴즈를 풀렸어요. (The teacher had the students solve the word quiz.)
- **안다 (to hug/hold) → 안기다 (to make someone hold/hug)**
  Causative: 제가 제 남편한테 아기를 안겼어요. (I made my husband hold the baby.)

---

### Special Cases: 타다, 보다, and 듣다

The instructor highlights verbs with unique nuances or overlapping forms.

**타다 (to ride) vs. 태우다 (to give a ride)**
There is a distinction between 차를 타다 (to use a car as transportation/drive) and 차에 타다 (the physical action of getting inside). Consequently, 차를 태우다 means "to give someone a ride," while 차에 태우다 means "to have someone get in the car."

**Causative vs. Passive Forms**
For some verbs, the causative and passive forms are identical. Context and particle usage are key to distinguishing them.
- **읽히다:** Can mean "to make someone read" (causative) or "to be read" (passive).
- **보이다:** Can mean "to show" (causative) or "to be seen" (passive).
- **들리다:** Can mean "to let someone hear/tell" (causative) or "to be heard" (passive).

The instructor notes that for 보이다 and 들리다, it is much more common to use the forms 보여주다 and 들려주다 when the meaning is causative.

---

### The Role of -아/어 주다

In causative sentences, adding the auxiliary verb -아/어 주다 is very common when the action is beneficial to the causee or performed as a favor. For example, instead of just 살렸다 (saved), using 살려 주셨어요 implies a helpful or respectful tone. Similarly, when thanking someone for an action, the pattern [Verb] + -아/어 줘서/주셔서 고맙습니다 is standard.
