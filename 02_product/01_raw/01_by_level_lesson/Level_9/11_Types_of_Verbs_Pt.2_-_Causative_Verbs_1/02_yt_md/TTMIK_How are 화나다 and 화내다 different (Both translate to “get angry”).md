## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor, Hyunwoo Sun, addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the difference between the verbs 화나다 and 화내다. While both are frequently translated into English as "to get angry," they represent two different perspectives on the emotion. This distinction is rooted in the fundamental difference between the verbs 나다 and 내다, which form a pair of "intransitive/passive" versus "transitive/active" actions.

The lesson begins by defining the core meanings of 나다 and 내다 and then applies these concepts to several common word pairs. By understanding the underlying logic of how these verbs function, learners can correctly choose between them based on whether they are describing an internal feeling that occurs naturally or an external action that a person chooses to perform.

---

### The Core Concept: 나다 vs. 내다
The instructor explains that the verb 나다 essentially means "to come out" or "to come into existence." It describes something that happens to a person or a situation, often suddenly or without deliberate effort. For example, if someone suddenly has money they didn't have before, you might ask where it "came from" using 나다. Visually, the instructor represents this with an arrow pointing toward a person, signifying something happening to them.

In contrast, 내다 means "to push out" or "to put out." It is an active verb describing something a person does intentionally. The visual representation for this is an arrow pointing away from a person, signifying an output or an action directed outward. This distinction is crucial for grammar: 나다 is typically used with the subject marking particles 이/가, while 내다 is used with the object marking particles 을/를 because it involves doing something to an object.

---

### Anger: 화나다 vs. 화내다
When applied to anger (화), the difference becomes clear. 화나다 (or 화가 나다) literally means "anger comes out." This describes the internal state of feeling angry. It is something that happens to you—you didn't necessarily choose to feel it; the emotion simply occurred. Because this is an internal feeling out of one's direct control, you cannot use it in the imperative form to tell someone "don't feel angry" (화나지 마세요 is incorrect).

화내다 (or 화를 내다) describes the outward expression of that anger. This is the "output"—yelling, acting out, or venting frustration. This is a choice or an action a person performs. Therefore, you can use the imperative form here: 화내지 마세요 means "don't get angry (at me/with me)," referring to the person's behavior and outward display of temper.

---

### Annoyance: 짜증나다 vs. 짜증내다
A similar logic applies to the word for annoyance or frustration, 짜증. The instructor explains that 짜증나다 (or 짜증나요 in formal speech) refers to the "first wave" of emotion. It means "it's annoying" or "I feel annoyed" because of a situation.

짜증내다 refers to the resulting behavior. It means acting annoyingly toward someone, complaining, or being sulky. If you tell someone 짜증내지 마, you are telling them to stop acting out their frustration or stop complaining to you.

---

### Time and Energy: 시간 and 힘
The instructor provides two more sets of examples to solidify the concept:

1.  **Time (시간):**
    - **시간이 나다:** This means "time comes out," or to have time become available in your schedule (e.g., due to a cancellation).
    - **시간을 내다:** This means "to make time" or to intentionally set aside time from a busy schedule to do something.

2.  **Energy/Strength (힘):**
    - **힘이 나다:** This describes the feeling of being energized or encouraged, often as a result of someone else's support. It is a feeling you receive.
    - **힘을 내다:** This is the active effort to "cheer up" or "summon strength." Because this is an active choice, the common encouragement "Cheer up!" is always 힘내세요, never 힘나세요.
