## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of causative verbs, which are verbs used to describe causing someone else to do an action. While Korean learners may already be familiar with the grammatical construction `Verb Stem + 개 하다` or `개 만들다` to express "making" someone do something, this lesson focuses on specific causative verbs that exist as independent words. These verbs are often more natural and concise than their grammatical counterparts.

The lesson arc moves from a review of the general "making" construction to a list of ten common causative verbs. It then concludes with a technical explanation of how particle usage (specifically the difference between subject markers and the dative marker 에게) changes when switching from the general construction to a specific causative verb.

---

### Review: The General Causative Construction
Before diving into specific verbs, the instructor reviews the standard way to express causation: taking a verb stem and attaching **개 하다** or **개 만들다**. For example, to say "I made my friend angry," one could use:
- **제 친구를 화나게 했어요.** / I made my friend angry.
- **제 친구를 화나게 만들었어요.** / I made my friend angry.

While these forms are grammatically correct and useful, they can sometimes sound stiff, boring, or overly literal depending on the context.

---

### Specific Causative Verbs
Causative verbs allow for more natural expression by using a single verb instead of a multi-word grammatical structure. For instance, instead of saying "I made Chul-soo wake up" (**철수가 일어나게 만들었어요**), it is much more natural to say "I woke up Chul-soo" (**철수를 깨웠어요**). The instructor emphasizes that learners do not need to learn a causative version of every single verb; rather, they should focus on a small group of commonly used ones.

The instructor provides ten essential causative verbs:
- **먹이다** (to feed someone) — from **먹다** (to eat)
- **죽이다** (to kill someone) — from **죽다** (to die)
- **깨우다** (to wake someone up) — from **깨다** (to wake up)
- **안치다** (to seat someone / make someone sit) — from **앉다** (to sit)
- **울리다** (to make someone cry) — from **울다** (to cry)
- **낮추다** (to lower) — from **낮다** (to be low)
- **높이다** (to raise) — from **높다** (to be high)
- **태우다** (to give someone a ride) — from **타다** (to ride)
- **알리다** (to inform / make someone know) — from **알다** (to know)
- **시키다** (to make someone do / force) — the causative form of **하다** (to do)

The instructor notes that **알리다** is the root of the common expression **알려주다** (to tell someone). Additionally, **시키다** is a very useful alternative to **하게 하다** or **하게 만들다**. For example, **일을 시켰어요** is a more natural way to say "I made someone work" than **일을 하게 만들었어요**.

---

### Particle Usage and Technical Nuances
A key distinction between using the general construction and specific causative verbs lies in the particles used for the person being "caused."

When using the **개 만들다** form, the person doing the action is often marked with the subject marker (**이/가**). In the sentence **철수가 일하게 만들었어요**, the literal meaning is "I made it so that Chul-soo is the one who works." Chul-soo is the subject of the working.

However, when using a specific causative verb like **시키다** or **먹이다**, the person often takes the dative marker **에게** (to). For example:
- **철수에게 일을 시켰어요.** / I forced the work to Chul-soo. (I made Chul-soo work.)
- **철수에게 빵을 먹였어요.** / I fed the bread to Chul-soo.

The instructor explains that this is because the causative verb treats the action or object as something being "given" or "forced upon" the recipient. Using specific causative verbs often aligns more closely with English logic (e.g., "I fed him" vs. "I made him eat"), making them easier for English speakers to use correctly once the vocabulary is mastered.
