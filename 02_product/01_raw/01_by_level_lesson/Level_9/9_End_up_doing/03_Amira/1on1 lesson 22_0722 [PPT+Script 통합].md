## Lesson Overview

This lesson focuses on refining advanced grammar structures and expanding the student's ability to express inferences, capabilities, and causal relationships. The session begins with detailed feedback on previous assignments, addressing specific spelling errors and clarifying the nuances of indirect questions (~는지) and intention-based reasons (~ㄹ/을 테니까).

The core of the lesson introduces three major functional areas. First, the "I guess" forms (~ㄴ/은가 보다 and ~나 보다) are taught as a way to make inferences based on direct observation, contrasting them with the more general "I think" (~것 같다). Second, the lesson explores the versatile bound noun "줄" in the context of ~ㄹ/을 줄 알다/모르다, which can mean either "knowing how to" do something or "knowing/thinking that" something is the case based on expectations. Finally, the lesson covers various "so that" connectors, including ~게 and ~도록, and their roles in causative constructions (~게 하다/만들다/시키다) and expressing natural outcomes (~게 되다).

### Slide 5 — ~나 보다 & ~ㄴ/은가 보다
- **On screen:** Grammar rules: A.V.S + 나 보다, D.V.S + ㄴ/은가 보다. A comparison table between ~것 같다 (Thoughts/Opinion, less certainty) and ~나 보다 (Inference/Conclusion, more certainty). Irregular verb rules: ㄹ is removed (살다 -> 사나 보다), ㅂ changes to 우 (덥다 -> 더운가 보다). A cartoon shows a character saying "저기요" (Excuse me) but being ignored, concluding "내가 목소리가 작은가봐" (My voice must be small).
- **Ji's teaching:** These forms are used to express a guess or inference. It follows the same rule as the context markers "나요?" or "가요?" used for kind questions. For action verbs, use 나 보다. For descriptive verbs, use ㄴ/은가 보다. The verb 보다 must always be in the present tense here; you cannot say ~나 봤어요. This is about making a conjecture *now* based on current observation. The core meaning is "It must" or "I guess." The big difference from ~것 같다 is that ~것 같다 is for general thoughts or opinions and doesn't require current observation. ~나 보다 *must* be based on facts you are currently observing. It shows higher certainty. For example, 비가 오는 것 같아요 (I think it's raining) has less certainty than 비가 오나 봐요 (It must be raining), which you'd say if you hear the rain or see the wet ground. Look at the cartoon: the character says "저기요" but no one hears, so they infer: 내가 목소리가 작은가봐 (My voice must be small).

### Slide 7 — ~줄(을) 알다 & 모르다
- **On screen:** Definition of "줄" as a bound noun meaning "that" or "how." A large table showing how the meaning changes based on the tense of the modifier (Future, Present, Past) and the tense of 알다/모르다. Red text indicates "How to" and Blue text indicates "Expectation." Note: ~던 줄 is almost never used.
- **Ji's teaching:** Many learners find this tricky because they don't know what "줄" means, but even Koreans don't really know! It's a bound noun that only works with 알다 and 모르다. Just translate it as "that" or "how." Because it's a bound noun, it needs a modifier before it (~는, ~ㄴ/은, ~ㄹ/을). The meaning changes depending on the tense of that modifier and the tense of 알다/모르다. Also, 알다 can mean "think that" rather than "know that" depending on context. The keyword here is "expectation."

### Slide 8 — ㄹ/을 줄(을) 알다 & 모르다 (Future + 줄)
- **On screen:** Focus on Future modifier (~ㄹ/을 줄). Highlights: "Only 2 combinations where 줄 means 'How'." Examples: 한국말 할 줄 아세요? (Do you know how to speak Korean?) and 할 줄 알 거예요 (must know how to). A section on "Know vs Think" with images of rain and a clear sky. A cartoon character says "그럴 줄 알았다!!" (I knew it!).
- **Ji's teaching:** When the modifier is in the future tense (~ㄹ/을 줄), there are only two cases where it means "how": when 알다/모르다 is in the present or future tense. For example: 남자친구분 한국말 할 줄 아세요? (Does your boyfriend know how to speak Korean?). Or: 열심히 배워서 아마 조금 할 줄 알 거예요 (He's worked hard, so he must know how to speak a little bit). In all other cases, "줄" means "that." When it means "that," the keyword is "expectation." If you expected rain and it rains, 비가 올 줄 알았어요 means "I knew it would rain." If it doesn't rain, it means "I thought it would rain." There's a common phrase: 그럴 줄 알았어! (I knew it! / That's what I thought!).

The example about your Korean skills expresses surprise because my expectation was exceeded. The movie example shows an expectation (that it wouldn't be sad) that was not met.

### Slide 10 — Present + 줄(을) 알다 & 모르다
- **On screen:** Examples using Present modifiers:
    1. 그 사람이 착한 줄 알았어요. (I knew/thought they were kind.)
    2. 이 문제가 쉬운 줄 알았는데 아니네요. (I thought this was easy, but it wasn't.)
    3. 아미라 씨가 한국어를 좋아하는 줄 알아요. (I know Amira likes Korean.)
    4. 친구가 지금 자는 줄 알았어요. (I knew/thought my friend was sleeping.)
    5. 엄마가 요리하는 줄 알았는데... (I thought Mom was cooking but...)
- **Ji's teaching:** When the modifier is in the present tense, "줄" always means "that." In the first example, if they were kind, you "knew"; if they weren't, you "thought." For the problem example, your expectation of it being easy was not met. If I say I know you like Korean, it's a fact, so it means "know."

### Slide 11 — Past + 줄(을) 알다 & 모르다
- **On screen:** Examples using Past modifiers:
    1. 그녀가 이미 도착한 줄 알았어요. (I knew she had already arrived.)
    2. 친구가 어제 집에 간 줄 알았어요. (I knew/thought my friend went home.)
    3. 네가 벌써 저녁을 먹은 줄 알았어. (I knew you had already eaten.)
    4. 그 가수가 데뷔한 줄 몰랐어요. (I didn't know that singer had debuted.)
- **Ji's teaching:** Again, "줄" means "that." In the first example, 이미 (already) confirms you "knew" she arrived. In the last example, it expresses surprise or receiving new information about the singer's debut.

### Slide 12 — So that ~게 / 도록
- **On screen:** Rule: V.S + 게 / 도록 means "So that." Both work with A.V.S and D.V.S and use the present stem. ~도록 is more formal. Example: 아이도 이해할 수 있게 / 있도록 설명해주세요 (Please explain so that a child can understand). A box lists 5 usages of 게.
- **Ji's teaching:** Both ~게 and ~도록 mean "so that." They can be used with descriptive or action verbs and only attach to the present stem. They aren't 받침-sensitive. There is almost no difference in meaning, but ~도록 is more formal. I recommend using ~게 as it's more natural in daily conversation. We'll focus on ~게 because it's a prerequisite for causative verbs.

### Slide 13 — D.V.S + 게 (Adverbs)
- **On screen:** Explains that D.V.S + 게 is one way to make an adverb, but it's not a consistent rule like English "~ly." Examples of other adverb endings: 이 (많이), 리 (빨리), 히 (완벽히). Examples of separate adverb words: 조금, 잘, 방금, 일찍.
- **Ji's teaching:** When ~게 attaches to a descriptive verb, it's one way to make an adverb. However, not every descriptive verb sounds natural with ~게. Many adverbs end in 이, 리, or 히, and some are just separate words entirely. Because there's no consistent rule like the English "~ly," I recommend learning adverbs as individual words, just like nouns.

### Slide 14 — A.V.S + 게
- **On screen:** A.V.S + 게 means "So that one CAN." Mostly used in suggestions and commands. A comparison table with ~려고: ~려고 requires the same subject, while ~게 requires different subjects. Examples: 집에 가게 택시 좀 불러줘 (Call a taxi so I can go home), 모두가 보게... (so everyone can see), 제가 앉을 수 있게... (so I can sit), 앰뷸런스가 지나가게... (so the ambulance can pass).
- **Ji's teaching:** When used with an action verb, ~게 means "so that one can." Even though "can" isn't explicitly there, the nuance is the same. It expresses intention, similar to ~려고, but with two big differences: with ~려고, the subject of both clauses must be the same. With ~게, the subjects should be different. Also, ~게 is mostly used in suggestions and commands, whereas ~려고 is not. For example: 집에 가게 택시 좀 불러줘 (Please call me a taxi so that I [can] go home).

### Slide 15 — ~게 하다 / 만들다
- **On screen:** V.S + 게 하다 / 만들다 means "To let / make someone do something." 만들다 is slightly more forcible. Permission is expressed as ~게 해주다. Forcing is expressed with 억지로 or 강제로. A box shows causative verbs that don't use suffixes: 먹이다, 살리다, 앉히다, 웃기다.
- **Ji's teaching:** ~게 is also used to express "to let" or "to make someone do something." ~게 만들다 feels a bit more forcible than ~게 하다, but they are mostly interchangeable. For example: 엄마가 아이에게 야채를 먹게 했어요 (Mom made the child eat vegetables). To express permission, use ~게 해주다, like: 선생님이 학생들에게 질문하게 해주셨어요 (The teacher let the students ask questions). To express force, add 억지로 or 강제로. This form is also the "last resort" for making causative verbs that don't work with the standard suffixes like 이/리/히.

### Slide 16 — 을/를 시키다
- **On screen:** 시키다 is the causative of 하다. Used when a verb can be separated into Object + 하다 (e.g., 운동하다 -> 운동을 시키다). Examples: 운동을 시켰어요, 야근을 시켰어요, 숙제를 시켰어요. Note: 시키다 also means "to order" food.
- **Ji's teaching:** 시키다 is the causative of 하다. When you have a verb that can be separated into an object and a verb, like 운동하다 (운동을 하다), it's much more natural to use 시키다. While 운동하게 하다 isn't wrong, 운동을 시키다 is more common. For example: 엄마가 아이에게 운동을 시켰어요 (Mom made the child exercise). Also, remember that 시키다 means "to order" when talking about delivery food.

### Slide 17 — ~게 되다
- **On screen:** ~게 되다 means "To become in such a way that" or "To end up" (natural occurrence). Contrast with ~고 말다 (implies a bad outcome). Examples: 한국말을 잘 하게 됐어요 (ended up becoming good), 못 가게 됐어요 (ended up not being able to go), 가게 됐어요 (ended up going), 헤어지게 됐어요 (ended up breaking up). A box explains 어쩌다 보니 (one thing led to another).
- **Ji's teaching:** Our final form is ~게 되다. It means "to end up" doing something. It implies a change in situation or a result that happened naturally or due to circumstances, rather than direct intention. For example: 한국에 살다 보니 한국말을 잘 하게 됐어요 (As I lived in Korea, I ended up becoming good at Korean). It's a natural progression. Another form, ~고 말다, also means "to end up," but it implies something bad happened or something you didn't want to happen occurred despite your efforts.

## Teaching Points

- **Inference with ~나 보다 / ~ㄴ/은가 보다:** Use these to express a conclusion based on what you are seeing or hearing right now. Use 나 보다 for action verbs and ㄴ/은가 보다 for descriptive verbs. It carries more certainty than the opinion-based ~것 같다.
- **The Bound Noun 줄:**
    - When paired with a future modifier (~ㄹ/을 줄) and present/future 알다/모르다, it means "knowing how to" do something.
    - In almost all other tense combinations, it means "knowing/thinking that" something is the case.
    - The meaning of 알다 shifts between "know" and "thought" based on whether the speaker's expectation was met.
- **Connectors for "So That":**
    - **~게 / ~도록:** Used to show purpose. ~게 is the standard conversational choice.
    - **Causatives:** Use ~게 하다 or ~게 만들다 to make someone do something. For verbs that are [Noun] + 하다, using [Noun]을/를 시키다 is more natural.
    - **Natural Results (~게 되다):** Use this to describe how a situation eventually turned out due to external factors or natural progression, rather than a deliberate, singular choice.
- **Adverb Formation:** While ~게 can turn descriptive verbs into adverbs, Korean adverbs are irregular. Many use 이, 히, or 리 endings, or are unique words. It is best to memorize them individually.
