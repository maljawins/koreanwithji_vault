## Teaching Points

Ji covers several advanced grammar structures and pronunciation tips in this part of the lesson:

*   **Past Stem + 다가**: Used when Action A is completed before being interrupted by Action B (translated as "and then"), or when Action B happens unexpectedly as a result of Action A (translated as "but then").
    *   **나 편의점에 갔다가 올게.** / I'm going to the convenience store and then I'll come back.
    *   **새벽 4시에 일어났다가 오후에 졸려졌어요.** / I woke up at 4 a.m. but then I became sleepy in the afternoon.
*   **다가 보니까 vs. 다가 보면**: Both involve "looking" (보다) at a situation while an action is interrupted.
    *   **다가 보니까**: Focuses on a **realization** or discovery. Action B is usually in the present or past tense.
        *   **살다 보니 이렇게 좋은 일이 생기네요.** / Now that I've lived (for so long), something this good happens.
    *   **다가 보면**: Focuses on a **natural outcome** or predictable consequence. Action B is usually in the present, future, or 겠다 form.
        *   **길을 따라서 가다 보면 오른쪽에 지하철역이 보일 거예요.** / As you go along the road, you'll see the subway station on the right side.
*   **러 vs. 려고 (Purpose/Intention)**:
    *   **러**: Only works with motion verbs (가다, 오다) and can be used in commands/suggestions.
    *   **려고**: Works with any action verb, expresses a stronger sense of purpose, and can be used with inanimate subjects to mean "about to."
        *   **꽃이 피려고 하고 있어요.** / The flower is about to bloom.
*   **려 + Connectors**: Ji shows how the intention marker 려 combines with other connectors:
    *   **려는**: Modifier form (e.g., a tiger trying to hunt).
    *   **려는데**: Intending to, but...
    *   **려던**: Was about to (past).
    *   **려면**: If you want/intend to.
    *   **려다가**: Was intending to, but then (interruption).
    *   **려니까**: As I was about to do A, B happened (often with a negative effect).
*   **Pronunciation of 려**: Ji explains that 려 can be difficult to pronounce. He introduces a common Seoul/Gyeonggi dialect alternative where 려 is replaced with ㄹ라고 (e.g., 하려고 becomes 할라고), which is easier for some learners as it uses a more distinct 'L' sound.

## Lesson Flow

Ji begins by finishing the explanation of **다가**, specifically how it functions with past stems to show completed actions followed by a change or unexpected result. He uses the common phrase **갔다 오다** (to go and come back) to illustrate this.

He then transitions to **다가 보니까**, first explaining the secondary meaning of **니까** as "as" or "when" (sequential action/twist of events). He shows a slide with a cat and a sofa to explain the "discovery" aspect: **집에 오니까 고양이가 소파에 오줌을 싸 놨네요** (As I came home, I discovered the cat had pissed on the sofa). He contrasts this with **다가 보면**, explaining that the former is for realizations and the latter is for natural consequences that will happen if an action continues.

Next, Ji moves to connectors of purpose: **러** and **려고**. He uses a table to highlight the differences in verb compatibility, sentence types, and subjects. He points out that **놀러와** (Come hang out) is a standard command, whereas **놀려고와** is incorrect. He also introduces the "about to" meaning of **려고** when the subject is an inanimate object like a flower or the sun.

The lesson gets more complex as Ji introduces a table showing **려** combined with six other connectors (**는, 는데, 던, 다가, 면, 니까**). He goes through example sentences for each, such as **한국에 살려면 비자가 필요해요** (If you want to live in Korea, you need a visa). When explaining **려니까**, he introduces the vocabulary **떨리다** (to be shaken/nervous) and explains its passive relationship to **떨다**.

Finally, Ji addresses the difficulty of pronouncing **려**. He models the sound and then shows a "pronunciation hack" using the Seoul dialect **ㄹ라고**. He uses a clip of the singer PSY to show this dialect in use. The lesson ends with Ji giving Amira her assignments, emphasizing listening practice, and testing her listening skills by explaining in natural Korean that a friend called him during the lesson.
