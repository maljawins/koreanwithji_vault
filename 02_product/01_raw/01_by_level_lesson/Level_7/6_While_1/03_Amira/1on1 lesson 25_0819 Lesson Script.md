**The meaning of ~(으)며 changes depending on the type of verb it's attached to.**

“며” VS “고”

* **With Descriptive Verbs: It means "and," but it has a nuance of listing things off, almost like listing adjectives.**

**Indirect Question Modifier + 지 - 1**

Now, what is 지 here. I’ll first tell you what it’s not. It’s NOT the context marker “지”, like 하지? 가지? Although it looks the same

Here it’s a bound noun that indicates **unknown or uncertain information**. And since it’s a bound noun, so you can add other markers after it like 지는 지가 지를 지도 지만 so on and so forth.

**~든(지)**

The next form looks similar to what we just covered. It’s really easy to make because you don’t have to change anything—it’s just **든지**. The **지** in **든(지)** is optional.

The core idea of **든지** is that it presents multiple options, and indicates you’re okay with any of these choices.

Now, one thing to note about 든지 is that many Koreans get **든지** and **던지** confused.

든지 is about a **choice between two or more options. And either option is fine.**

But 던지 is used to express what you felt about something in the past.

They sound similar so many Koreans use **던지** when they should be using **든지**. So, when you hear someone use **던지** to express a choice, remember that the correct form is **든지**.

**Action verb + ㄴ/은 지 되다– since doing**

This form has an entirely different meaning from the indirect question we saw earlier. Notice there's a space before **지** here, whereas the other one doesn't have a space.

**Meaning**

When we learned about modifiers, we talked about how adding **ㄴ/은** indicates a completed action.

This **지** indicates the duration of time from that completed action until the present moment.

So, the way it goes is **[ㄴ/은] 지 [Time] + (이/가) 되다**. The subject marker is optional.

It means "It has been [a certain amount of time] **since** [doing something]."

And the verb that follows is almost always conjugated to the past tense.

2. **Common Verbs Used:** "되다" is the most commonly used verb, others can also be used:

• **넘다 (neomda):** Means "to be more than" (e.g., "It's been more than a year since I ate a burger").

• **지나다 (jinada):** Means "to pass" (e.g., "Three years has passed since I broke my desk")

• **흐르다 (heureuda):** Literally means "to flow" (like a river) but in this context means "time goes by"
