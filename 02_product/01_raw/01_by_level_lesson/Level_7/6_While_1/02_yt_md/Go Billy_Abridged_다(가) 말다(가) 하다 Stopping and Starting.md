## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the intermediate-level grammar structure 다(가) 말다(가) 하다, which is used to describe actions that are performed inconsistently, interrupted, or done "on and off." The lesson is framed within a creative bus-commute setting, where the instructor uses a whiteboard to break down the components of the form. The core concept relies on understanding the interruption marker 다가 and the verb 말다, which means "to stop" or "to not do."

---

### Structure and Literal Meaning

The full form of this expression is constructed by taking an action verb stem and attaching 다 (or the full 다가), followed by the verb 말다 (optionally with 가), and finally ending with the verb 하다. The instructor notes that there are actually two variations within this structure: one that ends simply with 말다 and one that includes the final 하다.

The literal meaning is a combination of the grammar point 다가, which indicates that one action is interrupted by another, and the verb 말다 (to stop). When combined, they describe an action that starts but is interrupted before it can be completed. Therefore, the basic sense of the form is "to do something and then stop doing it without finishing."

---

### Using the Form with 말다 (Giving Up)

When the form ends with 말다, it often conveys the sense of starting an action but then stopping or giving up because of an interruption or a change in circumstances. The instructor provides an example involving a trip:

> **눈이 너무 많이 와서 여행을 가려다 말았어요.** / I was going to go on a trip, but I ended up not going (because it snowed too much).

In this sentence, 가려다 is a shortened form of 가려고 하다가. The speaker intended to go on a trip, but the action was interrupted by the heavy snow, leading them to stop or "give up" the plan.

---

### Using the Form with 하다 (On and Off)

The more common usage involves adding 하다 at the end: Action Verb Stem + 다(가) 말다(가) 하다. This version describes a repetitive cycle of starting and stopping, which is most naturally translated as doing something "on and off" or "occasionally." When using this full form, only the final 하다 is conjugated to reflect tense or politeness; the preceding 다가 and 말다가 components remain unchanged.

The instructor illustrates this with several examples:

> **어제는 하루 종일 비가 오다 말다 했어요.** / It rained on and off all day yesterday.
> **운전을 하다 말다 해서 아직도 잘 못해요.** / I still can't drive well because I've been doing it on and off.
> **이 펜은 자꾸 나오다 말다 해서 못 쓰겠어요.** / This pen keeps working on and off (the ink comes and goes), so I can't use it.

---

### Nuance and Negative Connotations

Beyond simply describing an intermittent action, the instructor explains that this form can carry a negative nuance. It can imply that someone is not putting their heart into a task or is acting half-heartedly. If someone starts and stops a task frequently, it suggests a lack of effort or commitment.

> **드라마를 보다 말다 했더니 무슨 내용인지 하나도 모르겠어요.** / I watched the drama on and off, so I don't have a clue what it's about.
> **학교를 가다 말다 했더니 친한 친구가 한 명도 없어요.** / I went to school on and off, so I don't have a single close friend.
> **이번에도 하다 말다 할 거면 그냥 하지 마세요.** / If you're just going to do it half-heartedly (on and off) again this time, just don't do it at all.

---

### Idiomatic Exception: 좋다 말았다

While the form is generally used with action verbs, there is a specific idiomatic exception for the descriptive verb 좋다 (to be good). The phrase 좋다(가) 말았다 (literally "it was good and then it stopped") is used to express disappointment when something almost happened or was "so close" but ultimately failed. It is equivalent to the English expression "I almost had it" or "It was almost good."

> **BTS가 상을 받을 줄 알았는데 좋다 말았어요.** / I thought BTS would win the award, but they almost had it (and then didn't).

---

### Alternative Form: 하다 안 하다 하다

The instructor mentions a variation where 말다 is replaced by the negative marker 안 and the repeated verb: 하다가 안 하다가 하다. This carries the exact same meaning of doing something "on and off." While this version is slightly less common and sounds more colloquial/conversational than the standard 말다 version, it is still frequently used.

> **철수는 변호사 시험 공부를 하다 안 하다 했는데도 시험에 붙었어요.** / Even though Chul-soo only studied for the bar exam on and off, he still passed.
