## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Hanja-based term 간 (間) as an alternative to the common Korean word 동안, both of which signify a "period of time." While 동안 is the standard, versatile choice for most learners, 간 offers a more formal or written nuance often found in official documents and announcements. The lesson focuses on when these two terms are interchangeable, the specific grammatical constraints of each, and the subtle differences in spacing and usage.

---

### Basic Usage of 동안 and 간
The word 동안 means a period of time and is typically translated as "for" when following a time duration. For example, in the sentence 한국어를 1년 동안 공부했어요 (I studied Korean for one year), 동안 attaches after the amount of time to indicate the duration of the activity. The instructor then introduces the Hanja 간 (間), which also means a period of time. In many contexts involving a duration of time, 간 can replace 동안 to convey the same meaning. Therefore, one can also say 한국어를 1년간 공부했어요 to mean "I studied Korean for one year."

---

### Stylistic and Formatting Differences
While the meaning remains the same, using 간 gives a sentence a more formal, fancy, or "written" feeling. Because of this, 간 is frequently seen in official writing, public announcements, or event posters rather than in casual spoken conversation. There is also a critical difference in orthography: 동안 requires a space between the time amount and the word, whereas 간 attaches directly to the preceding word without a space. Visually, the instructor demonstrates this by writing 1년 동안 (with a space) versus 1년간 (without a space).

---

### The 시간 Exception
A specific restriction exists when expressing duration in hours. The word for "hour" or "hours" is 시간. The instructor explains that you should not use 간 immediately after the word 시간 because it sounds repetitive and awkward to a native ear. For instance, saying 세 시간 간 (for three hours) is incorrect; one must use 세 시간 동안. However, 간 is perfectly acceptable with other time units such as days, months, or years.

---

### Grammatical Limitations: "While" vs. "Between"
The two words are not always interchangeable because they possess unique grammatical functions. 동안 can be used in the grammar pattern ~는 동안(에) to mean "while" doing an action. The instructor provides the example 내가 자는 동안에 철수가 TV를 봤어요 (While I was sleeping, Chul-soo watched TV). In this specific grammatical structure, 간 cannot be used; saying 내가 자는 간 is incorrect.

Conversely, 간 retains its literal Hanja meaning of "between" or "among," similar to the Korean word 사이. This can refer to physical distance or figurative relationships between people. The instructor illustrates this with the phrase 친구 간의 의리 (loyalty among/between friends). In this context, 동안 cannot be used because it only refers to time duration, not relational or physical space. Because 간 functions like 사이 in these instances, they can often be swapped, as in 친구 사이의 의리.
