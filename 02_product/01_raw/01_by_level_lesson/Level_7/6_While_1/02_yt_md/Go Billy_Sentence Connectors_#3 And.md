### Basic Sentence Connection: 고 and 그리고
The most fundamental way to connect two sentences or verbs is using the connector 고. It attaches directly to the verb stem of the first sentence. For example, to combine "Young-hee is kind" and "Young-hee is pretty," one uses 고 to create:
> **영희가 친절하고 예뻐요.** / Young-hee is kind and pretty.

This form works with present and past tense stems (e.g., 하고 or 했고). For the verb 이다 (to be), it optionally becomes 이고 after a consonant or simply 고 after a vowel in everyday speech. While 고 connects verbs, the instructor notes that nouns require different particles like 과/와, 이랑, or 하고. To make the connection sound more polite and allow for a natural pause, speakers often add 요 to the connector, resulting in 고요. Alternatively, 그리고 can be used as a standalone conjunction at the start of a new sentence to add information, though overusing it can sound unnatural.

---

### Sequential Actions and Logical Connection
When used with action verbs, 고 often implies "and then," showing a sequence of events. A key grammatical rule is that tense markers (past, future) or mood markers (like 고 싶다) usually only need to appear at the end of the final verb, as they apply to the entire sequence. However, the instructor warns that 고 should not be used for actions that are immediately sequential and logically connected (e.g., "I went to the library and [there] I studied"). In such cases, the connector 서 is required. Using 고 implies the actions are separate items on a "to-do list" rather than a single continuous event.

---

### Formal Listing: (으)며 and (이)며
The form (으)며 is a more formal, written alternative to 고. It carries the feeling of listing multiple verbs or characteristics. It attaches as 며 after vowels and 으며 after consonants.
> **이 장갑은 따뜻하며 부드러워요.** / These gloves are warm and soft.

When used with nouns, it follows the 이다 (to be) pattern, becoming 이며 (after consonants) or 며 (after vowels). This is often used to list two or more similar items, sometimes repeating the marker after every noun (A이며 B이며) to suggest there are even more examples beyond those listed.

---

### Dual Roles and Characteristics: 이자
The connector 이자 is used specifically with nouns to indicate that a person or thing holds two responsibilities, roles, or characteristics simultaneously. It translates to "A as well as B."
> **저는 가수이자 배우예요.** / I am a singer as well as an actor.

This form has a fancy or formal tone and is less common in casual conversation, where 이고 would typically be used instead. It highlights the coexistence of two states in one subject.

---

### Dual Purpose and Extra Benefits: 겸 and 겸사겸사
겸 expresses the idea of an "extra second benefit" or doing something for two purposes at once. With nouns, it is placed between them with spaces:
> **아침 겸 점심을 먹었어요.** / I ate breakfast slash lunch (brunch).

With verbs, it uses the (으)ㄹ 겸 structure. It often appears as "A(으)ㄹ 겸 B(으)ㄹ 겸 (해서) C," meaning "Doing C in order to achieve both A and B." This is the Korean equivalent of "killing two birds with one stone." Finally, 겸사겸사 is an adverbial version that can stand alone. It implies there are multiple reasons or benefits for an action without needing to list them all explicitly, often translated as "while I'm at it" or "might as well."
