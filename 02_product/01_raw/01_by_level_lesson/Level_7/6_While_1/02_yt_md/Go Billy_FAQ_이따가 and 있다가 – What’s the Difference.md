### Overview
The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for both Korean learners and native speakers: the difference between the homophones 이따가 and 있다가. While they sound identical in speech, their spellings and grammatical functions differ significantly. The video explores the historical roots of these words, their specific meanings, and why certain common phrases are technically redundant even if they are widely used in daily conversation.

The lesson is framed around a previous video where the instructor gave native Koreans a language test. Many participants struggled with a specific question involving these two words, leading the instructor to realize that the inclusion of the word 좀 (a little) made the "correct" answer more complex than it initially appeared.

---

### 이따가: A Matter of Time
The spelling 이따가 is used specifically as an adverb meaning "a little bit later" or "after a little bit of time has passed." It refers to a future point in time within the same day. Historically, this word actually originated from the same root as 있다가, but in the 1700s, it developed its own distinct usage and spelling to separate its temporal meaning from the verb "to exist."

When a speaker wants to say "Let's meet later," they should use this spelling:
> **이따가 만나요.** / Let's meet a little later.

---

### 있다가: A Matter of Space and Existence
The spelling 있다가 is a combination of the verb 있다 (to exist/to stay) and the grammar ending -다가, which indicates a transition from one action or state to another. This version is used to show that something happens after staying in a certain place or maintaining a certain state for a duration.

The instructor provides a visual example of this spatial transition:
> **집에 있다가 학교로 왔다.** / I stayed at home and then I came to school.

In this sentence, the speaker was physically present at home (집에 있다) and then transitioned to the action of coming to school. Unlike 이따가, which is a fixed adverb for "later," 있다가 retains its identity as a conjugated verb.

---

### The Redundancy of 좀 이따가
A major focus of the video is the phrase 좀 이따가. The word 좀 is a shortened form of 조금, which means "a little bit." Since 이따가 already inherently means "after a little bit of time," saying 좀 이따가 is technically repetitive—literally "a little bit after a little bit."

The instructor notes that the National Institute of Korean Language (국립국어원) considers 좀 이따가 to be grammatically incorrect due to this redundancy. However, in actual usage, native Koreans use it constantly. In their minds, adding 좀 simply adds emphasis, making it feel like "just a little bit later."

---

### Choosing the Right Spelling in Context
The choice between the two spellings can change based on the intended meaning of the sentence. If the speaker says "Let's leave after staying here for a bit," the correct spelling would be 있다가 because the focus is on the act of staying (existing) in the current location before leaving.

> **이따가 나가요.** / Let's leave later (temporal).
> **있다가 나가요.** / Let's leave after staying/being here (spatial/existential).

The instructor concludes that while 좀 이따가 is technically a "broken" phrase, it is the most natural-sounding choice when the speaker specifically means "later" in a temporal sense, as Koreans do not typically associate the "staying" meaning of 있다가 with the word 좀 in that specific context.
