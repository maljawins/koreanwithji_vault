## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson covers the intermediate-level connector ~(으)며, which functions as a more formal and written alternative to common connectors like ~(으)면서 (while) and ~고 (and). The instructor explains that while ~(으)며 shares the "simultaneous action" meaning of ~(으)면서, it also possesses a distinct "listing" function that differentiates it from the sequential "and then" nuance often found in ~고. The session transitions from verb-based conjugation to the noun-based form ~(이)며, illustrating how these structures are used to list qualities or items in a sophisticated, formal manner.

---

### Conjugation and Basic Usage of ~(으)며

The form is attached to a verb stem. If the stem ends in a consonant, you attach ~으며; if it ends in a vowel, you attach ~며. The instructor notes that this form follows the same conjugation rules and exceptions as other common endings like ~(으)면 (if/when). For example, if a stem ends in ㄹ, you simply attach ~며. If it ends in certain irregular consonants like ㄷ, the consonant often changes before adding ~으며.

Visually, the instructor highlights that ~(으)며 has two major uses. First, it can mean "while," functioning identically to ~(으)면서 to describe two actions happening at the same time. Second, it can mean "and," functioning similarly to the connector ~고. However, the instructor emphasizes that ~(으)며 carries a formal, written, or "fancy" feeling. It is rarely used in casual everyday speech and is much more common in literature, news, or formal presentations.

---

### Nuance: ~(으)며 vs. ~고

While both ~(으)며 and ~고 can translate to "and," they carry different nuances. The connector ~고 often implies a sequence of events—meaning "and then"—where the order of actions matters. In contrast, ~(으)며 does not imply a chronological order. Instead, it gives the feeling of listing off multiple facts, qualities, or examples simultaneously. Using ~(으)며 makes the speaker sound like they are providing a descriptive list rather than a timeline of events.

The instructor provides an example comparing the two:
> **철수가 똑똑하고 친절해요.** / Chul-soo is smart and (then) kind.
> **철수가 똑똑하며 친절해요.** / Chul-soo is smart and kind (listing qualities).

In the second sentence, the use of ~하며 suggests that being smart and being kind are just two items on a list of Chul-soo's traits. Other examples demonstrate this listing of descriptive verbs:
> **저는 이 코트가 따뜻하며 부드러워서 자주 입어요.** / I wear this coat often because it is warm and soft.
> **곰인형이 작으며 귀여워요.** / The teddy bear is small and cute.
> **샐러리는 칼로리가 적으며 건강한 음식이에요.** / Celery is a food that is low in calories and healthy.

---

### Noun Connector: ~(이)며

When connecting nouns, the form uses the verb 이다 (to be), resulting in ~(이)며. If the noun ends in a consonant, use ~이며; if it ends in a vowel, use ~며. Just like the verb form, this noun version is the formal equivalent of ~이고 and is used to list what something "is."

A unique characteristic of ~(이)며 is that it is frequently used twice in a sentence—once after the first noun and again after the second—to emphasize the "listing" or "such as" nuance. While using it once is sufficient for a simple "A and B" statement at the end of a sentence, using it twice within a sentence strongly implies that the items mentioned are just examples among many possibilities.

Example of single usage:
> **우리 강아지는 제 친구이며 가족이에요.** / My dog is my friend and my family.
> **저는 유튜버이며 선생님이에요.** / I am a YouTuber and a teacher.

Example of double usage (meaning "such as" or "including"):
> **친구들이며 가족들이며 부를 사람이 많아요.** / I have many people to invite, such as friends and family.
> **화장실이며 주방이며 청소할 게 많아요.** / I have lots of things to clean, such as the bathroom and the kitchen.
> **여기는 평일이며 주말이며 늘 오래 기다려야 해요.** / Here, you always have to wait a long time, whether it's weekdays or weekends.

---

### Spoken Alternative: ~도 그렇고

Because ~(이)며 is quite formal, the instructor introduces a more natural spoken alternative for everyday conversation: ~도 그렇고. This expression can be attached to nouns to provide the same "this and that" listing effect without the stiff, formal tone.

To convert a formal ~(이)며 sentence into a casual one, you can replace the markers as follows:
> **평일도 그렇고 주말도 그렇고 항상 오래 기다려야 해요.** / Weekdays too and weekends too, you always have to wait a long time.

This allows the speaker to maintain the nuance of giving multiple examples while staying within an appropriate informal speech level.
