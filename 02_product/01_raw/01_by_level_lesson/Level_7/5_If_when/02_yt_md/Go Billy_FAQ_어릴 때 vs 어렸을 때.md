## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the choice between 어릴 때 and 어렸을 때 when trying to say "when I was a child" or "when I was a kid." While both expressions are grammatically correct and often used interchangeably in casual conversation, they possess distinct grammatical structures and nuanced differences in usage. The video aims to clarify these differences by breaking down the underlying verbs and tenses.

The lesson begins by defining the core components of the phrases. The word 때 means "time," and the base verb is 어리다, which means "to be young like a child." The instructor notes a cultural nuance: 어리다 specifically refers to being child-like in age. If one wants to compliment an older person by saying they look youthful, the verb 젊다 (to be young/youthful) should be used instead, as calling an adult 어리다 can imply they look like a child.

---

### Grammatical Breakdown: Present vs. Past Tense
The primary difference between the two forms lies in the conjugation of the verb 어리다. The form 어릴 때 uses the future/prospective modifier form (ㄹ/을), which in this context functions as a general description of a state or time. Literally, it translates to "the time when one is young." Because it is a general description, it can be used to talk about childhood or being young in a broad, non-tense-specific way.

In contrast, 어렸을 때 uses the past tense conjugation (었). This makes the expression literally mean "the time when one was young." Because of this explicit past tense marking, 어렸을 때 is more limited in its application; it can only be used when referring specifically to a completed period in the past.

---

### Usage in Context
The instructor provides example sentences to illustrate how these nuances play out in real speech. For general statements about the state of being young, 어릴 때 is the natural choice.

> **어릴 때는 친구를 만들기가 쉬워요.** / When you are young, it is easy to make friends.

In this case, the speaker is making a general observation about the time of being young, not necessarily recounting a specific personal past event. However, when recounting a specific personal history, 어렸을 때 is frequently used to emphasize that the state existed in the past.

> **제가 어렸을 때 우리 가족이 가난했어요.** / When I was young, my family was poor.

A key takeaway the instructor emphasizes is that while 어렸을 때 is strictly for the past, 어릴 때 is flexible. It is perfectly common and correct for Koreans to use 어릴 때 even when the main verb of the sentence is in the past tense.

> **저는 어릴 때 키가 작았어요.** / I was short when I was young (literally: at the time of being young).

Even though "was short" (작았어요) is past tense, using 어릴 때 is acceptable because the word 때 (time) allows the phrase to function as a general temporal marker for "childhood" regardless of the sentence's overall tense.

---

### Additional Variations: 젊다 and 적
The instructor notes that these same rules apply to the verb 젊다 (to be youthful). One can say 젊을 때 for a general "when one is young/youthful" or 젊었을 때 for "when one was young."

Finally, the video introduces the word 적, which also means "time" or "experience" and can often be used interchangeably with 때 in these specific phrases. Learners may encounter:
- **어릴 적** / Childhood time
- **어렸을 적** / Childhood time (past emphasis)

These variations carry the same meanings as their 때 counterparts and are frequently heard in both conversation and literature.
