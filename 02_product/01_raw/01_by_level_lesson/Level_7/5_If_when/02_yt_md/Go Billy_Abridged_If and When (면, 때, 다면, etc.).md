### Expressing "When" with (으)ㄹ 때
The first form introduced is used to say "when" someone does an action. To conjugate this, you take a verb stem and attach 을 때 if it ends in a consonant, or simply the letter ㄹ followed by 때 if it ends in a vowel. For example, the verb stem of 하다 (to do) is 하, which ends in a vowel, so it becomes 할 때. The verb 먹다 (to eat) has the stem 먹, which ends in a consonant, resulting in 먹을 때.

The instructor highlights two important exceptions. First, for descriptive verbs ending in ㅂ, such as 춥다 (to be cold) or 맵다 (to be spicy), the ㅂ is removed and replaced with 우 before adding ㄹ 때, resulting in 추울 때 and 매울 때. Second, if a verb stem already ends in the letter ㄹ, such as 살다 (to live) or 팔다 (to sell), you simply attach 때 directly to the stem, resulting in 살 때 and 팔 때.

A critical conceptual tip provided is to think of 때 as the literal word for "time." Therefore, 갈 때 should be understood as "the time when I go." This perspective helps learners avoid common mistakes where they might use "when" in English for a situation that is actually hypothetical rather than a specific point in time.

> **시간이 있을 때 전화 주세요.** / Call me when you have time (at the time you have time).
> **공부할 때 우리가 못 만나요.** / We can't meet when I study (at the time of studying).
> **심심할 때 읽어보세요.** / Read it when you are bored.

---

### Hypothetical Situations with (으)면
While 때 refers to a specific time, (으)면 is used for hypothetical situations, translating to "if" or "when" depending on context. It is conjugated by adding 으면 to stems ending in a consonant and 면 to those ending in a vowel. The same ㅂ to 우 exception applies here (e.g., 추우면). However, for stems ending in ㄹ, you do not remove the ㄹ; you simply add 면 (e.g., 살면).

The instructor explains that (으)면 means "let's just say that [verb] happens." This distinction is vital. For example, 시간이 있을 때 means "at the specific time you have free time," whereas 시간이 있으면 means "hypothetically, if you happen to have time." Using 때 in certain contexts can be awkward; saying 결혼할 때 (when I get married) with a specific action might imply you want to do that action during the actual wedding ceremony, whereas 결혼하면 refers to the general state of being married in the future.

> **비가 오면 추워요.** / If it rains, it's cold.
> **밥 먹을 때 얘기하자.** / Let's talk while we are eating (at that specific time).
> **밥 먹으면 얘기하자.** / Let's talk if/when we eat (in that general situation).

---

### The "Strong If" Form: 다면
For a stronger emphasis on the "if" condition, the instructor introduces the 다면 form. This form cannot be translated as "when"; it is strictly a conditional "if." It is constructed by taking the plain form of the verb and adding 면. For example, 하다 becomes 한다면, 가다 becomes 간다면, and 먹다 becomes 먹는다면.

For the verb 이다 (to be), it becomes 이라면 after a consonant and 라면 after a vowel. Its negative counterpart 아니다 (to not be) becomes 아니라면. This form is often used for more remote or emphasized hypotheses.

> **한국 사람이라면 김치를 만들 수 있을 거예요.** / If you are a Korean, you will be able to make kimchi.
> **BTS를 정말 좋아한다면 한국어를 배우세요.** / If you really like BTS, learn Korean.

---

### Emphasis and Advanced Variations
To add even more emphasis to an "if" sentence, the word 만약(에) can be placed at the beginning of the sentence. This is optional but serves as a signal to the listener that a conditional statement is coming. It is very frequently paired with the 다면 form.

The instructor also covers several specialized temporal markers:
- **(으)ㄹ 때마다:** Translates to "every time" or "each time." For example, 볼 때마다 (every time I watch).
- **(으)ㄹ 무렵:** A more formal, often written form meaning "around the time when." For example, 해가 질 무렵 (around the time the sun sets).
- **(으)ㄹ 시:** A very formal marker derived from Hanja, typically found in advertisements, signs, or formal writing. It is often used after Sino-Korean nouns, such as 구매 시 (at the time of purchase).
