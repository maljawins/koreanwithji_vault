The learning arc begins with the most common versatile markers and moves toward specialized forms that express regret, emphasis, or approximate timing.

---

### The Basic Conditional: ~(으)면
The most fundamental way to say "if" or "when" is by using the suffix ~(으)면. This form is primarily used for hypothetical situations to express that something might or might not happen. While it can translate as both "if" and "when" depending on the context, the instructor suggests translating it as "if" when in doubt because of its hypothetical nature. To conjugate, attach 으면 to verb stems ending in a consonant and 면 to those ending in a vowel. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 하면, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹으면. Common exceptions apply, such as 눕다 (to lie down) becoming 누우면 and 살다 (to live) becoming 살면.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate its versatility:
> **시간이 있으면 같이 가요.** / If there's time, let's go together.
> **저는 눈이 오면 항상 코트를 입어요.** / I always wear a coat when it snows.
> **돈이 생기면 차를 살 거예요.** / If I get money, I'll buy a car.

A related transition word is 그러면, which comes from the verb 그렇다 (to be so). It is used to start a new sentence that connects to what someone else just said, functioning like "well then" or "if so." In informal speech, this is frequently shortened to 그럼.

---

### Conditionals with Nouns: (이)라면
When using the "if" form with the verb 이다 (to be), it becomes 이면 after a consonant and 면 after a vowel. However, a more common and emphatic version is (이)라면. This carries the same meaning of "if it is [noun]" but with more weight. For the negative 아니다 (to not be), the form becomes 아니라면.

> **저라면 이렇게 할 거예요.** / I'd do this if it were me.
> **철수가 제 친구라면 좋겠어요.** / It'd be good if (I wish) Chul-soo were my friend.

---

### Describing Time: ~때 and ~을 때
Unlike the hypothetical ~(으)면, the form 때 literally means "time" and is used to describe the specific time when an action occurs. It can be used with both nouns and verbs. With nouns, you simply add a space and 때, such as 점심때 (at lunch time). With verbs, you attach 을 때 after a consonant or ㄹ 때 after a vowel. This can be used in the present or past tense without a change in the core meaning.

> **따뜻할 때 바다를 보러 갈 거예요.** / When it's warm, I'll go to see the ocean.
> **한국에서 살았을 때 매일 한국어를 연습했어요.** / When I lived in Korea, I practiced Korean every day.

To distinguish between ~(으)면 and ~때, the instructor explains that ~(으)면 is for possibilities, while ~때 is for the actual time of occurrence. For example, 학교에 가면 재미있어요 means "It's fun if/when I go to school" (hypothetical), whereas 학교에 갈 때 많이 걸어야 돼요 means "When I go to school, I have to walk a lot" (describing the actual commute). Additionally, 때 can be combined with 마다 (every) to mean "whenever" or "every time," as in 공부할 때마다 잠이 와요 (Whenever I study, I feel sleepy).

---

### Emphatic and Formal "If": ~다면 and 만약
For even more emphasis on a hypothetical condition, the form ~다면 is used. Unlike ~(으)면, which can mean "when," ~다면 almost exclusively translates to "if." It is formed by attaching 면 to the plain form of a verb. To further reinforce the "if" meaning, the words 만약 or 만약에 (and the more formal/older 만일에) can be added to the beginning of the clause. These are optional but common in speech and writing to signal a conditional statement early.

> **만약에 내가 잠이 들면 깨워주세요.** / If I fall asleep, please wake me up.
> **만약에 비가 온다면 집에서 책을 볼 거예요.** / If it rains, I'll read a book at home.

---

### Hypothetical Past and Regret: ~었더라면
The form ~었더라면 is used specifically for hypothetical situations in the past that did not actually happen. It is frequently used to express regret or relief. The sentence usually ends with a "would have" construction like ~(으)ㄹ 거예요 or ~(으)ㄹ 뻔했어요 (almost happened).

> **선생님의 말을 들었더라면 시험을 통과했을 거예요.** / If I had listened to the teacher, I would have passed the test.
> **오늘 친구를 만나지 않았더라면 정말 후회할 뻔했어요.** / If I hadn't met my friend today, I almost (would have) regretted it.

---

### Formal and Written "When": 시, 무렵, and 즈음
The instructor introduces several advanced ways to express "when" or "around the time." The noun 시 (time) is used in formal writing, signs, and announcements in the same way as 때 but is never used in casual speech. Examples include 해외여행 시 (when traveling abroad).

To express "around the time," three forms are used:
1. **~때쯤**: Informal and common in speech.
2. **~무렵**: Slightly formal, used with both nouns and verbs.
3. **~즈음 (or ~즈음)**: Has a "fancy" or formal feeling and is less common than 무렵.

> **겨울이 끝날 무렵 독감에 걸렸어요.** / Around when winter was ending, I caught a flu.
> **한 살이 될 즈음 걷기 시작해요.** / A child starts to walk when they turn around one year old.

---

### Conditional Requirements: ~거든
The form ~거든 is used to express "if" or "when" specifically in the context of conditions or requirements being met. It often translates as "once something happens." Crucially, ~거든 is not used in the past or simple present tense; it is almost always followed by a command, a request, or a future tense statement.

> **기억이 나거든 언제든 알려주세요.** / Let me know anytime if you remember.
> **결과가 나오거든 다시 말해요.** / Let's speak again when the results come out.
> **누가 절 아냐고 묻거든 모른다고 해주세요.** / If someone asks if you know me, please say you don't.
