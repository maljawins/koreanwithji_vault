### Basic Conjugation: Verb Stem + (으)면 좋겠다
To say "I hope" or "I wish," speakers use the pattern: **Verb Stem + (으)면 좋겠다**. This literally translates to something like "if [verb] happens, it would be good." The instructor notes that the **(으)면** part is the standard conditional marker meaning "if" or "when."

The choice between **-면** and **-으면** depends on the verb stem:
- If the verb stem ends in a vowel, add **-면 좋겠다**.
- If the verb stem ends in a consonant, add **-으면 좋겠다**.

The instructor provides a basic example on screen:
> **빨리 하면 좋겠어요.** / I hope you'll do it quickly.

In this example, the verb **하다 (to do)** has the stem **하**, which ends in a vowel, so **-면 좋겠어요** is attached.

---

### Irregular Exception: 비읍 (ㅂ) Irregular
A critical exception is noted for verb stems ending in the consonant **비읍 (ㅂ)**, such as **덥다 (to be hot)**. These verbs do not follow the standard consonant rule. Instead, they conjugate similarly to the polite **요** form: the **ㅂ** is removed, and the syllable **우** is added before attaching the ending.

The instructor demonstrates this with a weather-related example:
> **내일 안 더우면 좋겠어요.** / I hope tomorrow isn't hot.

Here, **덥다** becomes **더우-** before adding **-면 좋겠어요**. Another example provided is:
> **김치가 매우면 좋겠어요.** / I hope the kimchi is spicy.

---

### Using Past Tense Stems for Emphasis
The instructor explains that this form can also be used with past tense verb stems. While using the present tense stem is grammatically correct, using the past tense stem often sounds "a bit stronger" or more emphatic in its expression of a wish.

Example comparison shown on screen:
- **오면 좋겠어요** (Present stem)
- **왔으면 좋겠어요** (Past stem)

Both are translated as "I hope [someone/something] comes," but the latter carries more weight. This is further illustrated with the following examples:
> **빨리 봄이 왔으면 좋겠어요.** / I hope that spring comes quickly.
> **저를 도와주면 좋겠어요.** / I hope you'll help me.
> **저를 도와줬으면 좋겠어요.** / I hope you'll help me (stronger).

The instructor concludes by showing that both the present and past tense versions are completely fine to use in daily conversation.
