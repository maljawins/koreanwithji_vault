**~지만 VS ~데**

Alright, let’s compare **~지만** and **~(으)ㄴ/는데.**

~지만 comes from 하지만 and has a similar nuance to “however,” so it has a stronger, clearer sense of contrast.
~데, on the other hand, comes from 그런데, which is softer. The contrast it shows feels more indirect or implied. So, depending on the context, ~데 can mean “and,” “but,” “yet,” or even “while.”

Also, ~지만 can only be used to connect sentences, whereas ~데 can be used both as a sentence connector and as a sentence ending.

**When**

Next, we’re going to learn how to say if and when. First, we have ㄹ/을 때.

Here’s how to make it

**If the verb ends in a 받침** – You add **을 때**:

* **받다** → 받 + 을 때 = **받을 때** (When I receive)

And if it doesn’t, You add **ㄹ 때**:

* **가다** → 가 + ㄹ 때 = **갈 때** (When I go)

For irregular ㄹ, you simply remove ㄹ, and add ㄹ 때, so essentially, all you need to do it add only 때.

**So, 살다** becomes 살 **때** (When I live)

For irregular ㅂ, you replace ㅂ with 우 and add ㄹ 때. So, 덥다 becomes 더울 때

Now, **때** means "moment" or "time." Think of it as **"at the time when"**. This form is used to refer to specific moments or situations.

For example:

* **집에 갈 때 전화해** → Call me **when you go home** (at the time when you go home)
* **제가 필요할 때 문자 주세요** → Text me **when I’m needed** (when you need me)

So again, this form focuses on **definitive situations** or **specific moments** contrary to what we’re going to learn next.

**ㄹ/을 때 More examples**

Here are more example sentences using **ㄹ/을 때**.

Notice that the **때** clause comes first in the sentence. For example:

* **음악을 들을 때 집중이 잘 돼요.**
  → I can focus well **when I listen to music**.
  In this sentence, **"음악을 들을 때"** (when I listen to music) comes first, followed by **"집중이 잘 돼요"** (I can focus well).

The rest of the examples are straightforward, and you can review them after the lesson.

**If & When
Verb stem + 면 / 으면**

Now, 면 / 으면 can both mean if AND when. But first, here’s how to make it:

If the verb ends in a 받침, just add **으면**. So, **닫다**, to close, becomes **닫으면**

**If the verb ends in a vowel, you just add 면. So 오다, to come, becomes 오면**

Now for irregular verbs, with ㄹ verbs like 알다, you simply add 면 — so it becomes 알면

And for ㅂ-irregular verbs like 덥다, meaning “to be hot,” change the ㅂ to 우 and then add 면.

That gives you 더우면

You can also add 만약(에) at the beginning of a sentence to make it feel more hypothetical or to emphasize that it’s an “if” situation. It’s optional, but really helpful when you want to make your intention clear.

**So, like I said, 면 and 으면 can mean both “if” and “when,” because unlike 때, 면 is more about a possibility. You’re talking about something that may or may not happen. So it’s more hypothetical.**

Also, in some dialects, especially in casual speech, you might hear it pronounced as **“믄.”**

**도착하면 연락 주세요.**
That means “Contact me when you arrive.”
This is a case where the speaker assumes the arrival will happen — so it’s more like “when,” not “if.”
Kind of like saying, “Let me know once you get there.”

Second:
**만약 너네 술 마실 거면 난 안 갈게.**
This means, “If you guys are going to drink, I won’t go.”
Here, **만약** sets up a clear “if” situation, and **거면** comes from **것이다 + 면** — basically, “if that’s what you’re planning to do.”
So the speaker is saying, “If that’s the case, then I’m out.”

**면 / 으면 More examples**

Before we move on to comparing 면 with **때**, I have some more example sentences here. But let’s just go through a couple of them. And I’m sure you can figure out which ones sound more like “if” and which ones feel more like “when.”

First one: **시간 있으면 같이 영화 보자.**
“If you have time, let’s watch a movie together.”
This one feels more hypothetical — we’re not sure if the person will have time or not, so it’s clearly an **if** situation.

Now compare that with: **창문을 열면 바람이 들어와요.**
“When you open the window, wind comes in.”
This is more of a factual, predictable result. The moment the window opens, the wind comes in — so this feels more like a **when**.

**으면 vs 때**

Now let’s compare **때** and **면** side by side, so you can really feel the difference.

**ㄹ/을 때** is used when something is definite or at least seemingly definite. So, it refers to a specific moment or time which means you’re talking about something that happened, is happening or will happen.

And **면 / 으면** is used for more hypothetical situations. So, it can mean both if and when depending on the context.

**면 / 으면 좋겠다**

Now this form **면 / 으면 + 좋겠다** is a super common structure to express **hope** or **wish** in Korean.

Literally, it means: *“It would be good if…”* But it naturally translates to: **“I hope…”**

And it’s very simple to use it. You take the verb stem and attach **(으)면 좋겠다**.
For example:
**저는 너가 새로운 환경에서 잘 지내면 좋겠어.**
Literally: *As for me, if you do well in a new environment, it would be good.*
But what it really means is: **I hope you do well in your new environment.**

Now, you can also use the **past tense stem** with **(으)면 좋겠다**. It still means the same thing, but it adds a little extra emotion or emphasis — like the English “If it *were* like this, that would be nice.”

Here’s a great example:
**빨리 여자친구가 생겼으면 좋겠어요.**
Literally: *If a girlfriend appeared soon, it would be good.*
But this naturally means: **I hope I get a girlfriend soon.**

And by the way, in Korean, we don’t say “to get a girlfriend.”
We say **“a girlfriend appears”** or **“happens.”** Just one of those fun little differences.
