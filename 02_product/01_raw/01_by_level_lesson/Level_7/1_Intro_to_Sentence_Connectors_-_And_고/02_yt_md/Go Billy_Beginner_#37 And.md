## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson introduces the various ways to express "and" in Korean, depending on whether you are starting a new sentence, connecting verbs within a single sentence, or linking nouns. The instructor explains the transition from using the standalone conjunction 그리고 to the more integrated verb ending 고, as well as the noun-linking particles 과 and 와. The lesson emphasizes that while these all translate to "and" in English, their grammatical functions and nuances—such as indicating a sequence of actions—differ significantly.

---

### Starting a Sentence with 그리고

When you want to start a new sentence and add information to a previous statement, you use the conjunction **그리고**. This is the most straightforward way to say "and" at the beginning of a sentence. The instructor provides several examples of how this functions to link two independent thoughts:

> **제가 아주 똑똑해요. 그리고 예뻐요.** / I am very smart. And I'm pretty.
> **저는 한국어 책을 사고 싶어요. 그리고 한국어를 배우고 싶어요.** / I want to buy a Korean book. And I want to learn Korean.
> **저는 한국 사람이에요. 그리고 영어도 잘해요.** / I am Korean. And I speak English well.

---

### Connecting Verbs with the Ending 고

To combine two sentences into one and say "and" in the middle, you use the verb ending **고**. This is attached directly to the verb stem. The instructor demonstrates that this is a more natural way to speak than using 그리고 to start a new sentence every time.

The conjugation rule is simple: **Verb Stem + 고**.

Examples shown on screen include:
- **똑똑하다 (to be smart)** becomes **똑똑하고**
- **사다 (to buy)** becomes **사고**
- **이다 (to be)** becomes **이고** (after a consonant) or **고** (after a vowel)

The instructor highlights a specific efficiency rule when using the **~고 싶다 (want to)** form. While it is grammatically correct to say **사고 싶고**, it is only necessary to use the **~고 싶다** form once at the very end of the sentence, even if there are multiple verbs.

> **저는 한국어 책을 사고 한국어를 배우고 싶어요.** / I want to buy a Korean book and learn Korean.

In this example, the "want to" meaning applies to both "buy" and "learn," even though it only appears at the end.

---

### Sequential Meaning: "And Then"

A critical nuance of the verb ending **고** is its function with action verbs. When used to connect action verbs, it often implies a sequence of events, translating as "and then." The first action happens, and then the second action follows.

> **저는 피자를 먹고 집에 가요.** / I eat pizza and (then) go home.

The instructor clarifies that this "and then" nuance only applies to action verbs, not descriptive verbs (adjectives).

---

### Connecting Nouns with 과 and 와

When the goal is to connect nouns rather than verbs or sentences, different particles are used: **과** and **와**. The choice between them depends on whether the preceding noun ends in a consonant or a vowel.

- **과** is used after a noun ending in a **consonant**.
- **와** is used after a noun ending in a **vowel**.

Examples provided:
> **한국과 미국에 갈 수 있어요.** / I can go to Korea and America. (한국 ends in a consonant)
> **저는 피자와 샐러드를 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to eat pizza and salad. (피자 ends in a vowel)

The instructor notes that **과** and **와** are considered polite particles. They are appropriate for formal or polite situations but are less common when speaking casually with close friends. Additionally, these particles can sometimes be translated as "with" depending on the context, such as **스테이크와 감자 (steak with potatoes)**.
