## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the difference between **아니고** and **말고**. While both words can be translated into English as "not," they serve distinct grammatical functions and are derived from different types of verbs. The lesson focuses on the underlying meanings of the base verbs—**아니다** (to not be) and **말다** (to stop/not do)—to help students determine which "not" is appropriate for a given context. By the end of the video, learners will understand that **아니고** is used for stating facts about what something is or isn't, while **말고** is used for choices, requests, or commands.

---

### Etymology and Base Meanings

The word **아니고** is the conjunctive form of the descriptive verb **아니다**, which means "to not be." It functions as the direct opposite of the verb **이다** (to be). When you use **아니고**, you are essentially stating that a specific fact is false before moving on to state what is true.

In contrast, **말고** is derived from the action verb **말다**, which means "to stop" or "to not do" something. While **말다** is rarely used as a standalone verb in modern conversation, it is frequently seen as a helper verb in negative commands (such as the **~지 마세요** pattern). When used as **말고**, it carries the nuance of "don't do X, but instead do Y" or "set X aside and choose Y."

---

### Using 말고 for Requests and Choices

The instructor uses a comparison of two sentences to illustrate the correct usage of **말고** when making a request. On screen, the following examples are shown:

- **이거 말고 그거 주세요.** / Not this, give me that please. (Correct)
- **이거 아니고 그거 주세요.** / It's not this, give me that please. (Incorrect/Awkward)

The first sentence is correct because **말고** implies an action or a choice. It effectively tells the listener, "Don't do this (giving me this item), but do that (giving me that item)." It sounds natural because the entire sentence is a request. The second sentence sounds awkward because **아니고** is a descriptive statement of fact ("It is not this"), which clashes with the imperative "give me that" in the second half of the sentence.

---

### Using 아니고 for Statements of Fact

To demonstrate when **아니고** is the correct choice, the instructor provides a different pair of examples regarding nationality:

- **영국 사람이 아니고, 미국 사람이에요.** / I'm not British, but I'm American. (Correct)
- **영국 사람 말고, 미국 사람이에요.** / Don't a British person, but I'm American. (Incorrect)

In this context, the speaker is stating a fact about their identity. Since being a certain nationality is a state of being rather than an action or a choice being made in the moment, the descriptive verb **아니다** (via **아니고**) must be used. Using **말고** here would imply you are telling someone to "stop being British" or "don't choose the British person," which makes no sense when simply stating who you are.

---

### Summary and Comparison

The instructor summarizes the two forms by providing a clear mental framework. **아니고** is used when you want to tell someone that something "isn't" a certain way or that they are "wrong" about a fact. **말고** is used when you want someone to "stop" one thing and "do" another instead, or when you want to "ignore" one option in favor of another.

The final comparison table shown through examples is:

> **물이 아니고 사이다예요.** / It's not water, it's soda.
> (Stating a fact about what the liquid is.)

> **물 말고 사이다를 주세요.** / Don't give me water, give me soda.
> (Making a request/choice between two options.)
