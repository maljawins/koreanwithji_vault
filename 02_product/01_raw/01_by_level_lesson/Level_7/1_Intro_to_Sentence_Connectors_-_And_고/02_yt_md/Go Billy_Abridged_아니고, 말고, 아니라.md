## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces three specific Korean words used to express the concept of "not": **아니고**, **말고**, and **아니라**. While all three can translate to "not" or "not that" in English, they serve distinct grammatical functions and carry different nuances. The lesson focuses on when to use each form—whether for correcting a fact, making a choice/request, or providing an emphatic explanation.

---

### Correcting Facts with 아니고

The word **아니고** is a combination of the verb **아니다 (to not be)** and the connector **~고 (and/and then)**. Grammatically, **아니다** is the opposite of **이다 (to be)** and is typically used with the subject markers **이** or **가** attached to the noun being negated. While **~고** usually means "and," in this context, it functions as a signal that the speaker is not finished talking and is about to provide the correct information.

The instructor explains that the structure "A가/이 **아니고** B" is used primarily to correct a mistake or provide a factual alternative. It literally means "It is not A, and (here is what it is) B." For example, if someone misidentifies a snack, you would say:

> **케이크가 아니고 떡이에요.** / It's not cake, it's rice cake.

Further examples demonstrate its use in various contexts, such as correcting a name or identifying the nature of an object:
- **철수가 아니고 영희예요.** / It's not Chul-soo, it's Young-hee.
- **물이 아니고 차예요.** / It's not water, it's tea.
- **이건 진짜 그림이 아니고 AI예요.** / This isn't a real drawing, it's AI.
- **별건 아니고 작은 선물이에요.** / It's nothing special, just a small gift.

---

### Choosing and Requesting with 말고

The word **말고** comes from the verb **말다**, which means "to not do" or "to stop doing." This is the same root found in the common prohibition **하지 마세요 (don't do it)**. When used as a connector, **말고** carries the nuance of "stop with A," "ignore A," or "forget about A," and focus on B instead.

Unlike **아니고**, which corrects a fact, **말고** is used when picking, choosing, or making a request. It often translates to "instead of" or "not A, but B" in the context of a preference. The instructor uses a restaurant scenario to illustrate the difference: if you want to order beef instead of pork, you use **말고** because you are making a choice, not stating that the pork "is not" pork.

> **돼지고기 말고 소고기 주세요.** / Please give me beef, not pork.

Other examples of choosing or expressing preference include:
- **나 말고 다른 사람한테 물어봐.** / Ask someone else, not me (Ignore me/Stop asking me).
- **빵 말고 밥이 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to eat a meal (rice), not bread.
- **오늘은 비 말고 눈이 오면 좋겠어요.** / I wish it would snow today, not rain.

---

### Emphatic Contrast with 아니라

The third form, **아니라**, also stems from **아니다** but uses the ending **~라**. This ending provides a much stronger sense of contrast than **아니고**. It is used when the speaker wants to place extra emphasis on the correction or provide a detailed explanation.

The instructor notes that **아니라** is particularly useful when you feel the need to clarify something firmly or when the contrast between the two items is significant. It sounds more like "It's not A, but *rather* it is B."

> **저는 한국 사람이 아니라 미국 사람이에요.** / I'm not a Korean; (rather) I'm an American.

This form is common in explanations where the speaker is correcting a misconception with a bit more "boost" or nuance:
- **드라마가 아니라 영화예요.** / It's not a drama; it's a movie.
