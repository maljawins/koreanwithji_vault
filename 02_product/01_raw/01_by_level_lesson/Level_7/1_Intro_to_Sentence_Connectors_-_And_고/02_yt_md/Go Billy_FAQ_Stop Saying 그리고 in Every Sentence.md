## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common habit among Korean learners: the over-reliance on the conjunction 그리고 (and). While 그리고 is a perfectly valid word, using it to start every sentence when adding information can make speech sound repetitive and unnatural to native ears. The lesson focuses on how to transition from using 그리고 as a standalone sentence starter to incorporating the particle 도 (also/too) into the structure of the sentence itself. This shift allows for a more fluid and sophisticated way of connecting ideas without constantly resetting the sentence with a formal "in addition."

---

### The Function and Limits of 그리고

The word 그리고 means "and" or "and then." It is strictly a sentence-starting conjunction, meaning it cannot be used to link two nouns directly within a single phrase (e.g., you cannot use it to say "cheese and bread"). Instead, it is used to add extra information to a previous statement. For example, after introducing your name and age, you might start a new sentence with:

> **그리고 저는 미국 사람이에요.** / And I am an American.

The instructor suggests that a more accurate mental translation for 그리고 is "in addition" or "also." While this is correct, starting every sentence with "in addition" in English would sound stiff; the same principle applies in Korean. It is best reserved for moments when you have finished a thought and realize you have one more distinct piece of information to add, or when you specifically mean "and then" to describe a sequence of events.

---

### Using 도 as a Natural Alternative

To sound more like a native speaker, the instructor recommends replacing the sentence-starting 그리고 with the particle 도 (also/too/even). Instead of starting a new sentence to add a detail, you attach 도 to the subject or object of the new sentence to imply the connection to the previous context.

The instructor provides a scenario where a roommate's music is too loud. A beginner might say:
> **집중이 안 돼요. 그리고 소리가 커요.** / I can't concentrate. And the sound is loud.

A more natural version replaces the subject marker with 도:
> **집중이 안 돼요. 소리도 커요.** / I can't concentrate. (And) the sound is also loud.

Even though the literal translation is "the sound also is loud," the pragmatic effect is "and it's loud." This usage effectively "bakes" the conjunction into the sentence, making the speech flow better.

---

### Comparison of 그리고 vs. 도 in Context

The instructor demonstrates this technique with a second example at a restaurant. After smelling the food, a learner might say:
> **냄새가 진짜 좋아요. 그리고 맛도 좋아.** / The smell is really good. And the taste is also good.

Note that in the second sentence, the instructor actually uses both 그리고 and 도. He explains that while this is possible, simply using 도 alone is often sufficient and more common:
> **냄새가 진짜 좋아요. 맛도 좋아.** / The smell is really good. (And) it tastes good too.

By removing 그리고, the speaker avoids the "wait, listen to me, I have more to say" nuance that 그리고 carries, opting instead for a smoother transition.

---

### When to Keep Using 그리고

Despite the advice to use it less, 그리고 remains necessary in specific situations. First, it is essential when you mean "and then" to show a chronological sequence of actions, as the particle 도 cannot express time order. Second, it is useful when you want to emphasize that you are not finished talking. Using 그리고 signals to the listener: "I have completed that point, but wait, here is one more thing." You can even combine them for extra emphasis, such as saying **그리고 소리도 커요** to mean "And furthermore, the sound is also loud."
