Lesson 86: Negating Nouns and Clauses (아니라, ~는 게 아니라)

Introduction

In this lesson, you will learn how to use the word 아니라 (from the word 아니다) to negate a preceding noun. In doing so, you will also see how you can negate an entire clause by applying ~는 것 to a verb, adjective or 이다. Let’s get started.

Something is, while something isn’t: 이/가 아니라

You learned way back in [Lesson 8](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-1-8/unit-1-lesson-8/) that you can use the word “아니다” to indicate that something is *not*something. For example:

Play[저는 선생님이 아니에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s1.mp3) = I am not a teacher
Play[나는 너의 친구가 아니야](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s2.mp3) = I am not your friend
Play[여기는 관광지가 아니에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s3.mp3) = Here (this place) is not a tourist attraction

Remember that ~이/가 should be attached to the noun before 아니다.

By replacing “다” with “~라,” you can create “아니라,” which can be placed between two clauses. The speaker uses “아니라” to contrast the two clauses – indicating that the first thing is *not* something, and the second *is* something*.*For example, if we look at a very simple sentence:

Play[그 사람은 학생이 아니라 선생님이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s4.mp3)

In this sentence, the speaker is indicating that the person is *not*a student, and is a teacher. For example:

Play[그 사람은 학생이 아니라 선생님이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s4.mp3) = That person is not a student, he is a teacher

아니라 cannot be conjugated into the past or future – rather, the final clause will indicate the tense. For example:

Play[제가 선생님이 아니라 학생을 도와주는 사람이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s5.mp3)
= I am not a teacher, I’m a student helper

Play[제가 선생님이 아니라 학생을 도와주는 사람이었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s6.mp3)
= I wasn’t a teacher, I was a student helper

Below are many more examples:

Play[저것은 사람이 아니라 눈사람이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s7.mp3)
= That is not a person, it is a snowman

Play[그게 쓰레기가 아니라 저의 영수증이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s8.mp3)
= That is not garbage, it is my receipt

Play[지금 뿌리는 것이 농약이 아니라 그냥 물이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s9.mp3)
= The thing that I am spraying now is not pesticide, it is just water

Play[가장 맛있는 한식은 김치가 아니라 삼겹살이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s10.mp3)
= The most delicious Korean food isn’t Kimchi, it is 삼겹살

Play[지금 냄비에 넣는 게 소금이 아니라 고춧가루예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s11.mp3)
= The thing I am putting in the pot now is not salt, it is red pepper powder

Play[집이 바다에서 가깝지만 집 근처가 해변이 아니라 절벽이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s12.mp3)
= The house is close to the ocean, but near our house isn’t a beach, it is a cliff

Play[여기는 캐나다 영역이 아니라 미국 대사관이라서 미국 영역이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s13.mp3)
= This place (here) is not Canadian territory, because it is the American embassy, it is American territory

Play[여기서 사는 것에 있어서 문제가 공장에서 나는 소음이 아니라 공장에서 나는 냄새요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s14.mp3)
= The problem about living here is not the noise coming from the factory, it is the smell coming from the factory

Notice that these types of sentences could easily be confused with adding ~라(서) to 아니다 to indicate the cause of something. You saw sentences like this created in [Lesson 37](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-37/). For example:

저는 선생님이 아니라(서) 그것을 잘 몰라요 = I don’t know that because I’m not a teacher

In the next section, I will introduce how 아니라 can be used not just after a simple noun, but how it can also be used after a noun described using ~는 것.

A clause is, while another clause isn’t: ~는 게 아니라

In [Lesson 26](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-26/), you learned how to describe a noun using a verb or adjective using ~는 것. It is common to create one of these nouns and use it immediately before 아니라. This allows the speaker to contrast the two clauses before and after 아니라 – indicating that one clause is *not* something, while the other clause *is*. For example:

Play[저는 밥을 먹는 것이 아니라 공부하고 있어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s15.mp3)  = I’m not eating, I’m studying
Play[그 여자는 예쁜 것이 아니라 못생겼어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s16.mp3) = That girl isn’t pretty, she is ugly

In practice (not just with this grammatical principle, but all the time in Korean) 것이 can be condensed to 게. When using 아니라 to negate a prior clause, this is almost always done. For example, the sentences above would sound more natural if 것이 were replaced with 게:

Play[저는 밥을 먹는 게 아니라 공부하고 있어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s17.mp3) = I’m not eating, I’m studying
Play[그 여자는 예쁜 게 아니라 못생겼어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s18.mp3) = That girl isn’t pretty, she is ugly

Below are many more examples:

Play[저는 인색한 게 아니라 그냥 돈이 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s19.mp3)
= I’m not cheap, I just don’t have any money

Play[파업을 금년에 하는 게 아니라 내년에 할 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s20.mp3)
= We don’t go on strike this year, we will do it next year

Play[길이 울퉁불퉁한 게 아니라 네가 운전을 못하는 거야](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s21.mp3)
= It is not that the road is bumpy, it is that you can’t drive well

Play[주전자가 망가진 게 아니라 버튼을 안 눌러서 안된 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s22.mp3)
= It is not that the kettle is broken, it doesn’t work because you didn’t press the button

Play[우리 둘 다 같은 시간이 일하는 게 아니라 교대하는 거에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s23.mp3)

Play[여기는 농산물을 파는 곳이 아니라 핸드폰을 파는 곳이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s24.mp3)

The first time I knew that my understanding of Korean grammar was getting really good was when I was trying to make the following sentence:

“The reason I am learning Korean is not for my girlfriend, but for her parents”

I wanted to say this sentence to a friend of mine, and I had never made a sentence like it before. I would like to walk you through my thought process when I first had to create a sentence like this.

As you know, you can use ~를/을 위해 to create the meaning of “for.” For example:

저는 한국말을 저의 여자 친구를 위해 배우고 있어요 = I am learning Korean for my girlfriend

You should also know by now that “위해” is actually an adjective (the fact that it is an adjective instead of a verb is irrelevant aside from the fact that it has to be conjugated as an adjective) with a “dictionary” form of “위하다.” Because it is an adjective, even though it is commonly used as “위해,” it can also be used as “위한” to describe an upcoming noun – much like 대하다, 관하다, 관련하다 ([Lesson 34](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-34/)), 의하다 and 인하다 ([Lesson 78](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/upper-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-4-lessons-76-83/lesson-78/)). For example:

이것은 저의 여자 친구를 위한 것이에요 = This thing is (a thing) for my girlfriend

In order to make the sentence that I wanted to say, I used my knowledge of 위하다, ~는 것 and ~는 게 아니라 and came up with:

제가 한국말을 배우고 있는 이유는 저의 여자 친구를 위한 게 아니라 그녀의 부모님을 위한 것이에요 = The reason I am learning Korean is not for my girlfriend, but for her parents

Note that you could just as easily say:

저는 저의 여자 친구를 위해 한국말을 배우고 있는 게 아니라 그녀의 부모님을 위해 배우고 있어요 = I am not learning Korean for my girlfriend, but I am learning it (Korean) for her parents

Though those two sentences essentially have the same meaning, there is a slight difference in emphasis in the two – the first sentence emphasizing the reason *why* I am learning the language.

Anyways, I thought I would share that story of how awesome I am.

The word “그것이” is often contracted to “그게” and is often placed before 아니라 to refer to a prior situation and indicate “it is not that.” For example:

Play[그게 아니라 나는 너를 그냥 보고 싶어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s31.mp3) = It’s not that, I just want to see you
Play[그게 아니라 저는 돈을 벌어야 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Lesson86s32.mp3) = It’s not that, I just need to earn money

But in these situations, there would always need to be some sort of context that would create a situation where these sentences could be used.

Finally, the construction “다름이 아니라” is often used in some specific formal situations. If one is talking to a co-worker/client/boss (etc…), people usually start off by making small talk about some other (non-work related) topic. If you want to stop the personal chit-chat and start talking about some real facts/business/whatever, saying the phrase “다름이 아니라” is similar to the English expression “alright, let’s get to business here.”
