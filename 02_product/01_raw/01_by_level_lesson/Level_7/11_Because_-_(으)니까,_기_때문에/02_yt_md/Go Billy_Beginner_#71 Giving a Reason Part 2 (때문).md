## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson introduces the third major way to express "because" in Korean: the 때문 form. While previous lessons covered (으)니까 and 아/어/여서, this form provides a more formal and straightforward tone. It is particularly common in written Korean and formal speech. The instructor explains how to attach this form to both nouns and verbs, details the specific grammatical constraints regarding commands and suggestions, and highlights a critical nuance when using it with people to avoid sounding accusatory.

---

### The 때문 Form: Usage and Formality

The 때문 form is primarily used in formal situations or in writing. Compared to other "because" markers, it sounds more direct and is often described as a "written expression." While it can be used in speech, it is less common in casual, everyday conversations. The instructor notes that its straightforward nature makes it ideal for reports, essays, or formal presentations where a clear cause-and-effect relationship needs to be established.

---

### Basic Sentence Structure with Nouns and Verbs

There are two primary ways to use this form depending on whether the reason is a noun or an action/state. To say "because of [noun]," you simply attach 때문 directly after the noun. If the sentence ends there, you add the copula, resulting in `Noun + 때문이에요`. For example, "It's because of that problem" becomes:
> **그 문제 때문이에요.** / It is because of that problem.

To use a verb as the reason, the verb must first be transformed into a noun using the `~기` nominalizer. The structure then becomes `Verb Stem + 기 때문이에요`. The instructor provides an example:
> **제가 매운 음식을 먹을 수 없기 때문이에요.** / It is because I cannot eat spicy food.

When placing this reason in the middle of a sentence to connect two clauses, the particle 에 is attached to 때문, forming 때문에. This functions as "Because [Reason], [Result]."
> **제가 매운 음식을 먹을 수 없기 때문에 김치도 먹을 수 없어요.** / I also can't eat kimchi because I can't eat spicy food.

---

### Tense and Vocabulary Nuances

Unlike the 서 form, the 때문 form can be attached to past tense verb stems, similar to the (으)니까 form. This allows for expressing reasons based on completed actions. The instructor demonstrates this with several examples:
> **맛있는 음식을 많이 먹었기 때문에 배가 불러요.** / I am full because I ate a lot of delicious food.
> **이 김치를 좋아하기 때문에 세 통을 샀어요.** / I bought three containers because I like this kimchi.
> **제가 공부를 많이 했기 때문에 과학 시험을 잘 볼 거예요.** / I will do well on the science test because I studied a lot.

In these examples, the instructor highlights specific vocabulary: `배가 부르다 (to be full)`, `통 (container)`, and the expression `시험을 잘 보다`, which literally means "to see a test well" but translates to "doing well on a test." Additionally, the instructor notes that `한국인` is synonymous with `한국 사람 (Korean person)`, explaining that one can refer to people from a country by adding `사람`, `인`, or the polite `분` to the country name.

---

### Constraints and Social Nuances: Blame vs. Gratitude

The 때문 form has a major grammatical restriction: just like the 서 form, it cannot be used in sentences that give a command or a suggestion. In such cases, the (으)니까 form must be used instead.

Furthermore, the instructor warns about using 때문 after a person. In Korean, saying `Person + 때문에` often implies that you are blaming that person for a negative outcome.
> **이게 다 아빠 때문이에요.** / This is all because of Dad (implies blame).

To express a positive reason involving a person (equivalent to "thanks to"), the word `덕분` should be used instead of `때문`.
> **이게 다 아빠 덕분이에요.** / This is all thanks to Dad.

---

### Conversation Practice: Honorifics and Future Tense

The lesson concludes with a dialogue between 지윤 and 현아 that incorporates the new grammar. A key takeaway from the dialogue is the use of the honorific suffix `~님`. While `사장` means boss, one should use `사장님` when referring to someone else who is a boss or addressing their own boss to show respect.

The dialogue also demonstrates a contraction in the future tense: the `~것이다` form combined with the "but" marker `~지만` usually becomes `~거지만`.
> **사장님을 만나러 갈 거지만 이따가 밤에 시간이 있어요.** / I will go to meet the boss, but I have time later at night.

Finally, the dialogue shows the use of 때문에 to decline an invitation politely:
> **사장님이랑 저녁 약속이 있기 때문에 저녁은 안 될 거예요.** / Because I have a dinner appointment with the boss, dinner won't work.
