### The -어서/아서 Expression
The first method for saying "because" involves adding -서 to the present tense stem of a verb or adjective. This suffix is derived from the sentence connector 그래서 (therefore). To conjugate, one must first determine the present tense form of the word (e.g., 아프다 becomes 아파) and then attach -서 to create 아파서 (because I am/was sick). In polite speech, this can stand alone as a response by adding the particle 요, resulting in 아파서요.

A critical rule emphasized by the instructor is that -어서/아서 does not take past tense markers like -았/었- within the reason clause. Even when referring to a past event, the present stem is used. For example, 아파서요 can mean both "because I am sick" and "because I was sick." The actual tense of the situation is clarified by the final verb in a full sentence. If the final verb is past tense, such as 아파서 병원에 갔어요 (I went to the hospital because I was sick), the reason is understood as past tense. If the final verb is present, such as 아파서 병원에 가요 (I am going to the hospital because I am sick), the reason is present.

---

### The -기 때문에 Expression
The second method, -기 때문에, is generally more formal and provides a clearer distinction between present and past tenses within the reason clause itself. For the present tense, the base stem of the verb or adjective is combined with -기 때문에. For example, the base stem of 아프다 is 아프, resulting in 아프기 때문에 (because I am sick).

Unlike the previous form, -기 때문에 explicitly allows for past tense marking. To express a past reason, the past tense stem is used before adding the suffix. For example, the past stem of 아프다 is 아팠, resulting in 아팠기 때문에 (because I was sick). When used as a standalone response in polite speech, the ending becomes -기 때문이에요. The instructor demonstrates this with the example of receiving a bad grade: 공부를 열심히 안 했기 때문이에요 (Because I didn't study hard).

---

### Sentence Structure and Word Order
The instructor highlights a fundamental difference between English and Korean sentence logic regarding causality. While English allows the reason to come either before or after the result (e.g., "I went because I was sick" or "Because I was sick, I went"), Korean strictly requires the reason to be stated first. The grammatically correct structure is always [Reason] + [Result].

When using -어서/아서 in a full sentence, the -서 acts as the bridge. When using the "because" noun form in a full sentence, the ending is -기 때문에 (using the particle 에). The instructor provides parallel examples to show they are often interchangeable in meaning:
- **아파서 병원에 갔어요.** / I went to the hospital because I was sick.
- **아팠기 때문에 병원에 갔어요.** / I went to the hospital because I was sick.

---

### Causality with Nouns and the "Be" Verb (이다)
When the reason is a noun (e.g., "because it is my birthday"), the rules for the "be" verb 이다 apply. For the -어서/아서 form, the conjugation depends on whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침). If there is a consonant, use -이어서 (or the common spoken variant -이라서). If there is no consonant, use -여서 (or -라서).
- **생일이어서요 / 생일이라서요** (Because it is a birthday)
- **친구여서요 / 친구라서요** (Because it is a friend)

For the -기 때문에 form with nouns, the "be" verb stem 이 is attached to the noun before adding the suffix, resulting in [Noun] + 이기 때문에. For example, 친구 생일이기 때문에 (Because it is my friend's birthday). Additionally, if you want to say "because of [Noun]" without the "be" verb (attributing a result directly to a person or thing), you simply use [Noun] + 때문에.
- **너 때문이야.** / It's because of you. (Often used for blaming)
- **날씨 때문에 안 갔어.** / I didn't go because of the weather.

---

### Spoken Contractions and Variations
In casual spoken Korean, the -이라서/라서 forms are very frequent. The instructor notes that the final 서 in these specific noun-based forms is optional and often dropped. This results in endings like -이라 or -라.
- **내일이 시험이라 공부해요.** / I'm studying because tomorrow is an exam.
- **주말이라 수업이 없어요.** / There is no class because it's the weekend.
- **한국어는 외국어라 어려워요.** / Korean is difficult because it's a foreign language.
