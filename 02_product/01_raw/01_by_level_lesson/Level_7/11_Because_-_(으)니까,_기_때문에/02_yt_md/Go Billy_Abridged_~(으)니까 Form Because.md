### Basic Conjugation and Mechanics
To use this form, you start with a verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach `으니까`. For example, the verb `먹다 (to eat)` has the stem `먹`, resulting in `먹으니까 (because/so [someone] eats)`. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach `니까`. The verb `하다 (to do)` has the stem `하`, resulting in `하니까 (because/so [someone] does)`.

There are two key exceptions to these rules. First, if a verb stem ends in the consonant `ㄹ`, you must drop the `ㄹ` before attaching `니까`. For instance, `살다 (to live)` becomes `사니까`. Second, for descriptive verbs ending in the consonant `ㅂ`, you remove the `ㅂ`, add the syllable `우`, and then attach `니까`. The instructor demonstrates this with `맵다 (to be spicy)`, which transforms into `매우니까`.

### Usage Nuances and Pragmatics
The `~(으)니까` form is used to provide a reason for something, but it carries the specific nuance of being the speaker's opinion or subjective reasoning. It is frequently used when the second half of the sentence is a command or a suggestion. The instructor lists three common sentence endings that pair well with this form: commands ("Do it"), suggestions ("Shall we?"), and "Let's" statements.

A major distinction is made between `~(으)니까` and the `~아/어서` form. While both mean "because," `~아/어서` cannot be used with commands or suggestions. Conversely, `~(으)니까` cannot be used to express reasons for emotions like being happy, sad, or thankful. For example, to say "Thank you for helping me," one must use the `~아/어서` form rather than `~(으)니까`. Additionally, `~(으)니까` has a more colloquial, spoken feel and is considered informal. For formal speeches or "fancy" contexts, the instructor recommends using the `기 때문에` form instead.

---

### Tense Compatibility and Sentence Endings
Unlike some other "because" forms, `~(으)니까` is compatible with past and future tenses. For the past tense, you attach the form to the past tense stem, such as `했으니까 (because [someone] did)`. For the future tense, you do not use the `~겠다` ending; instead, you use the `~(으)ㄹ 거니까` structure, which is a contraction of the future tense `~(으)ㄹ 것이다` plus `니까`.

This form can also be used to end a sentence directly to mean "It is because..." In these cases, you can add the polite particle `요` to the end, resulting in `~(으)니까요`. For example, `배고프니까요` means "It's because I'm hungry." The instructor also notes that the final `가` is technically optional, though it is used 90% of the time in speech to sound more natural. Using just `~니` is a less common, more formal-sounding alternative.

---

### Secondary Meaning: Realization
The instructor briefly notes that `~(으)니까` can have a completely different meaning unrelated to "because." It can indicate that the speaker realized something after performing an action. This "discovery" or "realization" usage is treated as a separate grammar point. If a sentence with `~(으)니까` doesn't seem to make sense as a "because" statement, it is likely functioning as this realization form.
