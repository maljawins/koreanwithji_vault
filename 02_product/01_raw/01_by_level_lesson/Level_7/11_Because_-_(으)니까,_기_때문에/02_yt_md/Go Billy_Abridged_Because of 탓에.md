## Teaching Points

### Overview
In this lesson, Billy explains how to express causality in Korean using the specific markers 탓 and 덕분. While many learners are familiar with the general "because" marker 때문에, this video focuses on the nuanced ways Koreans assign blame or credit. The instructor demonstrates these concepts through live examples written on a whiteboard, set against the backdrop of Gyeongbok Palace.

The lesson arc moves from simple noun-based expressions of fault and thanks to more complex sentence structures involving verbs and adjectives. By the end of the video, learners should understand how to use 탓에 to emphasize a negative cause and 덕분에 to express gratitude for a positive outcome, as well as how to use the verb 탓하다 to actively blame someone.

---

### Noun + 탓 and 덕분: Blame vs. Thanks
The instructor begins by contrasting 탓 (fault/blame) and 덕분 (favor/help). While 때문에 is a neutral "because of," using it with a person often carries a negative connotation, implying it is their fault. To avoid sounding rude when you want to say "thanks to someone," you should use 덕분. For example, 철수 덕분에 means "thanks to Chul-soo." This can be used with the copula 이다 at the end of a sentence, such as 철수 덕분이에요 (It is thanks to Chul-soo). Literally, 덕분 refers to a favor or help received from someone.

Conversely, 탓 is used to directly assign blame for a negative result. It literally means a fault or a bad cause. The instructor writes the possessive structure on the board: [Noun]의 탓 (Someone's fault). A common expression is 내 탓이 아니야 (It is not my fault), where 내 is the contraction of 나의 (my). Like 덕분, 탓 can end a sentence with the copula, as in 다 아빠 덕분이에요 (It is all thanks to Dad).

---

### Using 탓에 with Verbs and Adjectives
The core of the lesson focuses on the grammatical construction [Adjective Form] + 탓에. This is used in the middle of a sentence to explain a negative reason, or as 탓이다 at the end. Because 탓 inherently means "fault," this structure is strictly reserved for negative outcomes. You would never use it for something that made you happy.

When using action verbs, you must use the appropriate adjective-transforming suffix. For a past tense negative reason, you use the past tense adjective form. The instructor provides the example: 공부를 하지 않은 탓에 시험에서 떨어졌어요 (I failed the test because [of the fault of] not studying). While one could use the standard (으)니까 form, using 탓에 specifically emphasizes that the failure is being blamed on the lack of study.

Other examples demonstrated include:
- **비가 많이 온 탓에 옷이 젖었어요.** / My clothes got wet because it rained a lot.
- **늦잠을 잔 탓에 제때에 도착하지 못했어요.** / I couldn't arrive on time because I slept in late.
- **이미 돈을 다 쓴 탓에 비행기표를 살 수 없어요.** / I can't buy a plane ticket because I already spent all my money.

For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the present tense uses the standard adjective marker (ㄴ/은), such as 머리가 아픈 탓에 (because my head hurts). If the cause was in the past, the instructor explains the use of the 던 form, resulting in 무거웠던 탓에 (because it was [too] heavy).

---

### The Verb 탓하다: To Blame
Finally, the instructor introduces the verb form 탓하다, which means "to blame" or "to fault someone." This is used with the object marker 을/를. For example, 에이 탓을 하다 means "to blame A." Common conversational phrases include 내 탓 하지마 (Don't blame me) and 너 자신을 탓해 (Blame yourself). This allows the speaker to move from describing a cause to actively assigning responsibility to a specific person or entity.
