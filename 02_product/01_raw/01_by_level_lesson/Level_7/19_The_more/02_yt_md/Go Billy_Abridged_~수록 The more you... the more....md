### Grammar Construction and Conjugation
The full version of this grammar form combines a conditional statement with the progressive marker 수록. To build the first part, take a verb stem and attach ~(으)면. If the stem ends in a consonant, attach ~으면; if it ends in a vowel, attach ~면. The second part of the form requires taking the same verb stem and attaching ~(으)ㄹ 수록. Stems ending in a consonant take ~을 수록, while those ending in a vowel take ~ㄹ 수록.

The instructor emphasizes that the first part of the construction—the ~(으)면 part—is completely optional. While including it can add emphasis, the meaning remains identical if you only use the ~(으)ㄹ 수록 portion. On screen, the instructor demonstrates this with the verb 하다 (to do), showing the full form 하면 할수록 and noting that it is written as one word without a space between the verb and the grammar marker. For the verb 이다 (to be), the form simply becomes 일수록.

---

### Usage with Action and Descriptive Verbs
When used with action verbs, the form translates to "the more you do [verb]." The instructor provides a relatable example regarding food and nostalgia:

> **피자를 먹으면 먹을수록 고향 생각이 나요.** / The more I eat pizza, the more I think of my hometown.

In this sentence, the instructor explains that 고향 means hometown and 생각나다 is the verb for "to recall" or "to have a thought come to mind." Another example shows how the form applies to skill or efficiency:

> **이건 하면 할수록 더 빨라져요.** / You get faster the more you do this.

Here, the instructor points out the use of 빨라지다, which is the "to become" (~지다) form of 빠르다 (to be fast), combined with 더 (more) to show a progressive change.

When applied to descriptive verbs (adjectives), the meaning shifts to "the more something is [adjective]." This is demonstrated through a preference for appliances:

> **저는 냉장고는 크면 클수록 좋아요.** / As for me, when it comes to refrigerators, the bigger the better.

The instructor notes that this is the natural way to express "the bigger the better" in Korean, using the verb 크다 (to be big).

### Contextual Examples and Nuance
The instructor provides further examples to show how the form handles different stem endings, such as the "B" irregular verb 덥다 (to be hot). When discussing sales trends, the instructor writes:

> **아이스크림은 더우면 더울수록 잘 팔려요.** / The hotter the weather, the better ice cream sells.

In this case, 덥다 changes to 더우면 and 더울수록. The instructor explains that 팔리다 means "to be sold," and 잘 means "well," creating a cause-and-effect relationship between the temperature and sales volume.

A final example focuses on the visual appeal of art:

> **이 그림은 보면 볼수록 갖고 싶어요.** / The more I look at this drawing, the more I want to have it.

This uses the verb 보다 (to see/look) to show that the desire to possess the item increases with each viewing.

---

### The Expression 갈수록 and Related Idioms
A very common application of this grammar is the word 갈수록, derived from the verb 가다 (to go). Literally meaning "the more it goes," it functions as an adverb meaning "as time goes by" or "more and more." The instructor uses it to encourage learners:

> **한국어는 갈수록 쉬워져요.** / Korean gets easier as time goes by.

The lesson ends with a common Korean idiom: 갈수록 태산. The word 태산 refers to a large, majestic mountain. Literally, the phrase means "the further I go, the bigger the mountain." It is used to describe a situation that is getting progressively worse, similar to the English expression "out of the frying pan and into the fire."
