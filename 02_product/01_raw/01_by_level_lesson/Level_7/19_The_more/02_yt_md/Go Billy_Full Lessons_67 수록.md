## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form 수록, which is used to express the concept of "the more [A] happens, the more [B] happens." This structure allows speakers to describe proportional relationships between two actions or states. The lesson covers the basic conjugation rules for both consonant-ending and vowel-ending verb stems, as well as a common optional addition that enhances the natural flow of the sentence.

---

### Meaning and Usage of 수록

The primary function of 수록 is to indicate that as the degree of one action or state increases, the degree of a subsequent action or state also increases. It is the Korean equivalent of the English "the more... the more..." or "the [comparative]... the [comparative]..." construction. The instructor provides an introductory example to illustrate this:

> **빠르면 빠를수록 더 좋아요.** / The quicker you do it, the better.

In this example, the adjective 빠르다 (to be fast/quick) is used to show that increasing speed results in an increase in "goodness" or preference.

---

### Conjugation Rules

To create this form, the speaker must identify the verb stem and apply the correct suffix based on the final character of that stem. If a verb stem ends in a consonant, the suffix 을수록 is attached. If a verb stem ends in a vowel, the suffix ㄹ수록 is attached directly to the bottom of the stem.

The instructor also highlights an optional but very common way to use this grammar: adding the "if" form (면) before the 수록 form. This involves repeating the verb. For example, instead of just saying 공부할수록 (the more I study), a speaker can say 공부하면 공부할수록 (if I study, the more I study). This repetition is a stylistic choice that emphasizes the progression and is frequently heard in natural conversation.

---

### Sentence Examples

The instructor provides several sentences to demonstrate how 수록 functions with different verbs and contexts.

The first example shows the form used with the verb 공부하다 (to study):
> **한국어를 공부할수록 한국에 더 가고 싶어져요.** / The more I study Korean, the more I want to go to Korea.

The second example demonstrates the optional 면 repetition with the verb 먹다 (to eat):
> **이 과자를 먹으면 먹을수록 엄마가 생각나요.** / The more that I eat this candy, the more that I think of my mom.

The third example uses the verb 벌다 (to earn) to describe a common human desire:
> **돈을 벌수록 더 많이 벌고 싶어요.** / The more money you earn, the more that you want to earn.

The final example uses the verb 화장을 하다 (to put on makeup) combined with a subjective opinion:
> **화장을 할수록 더 예뻐지는 것 같아요.** / I think that the more makeup I use, the prettier I look.
