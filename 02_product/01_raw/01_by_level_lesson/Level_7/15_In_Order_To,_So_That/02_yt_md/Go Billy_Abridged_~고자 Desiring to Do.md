## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the intermediate-level grammar form ~고자, which is used to express a speaker's intention, desire, or purpose for performing a specific action. While the construction itself is simple, the form carries a distinctively formal, official, and "fancy" tone. It is most commonly found in speeches, formal announcements, or written documents rather than in casual daily conversation.

The lesson covers the mechanical rules of attachment, the semantic constraints regarding subjects and verb types, and a direct comparison with the more common intention marker ~(으)려고. By the end of the video, the instructor demonstrates how ~고자 functions as a formal bridge between an intended goal and the action taken to achieve that goal.

---

### Construction and Basic Usage
To create this form, you take an action verb stem and simply attach 고자. The instructor emphasizes that this form is strictly for action verbs; it should not be used with descriptive verbs. Furthermore, the attachment must always be to the standard verb stem, regardless of the tense of the overall sentence. One must never use a past or future tense stem before 고자. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하고자.

Once the ~고자 form is created, it is followed by another action verb in the sentence. This second verb represents the action taken because of the intention expressed in the first part. Visually, the instructor writes the formula on the board: **Action Verb Stem + 고자 + Action Verb**. This structure translates to "Because I intended/wanted/hoped/planned to do [Action A], I did [Action B]" or "For the purpose of doing [Action A], I did [Action B]."

---

### Subject and Clause Constraints
A critical rule for using ~고자 is that the subject of both the first verb (the intention) and the second verb (the resulting action) must be the same. Usually, this subject is the speaker (first person). For instance, if the speaker says they are doing something because they intend to eat, it must be the speaker who both intends to eat and performs the subsequent action. It is grammatically incorrect to say "Because Chul-soo intends to do something, I am doing this" using this specific form.

Additionally, the second clause must describe a positive action or an effort toward the goal. The instructor explains that you cannot follow ~고자 with a negative action or a "do not do" verb like 하지 않다 (to not do), 안 하다 (not do), or 못하다 (to be unable to do). For example, it makes sense to say "Intending to become a good teacher, I will work hard," but it is illogical to say "Intending to become a good teacher, I will not work hard."

---

### Comparison with ~(으)려고 and Examples
The instructor notes that ~고자 is essentially a formal version of the grammar form ~(으)려고. In almost any context where ~(으)려고 is used to show intention, ~고자 can be swapped in to make the sentence sound more official or professional. The meaning remains the same, but the "feel" of the sentence shifts from standard to formal.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the form in practice:

> **좋은 선생님이 되고자 앞으로도 계속 노력할 거예요.** / In order to become a good teacher, I will continue to work hard in the future.

In this example, the instructor shows how 되다 (to become) becomes 되고자. He also demonstrates a variation used by organizations or in official announcements:

> **옥수수 기름은 더 이상 쓰지 않고자 합니다.** / We intend to not use corn oil anymore.

Further examples highlight the use of the form in reflecting on past efforts or future plans:

> **패션 모델이 되고자 노력했는데 결국 모델이 되지 못했어요.** / I tried hard to become a fashion model, but in the end, I couldn't become one.
> **저는 의사의 꿈을 이루고자 열심히 공부했습니다.** / I studied hard to make my dream of becoming a doctor a reality.
> **겨울이 오기 전에 공사를 끝내고자 바쁘게 일하고 있습니다.** / We are working busily so that we can finish the construction before winter comes.

Finally, the instructor shows how the form can be used in a conditional "if" statement using the plain form + 한다면:

> **새 가구를 장만하고자 한다면 이번 행사를 놓치지 마세요.** / If you are intending to purchase new furniture, don't miss this event.
