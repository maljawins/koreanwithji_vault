## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on expressing purpose or intention in Korean, specifically how to translate the English phrase "in order to" or "so that." The instructor introduces two primary grammatical structures: one that is versatile and formal, and another that is more casual and restricted to specific types of verbs.

The learning arc begins with the more general form, explaining its construction and providing examples with both action and descriptive verbs. The lesson then transitions to a second, more specialized form used exclusively with motion verbs. By comparing these two methods, learners can understand when to use each based on the context of the sentence and the specific verb being used.

---

### The General Form: 기 위해(서)
The first and most versatile way to say "in order to" is by using the structure **기 위해** or **기 위해서**. To form this, you take the verb stem and attach **기**, which nominalizes the verb, followed by **위해**. The addition of **서** at the end is optional when the following clause contains an action verb. If the following part of the sentence does not contain an action verb, you simply use **위해**.

This form is appropriate for a wide range of situations. For example, to say "I went to school in order to study," you would say:
> **저는 공부하기 위해서 학교에 갔어요.** / I went to school in order to study.

Another example provided demonstrates its use with a descriptive verb in the following clause:
> **집을 사기 위해 돈이 필요해요.** / I need money to buy a house.

The instructor notes a specific grammatical point regarding the verb **필요하다 (to be necessary)**. Because it is a descriptive verb, the noun that is "needed" takes the subject marker **이** or **가** rather than the object marker **을** or **를**. In the example above, **돈 (money)** is followed by the subject marker **이**.

---

### The Motion Verb Form: (으)러
The second way to express "in order to" is using the suffix **(으)러**. This form is slightly less formal than the first and has a significant usage restriction: it can only be used when the following verb is a motion verb, such as **가다 (to go)** or **오다 (to come)**.

The conjugation depends on whether the verb stem ends in a vowel or a consonant. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you add **으로**. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you add **로**.

The instructor provides two examples to illustrate this motion-specific purpose marker:
> **빵을 사러 가게에 갔어요.** / I went to the store to buy bread.
> **한국말을 공부하러 왔어요.** / I came to study Korean.

In the first example, the stem of **사다 (to buy)** ends in a vowel, so **러** is attached. In both examples, the sentence ends with a motion verb (**갔어요** and **왔어요**), making this grammar choice correct. If the main verb of the sentence is not a motion verb, you must revert to using the **기 위해(서)** form.
