## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the beginner-level grammar form (으)러, which is used to express the purpose of going or coming to a place. While it literally translates to "in order to" or "for the purpose of," it most naturally translates to the English "to" in the context of movement. The lesson covers the conjugation rules for action verbs, the critical requirement for a following movement verb, and common exceptions. The instructor also provides a comparison with other "in order to" forms like 기 위해 and (으)려고 to clarify when (으)러 is the most natural choice.

---

### Conjugation Rules

To create this form, you must start with an action verb stem. The instructor demonstrates on a whiteboard that a verb stem is found by taking the dictionary form of a verb—which always ends in 다—and removing that final syllable. For example, the dictionary form 하다 (to do) becomes the stem 하.

The attachment depends on the final letter of the verb stem. If the stem ends in a consonant, you attach 으로. If the stem ends in a vowel, you attach 로. The instructor provides the following examples:
- **하다** (to do) → **하** + **러** → **하러**
- **먹다** (to eat) → **먹** + **으러** → **먹으러**

There is one specific exception for stems ending in the consonant ㄹ. If a verb stem ends in ㄹ, you do not use 으; instead, you simply attach 로. The instructor illustrates this with the verb 놀다 (to play/hang out), which becomes 놀러.

---

### Usage with Movement Verbs

The (으)러 form has a strict grammatical requirement: it must be followed by a movement verb. These are verbs that indicate going or coming, such as 가다 (to go) or 오다 (to come). This includes compound movement verbs like 나가다 (to go out), 들어가다 (to enter/go in), and 들어오다 (to come in).

The instructor emphasizes that you cannot use a movement verb *before* the (으)러 form. For instance, saying "I am going to Korea to go to Seoul" using 가러 or 오러 is grammatically incorrect. The purpose (the action before (으)러) should be a non-movement action verb, while the result (the action after (으)러) must be the movement.

---

### Sentence Examples and Word Nuances

The instructor provides several example sentences to show how the form functions in natural conversation.
> **지금 머리 자르러 가요.** / I am going now to cut my hair.
> **한국에 놀러 오세요.** / Come to Korea to hang out.
> **내일 영화 보러 갈래요?** / Do you want to go see a movie tomorrow?
> **한국에 한국어를 공부하러 가고 싶어요.** / I want to go to Korea to study Korean.

In the sentence about studying Korean, the instructor notes that the location particle (한국에) can be placed either before the purpose or after it, and both versions are natural. Another example demonstrates coming outside:
> **저는 운동하러 밖에 나왔어요.** / I came outside to exercise.

The instructor also clarifies the word 밥 (cooked rice), explaining that in the sentence **다 같이 밥 먹으러 가요** (Let's all go eat a meal together), 밥 refers to a general meal, not just a bowl of white rice. Further examples include:
> **제가 도와주러 갈게요.** / I will go to help you.
> **친구가 청소 도와주러 왔어요.** / My friend came to help me clean up.
> **형이 쓰레기를 버리러 나갔어요.** / My older brother went out to throw away the trash.
> **아빠는 땀 빼러 사우나실에 들어갔어.** / Dad went into the sauna room to sweat.

---

### Contextual Omission and Comparisons

In casual speech (반말), if the context is clear, the movement verb at the end of the sentence can be omitted. If a friend asks "Where are you going?" (어디 가?), you can simply answer "To eat" (밥 먹으러) without adding 가.

The instructor compares (으)러 with 기 위해 and (으)려고. While 기 위해 also means "in order to," it is much more formal and can be used with any verb, not just movement verbs. However, whenever a movement verb is involved, (으)러 is the most natural choice.

The form (으)려고 is used when the following verb is not a movement verb, such as "I registered for class (등록했어요) in order to lose weight (살을 빼려고)." Crucially, (으)려고 cannot be used with commands or suggestions. If you need to give a command or make a suggestion for a purpose that doesn't involve a movement verb, you must use 기 위해.
