## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson introduces the grammar form `(으)려고 하다`, which is used to express intention or a plan to do something. While learners may already be familiar with the `보다` form to express "trying" something, the instructor clarifies that `보다` literally means to do something and "see how it goes." In contrast, the `(으)려고` form focuses on the internal state of intending or planning to perform an action, often translating naturally as "I'm going to," "I intend to," or "I'm trying to."

The instructor breaks down the conjugation rules for action verbs, including irregular cases, and explains how the form can be shortened in casual or polite speech by omitting the verb `하다`. Beyond simple intention, the lesson covers how this form functions as "in order to," providing a more common informal alternative to the `위해서` form. Finally, the lesson introduces the sentence-final usage of `고(요)` as a way to soften speech and indicate that the speaker has more to say.

---

### Conjugating the Intending Form (으)려고 하다
To create this form, you must first identify the action verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach `으려고`. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach `려고`. This is then followed by the verb `하다`, which is conjugated into the desired tense or politeness level. The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate this:

- **지금 아침을 먹으려고 하고 있어요.** / I am trying to eat breakfast now. (Literally: I'm intending to eat breakfast now.)
- **한 시간 후에 돌아오려고 해요.** / I intend to come back after one hour.

Specific spelling rules apply to certain stems. If a verb stem ends with the consonant `ㄹ`, you simply attach `려고` without adding `으`. For example, `살다 (to live)` becomes `살려고`. If a verb stem ends with the irregular `ㅂ`, you remove the `ㅂ`, add `우`, and then attach `려고`. Thus, `눕다 (to lie down)` becomes `누우려고`.

---

### Shortening the Form and Usage Nuances
Similar to quoting forms, `(으)려고 하다` can be shortened by replacing `하다` with `요` in polite speech or removing it entirely in casual speech. This makes the expression more concise while maintaining the meaning of intention.

- **지금 하려고 해요** becomes **지금 하려고요.** / I'm trying to do it now.
- **나도 가려고 하고 있어** becomes **나도 가려고.** / I'm trying to go too.

The instructor notes that while both `(으)려고` and `보다` can translate to "try" in English, they represent different concepts. The `(으)려고` form does not imply that the action is being successfully carried out; it only signals the intention. It is also restricted to the first person (I/we) and second person (you/questions), much like the `고 싶다` (want to) form. You cannot use it to describe the intentions of a third person (he/she/they).

---

### Expressing "In Order To"
The `(으)려고` form can also be used to mean "in order to," functioning similarly to the `위해서` form. In informal spoken Korean, `(으)려고` is actually more common than `위해서` when used with action verbs. It links two clauses, where the first clause is the intention and the second is the action taken to achieve it.

- **한국에 가려고 한국어를 배웠어요.** / I learned Korean in order to go to Korea.
- **저는 살을 빼려고 운동하고 있어요.** / I'm exercising in order to lose weight.

The instructor provides a list of vocabulary and examples to reinforce this usage:
- **금과 은을 사려고 돈을 모으고 있어요.** / I'm saving up money in order to buy gold and silver.
- **동영상 하나를 만들려고 장비를 많이 샀어요.** / I bought a lot of equipment in order to make one video.
- **기회를 놓치지 않으려고 바로 전화했어요.** / I called right away in order to not miss the opportunity.
- **이유를 알려고 제가 사장님께 직접 물어봤어요.** / I asked the boss directly in order to know the reason.

---

### Softening Sentences with 고(요)
The lesson concludes with a look at the verb ending `고` (or `고요` in polite speech) used at the very end of a sentence. While `고` is typically used to connect two sentences (meaning "and"), using it as a final ending suggests that the speaker is not quite finished talking or wants to add an afterthought. This has the effect of making the sentence sound softer and less abrupt.

- **저는 영화를 정말 좋아해요. 드라마도 좋아하고요.** / I really like movies. I like dramas too (and...).
- **안녕하세요. 저는 빌리라고 해요. 제가 미국인이고요. 서른 네 살이에요.** / Hello. My name is Billy. I'm an American (and...), and I'm 34 years old.
