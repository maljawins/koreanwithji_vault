The lesson begins with a necessary review of the verbs 그렇다 and 그러다, which are often confused due to their similar appearances and identical informal conjugations. While both can be used to mean "to be like that" or "to do like that," they function differently as descriptive and action verbs respectively.

The instructor demonstrates how this form allows a speaker to summarize a series of actions or states without providing exhaustive details, making the speech sound more natural and casual.

---

### Review: 그렇다 vs. 그러다

Before diving into the grammar forms, it is essential to distinguish between these two verbs. **그렇다** is a descriptive verb meaning "to be so" or "to be like that." It is the root of the common expression **그래요** used to mean "Is that so?" or "Really?"

**그러다** is an action verb meaning "to do so" or "to act like that." It is a shortened form of **그렇게 하다**. Because **하다** can also mean "to say" in the context of quoting, **그러다** can also mean "to say that" (**그렇게 말하다**).

A point of confusion for learners is that both verbs conjugate to **그래** or **그래요** in the present tense and **그랬어요** in the past tense. Therefore, context is the only way to determine if the speaker is describing a state (그렇다) or referring to an action or statement (그러다). For example, **왜 그래요?** could mean "Why is it like that?" (descriptive) or "Why are you doing that?" (action), depending on the situation.

---

### Quoting with ~고 그러다

The first application of this structure is as a variation of the standard quoting form. Instead of ending a quote with **말하다** (to speak) or **하다** (to do/say), speakers often use **그러다**. This is strictly a spoken, informal everyday form and is not typically found in formal writing or official speech.

To form this, you take the plain form of the verb (the quoting form), add the particle **고**, and follow it with a conjugated form of **그러다**. For the verb **이다** (to be), the form becomes **이라고** or **라고**.

> **찰수도 갈 수 있다고 그랬어요.** / Chul-soo also said that he can go.
> **찰수가 한국에 이미 도착했다고 그랬어요.** / Chul-soo said that he already arrived in Korea.

---

### The "Hidden" Meaning: ~고 그러다 for Actions

The primary focus of the lesson is the use of **Action Verb Stem + 고 그러다**. Unlike the quoting form, this is attached directly to the verb stem (e.g., **하고**, **먹고**, **자고**). This form is used to finish explaining something without giving specific details, functioning like the English "and stuff" or "and yeah."

The instructor explains that this form "yada-yadas" over the details. It implies that the mentioned action is just one example of things the speaker does.

> **저는 가끔씩 여기서 잠도 자고 그래요.** / Sometimes I even sleep here and stuff.
> **요즘도 새벽 두 시에 요가하고 그래?** / Are you still doing, like, yoga and stuff at two in the morning?
> **저는 거기가 너무 좋아서 주말마다 가고 그러고 있어요.** / I love it there so much that I'm going there every weekend and stuff.

In the final example, the instructor uses the progressive form **그러고 있어요** to emphasize the ongoing nature of the action.

---

### Using ~고 그렇다 for Descriptions

When the preceding verb is a descriptive verb (adjective), the speaker must use **그렇다** instead of **그러다**. The function remains the same: it adds a nuance of "and stuff" or "it's like that" to the description, often adding a bit of emphasis or a casual tone to the sentence.

While both often conjugate to **그래요** in the present tense, the distinction becomes clear in other conjugations, such as **그렇죠** or **그렇긴 해요**.

> **역시 이건 언제 먹어도 맛있고 그렇죠.** / As expected, this is delicious whenever you eat it and stuff, right?
> **열 살 치고는 키가 좀 작고 그렇긴 해요.** / They are a bit short and stuff for a ten-year-old.
> **요즘도 코가 가렵고 그래요.** / My nose is still itchy and stuff these days.

The instructor concludes with an example showing how this form can be used with the connector **그러니까** (because/so):

> **가격이 너무 싸고 그러니까 안 살 수가 없었어요.** / The price was so cheap and stuff that I couldn't not buy it.
