### Basic Construction and Meaning

The form is created by taking any noun and attaching the particle 만 (just/only) followed by 해도. Because it functions like a particle, it does not matter whether the noun ends in a consonant or a vowel. The instructor explains the literal breakdown: 만 means "just" or "only," and 해도 is the conjugated form of 하다 (to do) with the ~도 (even/also) ending. In this specific grammatical context, the 하다 part represents the idea of "thinking" or "considering."

Therefore, the literal meaning is "even if I only think about [noun]" or "even if we are only talking about [noun]." It is used when the speaker is explaining a general situation and wants to provide a representative example to illustrate their point. Common English translations include "just [noun], for example," "even [noun] alone," or "take [noun], for example."

---

### ~만 해도 as an Emphatic Topic Marker

The instructor points out that ~만 해도 is functionally very similar to the topic marker 은/는. In many cases, a learner can mentally replace ~만 해도 with a topic marker to understand the basic meaning of the sentence. However, using ~만 해도 adds a specific layer of emphasis. It signals to the listener that the noun being mentioned is just one example out of many possibilities, but even this single example is enough to prove the speaker's point.

For instance, in the sentence "Even just last year, it wasn't this hot," the speaker uses 작년 여름만 해도 to emphasize that they aren't just talking about the distant past—even a very recent example like last summer illustrates the change in weather.

---

### Examples in Context

The instructor provides several sentences to demonstrate the usage. In the first example, 작년 여름만 해도 이렇게 덥지 않았어요 (Even just last summer, it wasn't this hot), the particle emphasizes the contrast between the current heat and the recent past. In the second example, 이 가방만 해도 10킬로가 넘어요 (Just this bag alone is over 10 kilograms), the speaker implies there are other heavy things, but this one bag is a sufficient example of the overall weight.

Further examples include 5년 전만 해도 집에서 일하는 사람은 많지 않았어요 (Even just 5 years ago, there weren't many people working from home) and 철수만 해도 누나가 셋이에요 (Take Chul-soo, for example; he has three older sisters). A more complex example shows the particle used within a contrastive sentence: 이 가게는 몇 달 전만 해도 손님이 많았는데 문을 닫았네요 (This store had many customers even just a few months ago, but it closed).

---

### Emphatic Variations: ~만 하더라도 and ~만 같아도

The instructor introduces ~만 하더라도 as a slightly stronger and more formal version of ~만 해도. It carries the exact same meaning but adds more weight to the example. An example provided is 김치만 하더라도 생각보다 종류가 다양해요 (Take Kimchi, for example; there are more varieties than you think).

For advanced learners, the instructor mentions two other related forms. The first is ~의 경우만 봐도, which literally means "even if you only look at the case of [noun]." This is a more literal way to express the same "take this example" nuance. The second is ~만 같아도. While 같아도 literally means "even if it is like," in this specific usage, it functions identically to ~만 해도, though it is less common.
