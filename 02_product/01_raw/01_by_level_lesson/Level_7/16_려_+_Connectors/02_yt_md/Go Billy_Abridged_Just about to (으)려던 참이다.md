## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces advanced grammar structures used to describe actions that were just about to happen or were intended at a specific moment. The lesson centers on the form `(으)려던 참이다`, which translates to "to be just about to [verb]." This is framed as an advanced lesson, requiring prior knowledge of the intentional form `(으)려고` and the retrospective descriptive marker `던`.

The learning arc begins with a simple adverb for "just" to set the context of recent actions, then moves into the core grammar structure. The instructor breaks down the literal components of the phrase to help students understand the underlying logic of the expression. Finally, the lesson covers tense variations and how to use the structure as a sentence connector to describe interruptions or coincidences.

---

### Expressing "Just Did": 방금
Before diving into the main "about to" form, the instructor explains how to say something "just" happened using the adverb `방금 (just)`. This is used with a verb conjugated in the past tense to indicate that an action occurred a few seconds or minutes ago. For example, `방금 했어` means "I just did it," and `방금 갔어` means "He just left." The instructor provides a more complex example, `그게 방금 일어난 일이야`, which translates to "That is something that just happened," where `일어나다 (to occur/happen)` describes the noun `일 (event/occurrence)`.

---

### The Core Structure: (으)려던 참이다
The primary focus of the lesson is the structure `Action Verb Stem + (으)려던 참이다`, meaning "to be just about to [verb]." The instructor explains the conjugation rules: attach `으려던` if the verb stem ends in a consonant, and `려던` if it ends in a vowel. This is typically used in the present tense (e.g., `참이에요`, `참이야`) to show an intention to do something right at the current moment.

The instructor notes that this form is most frequently used as a reply to someone else's question or suggestion. If someone asks what you are doing or invites you somewhere, you might respond that you were "just about to" do something else. The breakdown of the form is as follows:
- `(으)려` comes from `(으)려고`, indicating intention.
- `던` is a retrospective marker, implying a state that was ongoing.
- `참` means a "time," "moment," or "situation."
- `이다` is the verb "to be."

Literally, the speaker is saying, "I am [currently] in a situation/moment where I was intending to [verb]."

---

### Examples of (으)려던 참이다
The instructor provides several sentences to illustrate usage in context. In one scenario, a speaker declines an invitation because they are just about to study: `아니에요. 이제 도서관에서 한국어를 공부하려던 참이어서 오늘은 안 될 것 같아요 (No. I'm just about to study Korean at the library, so I don't think today will work)`. 

Another example shows a speaker agreeing to a task right before leaving work: `난 이제 퇴근하려던 참인데 그것까지만 해 놓고 갈게 (I'm just about to go home from work, but I'll at least take care of that before I go)`. Here, `퇴근하다` means to leave work, and `해 놓다` implies doing something in preparation for later.

Further examples include:
- `나는 찰수를 만나려던 참인데 같이 만나서 얘기 좀 할래?` / I am just about to meet Chul-soo; do you want to meet together and talk a bit?
- `배가 좀 아파서 약을 먹으려던 참이에요.` / I am just about to take some medicine because my stomach hurts a bit.

---

### The Adverb 막 and Colloquial Usage
The instructor introduces the word `막`, which can function similarly to `방금` to mean "just now" in colloquial speech. It is very common to see `막` paired with the `려던 참` form to emphasize the immediacy of the intention. However, `막` has other meanings depending on context, such as "wildly" or "like crazy" (e.g., `막 먹었어` - He was eating like crazy), or as a filler word similar to "like" in English.

Example with the grammar point:
> **막 김치를 담그려던 참이어서 오늘은 나갈 수 없을 것 같아.** / I was just about to make Kimchi, so I don't think I can go out today.

---

### Past Tense: (으)려던 참이었다
Using the past tense `참이었다` indicates that the speaker "was just about to" do something at a previous point in time. This is often used to tell someone that you were already planning to do the thing they just mentioned. The instructor mentions this is actually more common than the present tense version.

Examples:
- `안 그래도 거기 가려던 참이었어.` / I was just about to go there anyway (regardless of you mentioning it).
- `내가 밥을 먹으려던 참이었는데 같이 뭐 먹으러 갈까?` / I was just about to eat something, but do you want to go get something together?
- `마침 제가 매운 걸 잘 못 먹는다고 얘기를 하려던 참이었어요.` / I was just about to tell you just then that I can't really eat spicy food that well.

The word `마침` is highlighted as a common adverb meaning "just then" or "at that moment," which frequently appears with this structure.

---

### Sentence Connector: (으)려던 참에
When `참` is followed by the particle `에`, the structure functions as a sentence connector meaning "as I was about to" or "when I was about to." This is used to describe a situation where one action was intended, but then another event occurred simultaneously or interrupted it. The instructor distinguishes this from `참인데`; while `참인데` provides background or contrast, `참에` focuses on the specific timing of the coincidence.

Examples:
- `내가 나가려던 참에 갑자기 철수가 우리 집에 온 거야.` / Just as I was about to go out, Chul-soo suddenly came to our house.
- `핸드폰을 충전하려던 참에 누가 부딪혀서 액정이 깨져버렸어.` / Just as I was about to charge my cell phone, someone bumped into me and the screen broke.
- `지하철을 타려던 참에 집에서 가방을 놓고 왔단 걸 깨달았어.` / Just as I was about to get on the subway, I realized that I had left my bag at home.

The instructor also notes a similar but less common form, `(으)려던 차에`, which carries the same meaning but is used less frequently than `참`.
