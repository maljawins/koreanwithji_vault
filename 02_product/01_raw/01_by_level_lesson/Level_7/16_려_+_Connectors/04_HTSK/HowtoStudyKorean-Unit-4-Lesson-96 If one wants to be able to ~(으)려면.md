Lesson 96: If one wants to be able to: ~(으)려면

Introduction

In this lesson, you will learn how to combine two previous grammatical principles to come up with ~(으)려면. Let’s get started.

If one intends to: ~(으)려면

In [Lesson 32](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-32/) you learned how to connect two clauses with ~(으)려고 to indicate that one does an action for the intent or purpose of doing another. For example:

Play[소풍을 가려고 도시락을 쌌어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lesson96s1.mp3)
= I packed a lunch box with the intent of going on a picnic

Play[이제 취직을 하려고 이력서를 쓰고 있어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lesson96s2.mp3)
= Now I am writing a resume with the intent of finding a job

In[Lesson 43](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-43/) you learned how to connect two clauses with ~(으)면 to create the meaning of “if” or “when.” For example:

Play[식탁이 없으면 식사를 할 때 불편해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lesson96s3.mp3)
= If there is no dinner table, having a meal is uncomfortable

Play[그 사람이 저를 찾아오면 저한테 말해 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lesson96s4.mp3)
= If that person comes looking for me, please tell me

~(으)려고 and ~(으)면 can combine to form ~(으)려면. Its meaning is a combination of the two put together, as if one were to say ~하려고 하면… . Korean grammatical principles can often combine and contract with each other like this. Now that you have a good base of many of the simple ones, you will see this type of thing often as you progress to later lessons. For example, ~한다고 해 often combines and contracts to ~하대 which you will learn in [Lesson 102](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-5/lessons-101-108/lesson-102/).

The combined grammatical principle ~(으)려면 creates the meaning of “if one intends to,” or sometimes more simply “if one wants to.”

Sentences with ~(으)려면 by their nature often end with ~아/어야 하다, ~이/가 필요하다 ([Lesson 46](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-46/)) or imperative ending like ~세요 or 아/어라 ([Lesson 40](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-40/)). Below are many examples:

Play[그렇게 비싼 것을 사려면 돈이 많이 필요해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lesson96s5.mp3)
= If you intend to buy something that expensive, you need a lot of money

Play[서울에 가려면 지하철을 타세요!](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lesson96s6.mp3)
