Lesson 155: ~(으)려는 Describing an upcoming noun with~려고 하다

Introduction

In the previous two lessons, you saw that you can combine ~(으)려 with other grammatical principles to combine their meanings. In this lesson, you will continue to expand your understanding of ~(으)려 by connecting it to another grammatical principle.

~(으)려는

In [Lesson 26](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-26/) and [27](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-27/), you saw how you can use ~는 to describe an upcoming noun. For example:

Play[이 구어는 십대들이 사용하는 비밀 언어예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES001.mp3)
= This slang is a secret language that teenagers use

Play[한국어를 배우는 학생이 별로 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES002.mp3)
= There aren’t a lot of students who are learning Korean

Here, the noun is being described by the verb 배우다. The students who are learning. It’s possible to make this sentence more complex by using ~(으)려고 as well. For example:

Play[한국어를 배우려고 하는 학생이 별로 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES003.mp3)
= There aren’t a lot of students who are trying to learn Korean

You can attach ~는 directly to ~(으)려 to abbreviate it to ~(으)려는. Here, ~(으)려는 is an abbreviation of ~(으)려고 하는. For example:

Play[한국어를 배우려는 학생이 별로 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES004.mp3)
= There aren’t a lot of students who are trying to learn Korean

~(으)려던

In the previous section, you saw how ~(으)려는 is an abbreviation of ~(으)려고 하는. In [Lesson 27](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-27/) you saw how ~던 can be used to describe upcoming nouns. Instead of attaching ~는 to ~(으)려 like you saw in the previous section, you can also attach ~던 to create ~(으)려던. As you learned in [Lesson 27](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-6/lessons-126-133/lesson-127/), ~던 indicates that an action once happened and is no longer happening. You can use ~(으)려던 to describe a noun that was intended to happen but did not. For example:

Play[하려던 일을 멈추고 이것부터 해 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES020.mp3)
= Stop the thing that you are just about to do and do this first

Below are some examples:

Play[과일을 세척하려던 순간에 물이 다 떨어졌어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES021.mp3)
= Just as I was about to cleanse the fruits, we ran out of water

Play[후보자를 지지하려던 사람들은 예상하지 못한 발표에 당황했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES022.mp3)
= The people who were about to support the candidate were surprised by an unexpected announcement

Play[일찍 취침하려던 계획이 전화 한 통 때문에 엉망이 됐어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES023.mp3)

Play[새 여권을 발급하려던 사람들은 시스템 오류로 기다려야 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lesson155ES024.mp3)
= The people who were about to issue new passports had to wait due to a system error
