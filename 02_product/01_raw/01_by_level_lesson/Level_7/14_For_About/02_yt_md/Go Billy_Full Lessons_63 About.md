## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor explains how to express the concept of "about" in Korean. While English uses the single word "about" for multiple grammatical functions, Korean distinguishes between using "about" as an adjective (describing a noun) and as an adverb (describing a verb). The lesson focuses on two primary structures: `~에 대한` for modifying nouns and `~에 대해(서)` for modifying verbs. By understanding these two forms, learners can accurately describe subjects of books, songs, and movies, as well as topics of study or conversation.

---

### Adjectival Use: ~에 대한

When "about" is used to describe a noun—for example, "a book about food"—it functions as an adjective. In this case, the instructor demonstrates the formula: **Noun + 에 대한 + Noun**. The first noun is the topic, and the second noun is the object being described.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this pattern. To say "a book about food," you take `음식 (food)`, attach `에 대한`, and follow it with `책 (book)`, resulting in `음식에 대한 책`. Similarly, "a song about love" becomes `사랑에 대한 노래`, where `사랑` is love and `노래` is song. A full sentence example shown is:

> **저는 한국에 대한 영화를 봤어요.** / I saw a movie about Korea.

In this sentence, `한국에 대한` describes the movie (`영화`), specifying its subject matter.

---

### Adverbial Use: ~에 대해(서)

When "about" describes an action or a verb—such as "I read about food" or "I studied about art"—it functions as an adverb. The instructor introduces the formula for this usage: **Noun + 에 대해(서) + Verb**. The syllable `서` is noted as being completely optional; both `~에 대해` and `~에 대해서` are correct and carry the same meaning.

The instructor highlights the difference between "I read a book about food" (adjectival) and "I read about food" (adverbial). In the latter, there is no specific noun like "book" being described; instead, the act of reading itself is directed toward the topic of food.

Example sentences provided for this adverbial form include:

> **미술에 대해 공부했어요.** / I studied about art.
> **한국어에 대해서 배우고 싶어요.** / I want to learn about Korean.
> **미국 역사에 대해 알고 있어요?** / Do you know about American history?

In these cases, the expression `~에 대해(서)` directly precedes and modifies the verbs `공공부하다 (to study)`, `배우다 (to learn)`, and `알다 (to know)`.
