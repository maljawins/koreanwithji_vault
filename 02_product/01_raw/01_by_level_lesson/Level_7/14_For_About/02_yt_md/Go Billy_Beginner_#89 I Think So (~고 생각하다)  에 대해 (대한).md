## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces how to express personal thoughts and opinions in Korean. While learners may already know basic quoting forms, this lesson focuses on combining those forms with specific verbs and particles to say "I think that..." or "I think about..." something. The learning arc begins with the basic statement quoting form used with the verb for thinking, moves into particles used for "about" in both adverbial and adjectival contexts, and concludes with a comprehensive conversation practice.

---

### Expressing "I Think That..." with 고 생각하다

To express a thought or opinion about a statement, the instructor explains a two-step conjugation process. First, you use the statement quoting form **고**. This is specifically for quoting statements, not for questions, commands, or suggestions. Second, you add the verb **생각하다 (to think)** and conjugate it according to the desired politeness level or tense.

The instructor provides initial examples to show this in practice:
> **저는 좋다고 생각해요.** / I think that it's good.
> **저는 빌리 씨가 했다고 생각해요.** / I think that Billy did it.

The instructor notes that this form is highly versatile and can be used with various adjectives and verbs. For instance, when discussing someone's height, you can say:
> **저는 석진 씨의 키가 크다고 생각해요.** / I think that 석진 is tall.

In this context, the instructor defines **키** as a person's height or stature. He also provides the antonym: **키가 작다 (to be short)**.

---

### Nuances and Adverbs with 생각하다

The lesson covers several specific nuances when using the verb for thinking. First, the adverb **그렇게 (so / in that way)** is frequently used with **생각하다** to mean "I think so" or "I think that."
> **저는 그렇게 생각했어요.** / I thought so.
> **그렇게 생각하지 않아요.** / I don't think so.

Second, the instructor highlights a common mistake for English speakers: asking "What do you think?" In Korean, you do not use the word for "what" (**뭐**). Instead, you must use the adverb **어떻게 (how)**.
> **어떻게 생각해요?** / What do you think? (Literally: How do you think?)

Additional examples shown on screen include:
> **무료라고 생각했어요.** / I thought that it's free.
> **저 고양이가 뚱뚱하다고 생각해요.** / I think that cat over there is fat.
> **바람이 많이 불고 있다고 생각해요.** / I think it's very windy. (Literally: I think that wind is blowing a lot.)

---

### Thinking or Talking "About" Something: 에 대해(서)

When you want to say you are thinking or talking "about" a specific topic, you use the form **에 대해(서)**. The instructor explains that you take a noun and attach **에 대해** or **에 대해서**. The **서** at the end is optional and does not change the meaning. This form is followed by an action verb, such as thinking, talking, or learning.

Examples of this adverbial usage:
> **미나 씨에 대해 어떻게 생각해요?** / What do you think about 미나?
> **저는 그 장난감에 대해 알고 싶어요.** / I want to know about that toy.
> **할머니를 방문하는 것에 대해 생각했어요.** / I thought about visiting grandmother.
> **이곳에 대해 생각해 본 적이 있어요?** / Have you ever thought about this before?

---

### The Adjectival Form: 에 대한

A similar but distinct form is **에 대한**, which functions as an adjective. While it also translates to "about" in English, its grammatical role is to modify a following noun. The instructor suggests a helpful mental translation: "that is about."

The structure is **Noun + 에 대한 + Noun**. Examples include:
> **한국 역사에 대한 책이에요.** / It is a book about Korean history. (A book *that is about* Korean history.)
> **저는 한국 미술에 대한 프로젝트를 했어요.** / I did a project about Korean art.
> **북한에 대한 영화를 봤어요.** / I saw a movie about North Korea.

---

### Conversation Practice and Contextual Vocabulary

The lesson concludes with a dialogue between characters 지원 and 시현. This practice integrates the "I think" forms with previously learned grammar like **(으)ㄹ까요** (suggestions) and **거예요** (future probability).

Key vocabulary introduced or reinforced during the conversation:
- **동물원**: zoo
- **왜냐하면**: because / the reason is...
- **잘되다**: to work out well
- **매년**: every year
- **시끄럽다**: to be noisy
- **어린이**: kid / young child

A specific cultural and linguistic note is made regarding the verb **있다 (to exist/be)**. When used with a location, it often translates more naturally as "to stay."
> **저는 집에 있고 싶지 않아요.** / I do not want to stay at home. (Literally: I don't want to exist at home.)

The dialogue also demonstrates using the quoting form with a future probability:
> **저는 그런 파티가 동생에게 아주 재미있을 거라고 생각하는데요.** / As for me, I think that kind of party will be very fun for your younger sibling.
