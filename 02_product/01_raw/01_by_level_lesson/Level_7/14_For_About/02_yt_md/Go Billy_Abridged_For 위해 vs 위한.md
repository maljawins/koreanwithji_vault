## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor explores the various ways to translate the English word "for" into Korean, focusing primarily on the forms 위해 and 위한. While both are derived from the verb 위하다 (to be for), they function differently in a sentence: one acts as an adverb modifying a verb, while the other acts as an adjective modifying a noun. The lesson further distinguishes these from other "for" contexts, such as expressing personal opinions or thanking someone for a specific action, where 위해 would be grammatically incorrect.

---

### The Adverbial Form: 위해 and 위해서

The most common way to say "for the purpose of" or "for the benefit of" is using the form 위해. The instructor notes that adding the syllable 서 to make it 위해서 is completely optional and does not change the meaning. To use this with a noun, you attach the object marker 을 or 를 to the noun followed by 위해. Because this form functions as an adverb, it must be followed by a verb that describes the action being done for that noun.

> **마이크를 위해 선물을 샀어요.** / I bought a present for Mike.

The instructor demonstrates that this form can also be used with verbs by first transforming the verb into a noun using the ~기 nominalizer. While the object marker can technically follow the ~기 form, it is usually omitted. When used with a verb, 위해 is often best translated as "in order to."

> **한국어를 공부하기 위해 책을 만들었어요.** / I made a book in order to study Korean.

A formal or older variation of this form is 위하여, which functions identically to 위해.

---

### The Adjectival Form: 위한

When "for" is used to describe a noun rather than an action, the adjectival form 위한 is used. Unlike 위해, which is followed by a verb, 위한 must be followed by a noun. This creates a phrase meaning "a [Noun] that is for the purpose of [Noun/Verb]."

> **한국어를 배우기 위한 책이에요.** / It is a book for learning Korean.
> **저를 위한 선물 있어요?** / Is there a present for me?

The instructor emphasizes that the choice between 위해 and 위한 depends on what the "for" phrase is modifying. In the sentence "I cleaned the house for my mom," the cleaning is for the mom, so the adverb 위해 is required: **엄마를 위해 집을 청소했어요.** If one were to say **엄마를 위한 집을 청소했어요**, it would imply "I cleaned the house that is for my mom," suggesting the house itself belongs to or is designated for the mother, rather than the act of cleaning being a favor for her.

---

### Expressing "For Me" as an Opinion

A common mistake for learners is using 저를 위해 to mean "in my opinion." The instructor clarifies that 위해 implies a benefit or purpose, not a viewpoint. To say "for me" in the sense of "I think," there are three better alternatives:

- **제 생각에(는)** — Literally "In my thinking/opinion." This is the most direct way to state a thought.
- **저는** — Using the topic marker to signal "as for me" or "speaking of me."
- **저한테는** — Literally "to me," but very commonly used to express how something feels or appears to the speaker.

For example, to say "For me, it's a bit too spicy," one should use **저한테는 조금 매워요** or **제 생각에는 조금 매워요** rather than using 위해.

---

### "Thanks For": A Special Case

The instructor highlights that "Thank you for [something]" is a frequent source of errors. Using 위해 in this context (e.g., "Thank you for the purpose of the present") is incorrect in both languages. 

For a simple noun, one just states the noun followed by the thank-you verb:
> **선물 감사합니다.** / Thank you for the present.
> **셔츠 고마워.** / Thanks for the shirt.

To thank someone for an action ("Thank you for doing..."), Korean uses the ~아/어 주셔서 감사합니다 pattern. This literally translates to "Thank you for giving me the act of [verb]." The instructor breaks down the conjugation:
1. Take the verb and conjugate it into the ~아/어 form (as if adding 요).
2. Attach 주셔서 (honorific) or 줘서 (casual).
3. Add the thank-you verb (감사합니다 or 고마워요).

> **도와주셔서 감사합니다.** / Thank you for helping me. (Literally: Thank you for giving me help.)

If the verb already ends in 주다 (like 도와주다), you do not repeat the 주다 element; you simply conjugate the existing 주다 into the 서 form.
