### Overview
This lesson focuses on the nuanced differences between two major Korean conjunctions that translate to "so" or "because": **그래서** (and its suffix form **-아/어/해서**) and **그러니까** (and its suffix form **-(으)니까**). While both connect a reason to a result, they are not always interchangeable. The instructor breaks down the grammatical construction of each, their specific usage constraints, and the pragmatic contexts where one is preferred over the other.

The instructor then moves into a detailed comparison, highlighting three main areas of difference: general vs. shared knowledge, expressions of emotion, and imperative/propositive sentence endings. ### Grammatical Construction: -아/어/해서 vs. -(으)니까

The instructor first clarifies how these sentence connectors transform into conjunctions attached directly to verbs. For **그래서**, the suffix **-서** is added to the **present stem** (the conjugated form used in the present tense, such as `먹어` or `가`). For example, `먹다 (to eat)` becomes `먹어서`. A crucial rule for **-서** is that it always uses the present stem regardless of whether the intended meaning is past or present. One should never say `마셨어서`; it must always be `마셔서`.

In contrast, **-(으)니까** is attached to the **base stem** (the part of the verb before `다`). If the base stem ends in a consonant (받침), **-으니까** is used; if it ends in a vowel, **-니까** is used. For example, `먹다` becomes `먹으니까` and `마시다` becomes `마시니까`. Unlike **-서**, the **-(으)니까** form *can* be combined with past tense stems (e.g., `먹었으니까`), though this lesson primarily focuses on the present stem for simplicity.

---

### General Reason vs. Shared Knowledge

The primary distinction in usage relates to the relationship between the speaker and the listener regarding the information provided. **-아/어/해서** is the standard, "safe" choice for stating a general reason in a plain sentence where the listener might not know the cause.
> **많이 먹어서 배가 아파요.** / I ate a lot, so my stomach hurts.

**-(으)니까** is more natural when the listener is already aware of the reason being stated. It carries the nuance of "as you know" or "since it is the case that..." If you use **-(으)니까** for a reason the listener doesn't know, it can sometimes sound slightly rude or demanding, as if you expect them to already know. However, in contexts where both parties clearly see or know the reason, **-(으)니까** is very common.

---

### Reasons for Emotions

When expressing the reason for an emotion or a social greeting, **-아/어/해서** is almost always the correct choice. This applies to verbs like `고맙다 (to be thankful)`, `미안하다 (to be sorry)`, and `반갑다 (to be delighted)`.
> **만나서 반가워요.** / Nice to meet you (I am delighted because I met you).
> **늦어서 죄송합니다.** / I am sorry for being late.

Using **-(으)니까** in these social contexts sounds very awkward and unnatural. You should never say `만나니까 반가워요` or `늦으니까 죄송합니다`.

---

### Imperative and Propositive Sentences

This is a strict grammatical rule: when the second part of the sentence is an **imperative** (command/request) or **propositive** (suggestion), you **must** use **-(으)니까**. You cannot use **-아/어/해서** in these cases.

Imperative endings include **-세요**, **-아/어/해**, or **-지 마세요**. Propositive endings include **-자**, **-(으)ㄹ까요?**, or **-(으)ㄹ래요?**.
> **밖에 비 오니까 우산을 가져가.** / It's raining outside, so take an umbrella.
> **심심하니까 게임 할래요?** / I'm bored, so do you want to play a game?

The instructor emphasizes that even if the reason is a general one, the moment you make a suggestion or give a command, the conjunction must switch to **니까**.

---

### Emphasizing the Reason

A final nuance is that **-(으)니까** can be used to emphasize a reason, especially when answering a "Why?" question. While **-아/어/해서** is the standard answer for "Why?", using **-(으)니까** adds a layer of emphasis to the cause. This is frequently seen in K-pop lyrics (like BTS's "DNA") and K-dramas to make the dialogue more dramatic or emphatic.
> **왜 울어요?** / Why are you crying?
> **슬프니까 울어요!** / I'm crying because I'm sad! (Emphatic)
