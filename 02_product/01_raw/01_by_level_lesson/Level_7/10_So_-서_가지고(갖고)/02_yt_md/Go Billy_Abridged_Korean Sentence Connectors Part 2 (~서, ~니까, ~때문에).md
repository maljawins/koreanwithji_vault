## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on advanced sentence connectors used to create complex sentences in Korean. While basic connectors like 고 (and), 지만 (but), and 는데 (background/but) are essential, they often result in "flat" or unnatural sentences when used to translate English thoughts literally. The instructor introduces three primary ways to express "and then" or "because": ~서, ~니까, and ~때문에.

The learning arc begins with the versatile ~서, covering its dual roles in showing sequential action and cause-and-effect. The instructor then contrasts this with ~니까, which is better suited for commands and suggestions. Finally, the lesson covers the formal ~때문에 and the positive alternative 덕분 (thanks to), providing a comprehensive toolkit for linking ideas logically and naturally.

---

### The Sequential Connector ~서

The connector ~서 is used to show that two actions are strongly related and happen in a specific sequence. Unlike 고, which simply lists two facts (A and B), ~서 implies that action B happens directly after and as a result of being in the state of action A. For example, while "I go to the store and buy milk" could use 고, using ~서 is more natural because it implies you went to the store *to* buy the milk, and the buying happened right after arriving.

Conjugation for ~서 follows the standard 요 form rules, but the 요 is removed before adding 서. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 해서, 배우다 (to learn) becomes 배워서, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹어서, and 가다 (to go) becomes 가서. A critical rule is that this connector is only used in the present tense within the middle of the sentence, even if the final verb of the sentence is in the past tense. The verb 이다 (to be) has a special form: 이라서 after a consonant and 라서 after a vowel.

---

### ~서 as a Cause and Effect Marker

Beyond simple sequence, ~서 is the primary way to show a cause-and-effect relationship, often translated as "because" or "so." The instructor emphasizes thinking of it as "A caused B" rather than just a literal translation of "because." This is especially important when using descriptive verbs. For instance, 많이 먹어서 배가 아파요 (I ate a lot, so my stomach hurts) shows that the act of eating caused the pain.

There are strict limitations to this usage. First, it cannot be used with commands or suggestions. You cannot say "Because it is raining, take an umbrella" using ~서. Second, it is the required form when expressing the reason for an emotion. If you are saying "I am sorry because I am late" or "I am happy because I met you," you must use ~서 (e.g., 늦어서 미안해요).

---

### The Logical Reason Connector ~니까

When you need to give a reason for a command, request, or suggestion, you must use ~니까 instead of ~서. This form is created by attaching 니까 to the verb stem (e.g., 하니까). If the stem ends in a consonant, you add 으니까 (e.g., 먹으니까). There are specific irregular rules: for stems ending in ㄹ, the ㄹ is dropped (살다 becomes 사니까); for descriptive verbs ending in ㅂ, the ㅂ changes to 우 (덥다 becomes 더우니까).

The instructor notes that ~니까 is slightly more informal than other connectors. While it can be used in polite speech, it is often avoided in highly formal writing like essays. Unlike ~서, the two parts of a ~니까 sentence do not need to be as tightly related in a physical sequence; it simply provides a logical justification for the following statement, such as 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요 (Since it's raining, take an umbrella).

---

### Formal Reasons with ~때문에 and 덕분에

For formal situations and writing, ~때문에 is the preferred way to express "because of." It can be used with nouns by simply adding 때문에 (e.g., 친구 때문에 / because of a friend). To use it with verbs, you must first turn the verb into a noun by adding ~기 to the stem, resulting in ~기 때문에 (e.g., 좋아하기 때문에 / because I like).

A nuance of ~때문에 is that when used with people, it often carries a negative connotation of "blame" or "fault." If you want to say "because of you" in a positive sense, you should use 덕분 (thanks to). This follows the same structure: noun + 덕분에 (e.g., 친구 덕분에 / thanks to my friend) or noun + 덕분이다 at the end of a sentence to say "It is thanks to..." This distinction ensures the speaker's intent—whether grateful or accusatory—is clearly understood.
