Lesson 41: To Give: 주다, ~아/어 주다

Introduction

In the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-40/), you learned how to give commands using the imperative voice. In this lesson, you will learn how to use 주다 and how it can be used with the imperative voice. In addition, you will learn how to use ~아/어 주다 when an action is done for you. Let’s get started.

주다 = to give

주다 means “to give” and you already know how to use it in a wide variety of sentences when an object is being given. For example:

저는 저의 친구에게 돈을 줬어요 = I gave my friend money
아빠는 나에게 음식을 줬어 = Dad gave me food

When asking/telling/commanding another person to give something to you, you can attach an imperative ending that you learned in the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-40/). For example, if you want somebody to give you a book, you can attach any imperative form:

Play[그 책을 (나에게) 줘](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s1.mp3) = Give me that book
Play[그 책을 (나에게) 줘라](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s2.mp3) = Give me that book (usually pronounced as 주라)
Play[그 책을 (저에게) 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s3.mp3) = Give me that book

Other examples:
Play[맥주 한 병을 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-4.mp3) = Give me one bottle of beer (please)
Play[저 숟가락을 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-5.mp3) = Give me that spoon (please)
Play[밥을 많이 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-6.mp3) = Give me lots of rice
Play[왼손만 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s4.mp3) = Give me only your left hand
Play[6월 말에 돈을 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s5.mp3) = Give me the money at the end of June

The sentences above only involve *objects* being given. It is also possible to use 주다 when an action is being done for a person. Let’s talk about this next.

Verb stem + ~아/어 주다

By adding ~아/어 주다 to the stem of a word, you can imply that the action is somehow beneficial to you (or whoever the action is being done for); almost as if it were a favor that another person completed. In these cases, not only is the action completed, but it is completed *for*you (or whoever). For example:

그 선생님은 한국어를 저에게 가르쳤어요 = That teacher taught me Korean
Play[그 선생님은 한국어를 저에게 가르쳐 줬어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-9.mp3) = That teacher taught me Korean

Those sentences essentially have the same meaning, but by adding “~아/어 주다” the speaker is emphasizing that the teacher provided some sort of service/favor by teaching you.

Many more examples:

*Notice that*~(으)시*can be added to ~*아/어 주다*if the person acting deserves high respect.*

Play[엄마가 과자를 내 손바닥에 놓아 주었어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s6.mp3)
= Mom put some candy into my hands (on my palms) for me

Play[내년 초에 오빠가 돈을 보내 줄 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s7.mp3)
= My brother will send the money early next year

Play[동료가 그 일을 저에게 설명해 줬어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s8.mp3)
= A colleague explained that work to me

Play[할머니가 오늘 멀리에서 와 주셨어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s9.mp3)
= Grandma came from far away (for me/us) today

Play[경찰관은 밖에 있는 미친 사람과 상대해 줬어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s10.mp3)
= The policeman dealt with the crazy person outside (for me)

Play[제가 외국 사람이라서 이 회사에서 저를 상대해 주고 싶은 사람이 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s11.mp3)
= Nobody wants to deal with me at this company because I’m a foreigner

Play[선생님은 밤에 우리를 위해 어린이집을 잠깐 열어 줬어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s12.mp3)
= The teacher opened the daycare for us at night for a little bit (for us/me)

You learned in the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-40/) how to add imperative endings to words. If you want a person to do something *for*you, you can first add ~아/어 주다 to the stem of the word and add an imperative ending to 주다.

Notice the difference between the following sentences:

Play[빨리 오세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s13.mp3) = Come quick
Play[빨리 와 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s14.mp3) = Come quick (for me)

Notice that both sentences essentially have the same meaning. The first sentence is simply a command, but the second sentence (because of the nature of the word “주다”) implies that the desired action is beneficial to the speaker. Almost as if the action is a favor that the speaker would like to happen.

Therefore, adding ~아/어 주다 to a stem gives the sentence the meaning of “do \_\_ for me.” This often translates more simply to “Please, …..” For example:

Play[점심을 요리해 주세요!](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-19.mp3) = Please, make me lunch
Play[이것을 만들어 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-20.mp3) = Please, make this for me

The two sentences above have essentially the same meaning, but a slightly different feel than the following sentences:

Play[점심을 요리하세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s15.mp3) = Make lunch
Play[이것을 만드세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s16.mp3) = Make this

The only difference being that when using ‘주다’ you are specifically asking for some sort of ‘service.’ When you do not include ‘주다,’ you are just telling somebody to do something. However, by saying 주다, you are indicating that the person is doing something for you.

Many more examples:

Play[그 책을 제자리에 둬 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-23.mp3) = Please put that book back in its place
Play[볼에 이 로션을 발라 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s17.mp3) = Please put this lotion on my cheeks
Play[이것을 이마에 붙여 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s18.mp3) = Please stick this on my forehead
Play[음성 메시지를 남겨 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s19.mp3) = Please leave me a voice message
Play[우리 아이를 이 유치원에 받아 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s20.mp3) = Please accept our child into this kindergarten
Play[발목이 아파서 잠깐 봐 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s21.mp3) = My ankle is sore, so please look at it for a second
Play[이 양말을 오른발에 신겨 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s22.mp3) = Please put this sock on my right foot
Play[정답을 모르는 사람은 오른손을 올려 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s23.mp3) = The people who don’t know the answer, please raise your right hand

In [Lesson 36](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-36/) you learned about the word 보이다 and how it can be used to indicate that one can see something. ~아/어 주다 is often added to 보이다 to ask for something to “be shown” to somebody. 보여주다 and 보여 주다 (with and without the space) are acceptable. For example:

Play[얼굴을 보여주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s24.mp3) = Please show (me) your face
Play[발가락을 보여 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s25.mp3) = Please show me your toe
Play[해외무역건물이 어디 있는지 보여주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s26.mp3) = Please show me where the international trade building is

좀 is an interesting word that is commonly used in Korean – especially in speech. One way that it is used is as a shortened version of “조금.” As such, it can replace “조금” in sentences where appropriate, but this is usually reserved for speaking or dialogue in print. For example:

Play[날씨가 좀 추워졌어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s27.mp3) = The weather got a little bit cold
Play[저는 좀 더 올라갈 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s28.mp3) = I’m going to go a little bit higher
Play[이마가 좀 아파요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s29.mp3) = My forehead is a little bit sore
Play[제가 산 주식은 좀 비쌌어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s30.mp3) = The stocks I bought were a bit expensive

It is also common to find “좀” used in imperative sentences. When asking somebody to do something for you, using “좀” makes the request sound a little bit softer. This is almost impossible to translate to English, as its addition simply adds a feel to the sentence. For example:

Play[밥을 좀 많이 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s31.mp3) = Give me lots of rice
Play[이것을 좀 만들어 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s32.mp3) = Please, make this for me
Play[그 책을 제자리에 좀 둬 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s33.mp3) = Please put that book back in its place (for me)

There are other ways to ask for something in Korean that don’t need the imperative mood. For example, you could also say “Can you do this for me?” It would also be appropriate to use “좀” in sentences like this as well. This grammar has not been introduced yet, so I do not want to talk about it here. It will be talked about in [Lesson 45](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-45/).

Using 주다 with Negative Imperative Sentences

Just like you learned in the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-40/), you can tell a person *not* to do something by adding ~지 말다 to the stem of a word. You can also add ~아/어 주다 after ~지 말다 to have the same effect as described previously. That is, you are asking for somebody to not do something *for*you. For example:

그것을 말하지 마세요 = Don’t say that
Play[그것을 말하지 말아 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-41.mp3) = Please don’t say that

Those sentences essentially have the same meaning, but by adding “주다” the speaker is emphasizing that the listener provided some sort of service/favor by not doing the action. Below are many more examples:

Play[수업 시간 동안 자지 말아 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s34.mp3) = Please don’t sleep in class
Play[저를 쳐다보지 말아 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s35.mp3) = Please don’t stare at me
Play[제 엉덩이를 만지지 말아 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s36.mp3) = Please don’t touch my bum
Play[저를 감옥에 넣지 말아 주세요!](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s37.mp3) = Please don’t put me in prison!
Play[자기의 뿌리를 잊지 말아 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s38.mp3) = Please don’t forget your roots

Thank You For…

This is where everything starts to come together. You can use the concepts you learned in this lesson, the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-40/), and in [Lesson 37](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-34-41/lesson-37/) to say “thank you for….”

If you wanted to say, “thank you for listening,” you would have to use multiple concepts.

First off, a word: 들어보다 (to listen)

By adding ~아/어 주다 to 들어보다, we get: 들어봐 주다, which can be used many ways:

Play[그는 제 말을 들어봐 줬어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s39.mp3) = He listened to me
Play[제 말을 들어봐 주세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s40.mp3) = Listen to me, please

If you add ~아/어서 (because) to 들어봐 주다, you get:

들어봐 줘서…

which means something like “because you listen(ed).”

If you add “감사하다” (thank you) after ~아/어서, you get:

Play[들어봐 줘서 감사합니다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Lesson41s41.mp3) = Because you listened, thank you (thank you for listening)

Okay, one more time.

1) Take a verb: 요리하다 (to cook)
2) Add ~아/어 주다: 요리해 주다
3) Add ~아/어서: 요리해 줘서
4) Add 감사하다 = 요리해 줘서 감사합니다 = thanks for cooking

It seems really confusing because there are so many concepts wrapped in one sentence. More examples will help you get the hang of it:

Play[열심히 공부해 줘서 감사합니다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-50.mp3) = Thanks for studying hard
Play[이것을 가르쳐 줘서 감사합니다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-51.mp3) = Thanks for teaching that to me
Play[문을 열어 줘서 감사합니다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-52.mp3) = Thanks for opening the door for me

To make it *even* more confusing, often times ~(으)시 is added to ~아/어 주다 (because, usually when you are thanking somebody, you are trying to be formal). For example:

Play[이것을 가르쳐 주셔서 감사합니다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson41-53.mp3) = Thanks for teaching that to me
(가르치다 + ~아/어 주다 + ~(으)시 + ~아/어서 + 감사합니다)

And *that*is why I waited until Lesson 41 to teach you about this concept. There would have been no way that you would have been able to grasp everything I taught in this lesson if I had taught it earlier. It took us so long to get to the point where I was comfortable teaching concepts like ~아/어서 and ‘~(으)시,’ and now we are using both of those concepts *together*in addition to what you learned in this lesson.

Tough stuff, but hopefully you understood everything correctly.
