### Basic Construction and Meaning

The grammar form 더니 is created by attaching it directly to any verb stem without exceptions. Etymologically, it consists of two parts: 더, which indicates a past tense that the speaker personally witnessed or experienced, and 니, which functions similarly to the 니 in (으)니까, implying a realization or a causal link. Together, they create a sense of "I recall that this happened, and then I realized this other thing."

Visually, the instructor demonstrates that 더니 separates a sentence into two parts to provide contrast. The primary function is to emphasize that the second part of the sentence is a past event that was surprising or unexpected in light of the first part. While the default translation is often "and then," the nuance is closer to "and what do you know," "and but now," or "and as a result." It is essentially a storytelling form that sets up a "you'll never believe what happened next" scenario.

---

### Usage Rules for Action and Descriptive Verbs

When using 더니 with action verbs, speakers typically use the present tense stem, such as 하더니 (from 하다) or 가더니 (from 가다). Because the 더 component already carries a past tense meaning, a past tense stem is usually unnecessary. However, a speaker might use the past tense stem (e.g., 했더니) to emphasize that the second action occurred only after the first action was completely finished. For descriptive verbs, only the regular present tense stem is used.

A critical rule involves the subject of the sentence. When the subject is a third person (someone else), the standard stem + 더니 construction is used. However, when the speaker is talking about their own actions (first person), they must use the past tense stem followed by 더니. This results in the construction 았/었더니. On screen, the instructor highlights that seeing this specific past-tense-plus-더니 combination is a strong signal that the speaker is talking about themselves.

---

### Quoting and Idioms with 더니

The instructor introduces a shortened quoting form: taking the plain form of a verb and adding 더니, resulting in 다더니. This is a contraction of 다고 하더니 (someone said... and then). This form is used to contrast what someone said in the past with a surprising current reality. For example, if someone said they weren't good at something but then performed excellently, 다더니 would be the appropriate connector.

This quoting variation is particularly useful for incorporating idioms into natural speech. Instead of simply stating an idiom, Koreans often use the form 라더니 (for the verb 이다) to say "They say [idiom], and what do you know, [current situation]." The instructor provides the example of the idiom 싼 게 비지떡 (you get what you pay for), showing how it transforms into 싼 게 비지떡이라더니 to explain why a cheap item broke so quickly.
