### Usage and Meaning of 더니

The instructor explains that 더니 does not have a single direct translation in English; its meaning shifts depending on the context of the sentence. Its most common usage is to show contrast between two events or states. It often carries a nuance of surprise, indicating that the speaker observed or experienced something in the first clause, and the second clause presents a result or a contrasting situation that was unexpected.

The construction is straightforward: the speaker takes a verb stem and attaches 더니. While it can be used with various tenses, the instructor focuses on its use with past observations. Visually, the instructor demonstrates how the form bridges two ideas, such as going somewhere and finding a surprising result.

---

### Examples of Contrast and Discovery

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate how 더니 emphasizes the contrast between the two parts of a sentence. In these cases, the speaker is often reporting a personal observation.

> **친구 집에 갔더니 아무도 없었어요.** / I went to my friend's house, but nobody was there.

In this example, the first clause establishes the action (going to the house), and the second clause reveals the unexpected outcome (nobody being there). The use of 더니 highlights the gap between the expectation of seeing the friend and the reality of the empty house.

> **열심히 공부했더니 시험이 미뤄졌어요.** / I studied hard, but the test was postponed.

Here, the contrast is between the effort exerted (studying hard) and the fact that the effort was rendered moot by the postponement. The instructor notes that using 더니 here conveys the speaker's likely frustration or surprise at the turn of events.

> **가게에 갔더니 물건이 이미 다 팔려 있었어요.** / I went to the store, but everything was already sold out.

This example follows the same pattern of discovery: the speaker arrives at a location only to find a state of affairs that thwarts their original intent.

---

### Examples of Cause and Effect

Beyond simple contrast, 더니 can also show a cause-and-effect relationship where the second clause is a direct result of the behavior observed in the first clause.

> **밤 늦게까지 잠을 자지 않더니 결국 늦게 일어났구나.** / You didn't sleep until it was late, and in the end, you woke up late.

In this sentence, the speaker observes someone else's behavior (staying up late) and then comments on the natural consequence (waking up late). The instructor uses this to show that 더니 can link a past action to its eventual outcome.

> **오랜만에 친구에게 전화를 했더니 전화번호가 바뀌었어요.** / I hadn't called my friend for a long time, and his phone number had changed.

This final example combines discovery and result. The act of calling after a long time led to the discovery that the number was no longer valid.
