## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson explores two advanced variations of the grammatical connector 다가. While the basic 다가 is often taught as "while doing," these specific patterns focus on the sequential movement of objects between locations and the completion of actions before a shift in state or place. The instructor breaks down the conjugation rules, logical constraints, and semantic nuances that distinguish these forms from simpler connectors like 서 or 고.

The first half of the lesson focuses on the `~아/어/여다가` form, which describes performing an action on an object in one location and then moving that object to a second location to perform a subsequent action. The second half introduces the `~았/었/였다가` form, which emphasizes the total completion of an action before a second, often contrasting or unexpected, action occurs. Throughout the video, the instructor emphasizes that the subjects of both actions must be identical and provides practical examples to illustrate how these forms function in natural conversation.

---

### Moving Objects with ~아/어/여다가
The first form is constructed by taking an action verb stem and conjugating it as if it were the polite 요 form (adding 아, 어, or 여), then attaching 다가. The instructor notes that the 가 is optional and often dropped in speech. This form specifically shows that Action A happens first, followed by Action B at a different location.

A critical requirement for this form is the presence of an object. Because the grammar implies "carrying" or "bringing" the result of the first action to a new place, it cannot be used with intransitive verbs like 자다 (to sleep) because there is no object to move. Furthermore, it cannot be used with verbs that "dispose" of the object. For example, you cannot use 먹다 (to eat) as the first verb because once the food is eaten, it cannot be carried to a second location. Similarly, 입다 (to wear) is generally avoided because the action of wearing doesn't involve bringing an external object to a new place to finish a task with it.

While this form can often be replaced by the sequential connector 서 in a learner's mind for logical understanding, they are not identical. The instructor explains that 서 connects related actions (like going to the library and then studying), whereas `~아/어/여다가` specifically highlights the change in location while handling a specific noun.

**Example Sentences:**
> **이 우산을 가져다가 쓰세요.** / Take this umbrella and use it (somewhere else).
> **저기서 물고기를 잡아다가 먹을까요?** / Shall we catch fish over there and (bring them here to) eat them?
> **하얀 민들레를 처음 봐서 화분에 옮겨다가 심었어요.** / Because it was my first time seeing a white dandelion, I moved it into a pot and planted it.
> **동생 옷이 예뻐서 저도 사다가 입었어요.** / My younger sibling's clothes were pretty, so I also bought them (at a store) and wore them (at home).
> **날씨가 추워지면 여기서 두꺼운 이불을 꺼내다가 덮으세요.** / When the weather gets cold, take out a thick blanket from here and cover yourself with it.
> **저도 연을 갖고 싶다고 했더니 아빠가 바로 만들어다가 주셨어요.** / I said I wanted a kite, so my dad made one right away and gave it to me.

### Distinguishing "While" from "Location Change"
The instructor provides a warning: `~아/어/여다가` can sometimes look identical to the standard `~다가` (meaning "while") depending on the verb. For example, in the sentence "운동을 하러 가다가 친구를 만나서 얘기를 했어요" (While going to exercise, I met a friend and talked), the word 가다가 means "while." The distinction is found in the context: if there is no noun being "carried" or affected by the first action to be used in the second, it is likely the "while" form.

---

### Completed Actions and Contrast with ~았/었/였다가
The second form is created by attaching 다가 to the past tense stem of a verb (ending in 았, 었, or 였). This form indicates that Action A is completely finished before Action B begins. It is often synonymous with expressions like 다 하고 나서 (after finishing it all) or 다 한 다음에 (after doing it).

This specific pattern is frequently used to show a contrast between the two actions, often involving an unexpected result. Like the previous form, the subject of both actions must be the same person. It is commonly used with verbs of coming and going to describe stopping by a place or returning after a completed task.

**Example Sentences:**
> **학교에 갔다가 도서관에 갔어요.** / I went to school (finished there) and then went to the library.
> **금방 갔다 올게요.** / I'll go (finish what I need to do) and come back right away. (Commonly translated as "I'll be right back.")
> **그 영화관에 갔다가 전 여자친구를 봤어요.** / I went to that movie theater and (while there/after arriving) saw my ex-girlfriend.
> **카페 잠깐 들렀다가 학교로 갈까요?** / Shall we stop by a cafe for a bit and then go to school?
