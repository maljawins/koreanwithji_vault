## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the advanced grammar form ~다(가) 보면, which is used to express that if a certain action is continued over time, a specific result is expected to occur. The instructor explains that this form is a combination of the backgrounding/interruption marker ~다가 and the verb 보다 (to see) in its conditional form (~보면). Conceptually, it suggests that while you are in the middle of doing something, if you "see" or look at the situation, you will find a certain outcome.

The video provides a comprehensive breakdown of the form's construction, its natural English translations, and its grammatical constraints. A significant portion of the lesson is dedicated to comparing ~다(가) 보면 with similar-looking forms like ~다가는 and ~다 보니까 to help learners distinguish between results, warnings, and personal realizations.

---

### Construction and Meaning
To create this form, you take a plain present tense verb stem and attach ~다 보면. It is important to note that the past or future tense stems are never used; for example, with the verb 하다 (to do), you use 하 + 다 보면 to get 하다 보면. While the full form ~다가 보면 is grammatically correct and occasionally seen, it is much more common to drop the 가 and simply use ~다 보면.

The practical meaning of this form is "if someone continues doing something, then [result]." It implies a process where action A leads to result B. The instructor offers several natural English equivalents to capture the nuance: "once you do," "once you start," "if you keep doing," or even "bound to." For instance, if you keep cooking (요리하다 보면), you are bound to get good at it.

---

### Grammatical Constraints
A critical rule for ~다(가) 보면 is that the sentence cannot end in the past tense. Because the form describes a result that will occur if an action is continued into the future, the final clause must be in the present or future tense. You cannot say "if you keep doing A, B happened." The logic of the tense must flow forward from the ongoing action to the eventual outcome.

---

### Example Sentences
The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate the form in context:

> **매일 책을 읽다 보면 읽는 속도가 빨라질 거예요.** / If you keep reading books every day, your reading speed will get fast.

In this example, the ongoing action is reading every day, and the predicted result is an increase in speed.

> **계속 돈을 모으다 보면 이 집을 살 수 있을까요?** / If I keep saving up my money, would I be able to buy this house?

Here, the speaker asks about a potential future result (buying a house) based on the continued action of saving money (모으다).

> **매운 음식도 계속 먹다 보면 안 맵게 느껴질 거예요.** / If you keep eating spicy food, it won't taste (feel) spicy anymore.

This illustrates how a repeated action leads to a change in state or perception.

> **똑같은 일을 여러 번 반복하다 보면 꿈에서도 하게 되더라고요.** / If you keep doing the same thing over and over again, you end up doing it in your dreams too.

This example uses the ~더라고요 ending to express a result the speaker has personally observed or experienced.

---

### Nuance: "Bound To"
When translating from Korean to English, ~다 보면 often carries the nuance of "bound to," especially when describing natural consequences. The instructor uses the following example to show how this adds a specific flavor to the sentence:

> **아이들이 놀다 보면 싸울 수도 있죠.** / Kids are bound to get in fights when they play together.

Using ~다 보면 here is more descriptive than a simple "if" (~면). it suggests that in the process of playing, it is a natural, almost expected result that a fight might break out.

Another example of a natural progression:
> **계속 가다 보면 아는 길이 나오겠죠.** / If you keep going, there will be (you're bound to find) a street that you know.

---

### Comparison: ~다 보면 vs. ~다가는
While both forms can be translated as "if you keep," they have distinct uses. ~다 보면 is neutral and can be used for any result, whether positive or negative. In contrast, ~다가는 is almost exclusively used for negative results and often functions as a warning. Furthermore, ~다가는 can be used with past tense stems (했다 가는), whereas ~다 보면 is strictly present tense stem only.

Comparison example:
- **계속 굶다 보면 살이 빠질 것 같아요.** / I think you'll lose weight if you keep starving yourself. (Neutral observation of a result).
- **계속 굶다가는 건강을 해칠 거예요.** / You're going to hurt your health if you keep starving yourself. (Warning about a negative consequence).

---

### Comparison: ~다 보면 vs. ~다 보니까
The form ~다 보니까 (or the shortened ~다 보니) focuses on a realization rather than just a result. It implies that after the subject does an action for a while, they suddenly realize something. 

- **Action Verbs:** With action verbs, it translates as "I did something and before I knew it..." or "after [doing]... I realized."
- **Descriptive Verbs:** With descriptive verbs, it translates as "seeing as," "given that," or "since." Unlike ~다 보면, this form can end in the past tense.
