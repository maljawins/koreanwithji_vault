## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces two distinct ways to express the concept of "or" in Korean: **아니면** and **(이)나**. While both can be translated as "or" in English, they are used in different contexts based on whether a specific choice is being made or if options are simply being listed. The lesson focuses on the functional difference: **아니면** is used when a choice must be made between specific options (often in questions), whereas **(이)나** is used for listing possibilities where any option—or even none—might be acceptable.

---

### Making a Choice with 아니면

Whenever a choice has to be made between more than one option, the word **아니면** is used. Etymologically, this word literally means "if it is not," coming from the descriptive verb **아니다 (to not be)**. The instructor explains that while it can be placed between two nouns, it is most naturally used at the beginning of a second sentence to split a question into two parts.

When used between nouns, it looks like this:
> **바닷가 아니면 산도 괜찮아요.** / The beach or the mountains are okay.

However, the instructor emphasizes that it is most common in questions where you are asking someone to choose. For example:
> **바닷가에 갈까요? 아니면 산에 갈까요?** / Shall we go to the beach or the mountains?

The logic of **아니면** implies a "one or the other" scenario. If you say you will watch a movie or a drama using **아니면**, it implies that if you watch one, you will definitely not watch the other. Because of this exclusive nature, it is frequently used in questions to narrow down a decision.

---

### Examples of 아니면 in Context

The instructor provides several examples showing how **아니면** functions at the start of a second sentence to present a choice:

- **치즈 피자를 먹을 거예요? 아니면 소시지 피자를 먹을 거예요?** / Are you going to eat a cheese pizza or a sausage pizza?
- **젓가락을 쓰고 싶어요? 아니면 포크를 쓰고 싶어요?** / Do you want to use chopsticks or a fork?
- **단 커피를 좋아해요? 아니면 쓴 커피를 좋아해요?** / Do you like sweet coffee or bitter coffee?
- **짠 걸 먹고 싶어요? 아니면 매운 걸 먹고 싶어요?** / Do you want to eat something salty or something spicy?
- **조금 더 기다릴까요? 아니면 그냥 갈까요?** / Shall we wait a little bit more, or shall we just go?

Finally, the instructor reminds the viewer of the literal meaning "if it is not" with a specific sentence:
> **저 아니면 누구한테 물어볼 거예요?** / If it's not me, who will you ask? (Who will you ask if not me?)

---

### Listing Options with (이)나

The second way to say "or" is by using the particle **(이)나**. Unlike **아니면**, this is not used for making a definitive choice. Instead, it is used for listing possible options where the specific choice doesn't matter much. A better English translation for this form is often "or something." Using **(이)나** suggests that any of the listed options are fine, and potentially even something else not listed would be okay.

This form is a particle attached directly to a noun:
- Attach **이나** if the noun ends in a consonant (e.g., **옷이나**).
- Attach **나** if the noun ends in a vowel (e.g., **영화나**).

The instructor compares the nuance:
> **영화나 드라마를 볼 거예요.** / I'll watch a movie or a drama (or something).

In this case, the speaker might watch a movie, a drama, something else entirely, or even nothing at all. It is a much more casual and open-ended way of suggesting options.

---

### Examples of (이)나 in Context

The instructor provides examples where **(이)나** lists possibilities:

- **옷이나 모자를 살 거예요.** / I'll buy clothes, or a hat, or something.
- **서울이나 부산에 여행하고 싶어요.** / I want to go on a trip to Seoul or Busan.
- **따뜻한 커피나 우유를 드릴까요?** / Shall I give you some warm coffee, or milk, or something?
- **저는 볶음밥이나 김치찌개를 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to eat fried rice, or kimchi stew, or something.
- **우리 박물관이나 공원에 같이 갈까요?** / Shall we go together to the museum, or the park?
- **제가 어렸을 때 개구리나 뱀을 키우고 싶었어요.** / I wanted to raise a frog or a snake when I was young.

A key takeaway is that **(이)나** can be used with just a single noun to mean "or something" or "just." For example, **밥이나 먹어요** means "let's eat a meal or something."

---

### Conversation Practice

The lesson concludes with a dialogue between **희철** and **동의**. The instructor notes that **희철** is younger, so he uses polite speech (**존댓말**), while **동의** uses casual speech (**반말**).

> **희철: 형, 질문해도 돼요? 제 과제를 좀 도와줄 수 있을까요?** / Older brother (friend), can I ask a question? Could you help me a little bit with my assignment?
> **동의: 무슨 과제?** / What assignment?
> **희철: 수학이에요. 어려운 문제인데요.** / It's math. It's a difficult problem.
> **동의: 그래, 보자. 이거야?** / All right, let's see. Is it this?
> **희철: 이건 3이에요? 아니면 10이에요?** / Is this a 3 or a 10? (Using **아니면** for a specific choice).
> **동의: 그건 3이고 다음 문제는 8이야.** / That's a 3, and the next problem is an 8.
> **희철: 고마워요, 형. 다음에 밥이나 같이 먹어요.** / Thank you. Next time, let's eat a meal or something together. (Using **이나** to suggest a casual option).
