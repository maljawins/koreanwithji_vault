Lesson 49: There is no problem if: ~아/어도 되다

Introduction

In this lesson, you will build on your understanding of ~아/어도 to make sentences using 되다. Using these concepts, you will be able to indicate that here is no problem if something is or is not done. Let’s get started.

Another meaning of 되다

In previous lessons, you learned some uses of the word “되다.” For example in [Lesson 9](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-9-16/unit-1-lesson-9/) you learned that it can be used as a stand-alone verb which means “to become:”

Play[저는 선생님이 되고 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson17-38.mp3) = I want to be/become a teacher

In [Lesson 14](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-9-16/lesson-14-korean-passive-verbs/), you learned about its function in passive sentences. For example:

Play[세금은 값에 포함된다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/16.mp3) = The tax is included in the price

Another usage of “되다” is to indicate that there is “no problem” with something. Common translations for this can be “for something to be going well” or “for something to be working (well).” You can typically place “되다” after a noun to indicate that there is no problem with that noun. The word “잘” is often included in these sentences as its nature to mean “well:” For example:

Play[사업이 잘 되고 있어요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s1.mp3) = Is your business going well?
Play[일이 잘 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s2.mp3) = Is your work going well?
Play[여기서 Wi-Fi가 잘 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s3.mp3) = The Wi-Fi here works well
Play[심장수술이 잘 됐어요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s4.mp3) = Did the heart surgery go well?
Play[문제 해결이 잘 됐어요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s5.mp3) = Did the problem get solved okay?

This same usage is often used to say that something has “finished” doing whatever it should be doing, for example

Play[밥이 됐어요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s6.mp3) = Is the rice ready?/Has the rice finished cooking?

You can also use 되다 to tell somebody to stop giving you something. For example, if somebody is filling up a glass of water for you, and you want to say “Okay! That’s enough!” you can just say “됐어(요)!”

If you wanted to say the opposite – that is – that something is *not*going well, not working or is not finished, you should use the word 안 (even though the usual opposite of “잘” is “못”). “잘” can also be included in these sentences along with “안”  (just like how “잘” can be used alongside “못”). For example:

Play[밥이 아직은 안 됐어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s7.mp3) = The rice still isn’t ready
Play[여기서 Wi-Fi가 (잘) 안 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s8.mp3) = The Wi-Fi doesn’t work (well) here
Play[컴퓨터가 안 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s9.mp3) = The computer isn’t working
Play[로그인이 안 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s10.mp3) = The log-in isn’t working (I can’t log in)

Person 1: Play[축구 경기를 내일 보러 갈래요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49-new13.mp3) = Shall we go to see a soccer game tomorrow?
Person 2: Play[저는 가고 싶지만 내일은 안 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49-new13.mp3) = I want to go, but I can’t go tomorrow (tomorrow doesn’t work for me)

I want to talk about this usage of 되다 and how it can be applied in sentences with ~아/어도.

To be allowed to do: ~아/어도 되다

In the previous section, you learned how 되다 can be used to indicate that there is “no problem” with something.

In the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-48/), you learned how to attach ~아/어도 to the stem of a clause to create the meaning of “regardless of…” For example:

네가 일찍 가도 … = Regardless of if you leave early…

되다 (in the usage described in this lesson) is commonly used after ~아/어도 to indicate that there is no problem with the action being done. For example:

Play[네가 일찍 가도 돼](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s11.mp3) = Regardless of if you leave early, there is no problem

This typically translates to “one may” or “one is allowed to.” For example:

Play[네가 일찍 가도 돼](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s11.mp3) = “You may go early,” or “You are allowed to go early”

Below are many examples:

Play[아무거나 해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s13.mp3) = You can do whatever you want
Play[지금 문을 열어도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s14.mp3) = You may open the door now
Play[제일 편리한 것을 선택해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s15.mp3) = You may choose the most convenient one
Play[여기 있어도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s16.mp3) = You are allowed to be here
Play[당구를 여기서 쳐도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s17.mp3) = You are allowed to play pool here
Play[이 직장에서 수염을 길러도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s18.mp3) = You are allowed to have a beard at this job
Play[내일 시합에서 명예를 회복해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s19.mp3) = You can regain your honor during tomorrow’s game
Play[고기에 그 단단한 부분을 먹어도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s20.mp3) = You can/may even eat that hard/stiff part of the meat
Play[학생들이 들어가도 되지만 부모를 동반해야 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s0.mp3) = Students may enter, but they must be accompanied by a parent
Play[문제를 이해하지 못하면 감독관에게 물어봐도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s21.mp3) = You may ask the supervisor/proctor if you don’t understand the question

These types of sentences can also be used to ask questions. Here, the speaker is asking if there is any “problem” with an action occurring. For example:

Play[제가 일찍 가도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s22.mp3) = Regardless of if I go early, is there any problem?

These types of questions typically translation to “May…?” or “Is it alright if…” For example:

Play[제가 일찍 가도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s22.mp3) = May I go early?

Below are many examples:

Play[밥을 많이 먹어도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson49-26.mp3) = May I eat a lot?
Play[화장실에 가도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson49-27.mp3) = May I go to the bathroom?
Play[얼룩말에게 먹이를 줘도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s23.mp3) = May I give feed to the zebras?
Play[우리가 탁구를 해도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s24.mp3) = May we play table tennis?
Play[죄수를 보러 교도소에 들어가도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s25.mp3) = May we go into the prison to see the prisoners?

Play[옆에 있는 사람이 하는 말이 아주 불쾌해서 다른 자리로 가도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s26.mp3)
= What the person beside me is saying is unpleasant, so may I go to another seat?

Play[선생님! 이 문제를 파악할 수 없어요. 나중에 선생님의 교무실에 가도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s27.mp3)
= Teacher! I can’t grasp/understand this problem. May I go to your office later?

The final clause of all of these example sentences has been in the present tense. It is possible to conjugate 되다 to the past or future tenses to indicate that there would have been no problem if an action had been done, or will be done in the future. For example:

Play[그때 나가도 되었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s28.mp3) = It would have been okay if you left at that time
Play[문을 열어도 되었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s29.mp3) = It would have been okay if you opened the door

Play[그때 나가도 될 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s30.mp3) = It will be okay if you leave at that time
Play[문을 열어도 될 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s31.mp3) = It will be okay if you open the door

One doesn’t have to: 안 ~아/어도 되다

In the previous section, you learned how to indicate that there will be “no problem” if some action was completed. In order to indicate that there will be “no problem” if some action *doesn’t* occur, you can conjugate the verb before ~아/어도 negatively using 안 or ~지 않다. For example:

Play[네가 빨리 안 가도 돼](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s32.mp3) = Regardless of if you don’t leave quickly/early, there is no problem

This typically translates to “it is okay if one does not” or “one doesn’t have to.” For example:

Play[네가 빨리 안 가도 돼](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s32.mp3) = You don’t have to go quickly/early

Below are many examples:

Play[내일까지 다 안 해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s33.mp3) = You don’t need to do it all by tomorrow
Play[저는 밥을 벌써 먹어서 다시 안 먹어도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson49-39.mp3) = I don’t need to eat because I already did
Play[여기에 사인을 안 해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson49-40.mp3) = You don’t need to sign here
Play[이 음식에 소금을 안 넣어도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson49-41.mp3) = You don’t need to put salt on this food
Play[그런 것을 걱정 안 해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s34.mp3) = You don’t need to worry about that kind of thing

Play[그 시험을 합격하기 위해 공부를 안 해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s35.mp3)
= In order to pass that test, I don’t need to study

Play[이 학교가 과학 고등학교라서 인문학을 공부하지 않아도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s36.mp3)
= You don’t need to study the humanities at this school because it is a science high school

Play[그 나라에서 선생님이 되고 싶으면 특별한 자격이 있지 않아도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s37.mp3)
= If you want to become a teacher in that country, you don’t need any special qualification

Play[핸드폰에 연락처 기능이 있어서 요즘에 사람들은 기억력이 좋지 않아도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s38.mp3)
= Cell phones have contact functions in them so these days people don’t need to have a good memory

You could also generally see this form as the opposite of ~아/어야 하다, which you learned in [Lesson 46](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-46/). Notice the opposite meanings that these sentences have:

Play[그 시험을 합격하기 위해 공부를 안 해도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s35.mp3) = In order to pass that test, I don’t need to study
Play[그 시험을 합격하기 위해 공부를 해야 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s40.mp3) = In order to pass that test, I need to study

This form can be used to ask questions as well. For example:

Play[지금 밥을 안 먹어도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s41.mp3) = You don’t need to eat (rice) now?
Play[내일까지 다 안 해도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s42.mp3) = You don’t need to do it all by tomorrow?
Play[수염을 안 깎아도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s43.mp3) = You don’t need to shave your beard?
Play[자격증을 안 보여줘도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s44.mp3) = I don’t need to show you my certification?

Using Words like 좋다 and 괜찮다

In all of the sentences in this lesson using ~아/어도 되다, the common meaning applied to all sentences is “it is okay if…” or “there is no problem if.” Instead of using 되다, other words that have a similar meaning can be used as well. The two most common substitutes for 되다 in this usage are 좋다 and 괜찮다. For example:

Play[지금 문을 열어도 돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s14.mp3) = You may open the door now
Play[지금 문을 열어도 좋아요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s46.mp3) = You may open the door now
Play[지금 문을 열어도 괜찮아요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s47.mp3) = You may open the door now

Play[밥을 많이 먹어도 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Lesson49-26.mp3) = May I eat a lot?
Play[밥을 많이 먹어도 좋아요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s48.mp3) = May I eat a lot?
Play[밥을 많이 먹어도 괜찮아요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Lesson49s49.mp3) = May I eat a lot?
