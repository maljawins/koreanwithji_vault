## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor provides a comprehensive guide to expressing permission, prohibition, and general requirements in Korean. The lesson focuses on three primary grammatical structures: `-도 돼요` (permission), `-(으)면 돼요` (requirement/sufficiency), and `-(으)면 안 돼요` (prohibition). While these patterns appear similar, the instructor emphasizes that they carry distinct nuances and are used in different social contexts.

The pedagogical approach involves breaking down each expression into its constituent parts—conjunctions and the verb `되다`—to help students understand the underlying logic of the grammar. By understanding that `도` comes from "even if" and `면` comes from "if," learners can more intuitively grasp why these phrases mean "it is okay even if..." or "it works if..." The lesson concludes with a comparison of nuances and a discussion of the negative form `-도 안 돼요`.

---

### Permission with -도 돼요
The expression `-도 돼요` is the standard way to give or ask for permission, translating to "it is okay to [verb]" or "you may [verb]." To conjugate this, the verb is first put into the present tense (아/어/여 form) and then combined with `도 돼요`. The instructor explains that `도` originates from the sentence connector `그래도 (but still/even so)`. Therefore, the literal meaning is "it is okay even if you [verb]."

Examples of permission and concession:
> **내일 와도 돼요.** / It is okay to come tomorrow.
> **오늘 좀 늦게 자도 돼요?** / Is it okay for me to sleep a little late today?
> **라떼를 주문해도 돼요.** / You can order a latte (it's fine/that works too).

The instructor notes that `-도 돼요` can also be used in a "conceding" sense, where an original plan is changed but the new option is still acceptable. For instance, if a class was originally at 4:00 but is moved to 5:00, one might say `5시에 해도 돼요` to indicate that 5:00 "works too."

---

### Sufficiency and Requirement with -(으)면 돼요
The structure `-(으)면 돼요` translates to "you just have to [verb]" or "it works if you [verb]." Unlike the permission marker, this comes from the conditional `그러면 (if so/then)`. It is used to explain how something works, to give instructions, or to tell someone what they are supposed to do in a specific situation.

The conjugation rule for `-(으)면` uses the verb stem: add `으면` if the stem ends in a consonant (받침) and `면` if it ends in a vowel. The instructor highlights that this expression is often used by service staff or superiors to politely tell someone what to do.

Examples of instructions and requirements:
> **매일 공부하면 돼요.** / You just have to study every day (It works if you study every day).
> **종료 버튼을 누르면 돼요.** / You just have to press the "shut down" button.
> **저한테 주시면 돼요.** / You can just give it to me (I am the person in charge).

A key distinction is made between `저한테 주시면 돼요` and `저한테 주셔도 돼요`. The former implies the speaker is the correct person to receive the item, while the latter implies that even if the speaker isn't the one in charge, it is "permissible" to give it to them.

---

### Prohibition and Pleas with -(으)면 안 돼요
The expression `-(으)면 안 돼요` is used for prohibition, meaning "you should not" or "you are not allowed to." Literally, it translates to "it doesn't work if you [verb]." In a statement, it is a firm rule or a warning based on a standard or concern.

Examples of prohibition:
> **만지면 안 돼요.** / You should not touch it.
> **늦게 오면 안 돼요.** / You cannot come late.
> **내일 비가 오면 안 돼요.** / It shouldn't rain tomorrow (expressing a strong wish/concern).

When used as a question, `-(으)면 안 돼요?` changes nuance significantly. It becomes a "plea" or a strong suggestion, often used when the speaker really wants the listener to say yes. It is more emotional than the neutral permission request `-도 돼요?`. For example, `오늘 피자 먹으면 안 돼요?` sounds like "Can't we please eat pizza today?" whereas `오늘 피자 먹어도 돼요?` is a simple "Is it okay if we eat pizza today?"

---

### The Meaning of -도 안 돼요
The instructor clarifies a common point of confusion: `-도 안 돼요` is not the direct opposite of `-도 돼요`. Instead, it has two specific meanings. First, it means "it doesn't work even if [verb]," often paired with the adverb `아무리 (no matter how)`. Second, it means "even [verb] is not allowed," typically used to list an additional prohibition.

Examples of `-도 안 돼요`:
> **아무리 노력해도 안 돼요.** / No matter how much I try, it doesn't work.
> **아무리 들어도 이해가 안 돼요.** / No matter how much I listen, I don't understand.
> **애완동물을 키워도 안 돼요.** / Even raising a pet is not allowed (in addition to other rules).

---

### Understanding the Verb 되다
To ground these expressions, the instructor explains the base verb `되다`. It has three primary meanings:
1. **To become:** Used with the particles `이/가` (e.g., `선생님이 되다` - to become a teacher).
2. **To work/function:** Used when a device or system is operational (e.g., `핸드폰이 이제 돼요` - the phone works now).
3. **To be okay/fine:** Used to describe a situation that is acceptable (e.g., `늦어도 돼` - it's fine even if you're late).

The grammar points in this lesson primarily draw from the second and third meanings of `되다`.
