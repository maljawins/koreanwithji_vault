### Overview
The instructor introduces the essential grammar for expressing obligation and necessity in Korean, equivalent to "have to," "need to," or "must" in English. ---

### Reviewing 필요하다 (To Be Necessary)
Before diving into the new grammar, the instructor reviews 필요하다 (to be necessary). Because this is a descriptive verb, it cannot be used with the object marker (을/를). Instead, the thing that is needed is the subject of the sentence. The opposite expression is 필요가 없다 (to be unnecessary). An example provided on screen is:
> **저는 모자가 필요해요.** / I need a hat. (Literally: As for me, a hat is necessary.)

While 필요하다 is useful for needing objects, the instructor explains that a different structure is required to say one "needs to do" an action.

---

### The 야 Form: Construction and Meaning
The 야 form is the standard way to express "must," "have to," or "need to." It can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs. With action verbs, it indicates an obligation to perform an action. With descriptive verbs, it indicates that something needs to be or has to be a certain way.

The instructor breaks down the conjugation process into four steps:
1. Conjugate the verb into the standard 아/어 form (the same way you would for the polite present tense).
2. Do not add the 요 ending.
3. Attach 야 to the end of the stem.
4. Add the verb 되다 and conjugate it to the desired speech level. Alternatively, the verb 하다 can be used instead of 되다, though this is slightly less common.

The instructor demonstrates this with two primary examples:
- **가다 (to go) → 가 + 야 + 돼요 = 가야 돼요** (I have to go.)
- **먹다 (to eat) → 먹어 + 야 + 돼요 = 먹어야 돼요** (I have to eat.)

### Practical Examples and Nuances
The instructor provides a series of example sentences to illustrate the grammar in context. A key vocabulary note is shared: the verb 먹다 (to eat) is used when talking about taking medicine in Korean.

> **저는 약을 먹어야 돼요. 그래서 먼저 약국에 가야 돼요.** / I need to take medicine. So first, I need to go to the pharmacy.
> **저는 내년에 바닷가에 가고 싶으니까 지금부터 운동을 해야 돼요.** / Because I want to go to the beach next year, I need to exercise from now.
> **할아버지가 지난주에 돌아가셨어요. 그래서 이번 주는 장례식에 가야 돼요.** / My grandfather passed away last week. So this week I have to go to the funeral.
> **아버지께서 주무시니까 우리는 조용히 해야 돼요.** / We have to be quiet because my father is sleeping. (Note: 조용히 하다 is treated as an action verb meaning "behaving in a quiet way.")

Further examples cover more complex scenarios, such as travel and financial needs:
> **기차를 못 탔으니까 열차를 타야 할 것 같아요.** / I think I will have to take a regular train because I couldn't take the electric train.
> **우리는 내년 봄 아니면 여름까지 기다려야 돼요.** / We have to wait until next year spring or summer.
> **한국에 도착한 후에 돈을 환전해야 돼요.** / After I arrive in Korea, I have to exchange money.
> **비행기 표를 빨리 사야 돼요. 내일은 더 비쌀 것 같아요.** / I need to quickly buy a plane ticket. Tomorrow I think it will be more expensive.
> **제 건강에 대해 생각해야 돼요.** / I have to think about my health.

### Conversation Practice: Advanced Usage
The lesson concludes with a conversation between two characters, 찬열 and 경수. This section introduces how the 야 form can be used as an adjective or in the future tense.

The instructor highlights that **준비해야 할 게** (something I will have to prepare) uses the future tense adjective form. He also points out that since 되다 is an action verb, it conjugates to **되는** in the present tense adjective form, as seen in the phrase **준비해야 되는 것들** (things that you need to prepare).

The full conversation covers a workplace scenario:
> **찬열: 다음 미팅의 날짜를 아세요? 다음 달이에요?** / Do you know the date of the next meeting? Is it next month?
> **경수: 저도 몰라요. 제가 일단 이걸 해야 돼요. 그리고 확인해 볼게요.** / I don't know either. I have to do this first. And then I'll check.
> **찬열: 네, 알아봐 주세요. 제가 미리 준비해야 할 게 있어요.** / Yes, please find out. I have something I have to prepare in advance.
> **경수: 찬열 씨가 준비해야 되는 것들이 있어요?** / Are there things that you need to prepare, 찬열?
> **찬열: 네, 그리고 다른 일도 많아요. 그래서 제가 서둘러야 돼요. 시간이 별로 없어요.** / Yes, and there is also a lot of other work. Therefore, I need to hurry. I don't really have much time.
> **경수: 그럼 찬열 씨가 사장님께 연락해 보세요.** / Then, you try to contact the boss.
> **찬열: 네, 그렇게 할게요.** / Yes, I will do that.
