## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the essential grammar structures for expressing obligation and prohibition in Korean, specifically how to say "should" and "should not." The lesson focuses on the most common conversational forms of these expressions. The approach is rooted in understanding the literal meanings of these grammatical constructions, which helps learners grasp the underlying logic of why these specific words are used to convey advice or rules.

---

### Expressing "Should" with -(으)면 좋다

To express that someone "should" do something, the instructor explains that you take the verb stem and attach the conditional marker 면 (if), followed by the verb 좋다 (to be good). This construction literally translates to "it is good if [you do something]." By framing an action as being "good if done," it functions as a natural way to give advice or suggest a course of action in Korean.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate this pattern:

> **빨리 숙제하면 좋아요.** / You should hurry up and do your homework. (Literally: It's good if you do your homework quickly.)
> **병원에 가면 좋아요.** / You should go to the hospital. (Literally: It's good if you go to the hospital.)
> **한국어를 더 열심히 공부하면 좋아요.** / You should study Korean harder. (Literally: It's good if you study Korean more diligently.)

---

### Expressing "Should Not" with -(으)면 안 되다

When expressing that someone "should not" do something, the instructor introduces the most common pattern: attaching 면 (if) to the verb stem, followed by 안 되다 (to not work / to not be okay). This literally means "it won't be good if you do something" or "it doesn't work if you do something." While there are other ways to express prohibition, this is the standard conversational form taught in this lesson.

The instructor illustrates this with the following examples:

> **빨간 불에 길을 건너면 안 돼요.** / You shouldn't cross the street when it's a red light.
> **그거 먹으면 안 돼요.** / You shouldn't eat that.
> **수업 시간에 자면 안 돼요.** / You shouldn't sleep in class time.

The visual aids in the video reinforce these points by showing the text of the rules and examples clearly on screen as the instructor speaks. The lesson concludes with a humorous skit involving a character spilling almonds and being told what they should or should not do, providing a practical context for the grammar.
