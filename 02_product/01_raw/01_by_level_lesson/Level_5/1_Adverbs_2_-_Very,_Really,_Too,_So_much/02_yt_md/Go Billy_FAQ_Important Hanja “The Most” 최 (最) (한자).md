### The Origin and Meaning of 최 (最)

The character 최 (最) has a unique origin story involving an old-style Korean hat called a 감투. Originally, the character was a drawing of someone reaching out to grab or pull off someone else's 감투. In ancient Korea, these hats were worn by government officials, and forcibly removing one was considered an act of extreme rudeness or disrespect—comparable to spitting on someone. Over time, the meaning shifted from the act of being rude to the person wearing the hat themselves: a government official. Because government officials were seen as highly skilled or great individuals, the meaning evolved further to signify someone who is "great" or "skilled," eventually settling on its modern meaning: "the most" or "the best" among others.

### Superlatives of Quality and Value

The most common application of 최 is in creating superlative forms of adjectives. The instructor highlights several key pairs and individual terms:

- **최고 (the best / highest):** Combining 최 with 고 (high), this literally means "the most high." While it can refer to a physical height or a numerical value like **최고 점수 (the highest score)**, it is most frequently used to mean "the best." You can tell a friend "너 최고야!" (You're the best!) or refer to someone as the **최고의 한국어 선생님 (the best Korean teacher)**.
- **최저 (the lowest):** The opposite of 최고 in a numerical or positional sense, using 저 (low). It means "the most low." A common example is **최저임금 (minimum wage)**, literally the "lowest wage."
- **최악 (the worst):** Using 악 (evil/bad), this literally means "the most evil" or "the most bad." It is used for the worst-case scenarios or terrible experiences, such as saying "**오늘 진짜 최악이었어**" (Today was truly the worst).

### Size, Scale, and Limits

최 is also used to define the upper and lower bounds of scale and quantity.

- **최대 (the biggest / maximum):** Uses 대 (big) to mean the largest possible size or amount.
- **최소 (the smallest / minimum):** Uses 소 (small) to mean the smallest possible size or amount.

These two terms are frequently paired with the word 한 to create adverbs of limitation: **최대한 (at most / as much as possible)** and **최소한 (at least / at minimum)**.

---

### Time and Modern Trends

The character appears in several words related to time and the current state of things.

- **최근 (recently / lately):** Uses 근 (close). Literally "the closest" in terms of time, it refers to things happening nowadays. It is often used as **최근에** or **최근에는**.
- **최신 (the newest / latest):** Uses 신 (new). This is commonly seen in consumer contexts, such as buying the **최신 모델 (the newest model)** of a phone or gadget.
- **최종 (the final / last):** Uses 종 (end/finish). It means "the most end" or the absolute final stage of something, such as **최종 결과 (the final results)**.

---

### Personal Favorites: 최애 (最愛)

A very popular modern term is **최애**, which combines 최 with 애 (love). This literally means "the most loved" and translates to "favorite" or, in the context of fandoms, one's "bias." The instructor shares that his **최애 한국 드라마 (favorite Korean drama)** is the 2015 detective show *Signal* (시그널).
