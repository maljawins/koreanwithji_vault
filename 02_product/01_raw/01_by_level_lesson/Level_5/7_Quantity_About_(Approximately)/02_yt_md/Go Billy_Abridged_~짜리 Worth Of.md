## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the suffix **짜리**, which is used to indicate an amount, value, or quantity of something. The core concept is that it attaches directly to a word—almost always a counter word—to describe a noun's specific "worth" or "size." While the literal meaning remains consistent, the English translation varies significantly depending on the counter it follows, ranging from "worth" and "valued at" to "long" or "story" (for buildings).

The lesson arc moves from basic definitions and the mechanics of attaching the suffix to a variety of practical examples involving money, time, age, physical measurements, and quantity. By the end of the session, the instructor demonstrates how **짜리** functions like an adjective, allowing speakers to connect a specific quantity directly to a noun to create a descriptive phrase.

---

### Basic Usage and Mechanics
The instructor writes the formula **Word + 짜리** on the whiteboard, noting that the "word" is typically a counter word. He defines the core meaning as "worth (of)," "amount (of)," or "number (of)." A key visual point is that **짜리** sticks directly to the preceding word without a space. For example, when used with the currency counter **원 (won)**, it becomes **원짜리**.

A significant challenge with this suffix is its translation. The instructor explains that while it always means "an amount of," the English equivalent changes based on the context. With money, it might be "valued at"; with time, it might be "long"; and with floors of a building, it translates to "story." This flexibility allows it to function as a bridge, connecting a counter word to a noun as if it were an adjective.

### Money and Value Examples
The instructor provides several examples involving Korean currency. He writes **1000원짜리** and explains it can be translated as "worth 1,000 won" or "valued at 1,000 won." If you have a **1000원짜리 쿠폰**, it is a coupon worth 1,000 won.

A comparison example is shown on screen:
> **오천원짜리 우산이 오만원짜리 우산보다 낫네요.** / A 5,000 won umbrella is better than a 50,000 won umbrella.

The instructor notes that **오천원짜리 우산** literally means "an umbrella that is the amount of 5,000 won." He also highlights a specific use case where **짜리** refers to a physical bill or coin. For instance, **오만원짜리** can simply mean "a 50,000 won bill." He provides the sentence:
> **오만원짜리 한 장이면 돼요.** / One 50,000 won bill is enough.

Finally, he shows how to ask for the value of something using the question word **얼마 (how much)**:
> **이건 얼마짜리예요?** / How much is this worth?

### Time, Age, and Measurement Examples
The instructor demonstrates that **짜리** is equally common with other types of counters. When used with **시간 (hours)**, it describes duration.
> **세 시간짜리 영화를 봤더니 너무 피곤해요.** / I watched a three-hour-long movie, and as a result, I'm so tired.

For age, it attaches to **살 (years old)**. The instructor writes **5살짜리** and explains it describes a "five-year-old."
> **다섯 살짜리 애가 어떻게 요리를 해요?** / How can a five-year-old child cook?

Physical dimensions like height or length use **짜리** with units like **층 (floors)** or **미터 (meters)**.
> **거기서 120층짜리 건물도 봤어요.** / I also saw a 120-story building there.
> **어제 2미터짜리 오징어를 잡았어요.** / Yesterday, I caught a two-meter-long squid.

Lastly, he uses the counter **장 (sheets of paper/flat objects)** to describe the length of a letter:
> **동생에게 다섯 장짜리 편지를 받았어요.** / I received a five-page letter from my younger sibling.
