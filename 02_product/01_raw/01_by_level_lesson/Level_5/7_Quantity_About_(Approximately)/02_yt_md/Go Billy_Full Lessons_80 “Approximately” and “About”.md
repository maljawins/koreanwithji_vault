## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces how to make approximations and guesses in Korean, focusing on three primary words that translate to "approximately" or "about." The lesson covers the specific placement of these words in relation to nouns, their spacing rules, and the subtle differences in nuance between spoken and written contexts. The instructor uses a variety of practical examples involving measurements, time, and quantities to demonstrate these grammar points.

---

### Using 정도 for Approximations

The first word introduced is **정도**, which means "about" or "approximately." The instructor emphasizes a critical word order rule: in Korean, **정도** must be placed after the noun it is modifying. Placing it before the noun is incorrect and would be difficult for a native speaker to understand. Additionally, there must be a space between the noun and **정도**.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this placement:
- **삼미터 정도예요.** / It's about three meters.
- **이주일 정도 됐어요.** / It's been about two weeks.
- **책상에 의자가 여섯 개 정도 있어요.** / There are about six chairs at the desk.

---

### Using 쯤 for Approximations

The second word, **쯤**, carries the same meaning as **정도** but follows a different spacing rule. Unlike **정도**, **쯤** is attached directly to the end of the word it follows without any space. It is also placed after the noun. The instructor highlights that both **정도** and **쯤** are extremely common in everyday Korean.

Examples shown on screen include:
- **두 시간쯤 잤어요.** / I slept about two hours.
- **한국에 두 달쯤 있었어요.** / I was in Korea for about two months.
- **식빵은 한 조각에 백 칼로리쯤이에요.** / It's about 100 calories for a piece of bread. (Note: **조각** is the counter for "piece").

---

### Using 약 in Formal or Written Contexts

The third word, **약**, also means "approximately," but its usage differs significantly from the previous two. **약** is placed before the noun or quantity it refers to, rather than after. While it is perfectly acceptable to use in speech, it carries a slightly more formal or "textbook" feeling and is more frequently encountered in writing. The instructor suggests that beginners focus on mastering **정도** and **쯤** first before incorporating **약** into their active vocabulary.

Examples of **약** in use:
- **어제의 기온은 약 40도였어요.** / Yesterday, the temperature was approximately 40 degrees Celsius. (Note: **기온** refers to weather temperature).
- **약 5명이 왔어요.** / Approximately 5 people came.
- **가게에는 사과가 약 100개 있어요.** / There are approximately 100 apples in the store.
