### Overview

In this lesson, Miss Vicky introduces six essential particles for high-beginner Korean learners: 동안, 마다, 쯤/정도, (이)나, 밖에, and 처럼. These particles are fundamental for expressing duration, frequency, approximation, options, limitations, and comparisons. ### Expressing Duration with 동안

The particle 동안 is used to express "for" or "during" a specific length of time. It is frequently paired with duration counters such as 년 (year), 달/개월 (month), 주/주일 (week), 일 (day), 시간 (hour), 분 (minute), or 초 (second). For example, to say you learned Korean for one year, you use 1년 동안 배웠어요. The instructor provides several examples: 7개월 동안 일했어요 (worked for 7 months), 2주 동안 쉬었어요 (took a break for 2 weeks), 고향에 5일 동안 있었어요 (was in hometown for 5 days), and 한 시간 동안 기다렸어요 (waited for an hour).

A key grammatical nuance is that 동안 can be omitted when used with these duration counters because the counter itself already implies a length of time. Thus, 1년 배웠어요 or 1시간 기다렸어요 are perfectly natural. However, 동안 can also be used with time period nouns that are not counters, such as 방학 (school vacation), 겨울 (winter), or 3월 (March). In these cases, 동안 is mandatory to ensure clarity. For instance, you must say 방학 동안 여행을 했어요 (traveled during vacation) rather than 방학 여행을 했어요. The instructor highlights a specific contrast: 주말 동안 기다렸어요 means "I waited during the weekend" (duration), whereas 주말 기다렸어요 means "I waited for the weekend" (direct object).

### Frequency and Patterns with 마다

마다 indicates a consistent pattern and translates to "every," "each," or "once per." While often used with time, it can apply to any noun. Examples include 한 시간마다 (every hour), 삼일마다 (every three days), 교실마다 (every classroom), and 사람마다 (for every person). The instructor notes that while phrases like 일일마다 (every day) or 일년마다 (every year) are grammatically correct, Sino-Korean adverbs starting with 매 (every) are much more common in daily life: 매일 (every day), 매주 (every week), 매달 (every month), and 매년 (every year). Interestingly, while 매월 is the formal Sino-Korean term for "every month," native speakers prefer 매달 in casual conversation.

---

### Approximation with 쯤 and 정도

Both 쯤 and 정도 mean "around" or "about" and indicate approximation. They are meaning-wise identical and interchangeable, but they differ in writing conventions. 쯤 is a particle and must be attached directly to the preceding noun without a space, whereas 정도 is classified as a separate word and requires a space. Examples include 이 주일쯤 (about two weeks), 커피 두 잔 정도 (about two cups), and 사월쯤 (around April). For duration, one might say 삼십 분쯤 or 삼십 분 정도 (around 30 minutes).

---

### Options and Abundance with (이)나

The particle (이)나 has two primary functions. First, it expresses "either A or B" when listing options or expressing uncertainty. If the noun ends in a consonant, use 이나; if it ends in a vowel, use 나. Examples include 커피나 차 (coffee or tea), 국수나 밥 (noodles or rice), and 서랍이나 옷장 (drawer or closet). The instructor warns that (이)나 is not used for "choose one" questions (e.g., "Do you want coffee or tea?"). To ask a choice question, one should repeat the verb or use 아니면 (or): 커피 마실 거예요, 차 마실 거예요? or 커피 마실 거예요, 아니면 차 마실 거예요?

The second function of (이)나 is to emphasize abundance, meaning "as many as" or "as much as." This implies the amount is larger than expected. Examples include 바나나를 다섯 개나 먹었어요 (ate as many as five bananas) and 열 시간이나 잤어요 (slept for as many as ten hours).

---

### Limitation with 밖에

밖에 is used with a negative verb to express "only." While 만 also means "only," 밖에 emphasizes a sense of limitation or scarcity ("nothing but"). Literally, it means "outside of [noun], there is none." Therefore, it must always be followed by a negative construction like 없어요 (don't have), 안 (not), or -지 않다. Examples include 천원밖에 없어요 (only have 1,000 won), 두 개밖에 안 먹었어요 (only ate two), and 이거밖에 몰라요 (only know this).

---

### Comparison with 처럼

처럼 means "like" or "as... as" and indicates that an action or quality is similar to another noun. Examples include 가수처럼 노래를 잘해요 (sings well like a singer), 봄처럼 따뜻해요 (warm like spring), and 로봇처럼 정확하게 일해요 (works as precisely as a robot). It is a straightforward way to create similes in Korean.
