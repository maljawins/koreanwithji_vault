### Overview

The instructor introduces the Hanja character **量**, which carries the meaning of "to count," "quantity," or "amount." He emphasizes that the goal of learning Hanja is not necessarily to master writing the complex characters by hand, but to recognize their sounds and meanings to better understand the vast number of Korean words derived from them. This specific character is particularly useful for discussing portions, capacities, and measurements.

---

### Sound Changes: 양 vs 량

The character **量** has two possible pronunciations in Korean depending on its position within a word. When it appears at the very beginning of a word or stands alone, it is pronounced as **양**. However, when it appears in the middle or at the end of a word, it is pronounced as **량**. This follows standard Korean phonological rules regarding the initial sound 'r/l' (두음 법칙).

---

### Etymology and Basic Usage

The instructor explains the visual origin of the character **量**. Originally, it was a pictograph representing a funnel placed on top of a bundle or sack being filled with grain. This imagery directly relates to the act of measuring or counting how much of a substance is being collected.

In modern Korean, the word **양** is used by itself to mean "quantity" or "amount." The instructor highlights a common beginner mistake: using the adjective **많다 (to be many/much)** to describe a large portion of food. Saying **음식이 많아요** simply means there is a lot of food present (perhaps many different dishes on a table). To specifically express that a portion size is large, one should use **양**:

> **양이 너무 많아요.** / The quantity is too much. (The portion is too big.)

Conversely, to describe a small portion, one uses the opposite adjective:

> **양이 적어요.** / The quantity is small. (The portion is small.)

---

### Common Vocabulary Words

The instructor provides three key vocabulary words that utilize this Hanja character, noting specific pronunciation changes for each.

**1. 수량 (Quantity/Number)**
This combines **수 (number)** and **량 (quantity)**. It refers to the actual numerical count of items. For example, when checking an order to ensure the correct number of items arrived, one might say:
> **수량이 맞는지 확인하세요.** / Please check if the quantity is correct.

**2. 분량 (Amount/Portion)**
This combines **분 (portion/part)** and **량 (amount)**. Due to sound change rules, it is pronounced as **불량**. It refers to the overall amount or volume of something rather than a discrete count.
> **분량이 적어요.** / The amount is small.

**3. 용량 (Capacity/Volume)**
This combines **용 (shape/form)** and **량 (quantity)**. It is pronounced as **용냥** according to sound change rules. It refers to the capacity or volume of a container, such as a refrigerator or a computer hard drive.
> **용량이 큰 냉장고** / A high-capacity refrigerator.
