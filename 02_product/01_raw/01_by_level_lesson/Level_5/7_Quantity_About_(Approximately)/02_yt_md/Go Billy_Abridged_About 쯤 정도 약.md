### Expressing "About" with 쯤 and 정도
The most common way to say "about" is by using the particle 쯤. This is attached directly to a noun without any space. It is specifically used with amounts, numbers, and time. While it is not considered casual (반말), it is an informal word best suited for everyday conversation rather than formal settings. Examples include 시간쯤 (about an hour), 8월쯤 (around August), and 한 달 전쯤 (about a month ago).

The word 정도 serves a similar purpose but carries a slightly more formal tone and requires a space between the noun and the word. Unlike 쯤, 정도 is perfectly acceptable in both formal and informal situations. The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence 제가 다섯 개 정도 가져갈게요 (I will bring about five). Beyond simple approximation, 정도 also carries the meaning of "degree" or "amount," which allows it to be used in expressions like 이 정도 (this much/this degree). This is illustrated with the phrase 이 정도면 충분해 (if it's this much, it's enough).

---

### Using the Prefix 약
The word 약 is another way to express "approximately," but it functions differently as it is placed before the noun with a space. It is versatile enough for both formal and informal speech but carries a "fancy" or more sophisticated feeling compared to the other options. It is frequently used in contexts involving large numbers or investments, such as 그 회사에 약 2억 원을 투자했어요 (I invested approximately 200 million won in that company). Interestingly, 약 can be paired with 정도 or 즈음 in the same sentence for emphasis, as seen in 저는 매주 약 8시간 정도 책을 읽어요 (I read books for about approximately 8 hours every week). However, one should never use 약 with another 약.

---

### Intermediate Grammar: (을/ㄹ) 정도로
This intermediate structure uses 정도 to mean "to the degree that" or "so much that." It is formed by taking a verb stem and attaching 을 if it ends in a consonant or ㄹ if it ends in a vowel, followed by 정도로 (or optionally just 정도이다). This form is often used for exaggeration to describe how intense a situation is. For example, 배가 너무 고파서 생양파도 먹을 수 있을 정도예요 (I'm so hungry that I could even eat a raw onion). Another example provided is 버스에서 잠들 정도로 피곤했어요 (I was so tired to the degree that I fell asleep on the bus). It can also describe real-life hardships, such as 한 달 동안 고구마만 먹어야 할 정도로 가난했어요 (I was so poor I had to eat only sweet potatoes for a month).

---

### Specialized Forms: 쯤 하다 and (을/ㄹ) 쯤
The instructor introduces 쯤 하다 as a specific way to discuss the approximate cost of items. While 쯤이다 simply means "it is about [amount]," 쯤 하다 carries the nuance of "it costs about [amount]." For example, 만 원쯤 해요 (it costs about 10,000 won) is a natural way to describe a price.

Finally, the lesson covers (을/ㄹ) 쯤 (or 즈음), which is used with verbs to mean "around the time when." This is interchangeable with the form 무렵. The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence 기차가 정류장에 도착할 쯤 잠에서 깼어요 (I woke up around the time the train arrived at the station). This form requires the future presumptive marker (을/ㄹ) attached to the verb stem before adding 쯤.
