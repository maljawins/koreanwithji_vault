## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on two intermediate-level grammar forms used to express the concept of "each" or "per" in Korean: the particle 에 and the suffix 씩. While beginners may find these concepts challenging to integrate into complex sentences, they are essential for describing frequency, pricing, and distribution. The instructor explains that while both can translate to "each," they serve slightly different grammatical roles and are frequently used together for emphasis.

The learning arc begins with the multi-functional particle 에, specifically its use in counting and distribution. It then moves to 씩, explaining how it attaches to counter words to add nuance. Finally, the lesson demonstrates how these two forms interact and how 씩 can even modify certain adverbs to change their meaning from a general amount to a gradual process.

---

### The Particle 에 for "Each" or "Per"
While many learners first encounter 에 as a location marker (meaning "to," "at," or "in"), it also functions as a distribution marker when placed after a noun. In this context, it translates to "each," "per," "a," or "for." This usage is applicable to any countable noun, including units of time and currency.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this:
> **하루에 두 시간 공부해요.** / I study two hours a day (per day).
> **아보카도는 두 개에 삼천 원이에요.** / It's 3,000 won for two avocados.

In the avocado example, the instructor notes that "each" might feel clunky in English (each two avocados), so "for" is often a more natural translation. The sentence structure can also be inverted to put the price first:
> **아보카도는 삼천 원에 두 개를 살 수 있어요.** / You can buy two avocados for 3,000 won.

Another common usage involves frequency:
> **한 번에 천 원입니다.** / It's 1,000 won each time (for one time).

---

### Using 씩 for Emphasis
The word 씩 is a suffix that attaches to the end of a word, typically a counter word like 시간 (hour), 개 (object), or 명 (person). Like 에, it also translates to "each." However, 씩 is primarily used to add emphasis. While it is often optional, using it makes the speaker's intent clearer and the meaning of "each and every" stronger.

When 씩 is added to a sentence that already uses 에, it reinforces the distributive meaning:
> **하루에 두 시간씩 공부해요.** / I study two hours each day.

Adding 씩 here changes the nuance from a simple statement of "two hours a day" to a more emphatic "two hours each and every day." Other examples include:
> **삼십 분에 한 번씩 손을 씻으세요.** / Wash your hands once each thirty minutes.
> **일주일에 책을 몇 권씩 읽으세요?** / How many books do you read each week?
> **고등어를 이렇게 세 개씩 팔아요.** / We sell mackerel like this, three each.

---

### 씩 Without the Particle 에
It is possible to use 씩 on its own without the particle 에. When used this way, it still carries the meaning of "each" or "every," but it does not specify the frequency or the "what for" as explicitly as the 에 + 씩 combination does.

For example, when discussing price:
> **만 원씩이에요.** / They're 10,000 won each.

When 씩 is attached to an amount of time or a count without 에, it implies that an action is happening multiple times over a period.
> **이 약은 다섯 개씩 먹어야 돼요.** / I have to take five of this medicine (implying five pills every time it is taken).

A unique case occurs with the phrase "one time":
> **저는 한 번씩 피부과에 가요.** / I go to the dermatologist every once in a while.
In this context, 씩 transforms "one time" into a recurring, though perhaps irregular, event.

---

### 씩 with Adverbs
Beyond counters, 씩 can attach to specific adverbs to create new meanings related to gradual progression or frequency. The instructor highlights two primary examples that should be memorized as distinct vocabulary items:

1. **조금 (a little) vs. 조금씩 (little by little / gradually)**
   > **조금씩 배우고 있어요.** / I'm learning little by little.
2. **가끔 (sometimes) vs. 가끔씩 (sometimes, but not often)**
   > **영화는 가끔씩 봐요.** / I watch movies sometimes.
