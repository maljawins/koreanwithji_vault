## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the suffixes ~어치 and ~치, which are used to express the concept of "worth" or "value" in Korean. These markers allow a speaker to quantify items not by individual count, but by their monetary value, weight, or duration of time. The lesson focuses on the grammatical placement of these suffixes, the specific types of words they follow, and the nuances that distinguish them from other similar expressions like ~짜리.

The learning arc begins with ~어치 for money and measurements, moves into a comparison with ~짜리 to clarify usage contexts, and concludes with the time-specific suffix ~치. Throughout the video, the instructor uses a whiteboard to demonstrate sentence structures, showing how these suffixes typically appear between the noun being quantified and the verb.

---

### Expressing Value with ~어치
The suffix ~어치 attaches directly to a word representing a monetary amount or a measurement with a counter. It signifies an amount of something that is equal to the value of the preceding word. For example, 삼천 원 어치 (three thousand won's worth) means the quantity of an item you receive is exactly what three thousand won buys. This is primarily used for items that are sold by weight or bulk rather than individual units, such as meat, flour, potatoes, or small snacks like peanuts.

The instructor notes that while ~어치 is most commonly used with Korean Won (원), it can be used with any currency, such as 달러 (dollar). Visually, the instructor writes out the structure: **Word + 어치**, specifying that the "Word" should be a **Money/measurement word with a counter**. He demonstrates this with an example of buying meat:

> **고기를 100달러 어치 사서 너무 많아요.** / I bought a hundred dollars worth of meat, so there is too much.

In this sentence, the noun 고기 (meat) comes first, followed by the quantity 100달러 어치, and finally the verb. Another common usage is when ordering at a market, such as asking for a specific monetary worth of bean sprouts:

> **콩나물 이천 원 어치 주세요.** / Please give me two thousand won't worth of bean sprouts.

---

### ~어치 vs. ~짜리
A critical distinction is made between ~어치 and ~짜리. While both can be translated as "worth" in certain contexts, they function differently. The suffix ~짜리 is used for counting individual items that have a specific fixed value or price tag. For example, 5만원짜리 우산 refers to a single umbrella that costs 50,000 won. In contrast, 우산 5만원어치 would imply a bulk collection of umbrellas whose total combined value is 50,000 won. Because umbrellas are typically bought as individual units, using ~어치 in that context sounds unnatural unless one is buying a random assortment by value.

---

### Expressing Time Worth with ~치
When expressing "worth" in terms of time duration, the suffix ~치 is used instead of ~어치. Unlike ~어치, there is typically a space between the time word and ~치, with the exception of 하루치 (one day's worth), which is written as one word. This suffix originates from the Hanja for "value" (값), but in modern Korean, it is strictly used with time words like 시간 (hour), 일주일 (week), or 달 (month).

The instructor provides several examples to show how this functions as "an amount of [something] for [period of time]":

> **이틀 치 잠을 한꺼번에 잔 것 같아요.** / I feel like I slept two days' worth of sleep all at once.
> **약국에서 일주일 치 약을 살 거예요.** / I will buy a week's worth of medicine at the pharmacy.
> **여섯 달 치 방값을 미리 냈어요.** / I paid six months' worth of rent in advance.

The instructor also demonstrates a rhetorical or emphatic question using this suffix:
> **이게 다 도대체 얼마치야?** / How much [time] worth is all of this?

---

### Archaic Variant: ~만치
Finally, the instructor briefly mentions ~만치, which carries the same meaning as the common particle ~만큼 (as much as). He notes that ~만치 sounds significantly older and is less common in modern daily speech. An example provided is 이만치, which is synonymous with 이만큼 (this much):

> **이만치 했으면 됐어요.** / If you've done this much, that's enough.
