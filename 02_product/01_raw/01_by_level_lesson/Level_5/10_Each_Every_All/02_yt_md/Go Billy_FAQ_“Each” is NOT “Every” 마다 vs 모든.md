## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a frequent point of confusion for Korean learners: the difference between 모든 and 마다. While both are often translated as "every" in English, they function differently in Korean grammar and carry distinct nuances regarding how they group or individualize nouns. The lesson focuses on the grammatical placement of each word, their specific meanings ("every" vs. "each"), and how to choose the correct one when discussing time and frequency.

---

### Understanding 모든 (Every)
The word 모든 is an adjective that translates to "every." Because it is an adjective, it must be followed by a noun, and there is always a space between 모든 and the noun it modifies. It cannot function as an adverb (placed before a verb) or as a standalone noun. The instructor provides common examples such as 모든 것 `(everything)`, where 것 means "thing," and 모든 사람 `(every person/everyone)`, where 사람 means "person."

A key nuance of 모든 is that it refers to a group of multiple things or people as a single collective unit. When you use 모든 사람, you are describing every person within a group as one whole entity. Additionally, the instructor notes that 모든 should not be used with certain Sino-Korean (한자) words that already contain the meaning of "every," such as 매일 `(every day)`. Saying 모든 매일 would be redundant because the character 매 already means "every."

---

### Understanding 마다 (Each)
Unlike 모든, the word 마다 translates more accurately to "each." It is a particle that attaches directly to the end of a noun with no space. While 모든 looks at the group as a whole, 마다 focuses on the individual members of that group one at a time. For example, 사람마다 means "each person" or "every person individually."

The instructor highlights a very common grammatical use of this particle: the form 때마다. Since 때 means "time" or "when," 때마다 translates to "each time," "every time," or "whenever." This is demonstrated with the sentence:
> **냉면을 먹을 때마다 배가 아파요.** / Every time I eat cold buckwheat noodles, my stomach hurts.

In this example, the speaker is emphasizing that the stomach pain happens during each individual instance of eating the noodles.

---

### Key Differences: Grouping vs. Individualizing
The primary confusion between these two words stems from the fact that both can often be translated as "every" in natural English. To distinguish them, learners must look at the speaker's intent. 모든 is used when referring to the whole group as one. 마다 is used when referring to the individual parts of that group one by one.

Because of this focus on individual instances, 마다 is the standard choice for expressing frequency or how often something happens. It is almost always used with time nouns to indicate a recurring interval. The instructor provides examples such as 1분 마다 `(each minute / every one minute)` and 3일 마다 `(every three days)`. In these cases, the speaker is talking about those specific units of time individually rather than bunching all the minutes or days together into one collective group.
