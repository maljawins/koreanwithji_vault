### Overview
The instructor introduces two essential Korean expressions used to quantify "things": **모든 것** (everything) and **아무것도** (nothing/anything). While both are commonly used and relatively simple in structure, they are often overlooked in early Korean studies. The lesson breaks down the literal components of each word—showing how they are built from adjectives and nouns—and highlights the critical grammatical constraints that dictate when to use one over the other.

---

### Everything: 모든 것
The expression **모든 것** is the standard way to say "everything" in Korean. It is a combination of the adjective **모든**, which means "every" or "all," and the noun **것**, which means "thing." Together, they literally translate to "every thing." Because it functions as a noun phrase, it can take various particles such as the subject marker `이/가` or the object marker `을/를`.

The instructor provides the following examples to demonstrate its versatility:
> **모든 것이 좋아요.** / Everything is good.
> **모든 것이 맛있어요.** / Everything is delicious.
> **여기서 모든 것을 보고 싶어요.** / I want to see everything here.

Furthermore, since **모든** is an adjective, it can be used independently before other nouns to mean "every [noun]" or "all [noun]." Visual examples shown include:
- **모든 사람** (everyone / every person)
- **모든 생각** (every idea / all ideas)

---

### Nothing and Anything: 아무것도
The word **아무것도** is more complex in its construction and usage. It is composed of **아무** (any), **것** (thing), and the particle **도** (even/also). Literally, it translates to "even anything." However, its functional meaning is "nothing" or "not anything."

The most important rule for **아무것도** is that it must be used in negative sentences. It cannot appear in a positive sentence; the verb must end with a negative marker like **안** (not), **못** (cannot), or the negative ending **~지 않다**.

The instructor illustrates this with a comparison:
> **아무것도 안 먹었어요.** / I didn't eat anything.

If one were to say **아무것도 먹었어요**, the sentence would be grammatically incorrect because the positive verb **먹었어요** (ate) conflicts with the requirement for a negative environment. Additional examples of correct usage include:
> **아무것도 말하지 않았어요.** / I didn't say anything.
> **저도 아무것도 하지 않았어요.** / I didn't do anything either.
> **아무것도 못 봤어요.** / I couldn't see anything.
