## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Hanja character 매 (每), which carries the meaning of "each" or "every." The lesson focuses on recognizing the sound and meaning of this character within common Korean vocabulary rather than mastering its calligraphy. By understanding this root, learners can decipher a wide range of time-related expressions and formal terms.

The character 매 (每) is explained through its historical pictographic origin: a mother wearing a hairpin while taking care of her child. This imagery was intended to signify that a mother's duty is constant and unchanging, leading to the modern meanings of "every" or "each." In practice, this character is placed directly before other Hanja roots to indicate a recurring interval or a collective "every" of that category.

---

### Every Day: 매일 vs. 맨날
The most common word using this root is 매일 (every day), where 일 (日) signifies "day." The instructor addresses a frequent student question regarding the difference between 매일 and 맨날. While both are used to mean "every day" in modern speech, 맨날 is strictly informal and carries a slang-like nuance.

Historically, 맨날 is unrelated to the Hanja 매 (每). It originated from the word 만날, which meant "continuing on and on." Because of its informal nature, the instructor advises students to avoid 맨날 in formal writing or essays, opting for 매일 instead.

---

### Recurring Time Intervals: Week, Month, and Year
The character 매 is used to build a series of words for recurring time periods. For weekly occurrences, Korean uses 매주 (every week). For monthly occurrences, there are two options: 매월 and 매달. While both mean "every month," they use different roots for "month." 월 (月) is the Sino-Korean root, while 달 is the pure Korean word. Consequently, 매월 feels more formal and official, making it the preferred choice for news articles or academic essays, whereas 매달 is more common in daily conversation.

For yearly occurrences, the word is 매년 (every year). The instructor provides a critical warning regarding the root 년 (年). While it means "year" in Sino-Korean compounds like 매년, 내년 (next year), and 작년 (last year), or when counting years (e.g., 삼 년 for three years), it should never be used as a standalone noun for "year." In isolation, 년 is a highly derogatory swear word for a female. To say "year" as a general noun, one should use the pure Korean word 해 or expressions like 올해 (this year).

---

### Formal Expressions: 매번 and 매사
The lesson concludes with two additional vocabulary items. 매번 uses the root 번 (times) to mean "every time." The word 매사 is a more formal Hanja expression that translates to "everything" or "every single thing." Because of its formal weight, 매사 is typically found in official documents or literature rather than in casual conversations with friends.
