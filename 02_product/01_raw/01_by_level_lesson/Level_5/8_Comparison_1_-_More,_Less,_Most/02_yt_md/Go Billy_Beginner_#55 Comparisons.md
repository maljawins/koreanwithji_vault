### Basic Comparisons with 더 and 덜

The most basic way to make a comparison in Korean is by using the adverbs **더 (more)** and **덜 (less)**. These adverbs are placed directly before a verb or another adverb to modify its degree. For example, to say "I am smarter," you use the sentence **제가 더 똑똑해요**. Similarly, **그게 더 좋아요** translates to "That is better," or literally "That is more good." To express the opposite, the adverb **덜** is used in the same position. An example shown on screen is **이게 덜 비싸요**, which means "This is less expensive."

---

### Comparing Two Nouns with 보다

When you want to explicitly compare one noun to another, you must use the particle **보다 (than)**. This particle is attached to the noun that serves as the baseline for comparison—the thing you are comparing *against*. The noun being compared comes first, followed by the baseline noun with **보다** attached. The instructor notes that **보다** by itself already implies "more than" by default, even if you do not include the adverb **더**. However, it is very common and natural to include **더** or **덜** anyway to make the meaning explicit.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this structure:
- **민우 씨는 선아 씨보다 귀여워요.** / Minwoo is cuter than Seona.
- **저는 원숭이보다 더 똑똑해요.** / I am smarter than a monkey.
- **원숭이는 저보다 덜 똑똑해요.** / A monkey is less smart than me.
- **한국어는 영어보다 더 어려워요.** / Korean is more difficult than English.
- **이 컴퓨터가 저 컴퓨터보다 더 싸요.** / This computer is cheaper than that computer.

A key takeaway is that the noun followed by **보다** is always the "than" part of the English sentence.

### Same, Different, and Similar

To express that two things are the same, different, or similar, Korean uses specific descriptive verbs combined with "with" particles like **랑/이랑**, **하고**, or **과/와**.

To say something is the same as something else, use the verb **같다 (to be the same)**. For example, **그 셔츠가 제 셔츠랑 같아요** means "That shirt is the same as my shirt." To say something is different, use the verb **다르다 (to be different)**, as in **그 영화는 책하고 많이 달라요**, meaning "That movie is a lot different than the book." Finally, to say something is similar, use **비슷하다 (to be similar)**. The instructor demonstrates this with **뉴욕은 엘에이와 조금 비슷해요**, which translates to "New York is a little bit similar to LA."

---

### Using 처럼 to Mean "Like"

The particle **처럼** is attached to a noun to mean "like [noun]." Unlike **비슷하다**, which simply states that two things are similar, **처럼** is used to describe *how* something is done or *what* something is like in conjunction with another verb. The structure is: **Noun + 처럼 + Verb**.

Examples include:
- **저 원숭이는 사람처럼 똑똑해요.** / That monkey is smart like a person.
- **이 후식은 아이스크림처럼 시원해요.** / This dessert is cool and refreshing, like ice cream.
- **아빠는 엄마처럼 항상 바쁘세요.** / Dad is always busy, like Mom.
- **제 친구는 선생님처럼 잘 가르칠 수 있어요.** / My friend can teach well, like a teacher.

The instructor emphasizes that **시원하다** in the dessert example means "cool and refreshing" in a culinary context.

---

### Superlatives: The Most

To express the superlative—that something is the "most" or the "best"—Korean uses the adverbs **가장** or **제일**. These two words are interchangeable and are placed before a verb or an adverb.

The instructor provides a wide range of examples:
- **이 아이스크림이 제일 맛있어요.** / This ice cream is the most delicious.
- **한국어가 제일 어려워요.** / Korean is the most difficult.
- **제 컴퓨터가 가장 비싸요.** / My computer is the most expensive.
- **이 아이디어가 가장 좋아요.** / This idea is the best (literally, "most good").
- **제가 가장 빨리 했어요.** / I did it the fastest (literally, "most quickly").
- **제가 제일 많이 먹었어요.** / I ate the most (literally, "ate a lot the most").
