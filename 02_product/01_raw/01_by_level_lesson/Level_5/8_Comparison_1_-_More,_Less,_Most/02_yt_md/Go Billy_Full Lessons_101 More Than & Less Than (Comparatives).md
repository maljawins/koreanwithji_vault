### Overview

This lesson focuses on the adverbs 더 (more) and 덜 (less) and how they are used to create comparative sentences in Korean. The instructor begins by explaining how these adverbs function as simple modifiers placed directly before verbs or adjectives. By the end of the video, learners will understand how to express that something is "more than," "less than," or simply "more/less [adjective]" than something else.

---

### Basic Use of 더 and 덜

The word 더 means "more" and functions as an adverb. In Korean, using adverbs is straightforward: you simply place them directly before the verb or adjective you wish to modify. For example, if you want to say you want to eat more vegetables, you place 더 before the verb:

> **야채를 더 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to eat more vegetables.

Similarly, to ask for more of something, you can say:

> **더 주세요.** / Please give me more.

The adverb 더 can also modify descriptive verbs (adjectives). When used this way, it often translates to the English suffix "-er." For instance, while 맛있어요 means "is delicious," adding 더 creates a comparative:

> **피자가 더 맛있어요.** / Pizza is more delicious.

The opposite of 더 is 덜, which means "less." It follows the same placement rules, appearing directly before an action or descriptive verb. The instructor provides examples comparing preferences and traits:

> **저는 수지를 덜 좋아해요.** / I like Suzy less.
> **수지가 덜 예뻐요.** / Suzy is less pretty.

---

### Creating Comparative Adjectives

As noted, 더 is the primary way to add the meaning of "-er" to any descriptive verb in Korean. This is particularly useful when modifying nouns within a phrase. The instructor demonstrates this transformation with several common adjectives:

- **큰 케이크** (a big cake) → **더 큰 케이크** (a bigger cake)
- **빠른 차** (a fast car) → **더 빠른 차** (a faster car)
- **좋은 집** (a good house) → **더 좋은 집** (a better house)

This pattern allows for simple comparisons even without explicitly mentioning a second object of comparison in the sentence.

---

### Comparing Two Things with 보다

To make a full comparison between two people or things (e.g., "A is more [adjective] than B"), Korean uses the particle 보다. The structure involves taking the noun you are comparing something *to* and attaching 보다 to it.

The general form is: **[Noun] + 보다 (더) [Verb/Adjective]**

When expressing "more than," the word 더 is actually optional because the particle 보다 already carries the comparative meaning. However, including 더 often makes the sentence clearer and more natural. If you want to express "less than," you must include the word 덜.

The instructor provides several clear examples of this grammar in action:

> **제시카가 수지보다 더 예뻐요.** / Jessica is prettier than Suzy.
> **이 숙제가 그 숙제보다 더 어려워요.** / This homework is more difficult than that homework.
> **물이 사이다보다 더 맛있어요.** / Water is more delicious than soda.

When comparing prices or specific qualities like height, the same rules apply. To say something is less expensive, use 덜:

> **돼지고기가 소고기보다 덜 비싸요.** / Pork is less expensive than beef.

For height, the instructor uses the descriptive verb phrase 키가 크다 (to be tall). To say someone is taller, 더 is placed directly before the descriptive verb 크다:

> **철수가 영희보다 키가 더 커요.** / Chul-soo is taller than Yeong-hee.
