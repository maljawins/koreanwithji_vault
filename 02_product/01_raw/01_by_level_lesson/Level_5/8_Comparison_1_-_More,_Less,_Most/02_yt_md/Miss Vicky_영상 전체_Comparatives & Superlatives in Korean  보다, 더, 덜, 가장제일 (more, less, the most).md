### Overview
This lesson provides a comprehensive guide to expressing comparisons and superlatives in Korean. The instructor covers three main functional areas: expressing "more than" using the particle 보다 (than) and the adverb 더 (more), expressing "less than" using the adverb 덜 (less), and expressing "the most" using the interchangeable adverbs 가장 and 제일. 

The teaching approach focuses on structural patterns, particle placement, and the nuances of adverb omission. ### Comparatives: Expressing "More Than" with 보다 and 더
To say "more than [noun]" in Korean, the particle 보다 is attached directly to the noun being compared against. It is important to distinguish this particle from the verb 보다 (to see), even though they share the same form. The adverb 더 (more) is typically placed before the predicate (adjective or verb) to complete the comparative meaning.

The basic sentence structure follows: [Subject] + [Comparison Noun] + 보다 + 더 + [Adjective/Verb]. For example, to say "Autumn is warmer than winter," the sentence is:
> **가을이 겨울보다 더 따뜻해요.** / Autumn is warmer than winter.

The instructor notes that the word order is flexible; the 보다 phrase can appear before the subject without changing the meaning. Furthermore, because the particle 보다 already implies a comparison, the adverb 더 is often omittable in casual speech, though including it adds clarity and emphasis.

---

### Comparatives: Expressing "Less Than" with 덜
To express "less than," the structure remains the same as "more than," but the adverb 더 is replaced with 덜 (less). Unlike 더, the adverb 덜 cannot be omitted. If 덜 is removed, the presence of 보다 will lead the listener to assume a "more than" comparison, effectively reversing the intended meaning of the sentence.

Example sentences using 덜:
> **이 옷은 저 옷보다 덜 비싸요.** / This outfit is less expensive than that outfit.
> **라떼가 아메리카노보다 덜 써요.** / Latte is less bitter than Americano.

---

### Using Comparatives with Adverbs
Comparatives can modify adverbs as well as adjectives. When using adverbs like 빨리 (fast) or 열심히 (hard/diligently), the adverb 더 must be included to specify the nature of the comparison. For instance, "I study harder than last year" is:
> **저는 작년보다 더 열심히 공부해요.** / I study harder than last year.

However, a special rule applies to the adverbs 많이 (a lot/many) and 오래 (for a long time). When paired with 더 or 덜, these two specific adverbs are frequently omitted because the context of quantity or time is already implied. Thus, 더 많이 먹어요 (eat more) can simply be shortened to 더 먹어요. Conversely, for adverbs like 자주 (often) or 느리게 (slowly), omission is not allowed because it would result in a loss of specific meaning (e.g., changing "more often" to just "more" in terms of quantity).

---

### Superlatives: Expressing "The Most" with 가장 and 제일
Superlatives are expressed using the adverbs 가장 or 제일. These two words are completely interchangeable and mean "the most." They are placed directly before the adjective or verb they modify.

Example sentences:
> **여름이 가장 더워요.** / Summer is the hottest.
> **한국 음식이 제일 맛있어요.** / Korean food is the most delicious.

The instructor highlights a specific usage rule regarding the omission of 많이 (a lot) with superlatives. While 더 (more) allows for the omission of 많이 with almost any verb, 가장 and 제일 only allow this omission with a specific set of verbs: 좋아하다 (to like), 싫어하다 (to dislike), 잘하다 (to be good at), and 못하다 (to be bad at). For all other verbs, such as 일하다 (to work) or 운동하다 (to exercise), you must include 많이 or 오래 to maintain clarity. For example, "I worked the most" should be:
> **가장 많이 일했어요.** / I worked the most.
