## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common subscriber question regarding the difference between the Korean adverbs 어쨌든 and 아무튼, both of which are typically translated as "anyway" in English. While the two words are often interchangeable when used to transition between topics in a conversation, they possess subtle differences in nuance, formality, and additional grammatical functions. The lesson expands beyond these two primary terms to include more formal Hanja-based alternatives and related expressions that learners might encounter in speech or text.

---

### 아무튼 and 어쨌든: The Standard "Anyway"

Both 아무튼 and 어쨌든 are primarily used as sentence-starters to change the topic of a conversation. When a speaker wants to move on from a previous point and start talking about something else, they can use either word. The instructor notes that for most general purposes, they are essentially the same.

아무튼 has a common shortened version used in casual, slangy speech: **암튼**. This shortened form is strictly for informal situations and should not be used in polite or professional contexts. Similarly, 어쨌든 is used to transition topics, but it carries an additional grammatical layer that 아무튼 does not.

---

### The Unique Meaning of 어쨌든

The word 어쨌든 has a secondary meaning that translates to "no matter what you do" or "whatever you do." This usage is derived from the longer phrase 어떻게 했든 (no matter how it was done). 

The instructor explains a visual cue for identifying this specific meaning: if 어쨌든 appears in the middle of a sentence rather than at the very beginning, it is likely being used to mean "no matter what." He provides a visual example on screen:

> **비가 오든 어쨌든 나는 갈 거야.** / Whether it rains or whatever happens, I am going to go.

In this sentence, 어쨌든 functions as a connector meaning "no matter what happens." However, the instructor clarifies that this specific usage is not particularly common in everyday Korean. Beginners are advised to focus on the "anyway" transition meaning first. To express "no matter what" or "whether or not," it is more common to use the standard ~든 ending or the particle ~도 (even if).

---

### Formal Alternatives: 하여튼, 여하튼, 하여간

The instructor introduces a group of three related words that also mean "anyway" but carry a slightly more formal nuance because they are derived from Hanja (Chinese characters). These words are 하여튼, 여하튼 (which is simply the first two syllables of 하여튼 reversed), and 하여간.

Like the previous words, these are used to signal a change in the topic of conversation. Because of their Hanja roots, they feel a bit less informal than 아무튼 or 어쨌든. There is also a shortened version of these terms: **여튼**. This shortened form is used only in speech and carries a somewhat "strong" or blunt feeling, so it should be avoided when speaking to superiors, such as a boss.

---

### 그나저나: "By the Way" or "In Any Case"

Another expression that can translate as "anyway" is 그러나 저러나, which is almost always shortened to **그나저나**. While it functions as a topic transition, the instructor suggests that "by the way" or "in any case" are often better English translations.

그나저나 is specifically used when the speaker wants to actively close the current topic and pivot to something new. It carries the nuance of "Alright, that's enough of that; now let's talk about this." While it is less common than 아무튼 or 어쨌든, it is a natural part of conversational Korean.

---

### A Note on 어차피

The instructor concludes by clarifying a common point of confusion: the word 어차피. Although 어차피 is sometimes translated as "anyway" in English dictionaries, it does not function as a transition word for changing topics.

Instead, 어차피 means "anyway" in the sense of "either way," "no matter what," or "eventually." It describes a situation where the outcome will be the same regardless of other factors. Because its function is entirely different from the transition words discussed in the lesson, learners should be careful not to confuse it with transitionary adverbs like 아무튼.
