### Overview

The instructor introduces two distinct methods for expressing indefinite pronouns like "something," "somewhere," "someone," and "sometime" in Korean. The first method is a formal, rule-based grammatical construction that involves modifying question words with specific suffixes. The second method is a more advanced yet simpler approach used frequently in natural conversation, where the question words themselves take on indefinite meanings based entirely on sentence stress and intonation. The lesson covers the mechanics of both forms, provides practical sentence examples, and highlights critical nuances regarding emphasis and context.

---

### The Basic Grammatical Method: Question Word + (이)ㄴ가

The first way to create indefinite pronouns is by applying a specific formula to question words. If a question word ends in a consonant, you attach 이 followed by ㄴ가. If the question word ends in a vowel, you simply attach ㄴ가 to the bottom of the word. This creates a stable noun that functions just like any other noun in a sentence, allowing for the addition of particles like the object marker 를/을.

To say "something," the instructor uses the question words 무엇 or 뭐 (what). For 무엇, which ends in a consonant, the form becomes 무엇인가. For the more common 뭐, which ends in a vowel, the ㄴ is attached to the bottom to create 뭔가. An example sentence provided is:
> **뭔가를 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to eat something.

To say "somewhere," the word 어디 (where) is used. Since it ends in a vowel, ㄴ가 is added to create 어딘가. The instructor demonstrates its use with a location particle:
> **어딘가에서 봤어요.** / I saw it somewhere.

To say "someone," the word 누구 (who) becomes 누군가. This can be used in sentences such as:
> **누군가를 만났어요.** / I met someone.

Finally, the instructor provides a bonus word for "sometime" using 언제 (when), which transforms into 언젠가.
> **언젠가 할 거예요.** / I'll do it sometime.

### The Simple Conversational Method: Using Intonation

The second method is described as faster and simpler but requires careful attention to emphasis. In this approach, the base question words (뭐, 어디, 누구, 언제) are used without any suffixes to mean "something," "somewhere," "someone," or "sometime." The meaning is determined by whether the question word is stressed. If the question word is emphasized, it remains a question (e.g., "What?"). If the emphasis is placed on the verb or another part of the sentence instead, the question word shifts its meaning to an indefinite pronoun (e.g., "something").

The instructor illustrates this with the phrase 뭐 먹었어요. If the speaker emphasizes 뭐, the sentence is a question: "What did you eat?" However, if the emphasis is shifted to the verb 먹었어요, the sentence means "I ate something" or, as a question with rising intonation, "Did you eat something?"

This pattern applies across all the words. For "somewhere," the instructor compares:
- **어디 갔어요?** (Emphasis on 어디) / Where did you go?
- **어디 갔어요?** (Emphasis on 갔어요) / Did you go somewhere? / They went somewhere.

For "someone," the word 누구 follows the same logic. Emphasizing 누구 asks "Who did you meet?" while emphasizing the verb 만났어요 asks "Did you meet someone?" or states "I met someone."

---

### Subject Marker Exception: 누구 vs. 누가

A specific grammatical rule is highlighted when using the conversational method for "someone" as the subject of a sentence. In Korean, when the subject marker 가 is added to 누구, it naturally contracts to 누가. This contraction remains mandatory even when the word is being used to mean "someone" rather than "who." The instructor provides a comparison based on stress:
- **누가 이미 했어요?** (Emphasis on 누가) / Who already did it?
- **누가 이미 했어요?** (Emphasis on 했어요) / Someone already did it.

---

### Context and Usage Notes

The instructor emphasizes that the conversational method relies heavily on context. Using a question word like 누구 in isolation will always be interpreted as "Who?" rather than "Someone." The indefinite meaning only emerges within the flow of a full sentence where the lack of stress on the question word signals the shift in meaning. While the first method (using ㄴ가) is more explicit and easier for beginners to use without worrying about intonation, the second method is highly prevalent among native speakers.
