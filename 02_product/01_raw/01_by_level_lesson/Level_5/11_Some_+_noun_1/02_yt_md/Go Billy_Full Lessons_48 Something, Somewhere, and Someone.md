---

### Fixed Indefinite Pronouns: 뭔가, 어딘가, 누군가
The most straightforward way to say something, somewhere, or someone is to use specific words ending in ~ㄴ가. These are reliable because their meaning is fixed regardless of how you say them.

For "something," the instructor provides two options based on politeness levels. **무언가** is the more formal version, while **뭔가** is the casual and much more common contraction. He recommends beginners focus on **뭔가** first. An example sentence provided is:
> **뭔가 먹었어요?** / Did you eat something?

For "somewhere," the word is **어딘가**. This is often used with the location particle 에. The instructor demonstrates this with a sentence about an object's likely location:
> **어딘가에 있겠죠?** / It must be somewhere, right?

For "someone," the word is **누군가**. This is used when the identity of the person is unknown.
> **누군가 했어요.** / Someone did it.

---

### Using Question Words as Indefinite Pronouns
The instructor transitions to a more advanced "intermediate" method. In natural Korean conversation, the standard question words **뭐 (what)**, **어디 (where)**, and **누구 (who)** are frequently used to mean something, somewhere, and someone. The distinction between a question and an indefinite statement relies entirely on which part of the sentence is emphasized.

When the emphasis (higher pitch or stronger stress) is placed on the question word itself, the sentence is a standard information-seeking question. For example, in the sentence **뭐 했어요?**, if **뭐** is stressed, it means "What did you do?" However, if the emphasis is shifted away from the question word and onto the verb at the end of the sentence, the meaning changes. In this case, **뭐 했어요?** with a de-emphasized **뭐** means "Did you do something?"

---

### Emphasis and Meaning Shifts
The instructor provides further examples to illustrate how de-emphasizing the question word changes the meaning across different categories.

For locations, using **어디** with emphasis means "where," but de-emphasizing it turns it into "somewhere."
- **어디 갔어요?** (Emphasis on **어디**) / Where did he go?
- **어디 갔어요?** (Emphasis on **갔어요**) / Did he go somewhere?

For people, the words **누구** or **누가** follow the same rule. The choice between **누구** and **누가** depends on whether the person is the object or the subject of the sentence (since **누구** + subject marker **가** becomes **누가**).
- **누가 했어요?** (Emphasis on **누가**) / Who did it?
- **누가 했어요?** (Emphasis on **했어요**) / Did someone do it?
- **누구 만났어요?** (Emphasis on **만났어요**) / Did you meet someone?

The instructor concludes by noting that a native Korean speaker will immediately distinguish these meanings based on the speaker's intonation. To mean "something/somewhere/someone," one must be careful to "de-emphasize" the question word.
