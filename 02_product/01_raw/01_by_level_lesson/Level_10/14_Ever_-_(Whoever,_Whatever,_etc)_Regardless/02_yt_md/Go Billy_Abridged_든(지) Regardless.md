## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form 든 or 든지, which is used to express the concepts of "whether," "regardless," or "any choice is fine." The lesson focuses on how this form indicates that among multiple options, the specific choice does not matter to the speaker or does not change the outcome of the sentence. The instructor emphasizes that while it translates to "whether" in English, its core nuance is the lack of preference or the irrelevance of the choice.

The learning arc begins with basic conjugation and the common practice of using the form in pairs to show opposites. It then moves into more advanced applications, such as combining the form with question words to create "-ever" expressions like "whoever" or "anywhere." Finally, the instructor clarifies the differences between 든지 and similar-looking or similar-sounding forms like 이나, 거나, and the unrelated 던지.

---

### Basic Conjugation and Usage in Pairs
Conjugating this form is straightforward: one simply takes a verb stem and attaches 든 or 든지. Both versions carry the same meaning, though 든지 is more common. A unique characteristic of this form is that it is typically used twice in a sentence to present two different options. Usually, these options are opposites or distinct alternatives.

The instructor demonstrates this with several common pairings on the whiteboard. For instance, one can pair a verb with its negative form: 하든지 안 하든지 `whether you do it or not` or 있든지 없든지 `whether it exists or not`. Alternatively, one can use the "cannot" form: 하든지 못 하든지 `whether you can do it or you cannot`. When giving a command or expressing a choice between doing and stopping, the verb 말다 (to not do/stop) is used: 하든지 말든지 `whether you do it or don't`.

While the form is generally used with the plain verb stem, it can optionally be used with a past tense stem to add emphasis, such as 했든지 `whether you did` or 있었든지 `whether there was`. However, it is strictly forbidden to use a future tense stem like 하겠든지.

---

### The Nuance of "Any Choice is Fine"
The instructor explains that the primary function of 든지 is to show that multiple options exist and any of them is acceptable. It implies a sense of "it doesn't matter" or "regardless." This nuance is often felt by the listener as soon as the first 든지 is spoken.

For example, in the sentence 멀든지 가깝든지 꼭 가야 돼요 `Regardless of whether it is far or close, I must go`, the speaker establishes that the distance (far or close) has no impact on the necessity of going. Another example provided is 지금 하든지 나중에 하든지 상관없어요 `It doesn't matter whether you do it now or later`. Here, the speaker explicitly states there is no preference regarding the timing.

---

### Question Words and "-ever" Expressions
A very common use of 든지 is attaching it directly to question words. In this specific construction, a second verb is not required. This creates meanings equivalent to English "-ever" words, though the instructor notes that "any-" is often a more accurate translation in many contexts.

The instructor lists several key combinations:
- **무엇이든지** or **뭐든지** / whatever, anything
- **어디든지** / wherever, anywhere
- **누구든지** / whoever, anyone (Note: never say 누가든지)
- **언제든지** / whenever, anytime

An example sentence shown is 한국어는 누구든지 배울 수 있어요 `Anyone can learn Korean`. Another common phrase is 언제든지 전화하세요 `Call me anytime`. The instructor also points out that you can use a question word followed by a verb stem attached to 든지, such as 누가 하든지 상관없어요 `It doesn't matter who does it`.

---

### Comparisons and Common Mistakes
The instructor compares 든지 to the forms 이나 (used with nouns) and 거나 (used with verb stems). While all three can translate to "or," 든지 is specifically used when expressing that the speaker has no preference among choices or when an actual choice is being made. 이나 often implies "or something" or "or nothing at all" and does not carry the same "regardless" weight. Furthermore, 이나 cannot be used with the past tense, whereas 든지 can.

The instructor also warns against using the word 아무 (any) with 든지; one should not say 아무든지. Finally, a crucial phonetic warning is given regarding 던지. Although it sounds similar to 든지, 던지 is a completely different, unrelated grammatical form. Learners must be careful not to confuse the two, even if they hear native speakers occasionally blur the pronunciation.
