## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar forms ~단다 and ~란다, which are specific types of quoting forms in Korean. In general, a quoting form is used to communicate what someone else has said, asked, or yelled—essentially anything that would be placed within quotation marks. These specific endings are shortened versions of the standard indirect quoting forms 다고 한다 and 다고 합니다. While they function as quotes, they carry a distinct nuance often associated with an older speaker conveying information or advice to a younger listener, or the narrative voice found in traditional folk tales and fairy tales.

---

### Conjugation Basics

To create this form, the instructor explains that you must first conjugate a verb into its plain form and then attach the ending. For action verbs in the present tense, this involves taking the verb stem and adding ㄴ if it ends in a vowel, or 는 if it ends in a consonant. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 한다 in the plain form, which then transforms into 한단다. Similarly, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹는다 in the plain form, resulting in 먹는단다. If a verb stem ends in ㄹ, the ㄹ is removed before adding ㄴ followed by 다.

The instructor demonstrates that this logic extends to different politeness levels and tenses. Because the form is a contraction of 다고 한다 (informal/plain) or 다고 합니다 (formal/polite), you can choose between ~단다 and ~답니다 depending on who you are speaking to. Furthermore, it can be used in the past tense by using the past tense stem. For the verb 하다, the past tense stem is 했, leading to 했다고 한다, which shortens to 했단다 or 했답니다.

---

### Usage and Nuance

The instructor emphasizes that while ~단다 is technically just a shortened quote, it is most commonly used when an older person is teaching or giving advice to someone younger. It translates to English as "it is said that," "they say," or "I'm telling you that." This "it is said" feeling is why the form is a staple in Korean storytelling. Instead of simply stating a fact, a storyteller uses these endings to signal that they are recounting a known tale. For instance, instead of ending a story with "they lived happily ever after" using a standard past tense like 살았습니다, a Korean storyteller would likely say 살았답니다 to give it the proper narrative flavor.

---

### Using Nouns with 이란다 and 이랍니다

When quoting with nouns and the verb 이다 (to be), the conjugation changes slightly. Just as the standard indirect quote for nouns is 이라고 한다, the shortened versions become ~이란다 or ~이랍니다. This also applies to the negative verb 아니다 (to not be), which becomes 아니란다. 

A very common application of this noun form is in the future tense. The standard future tense construction is ~ㄹ/을 것이다. Since this ends in the verb 이다, it conjugates using the noun rules. For example, the phrase "will go" (갈 거예요) becomes 갈 거란다 or 갈 거랍니다 when quoted. This is frequently seen when giving warnings or predictions to younger people, such as telling a child that their stomach will hurt if they only drink soda.

### Example Sentences

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the form in context:

> **오래오래 행복하게 살았답니다.** / It is said they lived happily ever after.
> (오래오래: for a long time; 행복하게: happily; 살다: to live)

> **탄산음료만 마시면 배가 아플 거란다.** / They say if you only drink soda, your stomach will hurt.
> (탄산음료: soda/soft drink; 마시다: to drink; 배가 아프다: stomach hurts)

> **이 공사는 내년에 끝날 거란다.** / I heard this construction will finish next year.
> (공사: construction; 내년: next year; 끝나다: to finish)

> **엄마 눈에는 내가 제일 예쁘단다.** / My mom says that I am the prettiest.
> (눈: eyes; 제일: the most; 예쁘다: to be pretty)

> **사고가 나서 늦었답니다.** / I heard they were late because there was an accident.
> (사고: accident; 나다: to happen/occur; 늦다: to be late)

> **이게 요즘 제일 인기 있는 노래랍니다.** / I heard this is the most popular song lately.
> (요즘: lately/recently; 인기 있다: to be popular; 노래: song)

> **오늘 점심 중에 딸기만 맛있었단다.** / They say only the strawberries were delicious in today's lunch.
> (점심: lunch; 중에: among; 딸기: strawberry; 맛있다: to be delicious)
