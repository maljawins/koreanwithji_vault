Lesson 160: ~느냐: Whether or Not

Introduction

In this lesson, you will learn how to use a familiar grammatical principle, ~느냐 to create a more complex meaning than what you already have learned. Furthermore, you will learn to add to ~느냐 to make it even more complex. Let’s get started!

~느냐: Whether or Not

In [Lesson 53](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-3-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-3-lessons-51-58/lesson-53/), you first learned about ~느냐 as a way to indicate an indirect quote of a question. That is, to indicate that one asks a question. For example:

Play[입속에 뭐가 있느냐고 묻는 아이가 있었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s1.mp3)
= There was a kid asking what’s inside my mouth

Play[얘가 어디 가느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s2.mp3)
= They asked where this kid was going

Questions by nature are speculative. The fundamental purpose of ~느냐 is to indicate speculation. The ability of ~느냐 to indicate speculation allows it to have more uses than just indicating that one asks a question. ~느냐 can be used to speculate “whether” something occurs. For example:

Play[그들이 모두 화해하느냐는 모두가 관심있어하는 주제예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s3.mp3)
= The topic of whether they will all reconcile is something everyone is interested in

Here you see ~는 attached to ~느냐. This is to indicate that the entire speculation of “whether they reconcile or not” is itself the subject of the sentence. The use of a particle on ~느냐 is no different than when you saw ~고 attached to it when forming quoted questions. In the example sentences later (and in the next few lessons), you will see typical particles ~를, ~가 or ~에 attached to ~느냐 depending on its purpose in a sentence.

Very often, two opposite words are used with ~느냐 to indicate whether one is true, or the other. For example:

Play[물기가 아직 남아 있느냐 없느냐는 문제가 아니예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s4.mp3)
= It’s not a problem whether or not there are still droplets left

Below are many more examples:

Play[술잔을 비우느냐 남기느냐가 그의 결심을 보여 줄 것이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s6.mp3)
= Whether he empties or leaves the alcohol cup will show his determination

Play[매순간을 소중히 여기느냐 대충 사느냐가 삶의 질을 결정해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s7.mp3)
= Whether you cherish or just simply live each moment determines the quality of life

Play[이 약물이 효과적으로 작용하느냐가 치료의 성공 여부를 좌우해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s8.mp3)
= Whether this medicine acts effectively will determine the success of the treatment

Play[물기가 아직 남아 있느냐 없느냐가 문제예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s9.mp3)
= The issue is whether there are still droplets left or not

Play[그 몸짓에 의미가 있느냐 없느냐를 확인해 봐야 해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s10.mp3)
= We need to check whether that gesture has meaning or not

Play[그가 언제 퇴임하느냐를 궁금해하는 사람이 많아요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s11.mp3)
= Many people are curious about when he will retire

You saw in [Lesson 53](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-3-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-3-lessons-51-58/lesson-53/) that ~느냐 can’t be added to adjectives. This is true here as well. Instead, ~(으)냐 is added to adjectives. For example:

Play[손끝이 따뜻하냐 차가우냐가 문제예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s12.mp3)
= The issue is whether the fingertips are warm or cold

Play[이 박자가 맞느냐 틀리느냐가 중요해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s13.mp3)
= Whether this beat is right or wrong is important

Play[이놈이 정말 악하냐 아니냐를 판단하기 어려워요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s14.mp3)
= It’s hard to judge whether this guy is truly evil or not

Play[입속이 깨끗하냐 더러우냐를 확인해야 해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Lesson160s15.mp3)
= We need to check whether the inside of the mouth is clean or dirty
