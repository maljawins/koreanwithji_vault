## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Korean grammatical structure used to express "whether" or "if" in the context of things that are unknown. This is equivalent to English sentences like "I don't know whether he'll be there" or "I don't know if he'll be there." The lesson focuses on how this form changes based on whether the verb is an action verb or a descriptive verb, as well as how to conjugate for past, present, and future tenses.

The learning arc begins with basic conjugation rules for both verb types, moves into expressing the "or not" aspect of the phrase, and concludes by showing how this grammar is used with question words (who, what, where, etc.) and to express "how to" do something. Throughout the video, visual charts and example sentences illustrate the transformations.

---

### Conjugating Action Verbs
Action verbs are defined as verbs that describe an action, such as `먹다 (to eat)`, `하다 (to do)`, and `자다 (to sleep)`. The instructor notes that `있다 (to exist)` and `없다 (to not exist)` are also treated as action verbs for this grammar. The conjugation depends on the tense. For the present tense, you take the verb stem and add `~는지`. For the past tense, you add the past tense marker `~었/았/etc.` followed by `~는지`. For the future tense, you add `~(ㄹ/을)지` to the verb stem.

The instructor provides examples of these transformations using the verb `있다 (to be/exist)`:
- Present: `있는지`
- Past: `있었는지`
- Future: `있을지`

---

### Conjugating Descriptive Verbs
Descriptive verbs describe states or qualities, such as `친절하다 (to be friendly)`, `좋다 (to be good)`, and `쉽다 (to be easy)`. The verbs `이다 (to be)` and `아니다 (to not be)` are also categorized here. The conjugation rules differ from action verbs in the present tense. To conjugate a descriptive verb in the present tense, you add `~(ㄴ/은)지` to the verb stem. The past and future tense rules are the same as action verbs: `~었/았/etc. + 는지` for past and `~(ㄹ/을)지` for future.

The instructor highlights that many rules from the `~요` form apply, such as the `ㅂ` irregular. For example, `춥다 (to be cold)` becomes `추운지` in the present and `추울지` in the future. He also emphasizes the importance of knowing whether a `하다` verb is action or descriptive; `심심하다 (to be bored)` is descriptive and becomes `심심한지`, while `공부하다 (to study)` is action and becomes `공부하는지`.

---

### Expressing "Whether or Not"
To say "whether or not," you can repeat the verb in its negative form. For action verbs, you take the negative form and conjugate it the same way as the first part. Adding `아니면 (or)` between the two parts can make the sentence clearer. For descriptive verbs, you have two options: use the negative form of the verb or use the descriptive verb `아니다 (to not be)`.

The conjugation for `아니다` in this context is:
- Present: `아닌지`
- Future: `아닐지`
- Past: `아니었는지`

Example sentences shown include:
> **동생이 올지 안 올지 궁금하지 않아요?** / Aren't you curious whether or not your younger sibling will come?
> **지금 철수가 바쁜지 아닌지 알려주세요.** / Please tell me whether or not 철수 is busy now.

---

### Usage with Question Words and "How to"
The "whether/if" form is fundamentally about talking about things that are unknown. Because of this, it is frequently paired with question words like `뭐 (what)`, `누구 (who)`, `어떻게 (how)`, `왜 (why)`, `어디 (where)`, `언제 (when)`, and `얼마나 (how much)`.

Examples provided:
- `뭐가 가장 쉬운지 (what is the easiest)`
- `누가 제일 똑똑했는지 (who was the smartest)`
- `어떻게 했는지 (how you did it)`

Finally, the instructor mentions that this form can also mean "how to," as in:
> **나는 김치를 어떻게 만드는지 알고 있다.** / I know how to make kimchi.
