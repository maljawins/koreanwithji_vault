## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the essential grammar for expressing ability or knowledge regarding specific actions, equivalent to the English phrase "how to." The lesson focuses on two primary methods: a specialized grammatical structure using the noun 줄 (method/way) and a more literal construction using the adverb 어떻게 (how). By the end of the lesson, students will be able to state whether they know or do not know how to perform various tasks, such as swimming, riding a bicycle, or using chopsticks.

---

### Method 1: Using the Verb Ending -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다/모르다

The first and most common way to express "knowing how to" involves attaching a specific ending to the verb stem. To form this, the instructor explains that you take the verb stem and add the future tense modifier: use ㄹ if the stem ends in a vowel, or 을 if it ends in a consonant. This is followed by the noun 줄, which functions here to mean the "way," "method," or "knack" of doing something. Finally, you complete the sentence with the verb 알다 (to know) for affirmative statements or 모르다 (to not know) for negative ones.

The instructor provides several examples of this structure in practice:

- **수영 할 줄 알아요.** / I know how to swim.
- **자전거를 탈 줄 몰라요.** / I don't know how to ride a bicycle.
- **젓가락을 쓸 줄 아세요?** / Do you know how to use chopsticks?

In the final example, the instructor uses the honorific form 아세요 to demonstrate how the structure adapts to different levels of politeness when asking others about their abilities.

---

### Method 2: Using 어떻게 and the Ending -는지 알다/모르다

The second method is more descriptive and uses the adverb 어떻게 (how) at the beginning of the clause. For this construction, you take the verb stem and attach the ending -는지, which is used for connecting a question or an uncertain clause to a following verb. Like the first method, this is then followed by 알다 (to know) or 모르다 (to not know). This version is often used when the "how to" refers to a process or a specific way of reaching a destination or completing a task.

The instructor illustrates this method with the following examples:

- **어떻게 가는지 알아요.** / I know how to go there.
- **어떻게 해야 하는지 몰라요.** / I don't know what I need to do (literally: I don't know how I should do it).
- **이걸 어떻게 쓰는지 아세요?** / Do you know how to use this?

Visually, the instructor emphasizes that while both methods translate to "how to" in English, the first method often focuses on a learned skill or ability, while the second method focuses on the specific manner or process of an action.
