### The Basic "Whether" Connector: ~지
The form ~지 is primarily used when discussing unknown information. It indicates that the action or state described by the verb is something the speaker does not know or is inquiring about. This form is compatible with both action and descriptive verbs. Because it deals with uncertainty, it is frequently paired with question words like 어디 (where), 언제 (when), and 어떻게 (how), as well as verbs related to knowing or conveying information, such as 알다 (to know), 모르다 (to not know), 알려주다 (to let someone know), and 물어보다 (to ask).

To conjugate, the verb is turned into an adjective form based on the tense, followed by 지. In the present tense, action verbs use ~는지 (e.g., 하는지) while descriptive verbs use the standard adjective conjugation (e.g., 바쁜지, 예쁜지). The future tense uses ~(으)ㄹ지 (e.g., 할지, 먹을지). Notably, the past tense for all verbs uses the ending ~었/았/했는지 (e.g., 했는지).

> **어디 가는지 궁금해요.** / I'm curious where we're going.
> **내일도 바쁜지 물어볼게요.** / I'll ask if they're busy tomorrow, too.
> **뭘 먹을지 모르겠어요.** / I don't know what I will eat.
> **누가 했는지 알아요?** / Do you know who did it?

---

### Expressing "Whether or Not" with Opposites
To specifically say "whether or not," the ~지 form is used twice: first with the target verb and then with its opposite. This can involve using the negative 안 prefix or a completely different verb with an opposite meaning. For descriptive verbs, the verb 아니다 (to not be) can serve as the second verb.

- **하는지 안 하는지** (whether one does it or not)
- **좋아하는지 싫어하는지** (whether one likes it or dislikes it)
- **있는지 없는지** (whether it exists or not)
- **쉬운지 아닌지** (whether it is easy or not)

> **냉장고에 있는지 없는지 확인해 주세요.** / Please check if it's in the refrigerator or not.
> **철수가 저를 좋아하는지 싫어하는지 모르겠어요.** / I don't know if Chul-soo likes me or not.

A specific variation exists for action verbs in the future tense: the second verb can be 말다 (to stop/not do), resulting in the ending ~(으)ㄹ지 말지.

> **갈지 말지 안 정했어요.** / I didn't decide if I'll go or not.
> **영어를 공부할지 말지 고민이에요.** / I'm thinking if I'll study English or not.

---

### Perhaps Because: ~서 그런지
The connector ~서 그런지 combines ~서 (because), 그렇다 (to be so), and ~지 (unknown information). It literally translates to "whether it is so because..." and is used to mean "perhaps because" or "maybe because." It suggests a possible reason for a situation when the speaker isn't 100% certain.

> **잠을 늦게 자서 그런지 너무 피곤해요.** / Perhaps because I slept late, I'm so tired.
> **날씨가 추워서 그런지 오늘은 사람이 별로 없네요.** / Perhaps because the weather's cold, there aren't a lot of people today.

---

### Regardless of Whether: ~(으)나
While ~지 is for unknown information, the form ~(으)나 is used when an outcome happens regardless of which choice is made. It is often repeated (A ~(으)나 B ~(으)나) to mean "regardless of whether A or B." This form is less common in daily conversation, where ~든지 is preferred, but it appears in formal contexts and specific idioms like ~(으)나 마나 (whether one does it or not [it doesn't matter]).

> **비가 오나 눈이 오나 여기는 손님이 많아요.** / Whether it rains or snows, there are a lot of customers here.
> **지금 가나 나중에 가나 똑같아요.** / It doesn't matter if you go now or later.

---

### Emphatic "So Much That": ~던지
The form ~던지 combines the past retrospective ~더 with ~지. While it literally suggests unknown past information, it is idiomatic when used with adverbs like 얼마나 or 어찌나 (how much). It emphasizes that something was "so [adjective]" or happened "so much" that the extent is almost unknown. It functions similarly to saying "very [adjective] + so/because."

> **어찌나 맛있던지 세 그릇이나 먹었어요.** / It was so delicious I ate three bowls.
> **비가 얼마나 많이 오던지 우산을 썼는데도 다 젖었어.** / It rained so much, even though I used an umbrella, I got all wet.

A similar nuance can be expressed using ~(으)면 with 얼마나, typically ending the sentence in a rhetorical question like ~ㄹ까요? or ~겠어요? to express the speaker's strong opinion on the intensity of a situation.

> **얼마나 배가 고팠으면 딱딱한 빵을 먹었겠어요?** / They must have been so hungry to have eaten hard bread.

---

### Important Distinctions: ~든지 vs ~던지 and Time-based ~ㄴ 지
The instructor highlights two common areas of confusion. First, ~던지 (emphasis/past) is often mispronounced as ~든지 (regardless of choice/selection). While they sound similar, ~든지 is used for making a choice where any option is acceptable.

> **저는 뭘 먹든지 상관없어요.** / I don't care what I eat (no matter what I eat).

Second, the "whether" ~지 should not be confused with the "since" ~ㄴ/은 지. The latter is used only with action verbs to indicate how much time has passed since an action occurred. It is always used with a space before 지 and is followed by time-related verbs like 되다 (to become), 지나다 (to pass), or 오래되다 (to be a long time).

> **한국어를 공부한 지 1년이 됐어요.** / It's been one year since I (first) studied Korean.
> **철수가 밖에 나간 지 얼마나 됐어요?** / How long has it been since Chul-soo went outside?
