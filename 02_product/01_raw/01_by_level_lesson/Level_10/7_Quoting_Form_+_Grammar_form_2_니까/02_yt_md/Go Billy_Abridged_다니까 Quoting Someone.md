## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form 다니까, which is considered an advanced structure. While the logic behind it is straightforward, using it naturally in conversation requires a nuanced understanding of context and subject-verb relationships. This form essentially combines the standard Korean quoting form with the causal marker 니까 (because/since).

The lesson is divided into two primary functional uses of 다니까. The first use involves replying to a quote or a statement made by someone else (or oneself) to provide a reason for a subsequent action or observation. The second use is an informal sentence-ending expression used to restate and emphasize something the speaker has already said, often conveying a sense of annoyance or insistence when the listener hasn't properly acknowledged the first statement.

---

### Construction and Quoting Variations
The form 다니까 is a shortened version of 다고 하니까, which literally means "because (someone) says that..." Because it is built upon the quoting system, it changes based on the type of sentence being quoted. For plain statements with verbs, 하다 (to do) becomes 한다니까. For the verb 이다 (to be), it becomes 이라니까.

The instructor demonstrates that this form is highly versatile and can accommodate various tenses and sentence types. In the past tense, 하다 becomes 했다니까. It can also quote questions by using the 냐 quoting suffix, resulting in 했냐니까 (because I/they asked if...). For suggestions, the 자 form is used, resulting in 하자니까 (because I/they suggested doing...). Finally, for commands, the 라 suffix is used, resulting in 하라니까 (because I/they told someone to do...).

---

### Usage 1: Replying to a Quote as a Reason
The first major use of 다니까 is to provide a reason for an action based on something that was said. A critical rule for this usage is that the subject of the quote (the person who said the thing or the person the quote is about) must be different from the subject of the following clause. For example, if the first part is "Because they said X," the second part should be "let's do Y" or "I did Z."

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this "different subject" rule. In the sentence "They say the weather will be good next week, so let's take a trip together," the weather forecast is the source of the quote, while "we" are the subject of the trip.

> **다음 주는 날씨가 좋을 거라니까 그때 여행을 같이 가요.** / They say the weather will be good next week, so let's take a trip together then.

Another example shows how the form works when quoting oneself to explain a result. If a speaker says they did something alone and notices the listener's disbelief, the subjects are "I" (the speaker of the quote) and "they" (the ones showing disbelief).

> **제가 혼자서 했다니까 믿지 않는 눈치였어요.** / Because I said I did it by myself, they seemed not to believe me.

Further examples demonstrate quoting commands and questions:
> **음료수를 사오라니까 콜라를 사왔어요.** / I told them to buy a drink, and (because of that) they brought back Coke.
> **제가 그것도 못 참냐니까 그럼 직접 해보라던데요.** / Because I asked if they couldn't even handle that, they told me to try doing it myself then.

---

### Usage 2: Restating for Emphasis (Informal)
The second use of 다니까 occurs at the end of a sentence. In this context, it is used to restate something the speaker already said. This is typically used when the speaker is upset, annoyed, or frustrated because the listener didn't hear them or isn't listening. It carries the nuance of "I told you already!" or "I already said!"

This usage is strictly informal. The instructor warns that using this with a boss or a stranger would be considered rude because of the inherent tone of annoyance. It is most commonly used in the first person to emphasize the speaker's previous statement or position.

> **저는 너무 달아서 먹을 수 없다니까요.** / I (already) told you I can't eat it because it's too sweet!

It can also be used to insist on a prediction or a suggestion that has been ignored:
> **이번 주도 BTS가 1등일 거라니까요.** / I told you BTS is going to be number one again this week!
> **이 얘기는 그만하자니까요.** / I said, let's stop talking about this!
