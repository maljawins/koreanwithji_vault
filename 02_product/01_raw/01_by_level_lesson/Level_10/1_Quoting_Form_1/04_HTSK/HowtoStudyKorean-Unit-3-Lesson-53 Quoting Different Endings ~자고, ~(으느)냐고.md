Lesson 53: Quoting Different Endings: ~자고, ~(으/느)냐고

Introduction

In this lesson, you will add to your knowledge of what you learned in the previous lesson by learning more about making quoted sentences. In this lesson, we will look at how to quote sentences of suggestion (~자고) and sentences of question ((~으/느)냐고/). In addition, you will learn how to form direct quotes. Let’s get started.

I said “Lets…”: ~자고

In [Lesson 44](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-44/), you learned how to add ~자 to the stems of verbs to make a suggestion. For example:

[밥을 먹자](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/05/1-Lets-eat.m4a) = Let’s eat (rice)
[내일 공원에 가자](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/05/2-Tomorrow-lets-go-to-the-park.m4a) = Tomorrow, let’s go to the park

Play[애기가 침을 흘려서 애기에게 턱받이를 해 주자](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent3.mp3)
= Let’s put a bib on the baby because he is drooling

Play[사람들이 여기 맛이 없다고 불평해서 다른 식당에 가자](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent4.mp3)
= People complain that (the food in) this place isn’t delicious, so let’s go to another restaurant

By attaching ~고 to ~자, you can quote these types of sentences. For example:

Play[아빠가 밥을 먹자고 했어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent5.mp3)
= Dad said “Let’s eat”

Play[여자 친구가 공원에 가자고 했어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent6.mp3)
= My girlfriend said “let’s go to the park”

Play[선생님이 수업 시간 동안 열심히 공부하자고 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent7.mp3)
= The teacher said “let’s study hard during class time”

Play[우리가 서울에 가면 서울 곳곳을 구경하자고 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent8.mp3)
= When we go to Seoul, I said “let’s sight-see everywhere in Seoul”

Play[우리 아들은 나한테 자꾸 부산에 가자고 졸랐어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent9.mp3)
= My son kept pestering me and said “let’s go to Busan”

Play[여자친구가 산책하자고 했지만 저는 너무 피곤해서 안 갔어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent10.mp3)
= My girlfriend said “let’s go for a walk,” but I was too tired, so I didn’t go

Quoting Questions: ~(느)냐고 (Verbs)

When quoting a clause/sentence that is a question, a different quoting addition should be used. For example, if you want to say:

“I asked him what he likes”

You cannot use the regular quoting addition here. For example, this is not correct:

그가 무엇을 좋아한다고 물어봤어요

When a quoted clause/sentence ends with a verb, the addition of ~느냐고 should be used to indicate that the quote is a question. For example, if we were to correct the example from before:

Play[그가 무엇을 좋아하느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent11.mp3) = I asked him what he likes (another translation could be:)
Play[그가 무엇을 좋아하느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent11.mp3) = I asked him “what do you like?”

It is also possible to quote a question that was asking about the past or future. For example:

Play[우리 아빠는 나에게 어디 가느냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53new2.mp3) = My dad asked me where I am going
Play[우리 아빠는 나에게 어디 갔느냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53new3.mp3) = My dad asked me where I went
Play[우리 아빠는 나에게 어디 가겠느냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53new1.mp3) = My dad asked me where I will go

Of course, it is also possible to change the tense of 물어보다 (or 묻다) to indicate that you will ask in the past, present or future. For example:

Play[나는 여자 친구에게 뭐 먹었느냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent12.mp3) = I asked my girlfriend what she ate
Play[나는 여자 친구에게 뭐 먹었느냐고 물어보고 있어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent13.mp3) = I am asking my girlfriend what she ate
Play[나는 여자 친구에게 뭐 먹었느냐고 물어볼 거야](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent14.mp3) = I will ask my girlfriend what she ate

Below are many more examples:

Play[지진이 어떻게 생기느냐고 물어봤다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent15.mp3) = I asked how earthquakes happen/occur
Play[이 반팔을 입어야 되느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent16.mp3) = I asked if I have to wear this t-shirt
Play[사위가 어디 갔느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent17.mp3) = I asked where my son-in-law went

Play[저는 부장님에게 우리가 그렇게 해야 하느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent18.mp3)
= I asked our boss if we had to do it like that

Play[냉동실에서 왜 열이 나오느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent19.mp3)
= I asked why there is heat coming out of the freezer

Play[학생에게 학교 안에서 침을 왜 뱉었느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent20.mp3)
= I asked the student why he spat inside the school

Play[우리가 남들을 왜 잘 대해야 하느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent21.mp3)
= I asked why we need to treat others/strangers well

All of the examples above show ~느냐고 attached to the final verb in the quoted portion of the sentence – sometimes following the past tense ~았/었, and sometimes following the future tense ~겠. Korean dictionaries indicate that ~느냐고 is the correct addition when quoting a question that ends in a verb. That being said, Korean people will be more inclined to say that ~냐고 (without ~느) looks and sounds more natural (well, they sound quite similar). For example, all of the sentences above could also be written or said as:

Play[지진이 어떻게 생기냐고 물어봤다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent22.mp3)
Play[저는 부장님에게 우리가 그렇게 해야 하냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent23.mp3)
Play[냉동실에서 왜 열이 나오냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent24.mp3)
Play[이 반팔을 입어야 되냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent25.mp3)
Play[사위가 어디 갔냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent26.mp3)
Play[학생에게 학교 안에서 침을 왜 뱉었냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent27.mp3)
Play[우리가 남들을 왜 잘 대해야 하냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent28.mp3)

I asked a Korean grammar teacher about this. She said that both would be correct, even though I showed her that the correct usage for a verb is ~느냐고. I asked her if a student uses ~냐고 (without ~느) on her Korean language exam, would she mark it as incorrect. She said “No, because that’s the way we use it all the time.”

I’m not sure what to make of this, and it is difficult for me to suggest which one you should use. Personally, I suggest that you use the simple ~냐고 which is the form that is more commonly said by Korean speakers. ~냐고 is also the form that I have always used whenever I quote a question. However, you should keep in the back of your mind that the official usage is ~느냐고.

You can see ~느냐 used in a more complex way, to indicate uncertainty in [Lesson 160](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-7/lessons-159-166/lesson-160/). Why not take a look!

Okay, let’s move on to adjectives.

Quoting Questions: ~(으)냐고 (Adjectives)

When a quotation ends with an adjective, the addition of ~(으)냐고 should be used to indicate that it is a quoted question. ~으냐고 is added to adjectives ending in a consonant (except ㄹ) and ~냐고 is added to adjectives ending in a vowel (or if the final consonant is ㄹ). For example:

Play[저는 친구에게 그 여자가 예쁘냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent29.mp3) = I asked my friend if that girl was pretty
Play[아빠에게 지금 편안하냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent30.mp3) = I asked my dad if he is/was comfortable/relaxed
Play[너의 며느리가 아름다우냐고 물어보고 싶어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent31.mp3) = I want to ask if your daughter-in-law is beautiful
Play[냉동실이 왜 이렇게 추우냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent32.mp3) = I asked why the freezer is/was so cold
Play[이런 반팔이 집에 많으냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent33.mp3) = I asked if there were many of these types of t-shirts at home

As with verbs, the usage that you will hear in everyday speech/writing and the official usage is sometimes different. In speech, Korean people often eliminate the “으” that should be included if the adjective ends in a consonant. For example:

1. Play[이런 반팔이 집에 많으냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent33.mp3)
2. Play[이런 반팔이 집에 많냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent34.mp3)

Officially a) is the correct form, but you will often see and hear b) used. Most Korean people wouldn’t be able to tell you which one is correct – or they would assume that both are correct.

싶다 was first introduced in [Lesson 17](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-17-25-2/lesson-17/) but was also discussed in the previous lesson. 싶다 is also an adjective and therefore the above rules apply here as well. For example:

Play[그는 뭐 먹고 싶으냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent35.mp3) = He asked what do you want to eat
Play[그 사람이 나한테 앞날에 뭐 하고 싶으냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent36.mp3) = That person asked me what I want to do in the future

있다 and 없다 are very confusing in this usage. As you know, depending on the usage of 있다 and 없다, they can be adjectives or verbs. Typically, adjective conjugations are applied when they are adjectives, and verb conjugations are applied when they are verbs (makes sense). However, regardless of if they are being used as a verb or adjective, the verb-addition of ~느냐고 should be attached when quoting a question with 있다 or 없다. You can treat this as an irregular to this rule. I tried to get clarification from Korean grammar teachers as to why this happens, and they all told me “it’s just an irregular.”

*Just like with other verbs,*있느냐 *and* 없느냐 *are often spoken/written as* 있냐 *and* 없냐 *and would be seen as correct by Korean people.*

Here are some examples:

Play[저는 그에게 수영할 수 있느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent37.mp3)
= I asked him if he could swim

Play[아빠에게 엄마를 왜 함부로 대하고 있느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent38.mp3)
= I asked dad why he is treating mom disrespectfully

Play[동생에게 환경을 왜 파괴하고 있느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent39.mp3)
= I asked my brother why he is/was destroying the environment

Play[그 숙녀가 남의 눈을 피하고 싶어서 우연히 호텔을 찾아서 혹시 잠깐 들어갈 수 있느냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson53snew.mp3)
= The lady wanted to avoid other people(‘s eyes), so she fatefully found a hotel and asked if she could go inside

Quoting Questions with 이다

As with adjectives, when a quotation ends with 이다, the addition of ~(으)냐고 is added to 이다. Because the stem of 이다 is always “이” (i.e. always ends in a vowel) the addition of “~냐고 is always added and we never need to worry about the “~(으). For example

Play[나는 그 사람이 의사이냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent41.mp3) = I asked if that person is a doctor

As usual, if the noun that 이다 is attached to ends in a vowel (as above), the 이 can be eliminated:

Play[나는 그 사람이 의사냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent42.mp3) = I asked if that person is a doctor

If the noun ends in a consonant, 이 must be included. For example:

Play[나는 그 사람이 우리 선생님이냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent43.mp3) = I asked if that person is our teacher
*You might want to read my discussion about why*이*is included in these cases. I discussed this in the*[*previous Lesson*](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-3-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-3-lessons-51-58/lesson-52/)*.*

In [Lessons 21](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-17-25-2/lesson-21-asking-questions-in-korean-why-when-where-and-who/) and [22](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-17-25-2/lesson-22/) you learned about various question words. In those lessons, you learned how 이다 can be used with 누구, 뭐, 어디 and 언제. For example:

Play[방학은 언제야?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/Lesson-21-29.mp3) = When is vacation?
Play[그곳이 어디야?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/Lesson-21-34.mp3) = Where is that place?
Play[이것이 뭐야?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/Lesson-22-10.mp3) = What is this?
[그 사람은 누구야?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/05/33-Who-is-that-person.m4a) = Who is that person?

~냐고 can also be attached to these words when used with 이다. For example:

Play[방학이 언제(이)냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent48.mp3) = I asked when vacation is
Play[그곳이 어디(이)냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent49.mp3) = I asked where that place is
Play[그것이 뭐(이)냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent50.mp3) = I asked what that thing is
Play[그 사람이 누구(이)냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent51.mp3) = I asked who that person is

Below are more examples of ~냐고 being used with 이다:

Play[이 신사가 누구냐고 물어봤습니다](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent52.mp3)
= I asked who this gentlemen is/was

Play[저는 제 친구에게 한국으로 이사하고 싶은 이유가 뭐냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent53.mp3)
= I asked my friend ‘what is the reason you want to move to Korea?’

Play[제가 점원에게 그 핸드폰이 좋은 핸드폰이냐고 물어봤지만 그는 모른다고 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent54.mp3)
= I asked the salesperson if that cellphone is good, but he said that he didn’t know

Play[저 학교를 다니는 학생들이 똑똑한 학생들이냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent55.mp3)
= He asked if the students who attend that school are smart students

Also notice that by adding ~(이)냐고 to 이다, you can ask questions in sentences that use the ~ㄹ 것이다 future tense conjugation. For example:

Play[나는 그들에게 결혼식에 갈 거냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent56.mp3) = I asked if they were going to go to the wedding
Play[나는 그에게 어디 갈 거냐고 물어봤어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent57.mp3) = I asked where he is going to go
Play[저는 그에게 앞날에 뭐 할 거냐고 물어보고 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent58.mp3) = I want to ask what he will do in the future
Play[그는 저한테 여자친구랑 언제 결혼할 거냐고 물어봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent59.mp3) = He asked me when I will marry my girlfriend

Direct Quotes

Direct quotes are fairly easy now that you have the knowledge that was presented in the past two lessons. If you want to say something as a direct quote, the only difference is that the quoted sentence is conjugated the way it was originally said. For example, if I wanted to quote:

제가 하고 싶지 않습니다 = I don’t want to do it

An indirect quote would be:
그는 하고 싶지 않다고 했다 = He said he doesn’t want to do it

But a direct quote would be:
Play[그는 “제가 하고 싶지 않습니다” 라고 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent60.mp3) = He said “I don’t want to do it”

If you were to write the sentence above, you would need to include the use of quotations, but in speech (obviously), that is not necessary. Other example:

Indirect quote: 그는 너를 사랑한다고 했어 = He said I love you
Direct quote: Play[그는 “너를 사랑해”라고 했어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/09/Lesson53sent61.mp3) = He said “I love you”

To me this type of sentence always made sense to me. I always imagined that the directly quoted clause was acting as a noun, and therefore would require ~라고/이라고 being attached to it (usually ~라고 because most completed sentences end in a vowel).
