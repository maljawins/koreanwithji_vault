## Teaching Points

**1. Aggressive Quoting Ending: -다냐자라 + 니까**
Ji explains that replacing the standard quoting connector -고 (go) with -니까 (ni-kka) makes the statement more aggressive or insistent. It is often used when the speaker is annoyed that their previous statement or question was ignored.
*   **언제 만나?** / When are we meeting?
*   **언제 만나냐니까!** / I asked when are we meeting! (Implied: Why aren't you answering?)

**2. Quoting Form + Connectors (Contractions)**
Ji teaches that the sequence `[Plain Form] + 고 + 하 + [Connector]` is almost always shortened in speech by dropping the `고 하` (go ha) portion.
*   **-는 (neun):** 한다고 하는 → 한다는 (Shortened further to 한단)
*   **-는데 (neun-de):** 한다고 하는데 → 한다는데
*   **-면 (myeon):** 한다고 하면 → 한다면
*   **-면서 (myeon-seo):** 한다고 하면서 → 한다면서
*   **-아/어서 (a/eo-seo):** 한다고 해서 → 한대서
*   **Note:** You can drop the whole `고 하` block, but you can never drop *only* `하`. For example, `한다고면` is grammatically incorrect; it must be `한다면`.

**3. Hypothetical Conditional: -하면 vs -한다면**
Ji clarifies a common point of confusion for learners:
*   **-하면:** A simple "if" or "when" (e.g., "If I go...").
*   **-한다면:** A more hypothetical "if," translated as "If someone were to say..." or "Let's say that..." (e.g., "If I were to go...").
*   **Example from a variety show:** "고기와 밀가루를 멀리하면 오래 살 수 있지만, 그렇다면(그렇다고 하면) 오래 살 필요가 없어." (If you stay away from meat and flour, you can live longer, but if that's the case, there's no need to live long.)

**4. Short Quoting Form (Quotation Endings)**
This is the most common way to end a sentence when quoting someone in casual speech. It involves adding an extra stroke to the "다냐자라" vowels:
*   **다 → 대:** "그렇대요" (They say it's so.)
*   **냐 → 냬:** "뭐냬요?" (They asked what it is.)
*   **자 → 제:** "먹재요" (They suggested we eat.)
*   **라 → 래:** "오래 요" (They told me to come.)
*   **Special Case:** "뭐래?" is a contraction of "뭐라고 해?" (What is he/she saying?), often used dismissively or in response to something nonsensical.
