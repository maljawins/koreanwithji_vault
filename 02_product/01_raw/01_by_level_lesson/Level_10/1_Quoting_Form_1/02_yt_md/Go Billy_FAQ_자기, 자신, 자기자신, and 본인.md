## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the distinction between the words 자기, 자신, 자기자신, and 본인. While these words all generally relate to the concept of "self" or "oneself," they function differently depending on the level of formality, the specific grammatical role they play in a sentence, and the relationship between the speakers. The lesson breaks down each term individually, providing example sentences and usage rules to help students choose the correct word for various contexts, ranging from casual couple talk to formal writing.

---

### The Versatile Word 자기

The word 자기 primarily means "one" or "you," but it is specifically used to refer to a non-specific person. For example, in a sentence like "One must be patient if one wants something," 자기 would be the appropriate choice. Beyond this general usage, 자기 functions as a reflexive pronoun used to refer back to the subject of a sentence to avoid repetition. The instructor demonstrates this with a visual example:

> **어제 철수는 자기(의) 컴퓨터를 버렸어요.** / Yesterday, Chul-soo threw away his (own) computer.

In this sentence, 자기 replaces the need to repeat "Chul-soo." The instructor notes that while the possessive marker 의 can be attached, it is optional and frequently omitted in natural speech. This reflexive use applies regardless of whether the subject is singular or plural; it simply points back to whoever the subject is. Furthermore, 자기 is strictly a casual term. It is never used in formal speech or writing. A unique social application of 자기 is between couples, where it serves as a term of endearment similar to "honey" or "darling" (often as 자기야), or as a softer, more polite alternative to the potentially harsh second-person pronoun 너 (you).

---

### 자신 and the Emphatic 자기자신

자신 is similar to 자기 in that it can mean "one" or "you" in a general sense, but it is much more formal. It is common in formal speech and is the standard choice for written Korean. When used possessively in these formal contexts, it is more likely to retain the marker: 자신의. A key grammatical difference is that 자신 can be combined with other pronouns to create "self" words like "myself," "yourself," or "themselves." The instructor illustrates this by contrasting it with 자기:

- **저 자신** (me myself) — Correct
- **너 자신** (you yourself) — Correct
- **저 자기** — Incorrect
- **너 자기** — Incorrect

When you combine the two words into 자기자신, it functions as a more emphatic version of "oneself" or "yourself." It adds extra weight to the reflexive meaning, emphasizing that the action or state refers specifically and uniquely to that person.

---

### 본인: The Person Concerned

본인 is a distinct, highly formal term that does not translate directly to "oneself" in the reflexive sense. Instead, it refers to "the person in question" or "the person concerned" with a specific issue, situation, or problem. It is used to identify the specific individual who is the subject of a discussion or transaction. The instructor provides a formal example sentence:

> **본인에게 직접 물어보세요.** / Ask the person concerned directly. (Ask them yourself.)

In this context, 본인 points to the specific person who has the authority or knowledge regarding the matter at hand. Because it is a very formal term, it is rarely used in everyday casual conversation but is frequently encountered in legal, business, or official situations where clear identification of the "person concerned" is necessary.
