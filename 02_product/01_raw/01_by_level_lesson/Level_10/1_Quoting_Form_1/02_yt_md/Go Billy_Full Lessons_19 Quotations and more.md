## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces a fundamental grammar form used for making quotations in Korean, which functions similarly to the word "that" in English sentences like "He said that..." or "I heard that...". The lesson covers three primary types of quotations: statements (reporting what was said or heard), questions (restating a question), and "let's" sentences (reporting a suggestion). Throughout the video, the instructor provides conjugation rules for each type and demonstrates them with the help of a guest speaker, Autumn, who provides native-level examples.

---

### Quoting Statements

To quote a statement—something that was spoken or heard—the speaker must use the plain form of the verb, attach the marker 고, and then add a concluding verb such as 말하다 (to say), 하다 (to say/do), or 듣다 (to hear). The plain form conjugation is a prerequisite for this structure. The instructor notes that in the context of quotations, 말하다 and 하다 are interchangeable.

A special rule applies to the verb 이다 (to be). When quoting "to be," the form becomes 이라고 if the preceding noun ends in a consonant, and 라고 if it ends in a vowel. For the negative form 아니다 (to not be), the quoted form is always 아니라고.

The instructor provides several examples of quoted statements:
> **그가 날 사랑한다고 말했다.** / He said that he loves me.
> **동생이 내일 싸이 콘서트에 간다고 했다.** / My younger sibling said that they're going to a PSY concert tomorrow.
> **생강이 감기에 좋다고 들었어.** / I heard that ginger is good for colds.

A cultural and linguistic note is included regarding the word 너 (you). The instructor explains that 너 is informal and should only be used with close friends of the same age or younger people you know well. Using it with older people or strangers is considered rude. An example of this in a quotation is:
> **너 방금 내가 바보라고 했어?** / Did you just say I'm stupid?

---

### Quoting Questions

The second form is used for restating or quoting questions. This is constructed by taking the verb stem and adding 냐고, followed by a concluding verb, most commonly 물어보다 (to ask). This form is particularly useful for reporting what someone else asked or clarifying a question directed at the speaker.

Examples of quoted questions include:
> **나는 철수한테 영희가 누구냐고 물어봤다.** / I asked Cheolsoo who is Younghee.
> **제가 한국 사람이냐고요?** / Did you ask if I'm Korean?

The instructor points out that in the second example, the final verb (물어보다) is omitted. This is a common occurrence in spoken Korean because the intent to "ask" is already understood from the 냐고 marker.

---

### Quoting Suggestions ("Let's" Sentences)

The third form is used to quote "let's" sentences, such as reporting that someone suggested going somewhere or doing something together. This is formed by taking the verb stem and adding 자고, followed by a verb like 말하다 or 하다.

Examples of quoted suggestions:
> **엄마한테 해외 여행을 가자고 했어요.** / I told my mom let's go on an overseas trip.
> **오늘은 여기서 그만하자고요?** / Did you say let's stop here for today?

Similar to the question form, the final verb can be omitted in these sentences because the meaning of "saying" or "suggesting" is implied by the 자고 marker.

---

### Leaving Out the Final Verb

The instructor concludes the grammar explanation by emphasizing a general rule: in all three quotation forms (고, 냐고, 자고), the final verb can often be left out as long as the meaning remains clear to the listener. This is especially common in casual conversation where the context of saying, hearing, or asking is already established.
