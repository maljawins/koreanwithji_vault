## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the fundamental mechanics of indirect and direct quotation in Korean. Quoting forms are essential not just for reporting speech (he said, she said), but also for expressing thoughts, beliefs, and intentions. The lesson builds upon previous concepts of thinking verbs, showing that the grammar used for "I think that..." is essentially a quotation of one's own internal monologue.

The core of the lesson revolves around four distinct types of quotes: statements, questions, suggestions, and commands. Each type requires a specific conjugation pattern before the quoting particle 고. The instructor emphasizes that while 말하다 (to speak/say) is the most common verb to follow a quote, it can be replaced by a wide variety of verbs like 생각하다 (to think), 외치다 (to yell), or 전하다 (to convey/tell). The lesson concludes with important irregular cases, such as the transformation of the verb 주다 (to give) in command quotes.

---

### Quoting Statements with 다고
To quote a regular statement, the sentence must first be converted into the "plain form" before attaching the particle 고. For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the plain form is identical to the dictionary form. For example, 춥다 (to be cold) becomes 춥다고. For action verbs, the plain form is created by adding ㄴ or 는 to the verb stem. The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence "I said I want to go," where 가고 싶다 (want to go) is treated as a descriptive verb, resulting in 가고 싶다고 말했어요.

The instructor notes that the verb 이다 (to be) follows a slightly different pattern, becoming 이라고 after a consonant and 라고 after a vowel. Similarly, 아니다 (to not be) becomes 아니라고. A common colloquialism is also introduced: speakers often drop the final verb 말하다 and simply end the sentence with 고 or 고요. Ending a sentence with 사과라고요 effectively communicates "I said it's an apple" because the listener assumes the verb "said" from the quoting particle.

---

### Quoting Questions with 냐고
When the content inside the quotation marks is a question, the plain form is not used. Instead, the verb stem is attached to 냐고. This form is typically followed by the verb 물어보다 (to ask). For example, to quote the question "Do you want to eat cheese?", the speaker would say 치즈를 먹고 싶냐고 물어봤어요.

There are specific rules for certain verb stems. If a verb stem ends in ㄹ, such as 살다 (to live), the ㄹ is dropped before adding 냐고, resulting in 사냐고. For the verb 이다 (to be), it becomes 이냐고 after a consonant and 냐고 after a vowel. The instructor also mentions a common "accepted mistake" regarding ㅂ-irregular verbs; while grammatically one should change the ㅂ to 우 (e.g., 춥다 becoming 추우냐고), most native speakers simply say 춥냐고.

---

### Quoting Suggestions with 자고
Suggestions, or "let's" statements, are quoted by attaching 자고 to the verb stem. This is a direct reflection of the casual ~자 ending used for suggestions. For instance, "I said let's go" becomes 가자고 말했어요. This form is useful for repeating a proposal that may have been ignored or misunderstood.

To quote a negative suggestion ("let's not"), the verb stem is attached to ~지 말자고. This stems from the negative suggestion form ~지 말자. An example provided is 가지 말자고 말했어요 (I said let's not go). The instructor reminds learners that this specific negative form is required for suggestions, rather than the standard 안 or ~지 않다 negations.

---

### Quoting Commands with 라고 and the 주다 Exception
Commands are quoted by adding 으라고 to verb stems ending in a consonant, or 라고 to those ending in a vowel. To quote "I said do it," the verb 하다 becomes 하라고요. For negative commands ("don't do it"), the form ~지 말라고 is used, such as 하지 말라고 말했어요.

A critical exception occurs with the verb 주다 (to give). When quoting a command to give something, the verb 주다 usually changes to 달라고. This happens 99% of the time when the person giving the command is also the one intended to receive the item (e.g., "Give me the cat" becomes 고양이를 달라고요). The form 주라고 is only used in the rare 1% of cases where the speaker is commanding someone to give something to a third party (e.g., "Give Chul-soo the cat").

---

### Optional Verbs and 그러다
While 말하다 is the standard verb for "to say," the instructor explains that 하다 (to do) is often used as a shorthand for "to say" in quoting contexts. Additionally, the verb 그러다 (to say so/to do so) is very common in informal speech. When conjugated, it becomes 그래요 (present) or 그랬어요 (past). Using 그러다 adds the nuance of "saying that" or "saying so." For example, 시간이 없다고 그랬어요 means "He said (so/that) there is no time."
