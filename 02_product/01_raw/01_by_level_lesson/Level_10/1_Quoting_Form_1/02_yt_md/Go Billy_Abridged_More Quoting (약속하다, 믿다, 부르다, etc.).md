### The Foundation: Plain Form Review
Before any quote can be constructed, the verb must be converted into the plain form. The instructor explains that the plain form itself is neutral, neither inherently polite nor rude, but its conjugation depends on the type of verb. For action verbs in the present tense, stems ending in a consonant take `~는다`, while those ending in a vowel take `~ㄴ다`. A specific exception exists for stems ending in `ㄹ`; the `ㄹ` is removed before adding `~ㄴ다`. Examples include `하다 (to do)` becoming `한다`, `먹다 (to eat)` becoming `먹는다`, and `알다 (to know)` becoming `안다`.

Descriptive verbs are simpler, as the plain form is identical to the dictionary form, such as `바쁘다 (to be busy)` remaining `바쁘다`. For the past tense, any verb is conjugated to its past stem and then simply followed by `다`, such as `했다` or `먹었다`. The future tense can be formed using the `~겠다` ending or the `~ㄹ 것이다` ending, which is often shortened to `~ㄹ 거다` in the plain form. The instructor also notes that `있다 (to exist/have)` and `없다 (to not exist/not have)` remain unchanged in the plain form.

---

### The Quoting Structure and Common Verbs
The basic quoting arrangement involves taking the plain form of a verb and directly attaching the particle `고`. This is then followed by a verb that describes the action being taken with that quote. While most verbs follow this pattern, the instructor highlights critical exceptions for `이다 (to be)` and `아니다 (to not be)`. When used with the quoting particle, `이다` becomes `이라고` after a consonant or `라고` after a vowel, and `아니다` becomes `아니라고`.

Several common verbs are frequently paired with this form. `말하다 (to speak)` is the standard, though in natural conversation, the `말` is often dropped, leaving just `하다`. For example, `좋아한다고 했어요` means "I/someone said they like it." Other common verbs include `이야기하다 (to tell/talk)`, often shortened to `얘기하다`, `듣다 (to hear)`, and `외치다 (to shout)`. The instructor also introduces `그러다`, an informal but common verb used in spoken conversation to mean "to say" or "to be like that," conjugated as `그랬어요` in the past tense.

---

### Beyond Speaking: Trusting and Promising
The quoting form is not limited to speech; it is used with verbs of cognition and commitment. `믿다 (to trust/believe)` uses the quoting form to express "believing that" something is true. The instructor provides the example:
> **알아서 잘할 거라고 믿어요.** / I trust that you will figure it out and do a good job.

Similarly, `약속하다 (to promise)` can be used with the quoting form to mean "promising that" a certain statement is true or will happen.
> **다시 오겠다고 약속할게요.** / I will promise that I will come here again.

The instructor notes a distinction between using the quoting form and the `~기로` form with `약속하다`. While `~기로 약속하다` means "to promise to do" something (focusing on the decision), the quoting form `~다고 약속하다` carries the nuance of "promising that" a specific fact or future event will occur.

---

### Naming and Calling with 부르다
The verb `부르다 (to call)` uses a specific version of the quoting form to identify people or things by a name or title. Because this involves saying that "A is B," it utilizes the `이다` quoting forms `이라고` or `라고`. The structure is `[Noun] 이라고/라고 부르다`.
> **형이라고 부를게요.** / I will call you "older brother."
> **사람들은 저 원숭이를 왕자라고 불러요.** / People call that monkey "Prince."

---

### Advanced Verbs and Practical Examples
The instructor lists several other verbs that commonly utilize the quoting form to translate to "that" clauses in English. These include `예상하다 (to expect/forecast)`, `상상하다 (to imagine)`, `경고하다 (to warn)`, `기대하다 (to look forward to/expect)`, `판단하다 (to judge)`, `설명하다 (to explain)`, `요청하다 (to request)`, `발표하다 (to announce)`, and `주장하다 (to claim)`.

To illustrate these, the instructor provides complex sentences. To express an imagined scenario, one might say:
> **지구에 혼자 남았다고 상상해 보세요.** / Try imagining that you were left alone on the earth.

For professional or medical explanations:
> **수술 없이는 불가능하다고 설명해 드렸어요.** / I explained to them that it is impossible without surgery.

The instructor also clarifies that while indirect quotes are standard, the same forms can be used for direct quotes by quoting the exact sentence spoken, as seen in a judgment context:
> **이건 누구의 잘못도 아니다라고 판단했어요.** / They judged, "This is no one's mistake."

Finally, an example of a formal announcement is given:
> **조금 전에 바다에서 새로운 생명체를 발견했다고 발표했어요.** / They announced a little while ago that they discovered a new life form in the ocean.
