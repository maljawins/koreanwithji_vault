### Direct vs. Indirect Quotation
Direct quotation involves reporting someone's exact words using quotation marks. In Korean, the particle `라고` is attached to the quoted sentence, followed by a verb of saying like `말하다 (to say)` or simply `하다`. For example, "Jisoo said, 'I am Korean'" becomes `지수가 "저는 한국인이에요" 라고 (말)했어요`. The instructor notes that `말` in `말하다` is often omitted in speech, leaving just `했어요`.

Indirect quotation, on the other hand, embeds the meaning of the statement without necessarily using the exact words. This is equivalent to the English "that" clause (e.g., "Jisoo said that she is Korean"). In Korean, this requires changing the sentence ending to `-다고` or `(이)라고` depending on the word type and tense.

---

### Present Tense Indirect Quotation
The rules for quoting present tense statements vary significantly depending on whether the quoted word is an action verb, an adjective, or the copula `이다`.

For action verbs, the suffix `ㄴ/는` is added to the verb stem before `다고`. If the stem ends in a vowel, use `ㄴ`; if it ends in a consonant, use `는`.
> **저는 지수가 매일 한국어를 공부한다고 했어요.** / I said that Jisoo studies Korean every day.
> **엄마는 약을 매일 먹는다고 했어요.** / Mom said she takes medicine every day.

For descriptive verbs (adjectives), no extra suffix is needed. The stem is attached directly to `다고`.
> **엄마는 영어가 어렵다고 했어요.** / Mom said that English is difficult.
> **저는 지수가 착하다고 생각해요.** / I think that Jisoo is nice.

For the copula `이다 (to be)`, the form becomes `라고` or `이라고`. If the preceding noun ends in a vowel, use `라고`; if it ends in a consonant, use `이라고`.
> **지수가 한국인이라고 했어요.** / Jisoo said she is Korean.
> **저는 심효정이라고 해요.** / I am called Sim Hyo-jeong (My name is Sim Hyo-jeong).

---

### Quoting Negated Sentences
When quoting a negated sentence in the present tense, the rules follow the category of the base verb or adjective. If using the `안` prefix, the rule for the following verb/adjective applies. If using the `-지 않다` form, the `-지 않다` part itself determines the rule. Since `않다` functions as a verb when negating verbs and as an adjective when negating adjectives, the quotation suffixes follow accordingly.
- Negated Verb: `안 좋아한다고` or `좋아하지 않는다고`.
- Negated Adjective: `안 춥다고` or `춥지 않다고`.
- Negated `이다`: The negation of `이다` is `아니다`. Since `아니` ends in a vowel, it always takes `라고`, resulting in `아니라고`.

---

### Past Tense Indirect Quotation
Quoting the past tense is simpler because verbs, adjectives, and `이다` all follow the same pattern: conjugate the word into its past tense form (`았/었/였`) and add `다고`.
> **엄마는 아빠가 잠이 들었다고 했어요.** / Mom said that Dad had fallen asleep.
> **제 친구가 어제 안 추웠다고 했어요.** / My friend said it wasn't cold yesterday.
> **지수 아빠가 변호사였다고 들었어요.** / I heard Jisoo's dad was a lawyer.

---

### Future Tense Indirect Quotation
The future tense in indirect quotations typically uses the `-(으)ㄹ 거라고` form. This is derived from the future structure `-(으)ㄹ 것이다`.
> **지수가 공부를 열심히 할 거라고 했어요.** / Jisoo said she will study hard.
> **내일 비가 올 거라고 했어요.** / I said it might rain tomorrow.

The instructor notes a nuance regarding interchanging future and present tense in quotations. When the statement expresses a plan or determination, `-(으)ㄹ 거라고` and the present tense `-ㄴ/는다고` are often interchangeable. However, when making a prediction (e.g., "I think it will rain"), only the future form `-(으)ㄹ 거라고` is appropriate.

---

### Irregular Verbs in Quotation
Specific attention is given to irregular stems when applying quotation rules:
- **ㄹ Irregular:** For verbs like `살다 (to live)`, the `ㄹ` is dropped and replaced with `ㄴ` in the present tense quotation, resulting in `산다고`. In the future tense, it remains `살 거라고`.
- **ㄷ Irregular:** For verbs like `듣다 (to listen)` or `걷다 (to walk)`, the `ㄷ` changes to `ㄹ` only in the future tense (`들을 거라고`, `걸을 거라고`). In the present tense, they follow regular rules (`듣는다고`, `걷는다고`).
- **있다/없다:** Words ending in `있다` or `없다` (including present progressive `-고 있다` and the "want to" form `-고 싶다`) follow the adjective rule in quotations, meaning they take `다고` directly without `ㄴ/는`. For example, `공부하고 있다고` or `가고 싶다고`.
