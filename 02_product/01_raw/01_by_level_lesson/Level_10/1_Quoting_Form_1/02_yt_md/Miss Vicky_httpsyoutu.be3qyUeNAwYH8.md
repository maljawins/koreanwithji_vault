## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson covers three advanced forms of indirect quotation in Korean: quoting questions using `-냐고`, quoting commands or imperatives using `-(으)라고`, and quoting suggestions or invitations using `-자고`. The instructor builds upon the previous lesson on plain sentence quotations (`-다고` and `-이라고`) to show how different sentence types require specific markers when reported indirectly.

The instructor uses a live-stream format to engage with students, providing example sentences that require conjugation based on tense (present, past, future) and noun endings (받침 vs. no 받침). A significant portion of the lesson is dedicated to distinguishing between the indirect quotation `-냐고` and the indirect question marker `-는지`, explaining the specific verbs that typically follow each form.

---

### Indirect Quotation for Questions: -냐고

The marker `-냐고` is used to indirectly quote a question that someone asked. It is almost always followed by verbs meaning "to ask," such as `묻다` or `물어보다`. Unlike plain sentence quotations, `-냐고` is remarkably consistent across different tenses.

In the present tense, you simply attach `-냐고` to the verb or adjective stem. For example, to quote the question "What are you doing?" you take the stem of `뭐 하다 (to do what)` and add the marker to get `뭐 하냐고`. The instructor notes that while `뭐 하냐고 물어봤어요 (I asked what she's doing)` is correct, it is often interchangeable with the indirect question form `뭐 하는지 물어봤어요`.

For the `이다 (to be)` verb with nouns, the rule depends on the final consonant. If the noun ends in a `받침`, you use `이냐고`, such as `한국인이냐고 (asked if they are Korean)`. If there is no `받침`, you use `냐고`, as in `친구냐고 (asked if they are a friend)`.

In the past tense, the marker is attached to the past tense stem (`-았/었-`). For example, "asked if I did well" becomes `잘 봤냐고`, and "asked why he didn't go" becomes `왜 안 갔냐고`. In the future tense, the marker follows the future stem `-(으)ㄹ 거`, resulting in forms like `먹을 거냐고 (asked if they will eat)` or `마실 거냐고 (asked what they will drink)`.

---

### Comparison: -냐고 vs. -는지

The instructor clarifies a common point of confusion between `-냐고` (indirect quotation) and `-는지` (indirect question). The primary difference lies in the verbs that follow them.

The `-는지` grammar indicates uncertainty and can be followed by a wide variety of verbs, including `모르다 (to not know)`, `알다 (to know)`, `궁금하다 (to be curious)`, and `헷갈리다 (to be confused)`. In contrast, `-냐고` specifically quotes what someone actually said or is asking, so it is strictly used with `묻다` or `물어보다`.

> **지수가 뭐 하는지 몰라요.** / I don't know what Jisu is doing. (Correct)
> **지수가 뭐 하냐고 몰라요.** / (Incorrect/Awkward)

However, when the main verb is `물어보다 (to ask)`, both forms are grammatically correct and often interchangeable: `뭐 하냐고 물어봤어요` and `뭐 하는지 물어봤어요` both effectively communicate "I asked what she is doing."

---

### Indirect Quotation for Commands: -(으)라고

To indirectly quote an imperative sentence (a command or a request), the marker `-(으)라고` is used. This form is typically followed by verbs like `하다 (to say)`, `시키다 (to make/order someone to do)`, `추천하다 (to recommend)`, or `명령하다 (to command)`.

Because you cannot command a past action or an adjective state in Korean grammar, this form is only used with present tense verbs. The conjugation depends on the `받침`:
- Verb stems ending in a consonant take `으라고`. Example: `믿다 (to trust)` becomes `믿으라고`.
- Verb stems ending in a vowel take `라고`. Example: `하다 (to do)` becomes `하라고`.

The instructor provides several practical examples. If a father tells his child to take their time, it is reported as `천천히 하라고 했어요`. If a teacher tells students to study, it is `공부하라고 했어요`. To quote a negative command ("Don't do..."), the imperative negation `말다` is used. For instance, "The teacher told us not to chat" becomes `떠들지 말라고 했어요`. Note that `말다` is an irregular verb, so it becomes `말라고` rather than `말으라고`.

---

### Indirect Quotation for Suggestions: -자고

The final grammar point is `-자고`, used to indirectly quote a suggestion or invitation (the "let's" form). This corresponds to the plain style (반말) ending `-자`. Like the command form, it is only used with present tense verbs because suggestions apply to future actions, not past states or adjectives.

When quoting a suggestion, you attach `-자고` directly to the verb stem, followed by `하다` or `제안하다 (to suggest)`. This form is used regardless of the social status of the person being quoted; even if you are quoting a suggestion made to a grandfather, the internal quoted part uses `-자고`.

Examples include:
> **아빠한테 등산 가자고 했어요.** / I suggested to my dad that we go hiking.
> **친구한테 영화 보러 가자고 했어요.** / I suggested to my friend that we go see a movie.
> **동생한테 이제 그만 싸우자고 했어요.** / I told my younger sibling let's stop fighting now.

The instructor notes that `그만 싸우자고 (let's stop fighting)` can also be expressed using the negative suggestion form: `싸우지 말자고 했어요`.
