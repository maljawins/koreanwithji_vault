## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the indirect quotation markers in Korean: **-다고 하다**, **-는/ㄴ다고 하다**, and **-(이)라고 하다**. These structures are essential for intermediate learners as they allow for the reporting of information heard from others, the expression of thoughts and opinions, and the relaying of general facts. The instructor breaks down the usage based on parts of speech—adjectives, verbs, and nouns—and provides extensive practice through sentence transformation and dialogue.

The learning arc begins with the fundamental concept of information transfer: taking a statement from a speaker and relaying it to a third party. The instructor emphasizes that when using these forms, the subject of the quoted sentence is typically someone other than the speaker. The lesson concludes with a role-play exercise and a comprehensive summary of all conjugation rules and example sentences covered.

---

### Usage and Meaning of Indirect Quotations

The primary function of these markers is to deliver content heard from another person. For example, if a teacher says, "Next week is vacation," the student relays this by saying, "The teacher says next week is vacation." Visually, the instructor uses a chalkboard to show this transition:
> **선생님 say: "다음 주가 방학이에요."** / Teacher says: "Next week is vacation."
> **나 heard & say: "다음 주가 방학이라고 해요."** / I heard and say: "They say next week is vacation."

A critical grammatical point is that the subject of the quoted sentence is not the speaker (I), but another person. For instance, if Ming says, "I don't eat cucumbers," the reporter says, "Ming says he doesn't eat cucumbers."
> **밍 씨는 오이를 안 먹는다고 해요.** / Ming says he doesn't eat cucumbers.

Furthermore, the verb **하다 (to do/say)** in these structures can be replaced by other verbs depending on the nuance. The instructor lists several variations: **-다고 말하다 (to say that...)**, **-다고 그랬다 (said that...)**, **-다고 생각하다 (to think that...)** for opinions, and **-다고 들었다 (heard that...)** for relayed information.

---

### Conjugation Rules by Part of Speech

The instructor provides a clear visual chart on the chalkboard to demonstrate how the quotation marker changes based on the word class and the presence of a final consonant (받침).

For adjectives (A), the form is always **-다고 하다**, regardless of whether there is a final consonant or not.
- **많다 (to be many) → 많다고 하다**
- **크다 (to be big) → 크다고 하다**

For verbs (V) in the present tense, the marker depends on the final consonant. If there is a final consonant, use **-는다고 하다**. If there is no final consonant, use **-ㄴ다고 하다**.
- **먹다 (to eat) → 먹는다고 하다**
- **가다 (to go) → 간다고 하다**

For nouns (N), the marker is **-이라고 하다** if there is a final consonant, and **-라고 하다** if there is no final consonant.
- **방학 (vacation) → 방학이라고 하다**
- **휴가 (vacation/holiday) → 휴가라고 하다**

The instructor also notes that for past tense verbs, the form **-았/었다고 하다** is used, as seen in the example **학생이었다 (was a student) → 학생이었다고 하다**.

### Practice and Application

The lesson includes a series of transformation exercises where the viewer must convert direct speech into indirect quotations. These exercises cover various contexts, including news reports, teacher's instructions, and casual conversations.

- **News report:** "It will rain starting tomorrow" becomes **내일부터 비가 온다고 해요**.
- **Teacher's statement:** "This exam is difficult" becomes **이번 시험은 어렵다고 해요**.
- **Personal preference:** "The sport I like is tennis" becomes **테니스를 좋아한다고 해요**.
- **Past events:** "O-yeon already went home" becomes **오연 씨는 집에 갔다고 해요**.

A dialogue practice section follows, featuring a conversation about a person named Seong-eun. The dialogue demonstrates how these forms appear in natural speech:
> **A: 성은 씨는 언제 와요?** / When is Seong-eun coming?
> **B: 방금 출발했다고 문자가 왔어요.** / A text came saying she just departed.
> **A: 성은 씨 집에서 여기까지 얼마나 걸려요?** / How long does it take from Seong-eun's house to here?
> **B: 20분쯤 걸린다고 들었어요.** / I heard it takes about 20 minutes.

The instructor concludes by encouraging students to practice by writing their own sentences in the comments, using the phrase **여러분은 열심히 공부한다고 생각해요 (I think you all study hard)** as a final example.
