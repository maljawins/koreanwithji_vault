## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces a more natural alternative to the standard phrase 뭐라고요? (What did you say?) when asking for clarification or confirmation. While 뭐라고요? is a perfectly acceptable way to ask someone to repeat themselves, repeating it constantly can sound repetitive or unnatural. Instead, Korean speakers often repeat the specific information they heard back to the speaker using shortened quoting forms. This technique allows the listener to confirm exactly what they think they heard while sounding more like a native speaker. The lesson breaks down how to transform different types of sentences—statements, questions, suggestions, and commands—into these conversational quotes.

---

### Quoting Statements with ~ㄴ/는다고요?

To repeat a statement back to someone as a question (e.g., "You say you like anchovies?"), you must first convert the verb into its plain form. The instructor demonstrates this on screen with the example 멸치를 좋아해요 (I like anchovies). To quote this, the verb 좋아하다 is changed to its plain form 좋아한다. The rule for the plain form is simple: if the verb stem ends in a vowel, attach ㄴ다; if it ends in a consonant, attach 는다. For example, 받다 (to receive) becomes 받는다.

Once the verb is in the plain form, you simply attach the quoting marker 고. To make it polite and turn it into a question for confirmation, add 요 and use a rising intonation. The instructor describes the 고 as "floating quotation marks" that signal to the listener that you are repeating their words.

> **멸치를 좋아한다고요?** / You say you like anchovies?

---

### Quoting Questions with ~냐고요?

Quoting a question that someone else asked is even simpler than quoting a statement because you do not need the plain form. Instead, you use the question ending 냐. This is attached directly to the verb stem, followed by the quoting marker 고 and the polite ending 요.

The instructor uses the example of someone asking "Do you like anchovies?" (멸치를 좋아해요?). If you want to confirm that they are indeed asking you that, you take the stem of 좋아하다 (which is 좋아하-), attach 냐, and then 고요.

> **멸치를 좋아하냐고요?** / Are you asking if I like anchovies?

This form instantly signals that you are quoting their question back to them rather than asking a new question yourself.

---

### Quoting Suggestions with ~자고요?

When someone makes a suggestion using a "let's" form (e.g., "Let's go"), you quote it using the 자 ending. Even if the original suggestion was made politely, the quoted version uses the 자 form attached to the verb stem, followed by 고요.

The instructor illustrates this with the verb 가다 (to go). If someone suggests 가요 (Let's go), you repeat it back for confirmation by taking the stem 가-, adding 자, and then 고요.

> **가자고요?** / You're saying "let's go"?

---

### Quoting Commands with ~(으)라고요?

To quote a command or a request (e.g., "Eat the anchovies"), you use the command quoting ending (으)라. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you use 으라고요; if it ends in a vowel, you use 라고요.

Using the example 멸치를 먹어요 (Eat the anchovies), the instructor takes the stem of 먹다 (which is 먹-), attaches 으라 because it ends in a consonant, and adds 고요.

> **멸치를 먹으라고요?** / You're telling me to eat anchovies?

---

### The Special Case of 주다 (To Give)

The instructor highlights a critical exception when quoting commands involving the verb 주다 (to give). There are two different ways to quote "Give it," depending on who is receiving the item.

If the person speaking is asking for something for themselves (e.g., "Give it to me"), you must change 주다 to 달라고요 when quoting. This is the most common scenario. However, if the person is asking you to give something to a third party (e.g., "Give it to Chul-soo"), you use the standard command quote form 주라고요.

- **달라고요?** / You're asking me to give it to *you*?
- **주라고요?** / You're telling me to give it to *someone else*?

---

### The Simplest Method: Noun + (이)요?

If you understood the entire sentence except for one specific word, or if you just want to confirm a single noun, there is an even faster way to quote. You can simply repeat the noun and attach 이요 (after a consonant) or 요 (after a vowel).

The instructor demonstrates that if someone asks if you like anchovies, and you just want to clarify the word "anchovies," you can simply say:

> **멸치요?** / Anchovies?

This is a very common and efficient way to ask for clarification on a specific piece of information without needing to reconstruct the entire quoted sentence.
