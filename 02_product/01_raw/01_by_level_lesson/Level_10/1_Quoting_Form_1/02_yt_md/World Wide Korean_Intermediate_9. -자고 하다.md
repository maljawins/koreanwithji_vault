## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the intermediate Korean grammar structure **-자고 하다**, which is used for indirect quotations of suggestions or proposals. When a speaker wants to report a suggestion made by someone else (or themselves) to a third party, they use this form. It is the quoted version of the "let's" ending (청유형), such as **-자** or **-(으)ㅂ시다**.

The instructor guides the learner through the functional definition, the conjugation rules, and extensive practice exercises. The lesson concludes with a dialogue practice and a summary of all example sentences covered. By the end of the video, students should be able to naturally relay invitations and suggestions made in various social contexts.

---

### Function and Usage of -자고 하다
The primary function of **-자고 하다** is to deliver or report a suggestion (권유) or a proposal (제안) to another person. The instructor provides a situational example on a chalkboard graphic: a person named Ming says, "비가 오니까 내일 만납시다" (Since it's raining, let's meet tomorrow). To report this suggestion to another person named Mint, the speaker says:

> **밍 씨가 비가 오니까 내일 만나자고 했어요.** / Ming said let's meet tomorrow since it's raining.

A key stylistic point noted is that **-자고 하다** can be shortened to **-자하다** in spoken or written Korean without changing the meaning. For example, "만나자고 했어요" is equivalent to "만나자 했어요," and "켜자고 했어요" is equivalent to "켜자 했어요."

---

### Conjugation Rules
The conjugation for **-자고 하다** is straightforward because it only applies to verbs. A conjugation table displayed on screen clarifies that this form cannot be used with adjectives (형용사) or nouns (명사). 

For verbs, the rule is consistent regardless of whether the verb stem ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel. You simply remove **다** from the dictionary form of the verb and attach **-자고 하다**. The instructor demonstrates this with a series of practice transformations:
- **먹다** (to eat) becomes **먹자고 하다**
- **가다** (to go) becomes **가자고 하다**
- **듣다** (to listen/hear) becomes **듣자고 하다**
- **놀다** (to play) becomes **놀자고 하다**
- **일하다** (to work) becomes **일하자고 하다**

### Sentence Practice: Reporting Suggestions
The instructor provides several exercises where direct suggestions are converted into indirect quotations using **-자고 하다**. These examples cover various speech levels and social relationships:

1. **Direct:** "내일 시험이 끝나고 영화 보러 가자." (Let's go see a movie after the exam tomorrow.)
   **Reported:** 친구가 내일 시험이 끝나고 영화 보러 가자고 했어요.
2. **Direct:** "오늘부터 운동합시다." (Let's exercise starting today.)
   **Reported:** 친구가 오늘부터 운동하자고 했어요.
3. **Direct:** "12월에 결혼하자!" (Let's get married in December!)
   **Reported:** 남자친구가 12월에 결혼하자고 했어요.
4. **Direct:** "언니, 오늘 저녁에 피자 먹자!" (Older sister, let's eat pizza tonight!)
   **Reported:** 동생이 저녁에 피자를 먹자고 했어요.

Special attention is given to negative suggestions using **-지 말자** (let's not). When quoting a suggestion not to do something, the form becomes **-지 말자고 하다**:
- **Direct:** "내일은 지각하지 말자. 학교에 일찍 오자." (Let's not be late tomorrow. Let's come to school early.)
- **Reported:** 내일은 지각하지 말자고 했어요. 일찍 오자고 했어요.

### Dialogue and Summary
The lesson includes a reading practice where the instructor (A) and the student (B) engage in a dialogue. The dialogue reinforces the use of the grammar in a conversational flow:
- **A:** 밍 씨가 뭐라고 했어요? (What did Ming say?)
- **B:** 이제 한국 노래를 듣자고 했어요. (He suggested we listen to Korean songs now.)
- **A:** 그럴까요? (Shall we?)
- **B:** 그리고 조금 쌀쌀하니 에어컨을 끄자 했어요. (And he said let's turn off the air conditioner since it's a bit chilly.)

The video ends with a comprehensive summary slide listing all the key sentences taught, emphasizing that the form is used for conveying suggestions and proposals regardless of the presence of a final consonant in the verb stem.
