## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the mechanics of indirect quotation in Korean. Building upon the "plain form" covered in previous lessons, the instructor explains how to report what someone else said, asked, suggested, or commanded. The core of the lesson is divided into four distinct quoting structures, each corresponding to a different type of original sentence.

The instructor emphasizes that the choice of quoting ending depends entirely on the intent of the original speech. Whether a person made a simple statement, asked a question, made a suggestion (let's...), or gave a command (do it...), the verb must be conjugated into a specific quoting form before attaching the reporting verb. Throughout the video, the instructor provides detailed conjugation rules, including irregular verb handlings and common contractions used in daily conversation.

---

### Quoting Statements with 고 하다
The basic form for quoting a statement involves taking the plain form of a verb and attaching the particle **고** followed by the verb **말하다 (to speak)**. In practice, **말하다** is almost always shortened to **하다**. For example, to say "I said I like dramas," you conjugate **좋아하다** into its plain form **좋아한다**, add **고**, and then **했어요**, resulting in **저는 드라마를 좋아한다고 했어요**.

There are specific rules for the copula verbs **이다 (to be)** and **아니다 (to not be)**. **이다** becomes **이라고** after a consonant and **라고** after a vowel. **아니다** always becomes **아니라고**. An example of this is **저도 외국인이라고 했어요 (I said I'm also a foreigner)**. Furthermore, the entire **하다** verb can be omitted in casual speech or for brevity, leaving the sentence to end in **고요** (polite) or just **고** (casual). This is often used to repeat oneself or clarify a statement, such as **시간이 없다고요? (Did you say you don't have time?)**.

---

### Introducing Yourself with 이라고 해요
A practical application of the statement quoting form is a common way to introduce one's name. By using the structure **저는 [Name] (이)라고 해요**, you are literally saying "As for me, I am called [Name]." You attach **이라고** if the name ends in a consonant and **라고** if it ends in a vowel. Examples include **저는 석진이라고 해요 (My name is Seokjin)** and **저는 윤기라고 해요 (My name is Yoongi)**.

---

### Quoting Questions with 냐고 하다
When quoting a question, the verb stem is attached to the ending **냐고** followed by **하다** or **물어보다 (to ask)**. For example, "Did you ask if I play piano well?" becomes **제가 피아노를 잘 치냐고 물어봤어요?**. 

This form has several conjugation rules for irregular stems. If a verb stem ends in **ㄹ**, the **ㄹ** is removed before adding **냐고**, such as **살다 (to live)** becoming **사냐고**. For **ㅂ** irregular verbs, the **ㅂ** is removed and replaced with **우** before adding **냐고**, so **맵다 (to be spicy)** becomes **매우냐고**. For the verb **이다**, it becomes **이냐고** after a consonant and **냐고** after a vowel.

---

### Quoting Commands with (으)라고 하다
To quote a command or an instruction, you attach **으라고** to verb stems ending in a consonant or **라고** to stems ending in a vowel. This is followed by **하다**. For instance, "I told them to turn off the light" is expressed as **불을 끄라고 했어요**. 

Special stem rules apply here as well: if a stem ends in **ㄹ**, you simply attach **라고** without removing the **ㄹ**, so **살다** becomes **살라고**. For **ㅂ** irregulars, the **ㅂ** is replaced with **우** before adding **라고**, turning **굽다 (to bake)** into **구우라고**. To quote a negative command ("I said don't..."), you use the form **지 말라고**, based on the negative imperative **지 말다**.

---

### Quoting Suggestions with 자고 하다
When quoting a suggestion—something originally phrased as "let's"—the ending **자고** is attached directly to the verb stem, followed by **하다**. This form is used for reporting invitations or mutual plans. For example, **떡볶이를 먹으러 가자고요?** translates to "Did you say, let's go eat ddeokbokki?". Another example provided is **그냥 거짓말을 하자고 (I said, let's just lie)**.

---

### Topic and Subject Markers in Quotes
The instructor clarifies a common pattern regarding particles in quoted sentences. Usually, the person who is speaking (the "quoter") is marked with the topic marker **은/는**, while the person or thing the quote is about is marked with the subject marker **이/가**. While not a strict grammatical rule, this is the most natural way to frame these sentences. For example, **저는 김치가 맛있다고 했어요 (I said that kimchi is delicious)** sounds much more natural than using the topic marker on "kimchi" within that specific context.

---

### Special Case: 주라고 vs 달라고
A critical distinction exists when quoting the verb **주다 (to give)** in a command. The choice depends on who is receiving the item. You use **달라고** when the speaker is the one receiving the item ("He told me to give it to *him*"). You use **주라고** when the speaker is telling someone to give an item to a third party ("I told him to give it to *Minwoo*"). This also applies to auxiliary uses of the verb, such as **해 달라고 했어요 (I asked them to do it for me)**.
