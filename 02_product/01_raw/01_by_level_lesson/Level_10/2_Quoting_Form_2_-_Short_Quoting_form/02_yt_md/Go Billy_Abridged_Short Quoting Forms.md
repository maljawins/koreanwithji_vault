## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the concept of shortened quoting forms in Korean. While learners may already be familiar with the standard indirect quoting forms used for statements, questions, commands, and suggestions, this lesson focuses on the more concise versions used frequently in spoken conversation. These shortened forms carry the exact same meaning as their full counterparts but allow for faster, more natural speech. The lesson systematically breaks down how to transform each of the four main quoting types into their short forms across various tenses.

---

### Statement Quoting Forms: ~대(요) and ~래(요)

The most basic indirect quote is the statement. In the standard form, a verb like `하다 (to do)` becomes `한다고 해요 (says that [someone] is doing)`. To shorten this, the instructor demonstrates that the `~고 해` portion is compressed. For verbs ending in a vowel, you attach `~ㄴ대(요)`, and for those ending in a consonant, you use `~는대(요)`. For example, `먹는다 (to eat - plain form)` becomes `먹는대요 (says [someone] eats)`. The instructor provides the example sentence:

> **영수는 안 간대요.** / Young-su says he's not going.

This is a shortened version of `영수는 안 간다고 해요`. The instructor notes that the present tense can also imply future intent. For the past tense, the full form `~했다고 해요` shortens to `~했대요`. For the future tense, `~할 거라고 해요` shortens to `~할 거래요`. 

When quoting the verb `이다 (to be)`, the standard `~이라고 해요` (after a consonant) or `~라고 해요` (after a vowel) shortens to `~이래요` or `~래요`. The instructor illustrates this with a past tense example:

> **할아버지가 백년 전에 지은 집이랬어요.** / I said it was a house grandfather built a hundred years ago.

---

### Command Quoting Forms: ~래(요)

When quoting a command, such as "I said do it" or "He told me to come," the standard form uses the verb stem plus `~(으)라고 하다`. The shortened version of this is `~래(요)` or `~으래(요)`. The instructor demonstrates how `빨리 나오라고 해요` (says to come out quickly) becomes:

> **빨리 나오래요.** / [Someone] says to come out quickly.

Another example shows the use of this form for general instructions:

> **모두 조용히 하래요.** / I told everyone to be quiet. / [Someone] said for everyone to be quiet.

---

### Question Quoting Forms: ~냬(요)

To quote a question, the standard form is verb stem + `~냐고 하다`. This is often used when repeating a question someone else missed or emphasizing a question you already asked. The shortened version is `~냬(요)`. The instructor explains that this is a contraction of `~냐고 해요`. An example provided is:

> **왜 사냬요.** / [Someone] is asking why you are buying it.

This form is particularly useful for adding emphasis or showing surprise, as seen in the following example:

> **추운데 아이스크림은 왜 먹냬요.** / [Someone] is asking why you're eating ice cream when it's cold.

---

### Suggestion Quoting Forms: ~재(요)

The final main category is suggestions, which in the standard form use `~자고 하다` (let's...). This shortens to `~재(요)`. The instructor explains that this is used to report what someone suggested doing together. For example, `가자고 해요` (says let's go) becomes `가재요`. A full example sentence is provided:

> **비가 오니까 집에 있재요.** / Because it's raining, [someone] says let's stay home.

---

### Special Case: The Verb 주다 (To Give)

The instructor highlights a critical nuance when quoting commands involving the verb `주다 (to give)`. There are two ways to quote "told [someone] to give," depending on who is receiving the item. 

If the speaker is the one who will receive the item, you must use `달라고 하다`, which shortens to `달래요`. If the speaker is asking for the item to be given to a third party, you use `주라고 하다`, which shortens to `주래요`. The instructor provides an example of a younger sibling asking for candy for themselves:

> **동생이 사탕을 사달래요.** / My younger sibling said to buy them candy.

If the sibling were asking for candy to be given to someone else, `사주래요` would be used instead.
