## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form 다기보다는, a relatively long but highly useful expression used to provide a more accurate description of a situation. The lesson focuses on breaking down the form into its constituent parts—quoting, nominalization, and comparison—to show that it is not a random string of syllables but a logical construction. By the end of the lesson, students should understand how to use this form to say that "B is a better way to describe something than A."

The learning arc begins with the mechanical conjugation rules for verbs, adjectives, and the copula 이다. It then moves into a deep dive of the literal meaning ("more than saying...") before transitioning to natural English translations like "rather than" or "it's more that." The final portion of the video is dedicated to practical application through a series of example sentences covering various tenses and social contexts.

---

### Conjugation and Structure

To conjugate this form, one takes the plain form of a verb or adjective and attaches 기보다는. Since the plain form already ends in 다, the resulting combination is the full 다기보다는. The instructor demonstrates this with the verb 하다 (to do), which becomes 한다 in the plain form, resulting in 한다기보다는. For the verb 먹다 (to eat), the plain form is 먹는다, which becomes 먹는다기보다는. It is important to note that there are no spaces within this entire grammar block.

The instructor explains that this form is actually an abbreviation of a longer quoting structure. It consists of the quoting particle 고, the verb 하다 (used here as "to say"), the nominalization marker 기, the comparison marker 보다 (meaning "than" or "more than"), and the topic marker 는. For example, 먹는다고 하기보다는 literally means "more than the act of saying that (someone) eats." In conversational Korean, the topic marker 는 is optional and can even be shortened to a simple 니은 (ㄴ) resulting in 보단, though the full form is required for formal writing or tests.

---

### The Copula Exception: 이라기보다는

While the form generally follows the plain form, the copula 이다 (to be) follows the standard quoting rules for nouns. Instead of 이다기보다는, it becomes 이라기보다는 after a consonant or 라기보다는 after a vowel. This change to 라 is a common feature in Korean quoting forms. The instructor emphasizes that if a student already understands how 이다 changes in other quoting structures, this will not feel like an exception at all.

---

### Meaning and Usage

The literal meaning of the form is "more than saying that [A] happens/is true." However, in natural English, it is best translated as "rather than," "it's not so much that A, but B," or "it's more of B than A." The core function is to signal that while "A" might be a possible description, "B" is a much more accurate or appropriate way to describe the situation.

Because it is based on a quoting form, it is highly flexible. It can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs (adjectives). Furthermore, it can be used in different tenses. For the past tense, one simply uses the past tense plain form, such as 했다기보다는 (rather than saying I did it) or 이었다기보다는 (rather than saying it was).

### Example Sentences

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the nuance of "better description":

> **철수는 친구라기보다는 라이벌에 가까워요.** / Chul-soo is more of a rival than a friend. (Literally: More than saying Chul-soo is a friend, he is close to a rival.)

> **그건 실수라기보다는 습관인 것 같아요.** / I think it's more of a habit than a mistake.

> **커피를 좋아한다기보다는 커피향을 좋아해요.** / It's not so much that I like coffee, but rather I like the smell of coffee.

> **운동을 좋아해서라기보다는 건강을 위해서 하고 있어요.** / I'm doing it for my health rather than because I like it. (Note: here it is attached to the 서 causal form).

> **그 강아지는 사람을 싫어한다기보다는 무서워해요.** / That dog doesn't dislike people so much as it is afraid of them.

> **사랑이었다기보다는 집착이었던 것 같아요.** / I think it was more of an obsession than love.

> **부모님이 거의 매일 오셔서 제 집이라기보다는 부모님 집에 가까워요.** / Because my parents come almost every day, it's closer to being my parents' house than my house.
