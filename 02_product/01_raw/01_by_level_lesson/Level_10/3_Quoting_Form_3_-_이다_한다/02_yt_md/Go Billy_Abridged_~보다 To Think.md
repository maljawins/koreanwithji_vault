## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces a specific way to express the concept of "thinking" or "judging" using the verb 보다. While most learners are familiar with 생각하다 (to think) or 것 같다 (it seems like), 보다 offers a more direct and authoritative nuance, often translated as "the way I see it" or "I judge that." This form is highly common in natural Korean speech but is frequently overlooked in introductory textbooks.

The lesson follows a logical progression: first, a comprehensive review of the "plain form" and basic quoting mechanics (고), as these are the grammatical prerequisites for using 보다 in this context. Second, a comparison between 생각하다, 것 같다, and 보다 to clarify when each should be used. Finally, the instructor provides various example sentences demonstrating 보다 in different tenses and honorific levels to show how it functions as a tool for making professional or personal "calls" and judgments.

---

### Prerequisites: The Plain Form and Basic Quotes
Before using 보다 to mean "to think," one must master the plain quoting form. The instructor demonstrates on a whiteboard that for action verbs (AVS), the conjugation depends on the final letter of the stem: if it ends in a consonant, attach 는다; if it ends in a vowel, attach 니은다 (ㄴ다). A specific exception is noted for stems ending in ㄹ, where the ㄹ is removed before adding 니은다. Examples provided include 하다 becoming 한다, 먹다 becoming 먹는다, and 살다 becoming 산다. For descriptive verbs (DVS), the plain form is simply the verb stem plus 다, such as 춥다 remaining 춥다.

To turn these plain forms into quotes, the particle 고 is attached after 다. This results in forms like 한다고 or 춥다고. The instructor highlights the irregular behavior of 이다 (to be) and 아니다 (to not be). 이다 becomes 이라고 after a vowel or 이다고 after a consonant, while 아니다 becomes 아니라고. For past tense, the verb is conjugated normally and then followed by 다고, such as 했다고. For the future tense, one can use 겠다고 or the more common 거시다 ending, which becomes 거라고 (shortened from 것이라고).

---

### Comparing 생각하다, 것 같다, and 보다
The instructor distinguishes between three ways to say "I think." 생각하다 is described as expressing thoughts or opinions derived from internal factors—what is happening inside one's brain regardless of external evidence. 것 같다 is used for external factors, meaning "it seems like" or "it appears to be" based on what is observed. 

보다, the focus of this lesson, literally means "to see" or "to look," but when used after a plain quote, it translates to "the way I see it." It is used when a speaker judges or evaluates a situation. Unlike 생각하다, which is more subjective and personal, 보다 is more direct and implies the speaker is making a definitive "call" or judgment based on their evaluation of the facts. It is not used for pure, subjective opinions but for assessments where the speaker feels they are likely correct.

---

### Using 보다 in Practice
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the nuance of judgment. In the sentence "저는 이게 좋은 기회가 될 거라고 봐요," the speaker isn't just thinking it's a good opportunity; they are judging the situation and making a call that it will be one. Another example, "이것보다 더 나은 방법을 찾아야 한다고 봐요," suggests a firm evaluation that a better method is necessary.

The form can also be used in questions, such as "우리가 정말 결승전까지 갈 거라고 보세요?" which asks for the listener's professional or calculated judgment on whether the team will reach the finals. In the past tense, "저는 확실하게 하는 게 낫다고 봤어요," it expresses a judgment made at a previous time. Finally, the instructor notes that 보다 can be used with the 는데요 ending to provide a soft background or slight disagreement, as in "저는 이 조건을 다 갖춘 사람이 없을 거라고 보는데요" (The way I see it, there won't be anyone who meets all these conditions).
