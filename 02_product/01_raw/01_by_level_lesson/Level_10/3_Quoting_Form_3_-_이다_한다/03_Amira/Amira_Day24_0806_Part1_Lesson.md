*   **Spelling and Vocabulary Corrections:**
    *   Ji corrects `오늘` (today), noting Amira had a spelling error.
    *   He corrects `하무하다` to `허무하다` (to be futile/empty).
    *   He explains that `짜증나다` (to be annoyed/frustrated) uses the same `나다` (to happen/occur) as other expressions she has learned.
    *   Ji explains the onomatopoeic origin of `깜빡` (blinking/flickering), used in `깜빡하다` (to forget momentarily). He likens it to a memory "turning on and off," like walking into a room and forgetting why you are there.
    *   He corrects `한 년` to `1년` (one year) when discussing duration.

*   **Directions and Geography:**
    *   Ji teaches the Korean order for cardinal directions: `동, 서, 남, 북` (East, West, South, North).
    *   He explains that while English says "Northeast," Korean flips the order to "East-North": `동북` (Northeast). Thus, Northeast Africa is `동북아프리카`.

*   **Nuance and Sentence Endings:**
    *   **Conjecture:** Ji explains that when using `아마` (maybe/probably), it is more natural to end the sentence with a conjecture form like `~것 같아요` (it seems like/I think).
    *   **Value/Character:** He introduces `인성` (human character/morals) and explains that `가치` (value) as a noun isn't always needed if the sentence already implies importance, e.g., `가족이랑 인성이 정말 중요해요` (Family and character are really important).
    *   **Connectives:** Ji suggests using `그리고` (and) instead of `그래서` (so/therefore) when the two clauses don't have a strong cause-and-effect relationship, such as missing family and learning to cook.
