## Teaching Points

The primary focus of this recording is the **Quoting Form (Indirect Quotation)**. Ji breaks this down into four functional categories, unified by the mnemonic **다냐자라 (Da-Nya-Ja-Ra)** + **고 (go)**.

*   **The Four Types of Quoting:**
    *   **Statements (-다고):** Used for "someone says that..." For present tense, it uses the plain form (ㄴ/는다고). For past tense, it uses -았/었다고.
    *   **Questions (-냐고):** Used for "someone asks if..." Attached directly to the verb stem.
    *   **Suggestions (-자고):** Used for "someone suggests let's..." (e.g., 밥을 같이 먹자고 했어요 / My friend suggested we eat together).
    *   **Commands (-라고):** Used for "someone tells/orders to..." (e.g., 하라고 시켰어요 / Ordered me to do it).

*   **Special Conjugations and Nuances:**
    *   **이다 (to be) and 아니다 (not to be):** These change to -(이)라고 and 아니라고 respectively in statements, mirroring the direct quotation form.
    *   **Future Tense:** The form -ㄹ/을 것이라고 is almost always contracted to -ㄹ/을 거라고 in speech.
    *   **Direct vs. Indirect:** Ji explains that direct quotation uses quotation marks and always attaches 라고 regardless of sentence type, whereas indirect quotation changes based on the "다냐자라" rules.
    *   **Quoting Thoughts:** The form can be used with 생각하다 (to think) to quote one's own internal monologue (e.g., 해야겠다고 생각했어요 / I thought "I should do it"). This is contrasted with -ㄹ/을 줄 알았다, which indicates an expectation or assumption (I thought/expected it would be so).

*   **The Special Case of 주다 (to give):**
    *   **달라고:** Used when the speaker asks for something for themselves (e.g., "Give it to me").
    *   **주라고:** Used when the speaker tells someone to give something to a third party (e.g., "Give it to him").

*   **Pragmatic Usages as Sentence Endings:**
    *   **As a Question:** 뭐라고요? (What did you say?) or 누구라고요? (Who did you say?).
    *   **For Emphasis/Annoyance:** Repeating information using -다냐자라 + 고/니까 to show the speaker is repeating themselves (e.g., 어디냐고! / I asked where you are!).

## Lesson Flow

Ji then transitions to the **Quoting Form lecture**. He introduces the concept of indirect quotation and the mnemonic "다냐자라." He explains that verbs following the '고' particle are usually "message" verbs like 말하다 (to say), 듣다 (to hear), 전하다 (to tell/convey), and 생각하다 (to think).

Amira asks a **clarifying question**: "If I wanted to ask 'What does frog mean?', do I use this form?" Ji explains that for a simple definition of a noun, the quoting form isn't used; instead, one would use a standard subject-definition structure. He notes that the quoting form is specifically for quoting *sentences* or *thoughts*.

Ji then goes through the **Grammar Rules** for each type:
1.  **Statements:** He explains the use of the plain form and highlights the formal self-introduction: "저는 아미라라고 합니다" (I am called Amira).
2.  **Questions:** He shows how -냐고 is attached to stems, using examples like "어디 가냐고 물어봤어요" (Asked where I was going).
3.  **Suggestions and Commands:** He uses the example of a wife telling a husband to come home early (오라고 했어요) and a friend suggesting a meal (먹자고 했어요).

The flow moves into **Special Cases**, specifically the distinction between 달라고 and 주라고. Ji uses a family scenario (an uncle giving money for a sibling's doll) to illustrate who is receiving the action. He also introduces the four versions of "Self" (지, 자기, 본인, 당신), explaining the social hierarchy and respect levels associated with each.

Finally, Ji covers **Special Usages**, such as using the quoting form to mean "even if" or "just because" (e.g., 힘들다고 포기하면 안 돼요 / Just because it's hard doesn't mean you should give up). The recording ends as Ji explains how the form becomes more aggressive when 고 is replaced with 니까.
