## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form ~다니, a versatile ending used to express a wide range of emotional reactions to a specific fact or situation. While it is most commonly associated with surprise or disbelief—often translated as "I can't believe..." or "To think that..."—it can also convey happiness, sadness, or simple contemplation. The lesson covers the basic construction, common exceptions, and the distinction between reacting to a direct observation versus reacting to something heard or read.

The learning arc begins with the simplest form of the grammar, moves into its usage as both a sentence connector and a sentence ending, and then explores more complex variations involving quoting forms. By the end of the lesson, students should understand how to use ~다니 to react to actions, states, and even questions or suggestions made by others.

---

### Basic Construction and Meaning
The grammar is formed by taking a verb stem and attaching ~다니. If used at the end of a sentence to be polite, the particle 요 can be added to form ~다니요. While it can end a sentence, it is even more frequently used in the middle of a sentence to provide the reason for a subsequent emotional reaction. The instructor points out that the 니 at the end is related to the "because" form, meaning the structure literally translates to "because [verb/fact], [reaction]."

The form works with both descriptive verbs (states) and action verbs. It can be used in the present tense, such as 하다니 (to think that [someone] does), or the past tense, such as 했다니 (to think that [someone] did). There are two primary exceptions involving the copula:
- **이다 (to be)** becomes 이라니.
- **아니다 (to not be)** becomes 아니라니.

---

### Expressing Reactions
The primary function of ~다니 is to show a reaction to something. Common translations include "I can't believe," "To think that," or "Wow, to think that." The instructor emphasizes that while surprise and disbelief are the most frequent emotions expressed, the form is flexible. It can express happiness, as seen in the example:
> **일 등석 비행기를 탈 수 있다니 너무 기뻐요.** / I’m so happy to think that I can fly first class.

In informal speech, the second half of the sentence (the reaction) is often omitted because the emotion is implied by the context. Simply saying **일 등석 비행기를 탈 수 있다니!** conveys the speaker's excitement or surprise without needing to explicitly state "I am happy."

Other examples of reactions include:
> **한국어를 혼자서 공부했다니 믿을 수가 없어요.** / I can't believe you studied Korean all by yourself.
> **우리가 처음 만난 게 1년 전이라니 시간이 정말 빠르네요.** / To think that it's been a year since we first met, time really flies.

---

### Reacting to Information (Quoting Forms)
A second variation of this grammar involves the quoting form. This is used specifically when reacting to something the speaker heard from someone else or read, rather than something they observed directly. This is a shortened version of ~다고 하니까 (because [someone] said...).

The instructor illustrates the difference using an "anchovy birthday cake" scenario. If you see someone eating the cake, you use the basic form: **그것을 먹다니** (To think you are eating that!). However, if you only heard that they eat such a cake, you use the quoting version: **그것을 먹는다니** (To hear that you eat that!).

This quoting version can adapt to different types of speech:
- **Statements:** ~ㄴ/는다고 becomes ~ㄴ/는다니.
- **Suggestions:** ~자고 becomes ~자니 (Reacting to "Let's do...").
- **Commands:** ~라고 becomes ~라니 (Reacting to being told to do something).
- **Questions:** ~냐고 becomes ~냐니 (Reacting to a question).

An example of reacting to a question is shown:
> **제가 만든 게 맞냐니요?** / Are you really asking if I'm the one who made this? (Implies: "Of course I made it!")

### Further Examples of Surprise and Disbelief
The instructor provides several more sentences to demonstrate the "I can't believe" nuance in various contexts:
> **매일 보는 사람의 이름을 모른다니 말도 안 돼요.** / It doesn't even make sense that you don't know the name of someone you see every day.
> **철수도 떨어졌다니 시험이 정말 어려웠나 봐요.** / To think that even Chul-soo failed, the test must have been really difficult.
> **어린 아이가 혼자 산다니 그걸 누가 믿겠어?** / Who would believe that a young child lives alone?
