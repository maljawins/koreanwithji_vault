## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor provides a comprehensive guide to the various Korean verbs used to express "bringing" or "taking" objects and people. The lesson moves from the most basic verbs for inanimate objects to specific honorific forms for people, as well as nuanced verbs that imply packing or buying. A central theme of the lesson is how these base verbs combine with the directional verbs 가다 (to go) and 오다 (to come) to create specific meanings of "taking away" versus "bringing here."

---

### Basic Possession and Carrying: 가지다 and 갖다

The foundation for "bringing" or "taking" things is the verb 가지다, which means to have, carry, or hold. The instructor explains that while it can mean "to have" in a general sense, it specifically implies having something on your person right now. On screen, the instructor notes that this is frequently used with the ~고 있다 (currently doing) form to describe what someone is currently carrying.

A key linguistic variation is that 가지다 can be shortened to 갖다. The instructor clarifies that 가지다 is more formal and polite, while 갖다 is common in informal, everyday conversation. Both versions function identically in grammar patterns, such as 가지고 있다 or 갖고 있다. To illustrate, the instructor provides the question:

> **Pen 갖고 있어요?** / Do you have a pen? (Are you carrying a pen?)

The instructor also highlights the use of 가지다 with the ~고 싶다 (want to) pattern. While 원하다 (to want) exists in Korean, using 가지고 싶다 or 갖고 싶다 is the standard way to express wanting a physical object. For example:

> **저도 자전거를 갖고 싶어요.** / I also want to have a bike.

However, a nuance is noted: you only use 가지다 for things you actually want to possess or hold. If you want to "have" pizza in the sense of eating it, you must use 먹다 (to eat), resulting in 먹고 싶어요. Using 피자를 갖고 싶어요 would literally mean you want to hold the pizza or add it to a collection.

---

### Combining with Direction: 가져가다 and 가져오다

To express "bringing" or "taking," the base verb 가지다 combines with 가다 (to go) or 오다 (to come). This can be done using the ~고 connector or by conjugating the first verb. The instructor notes that the conjugated versions are generally more common in speech.

- **가지고 가다 / 가져가다**: To take something (carry and go).
- **가지고 오다 / 가져오다**: To bring something (carry and come).

The instructor demonstrates this with a command:
> **이거 가져가세요.** / Take this (and go).

And an example of someone bringing an item:
> **철수가 이걸 가져왔어요.** / Chul-soo brought this.

To say you "forgot" to bring something, Koreans often simply use the negative form 안 가져오다 (didn't bring). Alternatively, one can use the expression 집에 놓고 오다, which literally means "to put it down at home and come," implying you left it behind.

> **우산을 안 가져왔어요.** / I didn't bring my umbrella.

---

### Bringing and Taking People: 데리다 and 모시다

When the "object" being brought or taken is a person or an animal, 가지다 cannot be used. Instead, the verb 데리다 is used for peers, younger people, or pets. Like 가지다, it is almost always used in combination with directional verbs.

- **데려가다 / 데리고 가다**: To take (a person/animal) and go.
- **데려오다 / 데리고 오다**: To bring (a person/animal) and come.

Examples provided include:
> **친구도 데리고 갔어요.** / I took my friend too.
> **도서관에 강아지를 데리고 오지 마세요.** / Please don't bring your dog to the library.

For situations requiring high politeness or honorifics—such as escorting a grandparent or a teacher—the verb 모시다 is used. It carries the nuance of "supporting," "waiting upon," or "escorting" someone with care.

- **모셔가다 / 모시고 가다**: To escort/take (an esteemed person) and go.
- **모셔오다 / 모시고 오다**: To escort/bring (an esteemed person) and come.

The instructor explains a sentence involving a father and grandmother:
> **아빠가 할머니를 차로 모셔갔어요.** / Dad took grandmother by car (or to the car).
> **엄마가 할머니를 모시고 오셨어요.** / Mom brought grandmother (here).

---

### Nuanced Verbs: 챙기다, 사다, and 갖다 주다

The instructor introduces 챙기다, which means to gather, pack, or grab. When combined as 챙겨 가다 or 챙겨 오다, it implies taking care to pack or gather things before moving. While 가져가다 is a simple delivery of an item, 챙겨 가다 suggests you are making sure you have what you need.

> **음료수는 제가 챙겨 갈게요.** / I'll take care of bringing the drinks (I'll pack them and go).
> **그건 제가 챙겨 왔어요.** / I took care of bringing that (I gathered it and brought it).

Another common pattern involves 사다 (to buy). 사 가다 and 사 오다 literally mean "buy and go" or "buy and come," but are often translated simply as "bring" or "take" when the item is purchased on the way.

> **물도 사 와.** / Bring some water too (Buy water and come back).

Finally, the instructor covers 가져다 주다 and its shortened form 갖다 주다. This means to get/carry something and give it to someone. It is used when you are getting an item for someone else's benefit.

> **펜을 갖다 주세요.** / Please get me a pen (Get a pen and give it to me).

For honorific contexts where you are giving the item to a superior, the verb 주다 is replaced with 드리다, resulting in the spaced form 갖다 드리다 or 가져다 드리다.
