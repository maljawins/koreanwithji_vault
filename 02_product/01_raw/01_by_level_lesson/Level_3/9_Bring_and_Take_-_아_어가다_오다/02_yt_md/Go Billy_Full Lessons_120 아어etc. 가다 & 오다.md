## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the usage of the verbs 가다 (to go) and 오다 (to come) when they follow a conjugated action verb. This lesson is divided into two main parts: a literal usage and a more abstract, intermediate-level usage. The first part covers how these verbs describe physical movement while performing an action, such as "walking to" or "bringing something here." The second part explores how these verbs function as auxiliary verbs to indicate the temporal direction of an action—whether an action continues from the past into the present or will continue from the present into the future.

---

### Literal Movement: Action Verb + 가다/오다

In its most basic form, attaching 가다 or 오다 to a conjugated action verb indicates that the subject performs the first action and then moves. Specifically, 가다 indicates going somewhere away from the speaker, while 오다 indicates coming toward the speaker or the current location. For example, the verb 걷다 (to walk) becomes 걸어 가다 when you are walking to a destination and 걸어 오다 when you are walking to where you are now.

The instructor provides specific examples to illustrate the difference between the general verb and the directional compound:
- **걷다** — To walk (in general).
- **걸어 가다** — To walk somewhere else (walk and go).
- **걸어 오다** — To walk here (walk and come).

> **학교까지 걸어 갔어요.** / I walked to school.
> **학교에서부터 걸어 왔어요.** / I walked here from school.

If the speaker is just describing the act of walking fast without a specific destination or origin in mind, they should simply use the base verb:
> **빨리 걸었어요.** / I walked fast.

Other common combinations include:
- **들어 가다 / 들어 오다** — To enter (go in / come in).
- **올라 가다 / 올라 오다** — To go up / come up (stairs, vehicles).
- **가져 가다 / 가져 오다** — To take / bring something.
- **사 가다 / 사 오다** — To buy something and go / come.

---

### Intermediate Usage: Temporal Direction with 가다

Beyond physical movement, 가다 can be used to show that an action or state continues from the present into the future. It implies a progression or a "going forward" in time. The instructor notes that this is an intermediate-level concept and suggests re-reading the definition several times.

For example, while 잘 하다 means "to do well," 잘 해 가다 means "to do something well and continue doing it well into the future."
> **동생이 유학 생활을 생각보다 잘 해 가고 있어요.** / My younger sibling is living her exchange student lifestyle better than I thought. (Literally: ...is doing her exchange student life well and continuing to do so.)

Similarly, 잘 되다 (to go well) becomes 잘 돼 가다 to imply ongoing success.
> **일이 잘 돼 가나요?** / Is your work going well? (Implies: Is it going well now and will it continue to be okay?)

Other examples of this "future-facing" progression include:
- **거의 다 먹어 가고 있어요.** / I'm almost finished eating. (The action is progressing toward completion.)
- **남자친구와 점점 닮아 가고 있어요.** / I'm gradually getting more like my boyfriend. (The resemblance is increasing over time.)
- **잠이 늘어가고 있어요.** / I'm sleeping more. (The amount of sleep is increasing and continues to increase.)

---

### Intermediate Usage: Temporal Direction with 오다

Conversely, 오다 is used to show that an action started in the past and has continued up until the present moment. It describes a "coming from the past" toward now.

A primary example is the verb 살다 (to live) becoming 살아 오다.
> **한국에서 다섯 살 때부터 살아 왔어요.** / I've lived in Korea since I was five years old. (Implies living there continually from age five until this very moment.)

Other examples of actions continuing from the past:
> **대학교에 가면 남자친구가 생길 거라고 믿어 왔어요.** / I've believed (continually) that once I go to college, I'll get a boyfriend.
> **3년 전부터 봉사활동부 부장으로 활동해 오고 있어요.** / I've been (continually) working as the leader of the volunteering club from three years ago.
> **그 사람들은 특별한 물을 마시면 눈이 좋아진다고 속여 왔어요.** / Those people have been fooling people, saying that if you drink the special water, your vision will improve.

The instructor concludes by noting that while this form can technically be used with many verbs, it is most frequently seen with 하다 (to do), 살다 (to live), and 되다 (to become/go). Mastering these three combinations will cover the majority of common situations.
