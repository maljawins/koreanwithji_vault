**How Koreans use 가다 & 오다**

To say things like “Bring” or “Take”, it’s very important that you first understand the concept of 가다 – to go and 오다 – to come.

In English, you choose whether to use "go" or "come" with respect to the listener's perspective.

Let’s say you’re in your room and your mom shouts “Dinner’s ready!”

In English, you’d respond with “Coming!”, which means you’re COMING to the kitchen where your mom is.

But in Korean, we use 가다 and 오다 a bit differently from how English uses “Go” and “come.” And it all comes down to one thing: “Where are YOU, right now?”

In this case, you’d have to GO to the dinner table from your room, which is why in Korean you’d say “지금 가요!” – “I’m going now”

The key idea to understand is, you don’t think about where other people are, you only think about where YOU are. It’s about YOUR current position and you speak from YOUR perspective.

Simply put, you can only GO THERE, but you can’t GO HERE. You can only COME here, the same way you can’t COME there. So, I’d say it’s fair to associate 가다 with there and 오다 with here.

Here’s another scenario to help you understand the difference.

Jisoo and Jennie are talking on the phone. And there’s a party tonight. And most importantly, they’re both at home right now.

And Jisoo wants to know if Jennie is going to be there.

So, in English, she asks “Are you COMING to the party?”

But in Korean, it would be “오늘 파티 갈 거야?” – Are you GOING to the party?” Because she’s still at home and therefore would have to leave the houses in order to GO to the party.

A few hours later, Jisoo arrives first and calls Jennie to ask her when she’ll be here. Now, since Jisoo’s current location is at the party, she’d call and ask “언제 와?” – When are you coming?”

**Motion verb + 가다 / 오다**

So now that you understand the logic behind 가다 and 오다, let’s talk about how to say things like “I walked home” or “I ran to the gym”, using motion verbs.

Now, if you combine a motion verb with 가다 and 오다, what this does is it shows the direction that a motion happens.

Let’s use “걷다 to walk” as an example.

Now, 걷다 means to walk just in general not anywhere not going somewhere and not coming here. So if there’s a specific location or a destination where you’re walking TO, you’d need to use 걸어가다 or 걸어오다 depending on YOUR location.

By the way, 걷다 is an irregular verb. I talked about all the irregular verbs in level 2, lesson 7. so, you can go back and review the rules there. Here, ㄷ 받침 in the stem 걷 changes to ㄹ as the suffix that follows starts with a vowel 어.

So now, we have 여기 and 거기 – Here and there.

With 여기 here, you have to use 오다. Because you can only come here and not come THERE. So, “Walk over here” would be “여기로 걸어오세요.”

And “Walk over there” would be “거기로 걸어가세요.”

I’ll include some more motion verbs in the summary that are often used together with 가다 and 오다.

**Figurative – 가다 & 오다**

This next usage of 가다 and 오다 is not so much about going or coming to a physical location, but it has more to do with the direction in time.

And notice there should be a space in between the two verbs for this specific usage.

So, if you were to use 가다, it would mean that whatever action verb that you use continues from now into the future. This also happens in English where you’d use “go” to express a similar idea like in the phrase “How’s it going?”

The most common verb you’d use it with is ‘되다’. So, “일은 잘 돼 가요?” is how you’d say it in Korean.

As in, is the direction it’s going good into the future?

Another example: 딸이 아빠를 닮아가고 있어요.

닮다 means to resemble. So, this means “the daughter is looking more and more like her dad.”

Let’s talk about 오다 now. Again, we’re not talking about time, not location. So anytime you want to emphasize that the action started in the past and has been continuing until now, you can use 오다. And you’d conjugate 오다 to the past tense like 왔어요 or 왔습니다 for this specific usage.

The best way to translate this would be “To have been doing”.

한국어를 오랫동안 공부해 왔어요.

한국어 means Korean language and 오랫동안 means “for a long time” and 공부하다

이 순간을 기다려 왔어요 – I have been waiting for this moment.

So, in summary, 가다 and 오다 can show the direction of location or time from YOUR perspective, namely the speaker’s standpoint.

Again, this is a very important concept you need to understand before learning how to say “bring & take” using the form 아/어가다 and 아/어오다.

This time there is no space between the two verbs.

**가져가다 & 가져오다**

We learned about 가지다 in level 2, lesson 13. So, you can go back there to review about some common uses of 가지다. If you were to ask someone if they have something using 가지다 like “가지고 있어요?” it's not just asking if they have it in general but it's more like asking “Do you have it on you right now?”

Although its dictionary definition is “to have”, in this context, it’s more like saying to GET something.

And here, we’ve combined 가지다 with 가다 in 가져가다. To GET and GO. This is how you say “to TAKE something THERE”.

And 가져오다 would mean “to BRING something HERE” by the same logic.

You can also use the 고 form and say 가지고 가다 / 갖고 가다 and 가지고 오다 / 갖고 오다. Remember 갖다 is just a shortened version of 가지다.

Although the form is different, they mean exactly the same thing. Therefore, they are 100% interchangeable. So, you can use whichever you’d like.

Just note that these forms are only for THINGS, not people.

예문

A: 혹시 이 책 좀 빌려줄 수 있어?

B: 응, 가져가. / 응, 갖고 가.

A: 비가 많이 오네요. 혹시 우산 가져왔어요? / 갖고 왔어요?

B: 아니요, 어떡하죠?

A: 저 두 개 있어요. 하나 빌려 드릴게요.

B: 감사합니다.

**데려가다 & 데려오다**

For people, we have to use a different verb - 데리다. Note that 데리다 is never used by itself. According to the dictionary, it means “To make a person or animal stay together.”

So, the way it works is exactly like 가지다 but for people OR animals like your dog.

Just like how 가져가다 and 가지고 가다 are interchangeable, you can also use 데리고 가다 or 오다.

In spoken Korean, we often say 데꼬 가다 / 데꼬 오다. This is not a correct spelling so you’re not supposed to write like this, but just know that you’ll HEAR it in conversations all the time.

**모셔가다 & 모셔오다**

Next, we have 모셔가다 and 모셔오다. You can think of 모시다 as the honorific version of 데리다. So, you’d use these verbs to say take or bring people like your boss, or your grandma.

Now, unlike 데리다, 모시다 by itself IS used.

And 모시다 by itself has a very polite and formal feel to it. Unfortunately, it doesn’t have a 1 to 1 translation. Depending on the context, it can mean “to escort”, “to accompany” or even “to serve”.

So, you can see 모시다 implies that you're actually going to be taking good care of people you take or bring with you.

And again, you can alternatively say 모시고 가다 and 모시고 오다 to convey the same exact meaning.

**Examples**

**챙겨가다 & 챙겨오다**

Next we have 챙겨가다 and 챙겨오다.

챙기다 can be interpreted in many ways. It can mean to take, to grab, or to gather, or to pack. But I would say the one meaning that kind of encapsulates everything would be “To get” like “to get something together”

See, the difference of 챙기다 in relation to all the other verbs we’ve looked at so far is

ONE, And also, we can’t really use 챙기고 가다 or 챙기고 오다 unlike the other ones.

AND TWO, it is used for things you bring or take because you think you’ll need it.

For example, you can take your barbecue grill for the camping trip this Saturday. Or you can bring a bottle of wine to a party.

So, it has the feeling of like you're making sure to take it or bring it specifically because you thought you’d need it.

**가져다 주다 & 갖다 주다**

There's one more set of verbs we have to talk about. These are not technically “compound verbs” per se but I’d thought I’d talk about them since they’re very much relevant to the topic.

And again, 갖다 주다 is more commonly used but it's slightly more informal.

So! This form means to get something and then give it to someone. So, you can translate this form as “to get something for someone”

**데려다 주다 / 모셔다 주다**

Literally, this word means “to give the act of accompanying” like “Walking someone home” or “Giving someone a ride”

And if you wanted to be more specific, you can say “차로 데려다 주다” – to accompany someone by car. This is how Koreans say “Give someone a ride.”

And again, 모셔다 주다 is just the honorific version of 데려다 주다.
