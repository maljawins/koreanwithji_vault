## Lesson Overview

This lesson serves as a comprehensive review and refinement session, focusing on natural sentence construction and the fundamental building blocks of Korean grammar. The session begins with a detailed correction of previous assignments, where Ji emphasizes not just grammatical correctness, but the cultural nuances of how Koreans actually speak. A key example includes the preference for saying "going to the hospital" (병원에 가다) over "seeing a doctor" (의사를 만나다) due to the accessibility of clinics in Korea.

The core of the lesson revolves around the "S T L O H V" (Subject, Time, Location, Object, How, Verb) framework. Ji explains the logic behind Korean word order, which generally moves from broad concepts to specific details. This section includes a philosophical look at why the subject or time might come first in a sentence and how shifting word order changes the emphasis of the information being conveyed.

Finally, the lesson transitions into a structured review of the "Fundamentals"—a massive compilation of particles, pronouns, and verb conjugation rules (Present, Past, Future, and Honorifics). Rather than a one-way lecture, Ji designed this portion to be interactive, allowing the student to identify specific areas of confusion within these foundational topics for targeted clarification.

Notes explain that grammar (particles/endings) happens at the end of each spaced section and verbs always come last. A box explains that adverbs (How) usually come right before the verb.
- **Ji's teaching:** In Korean, we have the "Six Principles" (육하원칙) for 5W1H: 누가 (who), 언제 (when), 어디서 (where), 무엇을 (what), 어떻게 (how), 왜 (why). Generally, Korean sentences follow this order. What comes immediately before the verb is typically the most important information. For example, "집에서 한국어를 공부해요" emphasizes *what* you study at home, while "한국어를 집에서 공부해요" emphasizes *where* you study Korean.

## Teaching Points

- **Natural Phrasing:** In Korean, context and cultural habits dictate word choice. Use 병원에 가다 for medical visits and 지각하다 for being late to recurring commitments like school or work.
- **Sentence Logic (S T L O H V):** Korean follows a "Broad to Specific" flow.
    - **Order:** Subject → Time → Location → Object → Adverb → Verb.
    - **Emphasis:** Moving a word closer to the verb increases its importance in the sentence.
- **Topic vs. Subject Markers:**
    - **은/는 (Topic):** Used for general facts, introducing new topics, or showing contrast.
    - **이/가 (Subject):** Marks the subject of the verb and answers "Who/What/Which" questions (e.g., "Who did this?").
- **Particle Nuances:**
    - **도 vs. 만:** Must be placed immediately after the specific word being emphasized.
    - **밖에:** Always paired with a negative verb to mean "nothing but" or "only."
    - **에 vs. 에서:** 에 marks existence or movement "to" a place; 에서 marks where an action occurs or movement "from" a place.
- **Verb Conjugation:**
    - **The "Tree" Analogy:** The Verb Stem is the base. Suffixes (branches/leaves) determine tense and context, while the final ending (fruit) determines politeness/speech level.
    - **Honorifics:** Add 시/으시 directly to the stem before adding other suffixes. Some verbs have unique honorific forms (e.g., 먹다 → 드시다).
