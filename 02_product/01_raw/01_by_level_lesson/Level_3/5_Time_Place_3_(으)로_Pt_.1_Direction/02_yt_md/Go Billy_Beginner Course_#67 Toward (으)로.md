## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the particle (으)로, specifically focusing on its function as a direction marker. While students may already be familiar with the particle 에 to indicate a destination or a location where something exists, (으)로 offers a nuanced alternative that emphasizes the direction or the act of moving "toward" a place.

The lesson covers the grammatical rules for attaching the particle based on the final consonant or vowel of a noun, provides a variety of example sentences to illustrate natural usage, and concludes with a practical conversation between a passenger and a taxi driver. A key takeaway is that (으)로 is often more natural than 에 when expressing movement toward a destination, though it cannot be used to mark static existence.

---

### Grammatical Rules for (으)로
The particle (으)로 translates to "toward" or "in the direction of." In many contexts, it translates naturally as "to" in English. The instructor highlights a critical restriction: unlike 에, (으)로 cannot be used to mark the location where a noun exists (static location). It is strictly for movement and direction.

The choice between 으로 and 로 depends on the ending of the noun. If a noun ends with a consonant, attach 으로. If a noun ends with a vowel or the specific consonant letter ㄹ, attach 로. The instructor provides a visual aid showing a hand pointing to emphasize the directional nature of this particle.

---

### Directional Examples and "Way" Expressions
The instructor demonstrates the particle through several sentences. When used with the word 쪽 (side/direction), it creates expressions for "this way" or "that way." A specific spelling note is provided: the demonstratives 이 (this), 그 (that), and 저 (that over there) attach directly to 쪽 without a space.

- **우리 집으로 오세요.** / Come toward (to) our house.
- **저쪽으로 가세요.** / Go that way.
- **이쪽으로 천천히 오세요.** / Come this way slowly.
- **저는 지금 집으로 가고 있어요.** / I am going home (toward home) now.
- **맛있는 파스타를 먹으러 이탈리아로 갔어요.** / I went to Italy to eat delicious pasta.
- **빌리 씨는 거실로 걸어갔어요.** / Billy walked toward (to) the living room.
- **빌리 씨는 아까 여기로 걸어왔어요.** / Billy walked here a little while ago.

The instructor notes that in the sentence "I am going home," using 으로 sounds more natural than a literal translation of "to."

### Conversation Practice: Taking a Taxi
A dialogue between Johnny (조니) and a taxi driver (택시 기사) illustrates how (으)로 is used in a common real-world scenario.

> **조니: 실례합니다. 빌리 대학교로 갈 수 있으세요?** / Excuse me. Can you go to Billy University?
> **택시 기사: 빌리 대학교요? 네, 태워 드릴까요?** / Billy University? Yes, shall I give you a ride?
> **조니: 네, 태워 주세요. 얼마예요?** / Yes, please give me a ride. How much is it?
> **택시 기사: 거기까지는 만 원밖에 안 돼요.** / To there, it's only 10,000 won.
> **조니: 좋아요. 거기로 가 주세요.** / Good. Please go there (toward there).
> **택시 기사: 네, 빨리 타세요.** / Okay, get in quickly.
> **조니: 네, 감사합니다!** / Okay, thank you!

The instructor explains a specific nuance in the driver's response: **안 돼요** literally means "it will not become," but in this context, it functions as "it won't even be" or "it's only."

---

### Usage Notes and Comparison with 에
The lesson concludes with two important notes. First, when you are trying to say "to" a location in the sense of moving toward it, (으)로 often sounds more natural to native speakers than the particle 에. The instructor visually emphasizes this by showing a screen filled with many instances of "으로" and only one "에," recommending that students default to (으)로 for directional movement. Second, the instructor hints that (으)로 has several other uses beyond direction, such as marking a tool or method, which will be covered in future lessons.
