## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the particle (으)로, a versatile grammar point with numerous functions in Korean. This lesson focuses on its two most fundamental uses: indicating direction ("toward") and indicating a means or tool ("using"). The instructor explains the conjugation rules based on the final letter of the noun, compares the directional use with the particle 에, and provides a wide array of practical examples ranging from physical tools to abstract channels of communication. The lesson concludes with important cultural and linguistic notes on how to naturally express transportation, where (으)로 is often replaced by specific verbs.

---

### Conjugation Rules for (으)로

The instructor begins by outlining how to attach the particle to a noun. The choice between 으로 and 로 depends entirely on the final sound of the preceding word. If a word ends in a consonant, you attach 으로. If a word ends in a vowel, you attach 로. A unique exception exists for words ending in the consonant ㄹ; in these cases, you also just attach 로. The instructor demonstrates this on the whiteboard, showing that the ㄹ consonant behaves more like a vowel in this specific grammar rule.

---

### (으)로 as a Directional Marker ("Toward")

When attached to a location, (으)로 translates as "toward" or "to." While it often overlaps with the particle 에, the instructor highlights a key nuance: (으)로 emphasizes the direction of movement rather than just the final destination. It gives the feeling of "heading toward" a place. Visually, the instructor uses arrows on the board to show movement toward a point.

A critical distinction is made: 에 can be used with the verb 있다 (to exist/be) to show static location, such as 집에 있어요 (I am at home). However, (으)로 cannot be used this way. It is strictly for movement verbs like 가다 (to go) or 오다 (to come).

The instructor notes that (으)로 is particularly natural when used with words that already indicate direction. He writes the word 쪽 (way/direction) on the board and provides common examples:
- **이쪽으로 오세요** / Please come this way.
- **오른쪽으로 가세요** / Go toward the right.
- **왼쪽으로 가세요** / Go toward the left.
- **북쪽으로 가세요** / Go north (toward the north side).

He also mentions the word 방향 (direction), which is used for general directions or compass points like North, South, East, and West.

---

### (으)로 as a Marker of Means or Tool ("Using")

The second major use of (으)로 is to indicate the tool, method, means, material, machine, or channel used to perform an action. In this context, it translates to English words like "using," "by," "with," "out of," "on," or "via." The instructor lists these categories on the board to show the breadth of this function.

The instructor provides several example sentences to illustrate these different categories:
- **붓으로 그림을 그렸어요.** / I drew a drawing with (using) a brush.
- **저는 이 책으로 공부할 거예요.** / I will study with (using) this book.
- **두부는 콩으로 만들어요.** / Tofu is made of (using) soybeans.
- **컴퓨터로 수업을 들어요.** / I take class on (using) the computer.
- **그 공을 발로 차 주세요.** / Please kick that ball with (using) your foot.
- **사람을 옷으로 판단하지 마세요.** / Don't judge a person by (using) their clothes.
- **이 전화번호로 연락해 주세요.** / Please contact me at (via) this phone number.
- **지난주부터 유튜브로 요리를 배우고 있어요.** / I've been learning cooking on (via) YouTube since last week.

A special case is mentioned regarding the word "through." While (으)로 doesn't literally mean "through," it is used when a location is the method of entry. The instructor draws a bird on the board to illustrate:
- **새가 창문으로 들어왔어요.** / The bird came in through the window (using the window as the entry method).

Finally, it is used for administrative or formal channels:
- **이메일로 신청하면 되나요?** / Should I apply by (via) email?

---

### Natural Expressions for Transportation

The instructor concludes with a "Notes" section regarding transportation. While 차로 가다 (to go by car) is grammatically correct, it is often not the most natural way to speak in Korean, much like how English speakers prefer "I drove" over "I went by car."

He provides three more natural alternatives for describing how you traveled:

1. **Walking:** Instead of saying "by foot" (발로), use the verb 걷다 (to walk). It conjugates to **걸어서 가다** (to go by walking) or **걸어서 오다** (to come by walking).
2. **Driving:** If you are the one operating the vehicle, use 운전하다 (to drive). The natural expression is **운전해서 가다** (to drive there).
3. **Taking Public Transport:** For buses, taxis, or planes, use the verb 타다 (to ride/take). The common phrase is **타고 가다** (to take [a vehicle] and go) or **타고 오다** (to take [a vehicle] and come).

The instructor jokes that you should only say you "drove the bus" (버스를 운전해서 갔어요) if you are actually the bus driver.
