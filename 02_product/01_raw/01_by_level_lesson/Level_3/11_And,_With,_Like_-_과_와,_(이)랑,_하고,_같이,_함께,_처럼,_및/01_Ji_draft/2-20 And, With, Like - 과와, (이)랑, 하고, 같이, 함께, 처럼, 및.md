**AND**

**Alright, now, let’s learn how to say “and” in Korean.**

**In English, we use “and” in a lot of different ways, but in Korean, we have to think about *what* we’re connecting — is it nouns or verbs?**

**Noun AND Noun**

In Korean, there are three ways to say “and” when connecting noun and noun — and they’re all interchangeable. There’s hardly any difference but it’s just that 과/와 .

**is the more formal than the other two. You’ll often see it in writing or formal speech — for example, in news articles or presentations.**

* **Use 과 when the noun ends in a consonant (받침)**
* **Use 와 when it ends in a vowel**

**📍 Examples:**

* **치킨과 맥주 – Chicken and beer**
* **남자와 여자 – Men and women**

**Now, 하고 and 이랑 are mostly used in spoken Korean. But 하고 is not 받침-sensitive.**

**📍 Examples:**

* **펜하고 종이 – Pen and paper**
* **의자하고 책상 – Chair and desk**

**And 이랑 / 랑 is 받침 – sensitive**

* **이랑 is for words ending in a consonant**
* **랑 after a noun ending in a vowel**

**📍 Examples:**

* **빵이랑 커피 – Bread and coffee**
* **너랑 나 – You and me**

**Now, you might be wondering — “Which one should I use?”
☑️ They all mean the same thing, but 과/와 is more formal.
So if you want to sound natural when speaking, go with 하고 or (이)랑.**

**And these particles also mean “with”**

**WITH - 과 / 와, 하고, 이랑 / 랑**

**That’s right. They don’t just mean “and” — they also show who you’re doing something with.**

**And you can add these adverbs 같이 or 함께 which mean “together”, like “together with”.**

**Again, these two words are interchangeable but 함께 is more formal than 같이.**

* **So, 같이 is way more common in everyday conversation.**

**You don’t *need* to use 같이 or 함께 every time — but they help add emphasis that you’re doing the action together with someone.**

✅ Example 1

**엄마랑 같이 시장에 갈 거예요** → I will go to the market together with my mom.

✅ Example 2

**친구와 함께 영화를 봤습니다** → I watched a movie together with my friend.

✅ Example 3

**누구랑 같이 살고 있어? → Who are you living together with?**

**같이 & 처럼 = “like”**

And 같이 has another meaning which means “like”

👉 When 같이 is not used with 과/와, 하고, 이랑/랑, it doesn’t mean “together” anymore — it means “like”

**💡And you can also use 처럼. Again, 같이 and 처럼 are completely interchangeable. There’s almost no difference in meaning or formality.**

* **But 같이 is a little more common in everyday speech, while 처럼 can sound a bit clearer in writing or formal situations.**

**So you can pick whichever feels more natural for you.**

**✅ Examples**

**너는 천사같이 착해 → You’re kind like an angel.**

**아이같이 웃었어요 → (He/She) laughed like a child.**

**그 사람은 배우처럼 잘생겼어요 → He’s handsome like an actor.**
