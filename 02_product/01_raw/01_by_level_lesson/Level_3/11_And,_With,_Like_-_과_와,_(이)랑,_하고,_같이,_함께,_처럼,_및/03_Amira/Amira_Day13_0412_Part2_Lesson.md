## Teaching Points

Ji covers several advanced connector nuances and the specific mechanics of the verb "to need":

*   **아무 (Any) + Particle Nuances:**
    *   **아무나 + Negative:** Ji explains that while 아무도 + negative means "no one/nothing," 아무나 + negative means "not just anyone." It implies selectivity. On screen, Ji shows the example: "저는 아무나 좋아하지 않아요" (I don't like just anyone / I'm selective).
    *   **아무 + Noun + 이나/나:** This construction completes the idea of "anything or whatever" in a positive sense. It replaces standard particles like 이/가 or 을/를. Examples include 아무 옷이나 (any clothes) and 아무데나 (anywhere).
*   **Ways to say "Or":**
    *   **거나:** The verb version of 이나/나. It connects two verb stems. Ji notes there are no irregularities; the stem remains unchanged (e.g., 읽거나, 운동을 하거나).
    *   **그러거나 말거나:** A set expression meaning "whether they do that or not" or "I couldn't care less." It combines 그러다 (to do so) and 말다 (to stop/not do).
    *   **아니면:** Used when a specific choice matters ("If not this, then that"). It is derived from 아니다 (to not be) + 면 (if).
    *   **혹은:** A more formal, Sino-Korean version of 아니면, often used in professional contexts or writing.
    *   **또는:** Means "and/or." It suggests that both options are possible or valid.
*   **필요하다 (To need):**
    *   Ji emphasizes that 필요하다 is a **descriptive verb** meaning "to be necessary."
    *   **Grammar Rule:** Because it is descriptive, it **cannot** take the object marker 을/를. The thing needed must take the subject marker 이/가.
    *   **Marking the Person:** The person who has the need is marked with 에게/한테 (emphasizing who the need is directed to) or 은/는 (softening the sentence by making the person the topic).
