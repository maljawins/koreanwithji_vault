## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson provides a comprehensive guide to the various ways to express "and" and "with" in Korean. The instructor categorizes these expressions by their grammatical function—whether they connect nouns within a sentence or connect entire sentences—and by their level of formality. The learning arc begins with the most common beginner-level particles used to link nouns and progresses toward more formal, written, or specialized connectors used in advanced contexts. By the end of the lesson, students will understand not only the mechanics of conjugation for these particles but also the specific nuances and social contexts that dictate which "and" is most appropriate to use.

---

### Connecting Nouns: 과/와, (이)랑, and 하고

The most common way to say "and" or "with" in Korean is by attaching a particle directly to a noun. The instructor introduces three main sets of particles for this purpose, ranked by their level of politeness and formality.

The first set is **과** and **와**. These are considered polite and are frequently used in both polite (존댓말) and formal speech. The choice between them depends on whether the preceding noun ends in a consonant or a vowel. If the noun ends in a consonant, such as **집 (house)**, you attach **과** to get **집과**. If the noun ends in a vowel, such as **학교 (school)**, you attach **와** to get **학교와**. These particles can mean "and" when connecting two items, or "with" when indicating accompaniment.

The second set is **(이)랑**, which has an informal feeling. While not considered "slang" and perfectly acceptable in regular conversation, it should be avoided in extra polite or formal situations, such as giving a speech or doing schoolwork. Similar to the first set, **이** is added if the noun ends in a consonant: **빵 (bread)** becomes **빵이랑**. If the noun ends in a vowel, you simply use **랑**: **친구 (friend)** becomes **친구랑**.

The third particle is **하고**. This also carries an informal feeling but is slightly more formal than **(이)랑**. It is the most commonly used of the three in daily life. Unlike the others, **하고** does not change based on whether the noun ends in a consonant or a vowel; it is simply attached as-is to any noun.

---

### Expressing "Together With": 같이 and 함께

When using the particles above to mean "with," Koreans often add an adverb to emphasize the meaning of "together." The instructor demonstrates this by adding **같이 (together)** after the particle. For example, while **친구와 갔어요** means "I went with a friend," saying **친구와 같이 갔어요** translates more precisely to "I went together with a friend."

The instructor also introduces **함께**, which functions identically to **같이** but carries a more formal tone. While **같이** is much more common in everyday speech, **함께** is preferred in formal writing or speeches.

---

### Connecting Sentences and Adding Emphasis: 그리고, 또, and 또한

While the previous particles connect nouns within a single sentence, **그리고** is typically used at the start of a new sentence to mean "and," "also," or "in addition." The instructor cautions that **그리고** carries a strong sense of emphasis. Using it too frequently can make speech sound repetitive and awkward, similar to starting every English sentence with "And furthermore."

While **그리고** can technically connect two nouns (e.g., **감자튀김 그리고 딸기 쉐이크**), this is uncommon because it sounds like you are saying "French fries, and in addition to that, a strawberry shake." In most cases, using a noun-connecting particle like **하고** or **랑** is more natural.

The instructor then introduces **또** and **또한**. **또** functions similarly to **그리고** but sounds more formal and "fancy." **또한** is even more formal and is often used like a particle to mean "also" or "even" with heavy emphasis. For example, **청소 또한** means "cleaning as well (on top of everything else)," providing more weight than the standard particle **도**.

---

### Formal and Specialized Connectors: 및 and 에

For advanced or written contexts, the instructor introduces **및**. This is an official-sounding particle used almost exclusively in writing, functioning like a written ampersand (&) or a plus sign. It is commonly seen in titles of papers or technical descriptions, such as **효능 및 부작용 (effectiveness and side effects)**.

Finally, the instructor explains a specialized use of the particle **에**. While usually a location or time marker, **에** can mean "and" when connecting two nouns that are closely related or viewed as a pair. A classic example is **티셔츠에 청바지 (T-shirt and jeans)**. This usage implies that the items go together as a set. The instructor notes that this is much less common and should not be used for unrelated items.
