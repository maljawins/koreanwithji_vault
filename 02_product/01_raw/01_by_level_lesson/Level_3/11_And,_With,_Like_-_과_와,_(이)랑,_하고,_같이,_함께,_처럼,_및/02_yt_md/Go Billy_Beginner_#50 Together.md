## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces how to express the concept of "together" and "with" in Korean. The lesson builds upon previously learned particles used for connecting nouns and expands into conversational and formal variations. The learning arc moves from reviewing formal particles to introducing common spoken particles, and finally integrating adverbs that specifically mean "together" to add clarity and natural flow to sentences.

---

### Reviewing 과 and 와

The particles **과** and **와** were previously introduced as ways to say "and" when connecting two nouns. For example, the instructor shows the sentence:
> **이것과 그것을 주세요.** / Give me this and that.

Beyond meaning "and," these particles can also translate to "with." This applies to objects, such as in the sentence:
> **밥과 김치를 먹었어요.** / I ate rice with kimchi (or I ate rice and kimchi).

It also applies to people. To say you did something with someone, you attach the particle to the person's name:
> **빌리 씨와 공원에 갔어요.** / I went to the park with Billy.

The instructor notes that **과** and **와** are formal particles. They are primarily used in writing or when speaking in extra polite, formal situations.

---

### Using the Adverb 같이 (Together)

While particles can mean "with," Koreans frequently add the adverb **같이** (together) to make the meaning explicit. This is the most common way to express doing an action together with someone. The instructor demonstrates this by modifying the previous example:
> **빌리 씨와 같이 공원에 갔어요.** / I went to the park together with Billy.

Another example provided is:
> **오늘 빌리 씨와 같이 놀았어요.** / Today I hung out together with Billy.

The adverb **같이** can also be used on its own without a "with" particle if the context of who you are with is already understood or if you are speaking generally.
> **같이 놀고 싶어요.** / I want to hang out together.
> **같이 밥도 먹었어요.** / We also ate a meal together.

---

### Conversational Particles: 하고 and (이)랑

For regular everyday conversations that are not strictly formal, the instructor recommends two other particles: **하고** and **(이)랑**.

**하고** is versatile and can mean both "and" and "with." It does not change based on whether the noun ends in a vowel or a consonant.
> **저하고 빌리 씨는 같이 갈 거예요.** / Me and Billy will go together.
> **밥하고 김치를 더 주세요.** / Give me more rice and kimchi.
> **오늘 민우 씨하고 놀고 싶어요.** / Today I want to hang out with Minwoo.

**(이)랑** is the most common particle used in casual, non-formal speech. Unlike **하고**, it has two forms based on the final sound of the noun:
- Attach **이랑** after a consonant.
- Attach **랑** after a vowel.

Examples of **(이)랑** in use:
> **내일 저랑 만나세요.** / Meet with me tomorrow.
> **빌리 씨랑 같이 삼겹살을 먹었어요.** / I ate pork belly together with Billy.
> **그 사람이랑 공부하고 싶지 않아요.** / I don't want to study with that person.

---

### Formal Alternative: 함께

The instructor introduces **함께** as a formal synonym for the adverb **같이**. Both mean "together" and can be used interchangeably with "with" particles, though **함께** carries a more polished or literary tone.
> **저와 같이 가 주세요.** / Please go together with me.
> **저와 함께 가 주세요.** / Please go together with me.

The instructor provides a final comparison:
> **우리는 한국어를 같이 공부를 했어요.** / We studied Korean together.
> **우리는 한국어를 함께 공부를 했어요.** / We studied Korean together.

---

### Cultural Note: The Word 친구 (Friend)

During the conversation practice, the instructor clarifies the usage of **친구** (friend). In Korea, this word is typically reserved for people of the exact same "Korean age." If a friend is older or younger, Koreans usually use kinship terms like **오빠**, **언니**, **형**, or **누나** instead of calling them a "friend" in the Western sense.
