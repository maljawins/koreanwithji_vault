## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor explains how to express the concept of "like" in Korean. He clarifies that this lesson does not cover the verb 좋아하다 (to like), but rather focuses on "like" in the sense of "to be similar to" or "in the manner of." The lesson is divided into two main parts: using verbs to state that one thing is like another, and using an adverbial marker to describe how an action or state is performed like something else.

---

### Expressing Similarity with 같다 and 비슷하다

To say that one noun is like another noun, the instructor introduces the verb 같다 (to be like). This verb requires the use of the particles 와 or 과 attached to the noun being compared. The choice between the two depends on the final sound of the noun: use 와 after a vowel and 과 after a consonant. For example, to say "I even think like you," one would say 내 생각도 너와 같다. The instructor provides a social etiquette warning here, noting that using the word 너 (you) is considered rude unless speaking to someone of the same age or younger with whom you have a close relationship.

If the speaker wants to emphasize that something is "just like" or "exactly like" another thing, the word 똑 can be added directly before 같다. An example provided is 철수의 옷이 내 옷과 똑 같다, meaning "Chul-soo's clothes are just like mine." Conversely, if two things are similar but not identical, the verb 비슷하다 (to be similar) is used instead of 같다. This is demonstrated with the sentence 동생과 나는 키가 비슷해요, which translates to "My height is similar to my younger sibling's."

---

### Using 처럼 as an Adverbial Marker

When the goal is not just to state a similarity between two nouns but to describe an action or a state occurring "like" something else, the marker 처럼 is used. This marker functions as an adverbial "like" or "as." The instructor explains that 처럼 is attached directly to a noun and replaces other grammatical markers that might have been there, such as topic, subject, or object markers.

Unlike the previous verbs, 처럼 can be used in a variety of sentence structures and does not require a specific verb like 같다 to follow it. One example shown is 나는 너처럼 똑똑하지 않아, meaning "I'm not as smart as you." Another example is 엄마처럼 되고 싶어, which translates to "I want to be like my mom." This marker allows for more flexible descriptions of behavior and characteristics.
