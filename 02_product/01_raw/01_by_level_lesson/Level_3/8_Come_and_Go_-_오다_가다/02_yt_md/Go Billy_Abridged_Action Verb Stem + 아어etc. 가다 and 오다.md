## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces a versatile grammar pattern that combines action verbs with the directional verbs 가다 (to go) and 오다 (to come). By conjugating an action verb into its polite form (removing 요) and attaching 가다 or 오다, speakers can specify the physical direction of a motion or describe how an action progresses through time. The lesson is divided into two main parts: the beginner-level usage involving physical movement and the intermediate-level usage involving temporal progression.

The first half of the video focuses on how this form clarifies whether an action is moving away from the speaker (가다) or toward the speaker (오다). The second half shifts to a more abstract "direction in time," where 가다 represents an action continuing from the present into the future, and 오다 represents an action that started in the past and has continued up until the present moment.

---

### Physical Direction: Action Verb + 가다/오다
At the basic level, attaching 가다 or 오다 to a motion-related action verb indicates the physical direction of that action. The instructor explains that while a verb like 걷다 (to walk) describes the action in general, it does not specify a destination or origin. To say "I walk to school," one must use the directional form.

- **걷다** (to walk) vs. **걸어가다** (to walk there) / **걸어오다** (to walk here)
- **뛰다** (to run) vs. **뛰어가다** (to run there) / **뛰어오다** (to run here)

The instructor provides a comparison to illustrate the nuance: 뛰어갈 수 없어요 means "I cannot run there" (specifically to that location), whereas 뛸 수 없어요 means "I cannot run" (in general, perhaps due to a physical injury).

Several common compound verbs follow this logic:
- **데려가다 / 데려오다**: Used for bringing or taking people or animals.
- **가져가다 / 가져오다**: Used for bringing or taking inanimate objects.
- **사오다 / 사가다**: Buying something and then moving. For example, 아버지가 치킨을 사오셨어요 means "Dad bought chicken and brought it home (here)."

---

### Temporal Direction: Continuing into the Future (가다)
In intermediate usage, the direction is no longer physical but temporal. When 가다 is attached to an action verb (with a space between the conjugated verb and 가다), it signifies that the action is continuing from the present moment into the future. It emphasizes a "going off" into time.

The instructor uses the example of a project or studies. If someone asks how a project is going, they might use 잘 돼 가고 있어요? (Is it going well?). This implies the process is ongoing and moving forward in time. Another example provided is weight gain: "My weight keeps going up" is expressed as 몸무게가 늘어가고 있어요, showing a continuous upward trend into the future.

Example sentence:
> **앞으로도 한국어를 계속 공부해 갈 거예요.** / I will continue to study Korean from now on into the future.

---

### Temporal Direction: Continuing from the Past (오다)
Conversely, using 오다 with an action verb indicates that an action started at a point in the past and has continued all the way up to the present. This is often translated as "have been doing." It emphasizes the duration and the fact that the action has "come" to the current moment.

The instructor notes that while you could use a simple past tense verb like 살았어요 (I lived), using 살아왔어요 (I have been living) emphasizes the continuous nature of that residency from the past until now.

Example sentences:
> **한국어를 오랫동안 공부해 왔어요.** / I have been studying Korean for a long time.
> **이 회사에서 10년 동안 일해 왔어요.** / I have been working at this company for 10 years.
> **이 순간을 기다려 왔어요.** / I have been waiting for this moment.
