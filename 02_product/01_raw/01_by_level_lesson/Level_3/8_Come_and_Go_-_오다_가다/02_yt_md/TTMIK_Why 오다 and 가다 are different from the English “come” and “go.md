## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for English speakers learning Korean: the usage of the verbs 오다 (to come) and 가다 (to go). While they are often taught as direct equivalents to their English counterparts, the logic governing which verb to use depends strictly on the speaker's current physical location, rather than the destination or the listener's perspective. The lesson covers basic conjugations, compound verbs derived from these roots, and specific situational examples where direct translation from English leads to errors.

---

### Basic Conjugations

The instructor first establishes the fundamental forms of both verbs. For 오다 (to come), the present tense is 와요, the past tense is 왔어요, the future tense is 올 거예요, and the present progressive is 오고 있어요. For 가다 (to go), the present tense is 가요, the past tense is 갔어요, the future tense is 갈 거예요, and the present progressive is 가고 있어요. These forms serve as the building blocks for more complex expressions.

---

### Compound Verbs with 오다 and 가다

Many Korean verbs are formed by combining a descriptive action verb with either 오다 or 가다 to indicate the direction of the movement relative to the speaker. The instructor provides a comprehensive list of these compound verbs to illustrate the pattern.

- **나오다** (to come out) vs. **나가다** (to go out)
- **들어오다** (to come in) vs. **들어가다** (to go in)
- **돌아오다** (to come back) vs. **돌아가다** (to go back)
- **가져오다** (to bring something here) vs. **가져가다** (to take something there)
- **걸어오다** (to walk here) vs. **걸어가다** (to walk there)
- **찾아오다** (to come and visit) vs. **찾아가다** (to go and visit)
- **올라오다** (to come up) vs. **올라가다** (to go up)
- **내려오다** (to come down) vs. **내려가다** (to go down)

Additional examples include 달려오다/달려가다 (to run), 날아오다/날아가다 (to fly), 따라오다/따라가다 (to follow), 뛰어오다/뛰어가다 (to run/jump), and 넘어오다/넘어가다 (to come/go over a boundary like a wall). Understanding the core difference between 오다 and 가다 is essential for using all of these compound verbs correctly.

---

### The Core Logic: Speaker-Centric Perspective

The most critical distinction is that Korean logic does not consider the listener's location or the destination when choosing between 오다 and 가다. In English, if a mother calls her child for dinner, the child might say, "I'm coming!" because they are moving toward the listener. In Korean, this is impossible. The speaker must use 가다 because they are moving away from their current location.

The instructor uses a visual diagram to emphasize this: anything moving toward the speaker's current position is 오다, and anything moving away from the speaker's current position is 가다. You cannot "come" to a place where you are not already present. Therefore, an English speaker's perspective of "coming to join you" must be expressed as "going to where you are" in Korean.

---

### Situational Example: "Do you want to come, too?"

In a roleplay scene, one person says they are going to lunch and asks a colleague, "Do you want to come, too?" In English, "come" is used because the colleague would be joining the speaker's group. However, in Korean, the correct phrase is:

> **지금 밥 먹으러 갈 건데 같이 갈래?** / I'm going to eat now, do you want to go together?

The instructor explains that because the speaker is leaving their current location to go somewhere else, they must use 갈래 (from 가다). Using 올래 (from 오다) would only be correct if the speaker were already at the restaurant and calling the colleague to join them there.

---

### Situational Example: "I'll be there soon"

When telling someone you will arrive shortly, English often uses "come" (e.g., "I'm coming!"). In Korean, you must use 가다.

> **금방 갈게요.** / I'll be there soon. / I'll go right away.

If you were to say 금방 올게요, it would mean "I am leaving now, but I will come back here soon." This is because 오다 always refers to returning to or arriving at the speaker's current starting point. Similarly, when answering the question "When are you coming?" (언제 와?), if you are currently on your way, you must say:

> **지금 가요.** / I'm coming now. (Literally: I'm going now.)

---

### Situational Example: "Can I come in?"

The video begins and ends with a demonstration of entering a room. In English, a person at the door asks a person inside, "Can I come in?" In Korean, the person at the door is outside and moving into a space they are not yet in. Therefore, they must use 들어가다 (to go in).

> **들어가도 돼요?** / Can I come in? (Literally: Is it okay if I go in?)

The instructor notes that 들어와도 돼요? (Can [someone] come in?) is only used when the speaker is already inside and asking permission for a third party (who is outside) to enter. For example, asking a boss if a boyfriend waiting at the door can enter the office.
