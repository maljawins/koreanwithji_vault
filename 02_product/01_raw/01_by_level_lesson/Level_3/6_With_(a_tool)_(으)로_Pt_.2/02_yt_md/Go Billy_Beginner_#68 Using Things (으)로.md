## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the second major function of the particle (으)로. While the previous lesson taught its use as a direction marker meaning "toward" or "to," this lesson explains how it functions as an instrumental marker. In this context, it indicates the method, material, tool, or means by which an action is performed.

The instructor explains that because this particle covers a wide range of "means," it can translate into English in several ways depending on the context, such as "using," "by," "with," "in," or "out of." The lesson provides a variety of practical examples ranging from physical tools like pencils and bicycles to abstract means like languages and self-study.

---

### The Instrumental Function of (으)로
The particle (으)로 is used to show the method, material, machine, tool, channel, or means by which something happens. The instructor provides the example sentence 편지를 연필로 썼어요 (I wrote a letter with a pencil). In this sentence, the pencil is the tool or means used to complete the action of writing, so it is marked with 로.

Visually, the instructor notes that the translation of (으)로 is highly flexible. It can mean "using a pencil" or "by pencil" just as easily as "with a pencil." This flexibility applies to almost any noun that can be conceived as a "means" to an end.

---

### Practical Examples of (으)로 as a Means
The instructor presents a series of examples to demonstrate the breadth of this particle's usage. For transportation, one says 자전거로 갔어요 (I went by bicycle). For physical labor, 어제 저녁을 손으로 만들었어요 (Yesterday I made dinner by hand). It can even be used with body parts to indicate the sense used for an action, as in 눈으로만 볼 거예요 (I will only look with my eyes).

When discussing materials, the particle translates as "out of," such as in 비행기 세 대를 종이로 만들었어요 (I made three airplanes out of paper). For communication channels or languages, it translates as "in" or "by," seen in 영어로 설명해 주세요 (Please explain in English), 이따가 이메일로 보내주세요 (Please send it by email later), and 빌리 씨랑 전화로 얘기했어요 (I talked with Billy on the phone). Finally, it can mark an abstract method of achievement: 한국어를 독학으로 배우고 있어요 (I'm learning Korean with self-study).

---

### Conversation Practice: Borrowing a Phone and Travel Plans
The lesson concludes with a dialogue between 두 사람 (two people), 수지 and 신혜, incorporating the new grammar. 수지 explains that her phone isn't working and asks to borrow one to call a friend. 신혜 offers hers, saying 이 핸드폰으로 전화해도 돼요 (You can call with this cell phone).

During the conversation, a specific vocabulary point is highlighted: 제 것 or 제 거 means "mine." The instructor notes that 제 거 is a natural way to say "my thing" and is used without the particle 의. In the dialogue, the object marker is attached to form 제 걸 (mine [as an object]).

The dialogue ends with a discussion of travel plans. When asked how she will get to a friend's house, 수지 replies 지하철이랑 버스로 갈 거예요 (I will go by subway and bus). This demonstrates that (으)로 can be applied to a list of multiple means of transportation.
