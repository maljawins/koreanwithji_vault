## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the versatile particle (으)로, which has several distinct functions in Korean. While it can indicate direction (toward) when paired with movement verbs, this lesson focuses on its use to express the "means" or "method" by which an action is performed. This often translates to "using," "by," "with," "out of," or "for" in English.

The lesson is structured to first cover the mechanical rules of attachment, followed by the "means/tool" usage, and finally the "status/role" usage (as). A critical distinction is made between (으)로 and (으)로서, explaining when each is appropriate based on whether the expression modifies a verb or describes a person's capacity or position.

---

### Basic Conjugation Rules for (으)로
The choice between 으로 and 로 depends on the final sound of the noun it attaches to. If a noun ends in a consonant, you attach 으로. For example, `펜 (pen)` becomes `펜으로 (with a pen)`. If a noun ends in a vowel, you attach 로. For example, `자동차 (car)` becomes `자동차로 (by car)`.

There is a special rule for nouns ending in the consonant ㄹ. Even though it is a consonant, these nouns follow the vowel rule and only add 로. The instructor demonstrates this with the word `연필 (pencil)`, which becomes `연필로 (with a pencil)`.

---

### (으)로 as Method, Material, or Tool
In its most common usage, (으)로 indicates the method, material, machine, tool, channel, or general means used to complete an action. This is often conceptualized as "using [noun]." The instructor provides several examples to illustrate these different categories.

When talking about materials, you might say `책상을 나무로 만들었어요 (I made a desk out of wood)`, where wood is the material used. For communication channels or tools, you can say `핸드폰으로 철수 씨한테 전화했어요 (I called Chul-soo on my cell phone)` or `이메일로 보내주세요 (Please send it by email)`.

This also applies to transportation and body parts. For instance, `비행기로 한국까지 가고 싶어요 (I want to go to Korea by plane)` uses the particle to show the vehicle, while `손으로 종이를 접었어요 (I folded the paper with my hands)` shows the physical means. Finally, it is used for languages: `한국어로 설명할 수 있어요 (I can explain it in Korean)` or platforms: `빌리의 유튜브 채널로 한국어를 배우고 있어요 (I'm learning Korean through Billy's YouTube channel)`.

---

### (으)로 to Mean "As" or "In the Role Of"
The particle (으)로 can also express a role, status, or the capacity in which something is received or viewed. In these cases, it translates to "as," "for," or "in the role of."

Common examples include receiving items: `이거 선물로 받았어요 (I got this as a present / for a present)`. It can describe how you perceive someone: `철수 씨를 친구로 생각해요 (I think of Chul-soo as a friend)`. It is also used for specific categories like meals or sizes: `어제 저녁으로 피자를 먹었어요 (Yesterday I ate pizza for dinner)` or `이거 라지로 있어요? (Do you have this in a large?)`. In a professional context, you can say `선생님으로 고용됐어요 (I was hired as a teacher)`.

---

### The Capacity Marker: (으)로서
A vital grammatical nuance involves the distinction between (으)로 and (으)로서. While (으)로 can mean "as," it is strictly used when the expression is modifying a verb (like being "hired" as a teacher). If you want to speak about yourself or someone else in the capacity of a certain role—where the role itself is the subject of the thought rather than a modifier for a specific action—you must use (으)로서.

The instructor explains that (으)로서 is (으)로 with the suffix 서 added. It shows the capacity or position of the subject, such as a job or rank. For example, `언어를 좋아하는 사람으로서 한국어는 그렇게 어렵지 않아요 (As a person who likes languages, Korean isn't that difficult)`. Another example is `친구로서 하는 말인데 다단계 하지 마 (I'm saying this as a friend, but don't do multi-level marketing)`.

The instructor notes that while (으)로서 is grammatically correct for these "in the capacity of" situations, it can sound quite formal and is less common in casual spoken Korean. However, it is essential to recognize it in writing or formal speech.
