### Overview

This lesson focuses on two primary ways to express "using" or "with" in Korean: the particle (으)로 and the grammar form 가지고. While both can often be translated as "using," they carry distinct nuances and usage constraints. The instructor frames (으)로 as the versatile, standard way to indicate a tool, method, or means, suitable for all speech levels. In contrast, 가지고 is presented as an informal alternative that emphasizes the physical act of holding, carrying, or taking something to achieve a result.

The learning arc begins with a review of (으)로 conjugation and its broad semantic range, followed by an introduction to the structure and origin of 가지고. The instructor then provides a detailed comparison of the two, highlighting how speech levels and the degree of physical involvement dictate which form is more natural. The lesson concludes with a brief look at an advanced metaphorical usage of 가지고 to describe the cause or basis of an action.

### The Versatile Particle (으)로

The particle (으)로 is the standard way to indicate the tool, method, means, material, machine, or channel by which an action occurs. Its conjugation is straightforward: nouns ending in a consonant take 으로, such as 손으로 (using/with a hand), while nouns ending in a vowel or the letter ㄹ take 로, such as 상추로 (using lettuce) or 자동차로 (by car). While "using" is a reliable logical translation, more natural English equivalents include "by," "with," "out of," "via," or "by means of."

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this range. One might say 손으로 만들었어요 (I made it with my hands) to show the tool, or 자동차로 미국을 여행했어요 (I traveled America by car) to show the mode of transportation. However, a cultural nuance is noted: when discussing vehicles, it is often more common to use the verb 타다 (to ride/take) in the form 타고 가다 (to go by riding) or 타고 오다 (to come by riding) rather than just the particle. Other examples include 태블릿 피씨로 수업을 들어요 (I take classes on my tablet PC) and 과외로 돈을 모을 거예요 (I'm going to save money through private tutoring).

---

### The Informal Alternative 가지고

The form 가지고 (often shortened to 갖고) originates from the verb 가지다 (to have, take, or carry). When used as a grammar form after a noun, it typically follows the object marker 을/를. It carries the literal sense of "taking/having/carrying [noun] and then doing something." Because of this origin, 가지고 is strictly informal. It is appropriate for casual conversation with friends but should be avoided in formal writing or when speaking politely to strangers or elders.

While it can mean "using," its core nuance is physical possession or direct involvement. For instance, 신문을 가지고 거울을 닦았어요 (I wiped the mirror with a newspaper) implies you were physically holding the newspaper while wiping. Similarly, 상추를 가지고 김치를 만들어 볼까? (Should we try making kimchi with lettuce?) suggests the act of taking the lettuce and transforming it. The instructor notes that if a sentence sounds much more natural with "with" than "using" in English—implying holding or playing with something—가지고 is likely the better choice. A prime example is 아기가 공을 가지고 놀고 있어요 (The baby is playing with a ball); saying "using a ball" here would sound unnatural because the baby is physically interacting with the object rather than utilizing it as a tool for a separate task.

---

### Nuance Comparison and Advanced Usage

The primary differences between (으)로 and 가지고 are speech level and physical nuance. (으)로 is universal across all speech levels and focuses on the "means" or "method" in a general sense. 가지고 is informal and emphasizes "physically holding" or "taking." This distinction is clear in the phrase 그걸 갖고 뭐해? (What are you doing with that?), which implies the person is currently holding the object. While 그걸로 뭐해? is grammatically correct, it lacks that specific physical implication.

For advanced learners, the instructor introduces a metaphorical use of 가지고 where it indicates the cause or the specific object of an action, often translated as "for" or "about." This is seen in the sentence 긴장해서 실수한 걸 가지고 놀리지 마요 (Don't make fun of me for the mistakes I made because I was nervous). Here, the speaker is "taking" the fact of the mistakes as the basis for teasing. This usage is less common and typically involves abstract concepts rather than physical tools.
