### Expressing "Through" with 통해서 and 으로

The verb 통하다 literally means "to pass" or "to go through." Its conjunctive form 통해서 is used as an adverb to describe an action happening through or by way of something, or physically passing through a space. The instructor notes that the 서 is optional, and the form itself sounds slightly formal. To use it, you attach it after a noun using the object marker 을/를 with a space.

> **친구를 통해서 들었어요.** / I heard it through a friend.
> **창문을 통해서 벌레가 들어왔어요.** / A bug came in through the window.

For everyday speech, the particle (으)로 is often preferred over 통해서. It shows the tool, method, means, or channel by which something happens. Common translations include "using," "by means of," "with," or "through." However, the instructor warns that (으)로 can be vague. For instance, when doing something through a person or an institution, 통해서 is required to avoid sounding like you are literally "using" that person as a tool.

> **과외로 돈을 모을 거예요.** / I'll save up money through private tutoring.
> **인터넷으로 주문했어요.** / I ordered it through the Internet.
> **저를 통해서 사면 더 싸요.** / It's cheaper if you buy it through me. (Correct)
> **저로 사면...** / (Incorrect/Vague)

### Using 가지고 for Informal "With/Using"

The word 가지고, derived from 가지다 (to take/have/carry), functions similarly to (으)로 in informal speech to show what is being used. It carries a nuance of physically holding or carrying the object. It should be avoided in formal writing or polite speech. Like 통해서, it follows a noun marked with 을/를.

> **오이를 가지고 김치를 만들 거예요.** / I will make kimchi using cucumbers.
> **수건을 가지고 바닥을 닦았어요.** / I wiped the floor with a towel.

The instructor highlights a key difference: replacing 가지고 with (으)로 can change the meaning. For example, saying a child played "with" a toy (holding it) uses 가지고, whereas using (으)로 might imply the toy was the method of play rather than the object being held.

---

### Formal "Through Action": (으)ㅁ으로써

When the "through" refers to an action rather than a noun, the form (으)ㅁ으로써 is used. This is a formal, written expression. It is created by turning an action verb into a noun using the (으)ㅁ nominalizer and then attaching 로써. Note the double consonant ㅆ, which distinguishes it from the particle 으로서 (meaning "as/in the capacity of").

> **선크림을 바름으로써 피부를 보호할 수 있어요.** / I can protect my skin through putting on sunblock.
> **SNS에 사진을 올림으로써 팬들과 가까워지고 있어요.** / Lately, they're becoming close with their fans through uploading photos to SNS.

The instructor clarifies the distinction between 로써 (tool/method) and 로서 (status/position). While they sound similar, 로서 is used after nouns to mean "as a [noun]."

---

### "According to" and "Depending on": 따라서 and 에 따라서

따라서 comes from 따르다 (to follow). Used alone at the start of a sentence, it means "therefore" or "it follows that" in formal contexts. When used as 에 따라서 after a noun, it means "according to" or "depending on." This is frequently paired with the verb 다르다 (to be different) to create the expression "it depends on."

> **날짜에 따라서 장소가 달라요.** / The place is different depending on the date.
> **규칙에 따라서 행동해 주세요.** / Please act according to the rules.

To use this with verbs, you use the form ~지에 따라서 (depending on whether or not). This is common with question words like "who," "what," or "when."

> **무슨 요일인지에 따라서 달라요.** / It depends on what day of the week it is.
> **누가 오는지에 따라서 계획이 조금 바뀔 수 있어요.** / Depending on who comes here, the plans can change a little.

An older, less common variant is ~느냐/냐에 따라서. This follows the same logic but uses the interrogative endings 느냐 (for action verbs) or 냐 (for descriptive verbs).

---

### Citing Sources: 에 의하면 and 에 따르면

To cite a source or explain the basis of a fact, formal speech and writing use 에 의하면 (according to/relying on) or 에 따르면 (according to/following). These are interchangeable and used after nouns.

> **헌법에 의하면 모든 시민에게 투표권이 있습니다.** / According to the constitution, every citizen has a right to vote.
> **일기예보에 따르면 내일부터 비가 온대요.** / According to the weather report, they say it'll rain from tomorrow.

---

### Passive Agency and Cause: 에 의해서 and 에 의한

The forms 에 의해서 (due to/by) and 에 의한 (which is due to) are used in passive constructions or to show cause. 에 의해서 acts as an adverb, while 에 의한 acts as an adjective describing a noun.

> **태풍에 의해 도시가 파괴됐어요.** / The city was destroyed due to the typhoon.
> **스트레스에 의한 두통으로 고생하고 있어요.** / I'm suffering from a headache that's due to stress.

---

### Giving Examples: 예를 들어서, 들면, 들자면

The instructor explains three ways to say "for example" based on the phrase 예를 들다 (to give an example):
1. **예를 들어서:** The most common and direct way.
2. **예를 들면:** "If I give an example"; sounds softer and less direct.
3. **예를 들자면:** "If you let me give an example"; sounds like the speaker is contemplating the example.

---

### Expressing Opinions: ~건대 and ~하건대

The connector ~건대 is an older, formal form used with verbs of thinking, feeling, or wishing (생각하다, 바라다, 짐작하다). It carries the nuance of "I'll tell you what I [think/hope]..." and sounds like a confession or a sincere statement.

When used with 하다 verbs, it becomes 하건대, which often shortens to 건대. If the preceding syllable ends in ㄱ, ㅂ, or ㅅ, it simply becomes 건대 (e.g., 짐작건대). If it does not end in those sounds, it can shorten to 건대 or sometimes 컨대 (e.g., 원컨대).

> **바라건대, 이 제품에 대해 모르는 사람이 없게 해주세요.** / I'll tell you what I hope, is to please make sure that there's nobody who doesn't know about this product.
> **짐작건대, 철수는 오늘도 아무 말도 안 할 거예요.** / What I estimate is that 철수 will not say anything today either.
