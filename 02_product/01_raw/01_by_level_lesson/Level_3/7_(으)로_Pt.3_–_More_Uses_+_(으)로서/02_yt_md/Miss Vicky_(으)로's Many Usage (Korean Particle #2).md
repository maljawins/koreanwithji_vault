### Conjugation Rules for (으)로

The instructor explains that the choice between 로 and 으로 depends entirely on the preceding noun's ending. If a noun ends in a vowel (no 받침), 로 is attached directly. For example, 학교 (school) becomes 학교로. If the noun ends in a consonant (받침), the additional vowel 으 is inserted for ease of pronunciation, making it 으로. Thus, 집 (house) becomes 집으로.

There is one critical exception to this rule involving the 리을 (ㄹ) consonant. Even if a noun ends in a 받침, if that consonant is ㄹ, you do not use 으로. Instead, you use 로. The instructor demonstrates this with the word 팔 (arm), which becomes 팔로. Adding the extra vowel in this specific case is considered unnecessary and less comfortable for natural speech.

### Meaning 1: Direction

When used to point toward a direction, (으)로 is typically preceded by direction-based nouns. The instructor lists the seven most common direction words: 오른쪽 (right side), 왼쪽 (left side), 앞 (front), 뒤 (back), 위 (up), 아래 (down), and 밑 (down/under). Notably, 아래 and 밑 are interchangeable when expressing the direction "downward."

To illustrate these in use, the instructor provides several imperative sentences. 오른쪽으로 가세요 means "Please go right," while 왼쪽으로 오세요 means "Come to the left." The instructor also references the Wonder Girls' song "Like This" to show how these expressions appear in lyrics, specifically pointing out the lines "자 오른쪽으로 걸어봐" (Now try walking to the right) and "다시 또 왼쪽으로 가" (Go to the left again).

Further nuances are explored with verbs of movement like 올라오다 (come up), 올라가다 (go up), 내려오다 (come down), and 내려가다 (go down). While saying 위로 올라와 (come upward) might seem redundant because the verb already implies upward movement, the instructor clarifies that it is perfectly natural and common in Korean to include the direction noun for emphasis.

---

### Meaning 2: Destination — (으)로 vs. 에

The instructor tackles the common confusion between (으)로 and 에 when they both translate to "to" in English. While both can mark a destination, the focus differs significantly. 에 is used when the focus is strictly on the destination itself—the act of getting to a place. In contrast, (으)로 is used when the focus includes the starting point or the direction of travel from somewhere else.

For general statements like "I go to school" (저는 학교에 가요), 에 is the standard and most natural choice. However, if a starting point is mentioned or implied, (으)로 becomes necessary. For example, in the sentence "From here, where should I go?" (여기에서 어디로 가야 해요?), (으)로 is used because the question is framed relative to the current location (여기에서).

The instructor provides further examples to solidify this distinction. In the sentence "I commute from Gyeonggi-do to Seoul" (저는 경기도에서 서울로 통근해요), 로 is used because the starting point (Gyeonggi-do) is explicitly stated. Similarly, "Jeff went from home to school in the morning" (제프는 아침에 집에서 학교로 갔어) uses 로 to connect the origin and the destination.

---

### Meaning 3: Means of Transportation and Tools

The third usage of (으)로 is to indicate the method, tool, or instrument used to perform an action, equivalent to "by" or "with" in English. When applied to transportation, it marks the vehicle used: 지하철로 통근해요 (I commute by subway) or 버스로 가자 (Let's go by bus).

When applied to tools or instruments, it indicates what was used to complete a task. Examples include 연필로 썼어요 (I wrote with a pencil) and 펜으로 쓰세요 (Please write with a pen). The instructor notes the spelling difference between 펜 (writing pen) and 팬 (a fan of a celebrity), though they sound similar.

A humorous idiomatic expression is introduced: 이거 발로 썼니? (Did you write this with your foot?). This is a playful or mean way to criticize someone's poor handwriting or work. The instructor also references a 2002 World Cup theme song to teach the verb 차다 (to kick), resulting in the phrase 발로 차 (kick it with your foot). Finally, the particle can even apply to digital tools, such as 스카이프로 영상통화 했어요 (I did a video call using Skype).

---

### Meaning 4: Choice

The final usage marks a choice among several options. In this context, (으)로 indicates "as" or "which one" in a selection process. The instructor uses a shopping dialogue to demonstrate: when looking at two pretty items and asking "Which one shall we buy?", the question is 뭐로 살까?. The response "Let's buy that one" is 저거로 사자.

This usage is also common in restaurants or cafes. A waiter might ask 어떤 거로 드릴까요? (Which one shall I give you / What would you like?). The customer then marks their choice with the particle: 아메리카노로 주세요 (Please give me an Americano) or 떡볶이로 주세요 (I'll have the tteokbokki).

The instructor explains that even when the options aren't explicitly listed, (으)로 implies a selection was made. For instance, if someone asks 어디로 갈까? (Where shall we go? / Which place shall we choose?), and the answer is 한남동으로 가자 (Let's go to Hannam-dong), it implies that Hannam-dong was chosen out of several possible neighborhoods to visit.
