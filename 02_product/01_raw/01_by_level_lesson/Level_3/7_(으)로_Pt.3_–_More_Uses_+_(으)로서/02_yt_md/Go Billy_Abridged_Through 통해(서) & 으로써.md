## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson covers two primary ways to express the concept of "through" or "by means of" in Korean: the beginner-level form 통해서 and the more formal, written form 으로써. The instructor explains the grammatical construction of each, their specific nuances, and when to use them versus simpler particles like 으로. Additionally, the lesson explores the versatile verb 통하다, which serves as the root for the first grammar point but also carries idiomatic meanings related to mutual understanding and communication.

---

### Expressing "Through" with 통해서

The first form is Noun + 을/를 통해(서). This is a beginner-friendly structure where you take a noun, attach the appropriate object marker (을 after a consonant, 를 after a vowel), and follow it with 통해. The final 서 is completely optional and does not change the meaning. This form originates from the verb 통하다, which means "to pass" or "to go through." When you use this structure, you are literally saying that something happens by "passing through" that noun.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate how this translates to "through" or "by way of" in English:

> **책을 통해서 수학을 배웠어요.** / I learned math through a book.
> **뉴스를 통해서 들었어요.** / I heard it through the news.
> **친구를 통해서 만났어요.** / We met through a friend.

Beyond abstract means, it can also describe physical passage. The instructor illustrates this with a sentence about light:

> **창문을 통해서 햇빛이 들어와요.** / Sunlight comes in through the window.

While 통해서 is useful, it carries a slightly formal feel. In everyday casual conversation, Koreans often prefer simpler particles. For instance, instead of saying you learned through a book with 통해서, you might use the 으로 particle to mean "using a book" (`책으로`). Similarly, instead of saying you heard something through a friend, you might simply say you heard it from a friend using 한테 (`친구한테 들었어요`).

---

### Idiomatic Uses of 통하다

The root verb 통하다 has a specific idiomatic usage that is very common in daily life. It can mean "to get one another," "to be on the same page," or "to match" in terms of feelings or communication. You can use it by itself or specify what exactly is "matching" by using the subject marker 이/가.

Common expressions include 마음이 통하다 (to have matching hearts/feelings) and 말이 통하다 (to be on the same page/to communicate well). The instructor demonstrates a negative example of this:

> **엄마랑은 말이 안 통해요.** / I can't communicate well with my mom. (Literally: My words don't match with my mom.)

---

### Formal "Through" with 으로써

The second form is Action Verb Stem + (으)ㅁ으로써. This is a more advanced, formal structure primarily found in written Korean. To form it, you take an action verb stem and turn it into a noun by adding 음 (after a consonant) or 미음 (after a vowel), then attach 으로써. This translates to "through doing [verb]" or "by means of [verb]."

The instructor emphasizes a critical spelling distinction: this form uses 으로써 (with ㅆ), which indicates a tool or method. It is distinct from 으로서 (with ㅅ), which indicates a status or role (e.g., "as a teacher"). 

Unlike 통해서, this form cannot be used for physical passage (like going through a window). It is strictly for the method or action taken to achieve a result. Examples shown on screen include:

> **책을 읽음으로써 한국 역사를 배웠어요.** / I learned Korean history through reading books.
> **봉사를 함으로써 얻는 행복이 커요.** / The happiness gotten through doing service is big.

Because of its formal and written nature, you will see this in books or news reports more often than you will hear it in spoken conversation.
