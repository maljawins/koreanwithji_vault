### Overview
This lesson introduces the grammar form 통해서, which translates to "through" in English. The instructor explains that this form is derived from the verb 통하다, meaning "to pass through" or "to go through." While it is a versatile expression that can be used for physical movement, methods of learning, or social connections, it carries a slightly formal nuance.

### Construction and Basic Meaning
The form 통해서 is built from the verb **통하다 (to pass/go through)**. When used as a grammar marker, it literally conveys the sense of "passing through" or "going through" something to achieve a result or reach a destination. The instructor notes that the final syllable 서 is optional; learners will frequently encounter both **통해서** and **통해** with no change in meaning.

To use this form, you take a noun and attach the appropriate object marker, followed by 통해서. The visual formula shown on screen is: **Noun + [을/를] 통해(서)**. This structure can be used in almost any context where "through" works in English, such as learning through a medium, moving through a physical space, or hearing news through a specific person.

---

### Example Sentences
The instructor provides four key examples to demonstrate the versatility of the form across different contexts:

> **저는 책을 통해서 한국어를 배웠어요.** / I learned Korean through a book.
> **새가 창문을 통해서 날아 들어왔어요.** / The bird flew in through the window.
> **캠핑을 통해서 새로운 걸 많이 배웠어요.** / I learned many new things [through camping].
> **학교 친구를 통해서 그곳에 대해 들었어요.** / I heard about that through a school friend.

These examples illustrate that 통해서 can handle abstract methods (learning from books or experiences), physical paths (flying through a window), and social intermediaries (hearing news through a friend).

---

### Formal Nuance and Conversational Alternatives
A critical point of the lesson is that 통해서 sounds somewhat formal and is less common in casual, everyday conversation. While it is perfectly correct, native speakers often prefer simpler particles depending on the specific intent of the sentence.

If the "through" refers to a tool or method, the particle **(으)로** is often a more natural choice. For instance, instead of using 통해서 with a book, one could say:
> **저는 책으로 한국어를 배웠어요.** / I learned Korean using a book.

If the "through" refers to receiving information from a person, the particles **에게(서)** or **한테(서)** (meaning "from") are more common. Instead of saying you heard something "through" a friend, you would typically say you heard it "from" them:
> **학교 친구한테 그것에 대해 들었어요.** / I heard about that from a school friend.

The instructor concludes by encouraging learners to recognize 통해서 when they see it in formal writing or news, but to lean toward these simpler particles for their own spoken Korean.
