### Overview
This lesson focuses on essential Korean direction and location words used for navigating and giving instructions. The instructor introduces various nouns and verbs that describe "which way" or "which side" something is located, starting with the versatile bound noun 쪽 and moving into more specific terms like cardinal directions and relative sides.

The learning arc begins with basic directional markers and their common particle pairings, then transitions into compass directions and relative location words like "across from" or "opposite." Finally, the lesson covers practical verbs for movement, such as turning and going straight, which are indispensable for real-world navigation and giving directions to others.

### The Directional Noun 쪽
The word **쪽** is a noun that means "direction" or "way." It is a bound noun, meaning it cannot be used by itself and must be preceded by a descriptive word. On screen, the instructor writes **쪽** and notes its English equivalents as `direction` or `way`. He demonstrates that it is frequently paired with specific particles to indicate movement or state: **으로** (toward), **에** (at/to a location), **이/가** (subject marker for description), and the verb **이다** (to be).

The most common combinations involve the demonstrative pronouns: **이쪽 (this way)**, **그쪽 (that way)**, and **저쪽 (that way over there)**. These are written as single words without spaces. The instructor provides a practical example of how to use these in a sentence:
> **저쪽으로 가세요.** / Please go that way.

He also introduces the question form **어느 쪽이에요? (Which way is it?)** to ask for directions.

---

### Left and Right Directions
Beyond demonstratives, **쪽** is used to form the words for "left" and "right." The instructor explains that **오른** means right and **왼** means left. When combined with **쪽**, they become:
- **오른쪽** — Right (direction/way)
- **왼쪽** — Left (direction/way)

To use these for navigation, the instructor introduces the verb **꺾다**, which means "to turn." He writes the following instruction on the board:
> **왼쪽으로 꺾으세요.** / Turn left (literally: Turn toward the left way).

Additionally, these words can function as adjectives to describe the location of an object. For example, if comparing two things, one might say:
> **왼쪽 강아지가 더 귀여워요.** / The dog on the left is cuter.

### Cardinal Directions
The instructor covers the four cardinal directions found on a compass. He notes that while English speakers usually say "North, South, East, West," the standard Korean order is "East, West, South, North." To help students remember this, he provides the English mnemonic: "Eat Warm Spicy Noodles."
- **동 (East)**
- **서 (West)**
- **남 (South)**
- **북 (North)**

These syllables are combined with **쪽** to create the full directional words: **동쪽 (east)**, **서쪽 (west)**, **남쪽 (south)**, and **북쪽 (north)**. Examples of these in use include:
> **해는 동쪽에서 떠요.** / The sun rises in the east.
> **미국의 남쪽에는 멕시코가 있어요.** / Mexico is in the south of America.

For intermediate directions like "Northeast" or "Southwest," the instructor explains that the first syllable is always either North (**북**) or South (**남**), followed by East (**동**) or West (**서**), resulting in words like **북동쪽 (northeast)** or **남서쪽 (southwest)**.

---

### Describing Sides with 편
While **쪽** refers to a direction or way, **편** refers to a "side" of something. The instructor highlights three common words using this root:
- **건너편** — The other side across (from **건너다**, to cross).
- **반대편** — The opposite side (from **반대**, to oppose).
- **맞은편** — The opposite facing side (from **맞다**, to face).

These are used to describe locations relative to landmarks:
> **길 맞은편에 도서관 있어요.** / There is a library on the other side of the street.
> **백화점 건너편에 카페가 있어요.** / There is a cafe across from the department store.
> **실수로 반대편 버스를 탔어요.** / I took the bus on the opposite side (wrong side) of the road by mistake.

A secondary usage of **편** is "taking sides" in an argument or competition:
> **당신은 누구 편이에요?** / Whose side are you on?

---

### General Direction and Movement Verbs
The instructor introduces **방향** as a regular noun meaning "direction." Unlike **쪽**, it can stand alone.
> **방향을 잃었어요.** / I lost my way / I lost my bearings.

Finally, the lesson concludes with specific verbs for giving driving or walking directions:
- **직진하다** — To go straight.
- **우회전하다** — To make a right turn (using the Hanja **우** for right).
- **좌회전하다** — To make a left turn (using the Hanja **좌** for left).
- **건너다** — To cross.
- **지나다 / 지나가다** — To pass by / To go past.

Practical application sentences for these verbs include:
> **신호등을 지나서 우회전하세요.** / After you pass the traffic light, make a right turn.
> **횡단보도로 건너세요.** / Cross at the crosswalk.
> **그냥 지나가세요.** / Just go by / Just pass by.
