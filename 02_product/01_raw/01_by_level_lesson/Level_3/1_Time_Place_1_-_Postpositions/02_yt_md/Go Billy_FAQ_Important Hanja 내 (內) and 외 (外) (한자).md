### Overview

The instructor introduces a pair of high-frequency Hanja (Sino-Korean characters) that are essential for expanding Korean vocabulary: 내 (內) and 외 (外). Rather than focusing on the mechanics of writing the characters, the lesson emphasizes learning their sounds and core meanings to help students recognize them in both familiar and new words. By understanding these roots, learners can decode complex nouns related to location, nationality, and even family relationships.

---

### Core Meanings: 내 (Inside) and 외 (Outside)

The first character, **내 (內)**, carries the meaning of "inside." Visually, the character is a pictograph representing a person inside a house (a box). In pure Korean, this corresponds to the word **안 (inside)**. The second character, **외 (外)**, means "outside." Its visual origin relates to the moon and performing divination outdoors at night. In pure Korean, this corresponds to the word **바깥 (outside)**. These two characters form a natural pair of opposites used across many different contexts.

---

### City and Country Contexts

The instructor demonstrates how these characters combine with other Hanja to describe locations relative to a city or country. By taking the character **시 (市)**, which means "city" (as seen in 도시 or 서울시), we get two common words: **시내 (downtown/within the city)** and **시외 (outside the city)**. A practical example is a **시외 버스 (intercity bus)**, which travels outside the city limits.

Similarly, when combined with **국 (國)**, meaning "country" (as in 한국 or 미국), we get **국내 (domestic/within the country)** and **국외 (outside the country)**. While 국내 is the standard term for domestic flights or travel (**국내여행**), the instructor notes that for "international" or "abroad," Koreans more commonly use **국제 (international)** or **해외 (overseas/abroad)** rather than 국외. This highlights that while Hanja roots are logical, usage patterns determine which words are most common. Additionally, by flipping the characters to form **외국 (foreign country)**, we can create words like **외국인 (foreigner)** and **외국어 (foreign language)**.

---

### Indoor, Outdoor, and Tutoring

The character **실 (室)** means "room," leading to the terms **실내 (indoors/inside a room)** and **실외 (outdoors)**. Although 실 literally means room, 실내 is used generally for the interior of any building. This is frequently seen on signage, such as **실내에서 금연 (no smoking indoors)**. 

Another interesting application is the word **과외 (tutoring)**. The character **과 (課)** refers to a lesson or subject. Therefore, 과외 literally means "outside of regular lessons." This refers to additional private education, often specified as **개인과외 (private/personal tutoring)**.

---

### Family Titles and the Mother's Side

A critical and unique usage of **외 (外)** occurs in family titles to specify the maternal side of the family. In this context, 외 does not literally mean "outside" in a spatial sense, but rather "outside the main (patriarchal) line." Common titles include:

- **외할아버지** / maternal grandfather
- **외할머니** / maternal grandmother
- **외삼촌** / maternal uncle

The instructor explains that the opposite of this 외 is not 내, but **친 (親)**, which means "close." This is used for the paternal side, such as **친할아버지 (paternal grandfather)**. The instructor suggests this distinction likely stems from Korea's traditional patriarchal society, where the father's side was considered the "internal" or primary family line through which the last name is passed down, while the mother's side was viewed as the "external" line.
