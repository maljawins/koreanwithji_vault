### Core Meanings: 전 (前) and 후 (後)
The character 전 (前) carries the primary meanings of "front" or "before." Its opposite is 후 (後), which means "behind" or "after." These two characters form a pair that defines spatial and temporal relationships in many Korean words. The instructor demonstrates this basic contrast with the words 전진 (forward/advancing) and 후진 (backward/reverse). In the context of driving, 후진 is the standard term used when a car is in reverse.

---

### Time and Food Vocabulary
The Hanja roots are essential for understanding common time-related terms. By combining the character 오 (noon) with these roots, we get 오전 (AM/before noon) and 오후 (PM/after noon). Similarly, the instructor introduces the Hanja root 식 (eating) to explain the word 후식 (dessert). Literally meaning "after eating," 후식 refers to anything consumed once a meal is finished.

---

### Sports and Parts of a Whole
When dealing with things divided into halves, the Hanja root 반 (half) is used. This creates the terms 전반 (the first half/part) and 후반 (the second half/part). These are frequently used in sports. By adding the root 전 (game/battle), we get 전반전 (the first half of a game) and 후반전 (the second half of a game). The instructor provides a practical example sentence:

> **축구 경기 후반전이 시작했어요.** / The second half of the soccer game has started.

---

### Martial Arts and Directions
In specialized contexts like Judo, these roots help define directions of movement. The root 방 means "direction," 낙 means "fall," and 법 means "method." Combining these with our primary roots results in 전방낙법 (forward fall method) and 후방낙법 (backward fall method), which are essential techniques for learning how to fall safely in martial arts.

---

### Using 전 to Mean "Ex" or "Former"
Because 전 means "before" or "previous," it is used as a prefix to indicate a former status or an "ex" relationship. This is applied to both personal relationships and professional titles:
- **전 남자친구** (ex-boyfriend)
- **전 여자친구** (ex-girlfriend)
- **전남편** (ex-husband)
- **전 아내** (ex-wife)
- **전회장** (former chairman/president of a company)

---

### Grammatical Applications: 전에 and 후에
The concepts of "before" and "after" are also codified into specific grammatical structures used with action verbs. To say "before doing [verb]," the pattern is **Action Verb Stem + 기 전에**. To say "after doing [verb]," the pattern is **Action Verb Stem + (으)ㄴ 후에**. The instructor illustrates these with parallel examples:

> **친구를 만나기 전에 숙제를 했어요.** / I did my homework before meeting my friend.
> **친구를 만난 후에 숙제를 했어요.** / I did my homework after meeting my friend.
