## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the concept of prepositions by using a simple English analogy: a preposition describes any position an airplane can occupy in relation to a cloud. While English uses prepositions that precede the noun, Korean uses "postpositions" that function similarly but follow the noun. This lesson focuses on the most common spatial markers used to describe where objects or people are located.

---

### Word Order and Structure

The primary difference between English and Korean spatial markers is their placement. In English, we say "outside of the house," where the word "outside" comes before "house." In Korean, the order is reversed: the noun comes first, followed by the spatial word. Therefore, "outside of the house" becomes "house outside" in a literal Korean translation. This rule applies to all spatial markers in the language.

---

### Common Spatial Markers

The instructor provides a list of essential spatial words that learners will frequently encounter. These words are used to build location-based sentences:

- **위** / above, on
- **아래** / below, under
- **앞** / front
- **뒤** / behind
- **옆** / side, next to
- **안** / inside
- **밖** / outside

While this is not an exhaustive list, mastering these seven terms allows a learner to describe most basic physical relationships between objects.

---

### Example Sentences

The instructor demonstrates how to use these markers in complete sentences. Note how the spatial marker (e.g., **위**, **앞**) follows the noun it describes (e.g., **책상**, **학교**) and is typically followed by the location particle **에** and the verb **있다** (to be/exist).

> **가방이 책상 위에 있어요.** / The bag is on the desk.
> **저는 학교 앞에 있어요.** / I am in front of the school.
> **우리 집 뒤에 갔어요.** / He went behind our house.
> **내 옆에 있어 줘요.** / Stay by my side.
> **환자가 병원 안에 있어요.** / The patient is in the hospital.
> **차가 밖에 있어요.** / The car is outside.

The instructor emphasizes that learning these markers makes Korean speech sound significantly more natural and greatly expands a learner's ability to describe the world around them.
