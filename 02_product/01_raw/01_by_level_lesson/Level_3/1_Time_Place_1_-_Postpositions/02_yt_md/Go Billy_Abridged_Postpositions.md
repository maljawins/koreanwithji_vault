## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the fundamental concept of Korean postpositions by contrasting them with English prepositions. While English uses words like "in," "on," or "at" before a noun, Korean uses equivalent words after the noun. A critical takeaway of this lesson is that Korean postpositions are not a special grammatical category; they are regular nouns that function as location words. Because they are nouns, they require standard particles like the location markers 에 or 에서 to function correctly within a sentence. The lesson covers common spatial nouns and provides detailed nuances for pairs that are often confused by learners, such as 아래 vs. 밑 and 안 vs. 속.

---

### Prepositions vs. Postpositions

The instructor explains the linguistic roots of the terms: "pre" means before and "post" means after. In English, prepositions show place, direction, or time and are placed before the object, such as "inside the cloud." In contrast, Korean uses postpositions, which are placed after the noun they describe. The instructor notes that the technical terms for these are 전치사 (preposition) and 후치사 (postposition), where the Hanja 전 means before and 후 means after.

---

### Postpositions as Regular Nouns

A major point of emphasis is that postpositions in Korean are grammatically treated as normal nouns. This means they can take particles just like any other noun. For example, to say something is "inside the car," you use the word 안 (in/inside). However, simply saying the noun is not enough to show location; you must add a location marker.

> **차 안에 있어요.** / It is in the car.

The instructor demonstrates that because these are nouns, you could technically use the possessive marker 의, though it is rarely done in natural speech. Writing 차의 안 (the car's inside) is grammatically possible but unnecessary. Furthermore, as nouns, these words can act as subjects or objects of a sentence.

> **건물 안이 더 더워요.** / The inside of the building is hotter.
> **다리 아래를 보세요.** / Look under the bridge.

### Common Spatial Postpositions

The instructor introduces several essential spatial nouns used as postpositions. Each is used by placing it after a noun and attaching a particle.

**안 (In / Inside)**
Used for physical location inside an object.
> **차 안에 있어요.** / It is in the car.

**박 (Outside)**
Used to denote the exterior of a space.
> **밖에 비가 와요.** / It is raining outside.
> **집 밖에 비가 와요.** / It is raining outside the house.

**앞 (Front)**
Used for the area in front of something or moving forward.
> **카페 앞에서 기다리고 있어요.** / I am waiting in front of the cafe.

**뒤 (Behind)**
Used for the area at the back of an object.
> **강아지가 문 뒤에 숨었어요.** / The dog hid behind the door.

**위 (Top / On / Above)**
Used for the surface or the space above something.
> **나무 위에 원숭이가 있어요.** / There is a monkey on top of the tree.

**옆 (Side / Next to)**
Used for the area beside an object.
> **집 옆에 차를 주차했어요.** / I parked the car next to the house.

---

### Emphasizing with 바로

While not a postposition itself, the adverb 바로 (right / directly) is introduced as a way to provide emphasis. It is placed immediately before a spatial noun or time word to mean "right there" or "right then." Examples include 바로 지금 (right now) and 바로 앞 (right in front).

---

### Nuance Comparison: 아래 vs. 밑

Both 아래 and 밑 can be translated as "below" or "under," but they carry different nuances. 아래 refers to being below a certain level or relative height. For example, if a cat is below the level of a window, you use 아래.

> **창문 아래에 고양이가 있어요.** / There is a cat below the window.

In contrast, 밑 implies being underneath an object, often suggesting the base or foundation of that object. It is used when something is physically under the bottom surface of something else.

> **책상 밑에서 돈을 주웠어요.** / I picked up money from under the desk.

The instructor notes that 아래 is exclusively used for "below" in documents (e.g., "sign below"). However, both can be used metaphorically to mean "under the influence/instruction of" someone.

---

### Nuance Comparison: 안 vs. 속

Both 안 and 속 mean "inside," but they have distinct applications. 안 is the general word for physical interior space and is the most common choice for everyday objects like bags. 속 carries the nuance of being "deep inside" or "within."

While they are interchangeable for physical objects like a stomach (배 안 / 배 속), 속 has unique metaphorical uses. It is used for the "inside" of one's heart or mind (마음속) and for being within a situation. Additionally, 속 is used for being "surrounded by" something, such as deep in a forest or mountains.

> **숲 속에 곰이 살아요.** / A bear lives deep in the forest.
> **산속에 호랑이가 살아요.** / Tigers live in the mountains.

The instructor provides a "pro tip": if you are unsure which to use for a physical object, choose 안.
