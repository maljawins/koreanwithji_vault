## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common frustration for Korean learners: the words 밑 and 아래 both translate to "under" or "below" in English dictionaries, making their differences nearly impossible to discern through translation alone. While the two words are interchangeable in many everyday contexts, they possess distinct nuances rooted in their formal Korean definitions. 밑 refers to being located directly underneath a specific object, while 아래 is defined more broadly as being at a lower level or position relative to something else.

The lesson moves from these basic definitions to practical usage, demonstrating where they overlap and where they diverge. The instructor uses visual aids and specific spatial examples—such as chairs, stairs, and mountains—to show how choosing one word over the other can change how a listener perceives the physical location of an object. The goal is not to fear being "wrong," as both are often acceptable, but to understand how a native speaker might interpret the spatial relationship differently based on the choice of postposition.

---

### General Overlap and Interchangeability
In most common situations, 밑 and 아래 can be used interchangeably without any loss of meaning. For example, if you want to say that something is below a physical object like a chair or a bridge, both words are perfectly correct. The instructor writes out the examples:

- **의자 밑** / Below the chair
- **의자 아래** / Below the chair

This flexibility also extends to figurative language, such as describing someone's influence or a student-teacher relationship. To say you "studied under" a certain person, you can use either word:

> **사람 밑에서 배웠어요.** / I learned under [that] person.
> **사람 아래에서 배웠어요.** / I learned under [that] person.

---

### The Exception: Documents and Digital Content
There is one specific context where the two words are not interchangeable. When referring to information located further down in a document, on a website, or on a printed page (equivalent to "see below"), you must use 아래. Using 밑 in this context sounds unnatural to native speakers. The instructor provides a formal example sentence often found in tests or instructions:

> **아래의 문장을 읽고 질문에 대답하시오.** / Read the sentence below and answer the question.

In this specific case, substituting 아래 with 밑 would be incorrect.

---

### Nuance: Base vs. Relative Level
The core difference in nuance lies in how the space is perceived. The instructor explains that 밑 often carries the connotation of the "base" or "foundation" of an object, frequently implying that the item is on the ground directly beneath the object. In contrast, 아래 emphasizes a "relative level," suggesting something is simply lower than the reference point.

The instructor illustrates this with the example of a storage area (창고) near stairs (계단):

- **계단 밑에 창고가 있어요.** / There is storage under the stairs. (This suggests the storage is at the very bottom/base of the stairs, likely on the floor level directly underneath the structure.)
- **계단 아래에 창고가 있어요.** / There is storage below the stairs. (This emphasizes that relative to the stairs, the storage is at a lower level, which could potentially mean a basement level or just generally lower in space.)

A similar distinction applies to a mountain (산):

- **산 밑에 돌이 있어요.** / There is a stone at the bottom of the mountain. (This implies the stone is at the base/foot of the mountain.)
- **산 아래에 돌이 있어요.** / There is a stone below the mountain. (This could imply the stone is literally underground, below the level of the mountain itself.)

While both might be used to describe a stone at the foot of a mountain, 밑 is the preferred and more natural choice when you mean "at the base."
