## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the versatile particle 에서, contrasting it with the previously learned particle 에. While both relate to location, they serve different grammatical functions depending on whether the sentence describes a state of being, a destination, or an active event. The instructor guides the learner through the two primary uses of 에서: marking the location of an action and indicating a point of origin.

The learning arc begins with a review of 에 to establish a baseline, then introduces 에서 as the "action location" marker. From there, the lesson transitions into using 에서 to mean "from," specifically in the context of stating one's origin. Finally, the instructor introduces a polite sentence-ending form, 이오/요, which allows speakers to remain polite even when omitting verbs in short responses.

---

### 에서 for Locations of Action
The primary use of 에서 is to mark the location where an action happens. This is distinct from the particle 에, which marks the location where something exists (with 있다/없다) or the destination one is moving toward (with 가다/오다). The instructor provides a clear comparison: use 에 for "to school" or "in my room" (existence), but use 에서 for "at the library" or "at the hospital" when an activity like studying or working is taking place.

Examples of 에서 as "at" or "in":
- **우리 아들은 도서관에서 공부해요.** / My son studies at the library.
- **진영 씨는 병원에서 일해요.** / Jinyeong works at the hospital.
- **저는 한국에서 살고 싶어요.** / I want to live in Korea.

A special note is made for the verb 살다 (to live). While living is an action, it is also a state of being. Therefore, 살다 is an exception that can be used with either 에 or 에서 with no change in meaning. Both **저는 미국에 살요** and **저는 미국에서 살아요** are grammatically correct.

---

### 에서 for Origin (From)
The second major function of 에서 is to indicate "from" a location. This usage often appears with verbs of motion to show where a movement started. The instructor demonstrates this with examples such as **빌리 씨는 학교에서 나갔어요** (Billy went outside from school) and **빌리 씨는 이제 집에서 올 거예요** (Billy will come from home now).

This "from" meaning is most commonly used to state one's nationality or hometown by combining a location with 에서 and the past tense of 오다 (to come). To ask someone where they are from, the question is **어디서 왔어요?**. The response follows the pattern: [Location] + 에서 왔어요.
- **저는 한국에서 왔어요.** / I am from Korea.
- **저는 미국에서 왔어요.** / I am from America.

The instructor notes that in natural speech, 에서 is frequently shortened. **어디에서** becomes **어디서**, **여기에서** becomes **여기서**, **거기에서** becomes **거기서**, and **저기에서** becomes **저기서**.

---

### Making Short Sentences Polite with 이오/요
When answering questions, it is common to give short one-word answers. However, in Korean, simply saying a noun like **미국** (America) without a verb can sound blunt or impolite. To maintain politeness without using a full sentence, speakers can attach the ending 이오 or 요.

The rules for attachment are based on the final sound of the word:
- Attach **이오** after a word ending in a consonant: **미국이오** (America [polite]).
- Attach **요** after a word ending in a vowel: **저요** (Me [polite]).
- Attach **요** if the word ends with the consonant ㄹ.

This form can be attached to nouns, adverbs, or even other particles to turn them into polite fragments. Examples include **저는요** (As for me?), **언제요?** (When?), **어디요?** (Where?), **누가요?** (Who?), and **정말요?** (Really?).
