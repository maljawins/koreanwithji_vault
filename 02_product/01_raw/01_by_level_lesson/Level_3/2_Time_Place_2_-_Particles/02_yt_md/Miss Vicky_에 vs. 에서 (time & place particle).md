## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor provides a comprehensive breakdown of the particles 에 and 에서, which both function as time and place markers in Korean. The lesson is structured to first address their roles in time expressions and then move into the more complex distinctions between them when used as place markers. The instructor emphasizes that while these particles may seem interchangeable to beginners, their usage is strictly governed by the nature of the following verb—specifically whether the verb indicates a state of existence or a dynamic action.

The teaching style utilizes handwritten notes and real-time sentence construction to illustrate how these particles transform the meaning of a sentence. By the end of the lesson, students should understand not only the basic "at/in" meanings but also the directional "to/from" functions and common colloquial abbreviations used by native speakers.

---

### 에 and 에서 as Time Particles
When used with time nouns, 에 generally means "at," "in," or "on" a specific time. The instructor lists several categories of time nouns that take 에: specific hours like 한시에 (at 1 o'clock), times of day such as 아침에 (in the morning), days of the week like 월요일에 (on Monday), and broader periods like 주말에 (on the weekend). It also applies to months, years, and seasons. For example, one might say 저는 봄에 제프를 만났어요 (I met Jeff in spring). It further extends to specific periods of activity like 방학에 (during vacation) or 점심 시간에 (at lunch time).

A critical exception is noted for the words 오늘 (today), 내일 (tomorrow), and 어제 (yesterday). These words are used independently without the particle 에. Saying 오늘에 is grammatically incorrect; one should simply use 오늘.

In contrast, when 에서 is used as a time particle, it means "from." It is almost always paired with the particle 까지 (until/to) to create a time range. For example, 한 시에서 두 시까지 (from 1 o'clock to 2 o'clock). The instructor notes that in time expressions, 에서 can often be replaced by 부터, which also means "from," though 에서 remains perfectly acceptable.

---

### 에 vs. 에서: Presence vs. Action
The most common point of confusion for learners is using 에 and 에서 with places. The instructor establishes a clear rule: 에 indicates the location of a person or object's presence or existence, while 에서 indicates the location where a specific action takes place.

To identify which to use, one must look at the verb. If the verb is 있다 (to be/exist), 살다 (to live), or 존재하다 (to exist), the particle 에 is used. For example, 저는 도서관에 있어요 (I am in the library). If the verb is an action verb like 공부하다 (to study), 일하다 (to work), or 데이트를 하다 (to date), the particle 에서 is used. For example, 저는 도서관에서 공부해요 (I study in the library).

The instructor highlights a few specific verbs that follow the "presence" rule with 에:
- **있다 (To be):** 저는 한국에 있어요 (I am in Korea).
- **살다 (To live):** 저는 한국에 살아요 (I live in Korea). Note: 살다 is unique because both 에 and 에서 are technically allowed, though 에 is often considered more natural for general residence.
- **존재하다 (To exist):** Used for the existence of things, such as creatures on Earth.
- **출석하다 / 참석하다 (To be present/attend):** Used for attending class or events, e.g., 과학 수업에 출석했어요 (I was present in science class).

For 에서, any dynamic action verb applies. The instructor provides a humorous example using bathroom slang: 저는 화장실에서 일을 봐요 (I am doing my "business" in the bathroom). Here, 일을 보다 (literally "to see work") is the action, necessitating 에서.

---

### 에 vs. 에서: Directional "To" and "From"
The second major distinction involves movement. When paired with verbs of motion like 가다 (to go), 오다 (to come), or 도착하다 (to arrive), 에 means "to" a destination.
- **에 (To):** 학교에 안 갔어요 (I didn't go to school).
- **에 (To):** 내일 우리 집에 와 (Come to my house tomorrow).
- **에 (To):** 공항에 도착했어요 (I arrived at/to the airport).

Conversely, 에서 indicates the starting point, meaning "from." This is most famously seen in the phrase 어느 나라에서 왔어요? (Which country are you from?). It marks the origin of the movement. The instructor demonstrates a complex sentence: 학교에서 집으로 갔어요 (I went home from school). In this context, 에서 marks the start (school), and the instructor notes that while 에 can mean "to," using 으로 for the destination in this specific "from-to" construction often sounds more natural, a topic reserved for the next lesson.

---

### Colloquial Abbreviation: 에서 to 서
The instructor concludes with a practical tip on native speech patterns. In casual conversation, Koreans habitually shorten 에서 to just 서. This abbreviation can happen regardless of whether the particle means "at/in" or "from."
- **강남서 마포까지** (From Gangnam to Mapo)
- **사무실서 일해요** (I work at the office)
- **캐나다서 왔어요** (I came from Canada)

While using the full 에서 is never wrong, hearing 서 after a place noun might confuse learners if they aren't aware it is simply a shortened version of 에서. The meaning (whether "at" or "from") is determined by the verb and the context of the sentence.
