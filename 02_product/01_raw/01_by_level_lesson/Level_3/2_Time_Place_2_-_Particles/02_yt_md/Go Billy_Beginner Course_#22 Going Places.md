## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the essential particle 에, which is used to mark locations in Korean sentences. This lesson focuses on two primary functions of 에: indicating a destination when moving (going to a place) and indicating a location where someone or something exists (being at a place). By the end of the lesson, students will be able to construct basic sentences about their movements and their current locations using the verbs 가다 (to go) and 있다 (to exist).

---

### The Particle 에 for Destination
The particle 에 attaches directly after a noun to mark it as a location. When used with movement verbs like 가다 (to go), it translates to the English preposition "to." The instructor provides a clear structural example: 빌리는 서울에 가요 (Billy goes to Seoul). In this sentence, 에 is attached to the noun 서울 (Seoul) to show that it is the destination of the action.

The instructor presents a conversation practice between two characters, 주연 and 경화, to demonstrate this in a natural context. 주연 says, "저는 집에 가고 싶어요" (I want to go home), where 에 is attached to 집 (house/home). 경화 responds, "저는 학교에 가고 싶어요" (I want to go to school), attaching 에 to 학교 (school). These examples show how 에 functions as a destination marker regardless of the specific place noun used.

---

### The Particle 에 with the Verb 있다
Beyond marking a destination, 에 is used to mark the location where someone or something is currently located. In this context, it translates to "at" or "in" in English. This usage frequently pairs with the verb 있다, which literally means "to exist" but translates naturally to "is," "am," or "are" when describing location.

The instructor provides the example sentence 빌리는 집에 있어요 (Billy is at home / Billy exists at home). Here, 에 marks the location where the subject exists. To practice this, the instructor encourages viewers to use a provided vocabulary list to create their own sentences. The vocabulary includes:
- **영화관** (movie theater)
- **병원** (hospital)
- **공원** (park)
- **도서관** (library)
- **은행** (bank)

---

### Important Usage Notes and Restrictions
The instructor concludes with three critical rules for using the particle 에. First, 에 is strictly a location marker; it can only be used with places, not with people or animals. To say "to a person," a different particle must be used, which will be covered in a future lesson.

Second, while the verb 있다 can also be used to express possession (to have something), its primary literal meaning is "to exist." The possessive usage will also be detailed in a later lesson.

Finally, a common grammatical mistake for learners is using the object marker (을/를) with the verb 있다. The instructor emphasizes that you must always use the location particle 에 when stating where something exists. For example, you must say 에 있어요, and never use the object marker with 있다 in this context.
