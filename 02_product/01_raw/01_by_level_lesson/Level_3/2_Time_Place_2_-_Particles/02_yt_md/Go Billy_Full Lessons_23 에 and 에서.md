## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the essential Korean particles 에 and 에서, which are used to indicate location, destination, and origin. While both particles relate to places, they function differently depending on the verb used in the sentence. The instructor also introduces the time-related particles 부터 and 까지 to express "from" and "until." The lesson builds upon previous concepts of directionality, shifting the focus from people to locations and timeframes.

---

### The Particle 에: Destination and Existence

The particle 에 has two primary functions. First, it acts as a destination marker meaning "to." It is used with verbs of motion like 가다 (to go) and 오다 (to come) to indicate where one is headed. The instructor provides the example 저는 학교에 가요 (I go to school). He notes that while English sometimes omits the word "to" (as in "I'm going home"), Korean strictly requires a particle like 에 after the destination. This is seen in the sentence 빨리 집에 와요 (Come home quickly), where 에 follows 집 (home).

The second use of 에 is to show where something or someone exists, typically paired with the verbs 있다 (to exist/to be) and 없다 (to not exist/to not be). In this context, it translates to "at" or "in." For example, 저는 학교에 있어요 translates to "I am at school" or "I am in school." Conversely, 사무실에 연필이 없어요 means "There are no pencils in the office." A critical grammatical note is that 에 cannot be used with the copula 이다 (to be). While you can say 저는 학교에 있어요 to describe your location, saying 저는 학교에 이에요 is grammatically incorrect because 이다 expresses identity or equality rather than location.

---

### The Particle 에서: Origin and Action Locations

The particle 에서 also serves two main purposes. First, it indicates origin, meaning "from a location." This is commonly used with the verb 오다 (to come) to state where someone is from. The instructor uses the example 저는 미국에서 왔어요 (I'm from America / I came from America). It can also be used to show a range from one place to another, such as 병원에서 집에 (from the hospital to home).

The second, and perhaps most distinct, use of 에서 is to mark the location where an action takes place. Unlike 에, which marks existence with 있다, 에서 is used with action verbs like 공부하다 (to study) or 먹다 (to eat). For instance, 저는 도서관에서 공부해요 (I study in the library) uses 에서 because studying is an active process. Other examples include 그는 학교에서 밥을 먹어요 (He eats at school) and 철수는 주방에서 요리합니다 (Chul-soo cooks in the kitchen). 

The instructor highlights a specific exception involving the verb 살다 (to live). Although living is technically an action, it is a special case where both 에 and 에서 are acceptable. Therefore, both 그 사람은 LA에서 살아요 and 그 사람은 LA에 살아요 are correct and carry the same meaning.

---

### Time Particles: 부터 and 까지

To express "from" and "until" in the context of time or location, Korean uses 부터 and 까지. These are distinct from the particles used for people. 부터 means "from" (starting point), and 까지 means "until" or "up to" (end point). The instructor references the video's introduction where he says 한시부터 세시까지 (from one o'clock until three o'clock). Another example provided is 두시부터 열시까지 (from two o'clock to ten o'clock). These particles are straightforward and follow the noun they modify to establish a specific timeframe or physical range.
