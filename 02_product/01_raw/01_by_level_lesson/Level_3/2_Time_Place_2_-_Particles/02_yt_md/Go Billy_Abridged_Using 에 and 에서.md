## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson provides a comprehensive breakdown of the Korean location and time particles 에 and 에서, along with the directional and temporal markers 까지 and 부터. The instructor focuses on the functional differences between these particles, specifically how they distinguish between a location where something simply exists or is moving toward, versus a location where a dynamic action takes place.

The learning arc begins with the basic "existence" and "destination" functions of 에, moves into the "action" and "origin" functions of 에서, and concludes with how to express ranges of space and time using 까지, 부터, and 에서. Throughout the video, the instructor uses a whiteboard to visually map out these relationships, providing clear example sentences and highlighting common exceptions and optional usage rules.

---

### The Location and Destination Marker 에
The particle 에 is primarily defined as a location marker that indicates where something exists or where someone is going. On the whiteboard, the instructor notes that it marks a location for "going 'to'" or where something "exists." It is frequently translated into English as "to," "in," or "at," depending on the context of the sentence.

When used to indicate a destination, it follows the noun representing the place. For example, in the sentence 저는 집에 가요 (I go home), the particle 에 marks the house as the specific destination of the movement. The instructor points out that while English speakers don't say "I go to home," the Korean structure requires the location marker to define the relationship between the noun and the verb.

When used to indicate existence, 에 is paired with verbs like 있다 (to exist/be) or 없다 (to not exist/not be). The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence 철수는 지금 집에 있어요 (Chul-soo is at home right now). Here, the particle indicates the static location of the subject. This also applies to relative positions, such as 책은 책상 위에 있어요 (The book is on top of the desk), where 에 is attached to 위 (top/above) to mark the specific location of existence.

An important grammatical exception is introduced for the words 어디 (where), 여기 (here), 거기 (there), and 저기 (over there). For these specific words, the particle 에 is completely optional and is frequently omitted in natural speech. One can say either 어디에 가요? or simply 어디 가요? (Where are you going?) without any change in meaning.

---

### The Time Marker 에
Beyond physical locations, 에 also functions as a time marker, indicating the specific point in time at which an action occurs. The instructor describes this as marking a "location in time." For example, 셋시에 만나요 (Let's meet at three o'clock) uses 에 to pinpoint the exact hour of the meeting.

The instructor emphasizes that 에 should be used with specific times and certain time nouns like 아침 (morning), 점심 (lunchtime/afternoon), and 저녁 (evening). However, there are several common time words that never take the particle 에: 어제 (yesterday), 오늘 (today), and 내일 (tomorrow). Additionally, words starting with 매 (every), such as 매일 (every day), 매주 (every week), and 매년 (every year), do not use 에.

A specific rule for multiple time nouns is also covered: when a sentence contains a string of time-related words, the particle 에 is only attached to the last one. Instead of saying "Friday-에, afternoon-에, one o'clock-에," a Korean speaker says 금요일 오후 한 시에 (At 1:00 PM on Friday).

---

### The Action Location Marker 에서
The particle 에서 is used to mark the location where a specific action or activity takes place. Unlike 에, which is for existence or destination, 에서 is for "doing" something. The instructor illustrates this with the example 저는 그 식당에서 일해요 (I work at that restaurant). Because "working" is a dynamic action, 에서 is required instead of 에.

Other examples provided include 노래방에서 노래를 불러요 (I sing a song at a karaoke room) and 저는 매일 도서관에서 공부해요 (I study at the library every day). In these cases, the library or karaoke room is the "stage" where the action happens. The instructor notes that if you were just "in" the library without performing a specific action, you would use 에, but the act of studying (공부하다) necessitates 에서.

A unique case is the verb 살다 (to live). The instructor explains that both 에 and 에서 are acceptable with 살다. You can say 영국에 살아요 or 영국에서 살아요 (I live in England). This is because living can be viewed as both a state of existence and a continuous action.

---

### Expressing "From" and "Until" with 에서, 부터, and 까지
The particle 에서 has a second function: it can mean "from" a location. When paired with movement verbs like 오다 (to come), it indicates the point of origin. For example, 집에서 왔어요 means "I came from home." This is also how you state your nationality or origin: 저는 미국에서 왔어요 (I am from America).

To express a range from one point to another, Korean uses 에서 (from) and 까지 (until/to). The instructor provides the example 집에서 학교까지 걸어갔어요 (I walked from home to school). 까지 can be used for both physical distance and time, meaning "up to" or "until." For example, 내일까지 해주세요 (Please do it by tomorrow) or 2시까지 시간 있어요 (I have time until 2:00).

For time specifically, the particle 부터 is used to mean "from," rather than 에서. While 에서 is for locations, 부터 is for time. To say "from 2:00 to 3:00," the correct phrasing is 두 시부터 세 시까지.

Finally, the instructor notes a contraction: when 에서 is used with 여기, 거기, or 저기, the 에 is often dropped, leaving just 서. For example, 거기서 (from there) or 어디서 왔어요? (Where are you from?). While the 에 is optional in these cases, the 서 must remain to indicate the "from" or "action location" meaning.
