**Time & Location Particle 에 & 에서**

Okay, we’ve seen the particle “에” some example sentences we used in level 1, which I briefly explained it as the location marker.

Now, there is one more location marker and that’s 에서.

A lot of learners get confused between the two. So in this lesson, we’re going to go over each one step by step.

Before we talk about them in depth, just keep in mind that 에 can mark both TIME & LOCATION but 에서 can mark only the location.

**When 에 marks the time**

First of all, let’s look at how to use 에 to mark the time in a sentence.

에 is used to mark a specific time when an action occurs.

So you would say 저는 오후 한시에 점심을 먹어요 meaning at 1 o’clock in the afternoon

오후 means afternoon.

And you only add it to the final noun only. So it’s not 오후에 열두시에, it’s just 오후 열두시.

Same thing happens here.

금요일 밤에 만나자

And this applies to 에서 as well.

**Do not add 에 after these words**

But there are some words where you shouldn’t add 에 to. And that’s because these words are treated like adverbs which means you don’t need 에 to indicate that that when it happening.

I have added a list of words and their meaning in a separate file so you can download it now.

And let’s use two of those words.

어제 means yesterday.

To say “I studied Korean yesterday”, it’s “저는 어제 한국어를 공부했어요”.

It’s not 어제**에,** it’s somewhat similar to how you don’t say “On yesterday” in English. So t’s just 어제

Same with 항상 which means “always”

Its not 항상에 it’s just 항상 so to say “I always sleep early” is “저는 항상 일찍 자요”

**When 에 marks the location.**

Next let’s look at when 에 marks the location.

First, when there’s a movement involved to a certain location.

So, it’s very similar to “TO” in English.

저는 가게에 갔어요 I went “to” the store

바지를 옷장에 넣었어요. I put the pants in the closet, or more like “into” the closet

Secondly, 에 is used to note a state of simply existing. There’s nothing happening.

책상에 열쇠가 있었어요. The key was ON the desk. Nothing happened here, right? The key didn’t do anything. It was just there on the desk.

식당에 사람이 많았어요. There were many people in the restaurant. Again, nothing happened here. People just were in the restaurant.

**여기 저기 거기**

[여기 Here 저기 Over there 거기 There]

In the lesson 6, A is B, while talking about 이거 – this, 저거 – that over there, 그거 – that, I also briefly introduced you to 여기, 저기, and 거기.

Now, notice 여기 저기 and 거기 start with the same consonant as these, ㅇ ㅈ and ㄱ.

Same idea but for places.

여기 is here.

저기 is “over there” as in, it’s far from you but you can still see it.

거기 is just “there”. You use 거기 in 2 cases. For places that are “kind of” close to you but not close enough to call 여기 like “Right there” “바로 거기” And it’s also used for places that are not in your sight like “That place we went the other day”

**에 is optional for 여기 저기, and 거기**

These words INHERENTLY indicate locations. So for these words, 에 is optional.

**Example sentences**

여기(에) 과자가 있어요 – Here is a cookie

저기(에) 사람이 많아요 – There is a lot of people

거기(에) 없었어요 – It wasn’t there.

**When 에 is necessary**

However, for regular nouns, you cannot drop 에 because your sentence can be vague.

For example, if you wanted to say “I’m home”, you have to say “저는 집에 있어요”.

Because “집 있어요” will be interpreted as “집이 있어요” which means “I have a house.”

Not what you wanted to say.

**에서 marks the location**

Now, if something IS happening at a location? That’s when you use 에서.

So, 집에서 저녁을 먹었어요. I ATE dinner at home. The eating took place at home. And in this case, we use 에서.

Here’s another example.

공항에서 만나자 – Let’s MEET at the airport.

**Special Case 살다 – To live**

저는 한국에 살아요. I live in Korea.

저는 한국에서 살아요

With 살다, to live, both versions are correct. Because act of living can be both stationary and active.

This is sort of the only action verb which you can use both 에 and 에서.

But, Koreans use 에 살다 much more than 에서 살다.

**에서 - From**

에서 could also mean “from”

영어를 책에서 배웠어요 – I learned English from a book.

So although 책 the book is technically not a location, figuratively, it could be treated like a location.

So, this would mean I learned English from a book.

**에서 - Abbreviation**

And 에서 could be abbreviated to just 서 after certain words.

Those are 어디 – where 여기 – here 저기 – over there and 거기 - there

But to mark a specific location like “미국에서” from U.S, you would have to say 에서 not 서.

**에 VS 에서 Summary**

So, this is the comparison of 에 and 에서.

Although both 에 & 에서 mark locations, only 에 can mark the time.

에 as a location marker, marks where something exists or the movement of a noun.

And 에서 as a location marker, marks where something is happening. And it could also mean from

And now, we can make longer sentences like these, using what we learned about verbs.

S T L O V

저는 아침에 욕실에서 샤워를 했어요.

저는 저녁에 집에서 옷을 세탁기에 넣었어요.

**부터**

부터 is another particle that means from and it marks the absolute starting point. That’s the main difference of 부터 from 에서. So if you just casually wanted to say “from a place”, you can use 에서. And 부터 sounds like “All the way from” or “Since”

처음부터 우리는 항상 친구였어요

몇 시부터 시간이 되세요?

Here’s a little reminder about 되다 where one of its meanings is “to be okay / to be acceptable”. 시간 means time. So 시간이 되다 means you’re free

서울역부터 부산역까지 세 시간 걸려요

From Seoul to busan, it takes 3 hours

And 부터 can be used to mark any noun and it means “first”.

빨리 샤워부터 해.

Take a shower, first. Before doing anything else, go take a shower.

**까지**

까지 can also mark time, location. And it means Until, up to.

교수님은 저녁까지 일하세요

저는 세시까지 시간이 있어요

저는 집에서 회사까지 지하철을 타요

And it can also mean “even” when you put it after a noun.

Show “나까지” – Even I/me

This doesn’t happen in English. But in Spanish, you can say something like “Hasta yo”. “Hasta” means “Until” like in the phrase “Hasta luego” – Until next time. And “yo” means “I”.

Put em together, “Hasta yo” means “Even I”.

“Hasta yo tengo que hacerlo?” – Even I have to do it? It’s like how did it get to this point (until me) where even I have to do it?

And it’s exactly the same concept in Korean.

So, 그것까지 했어? Means did you that too? like did you go so far as to do that?

**FROM A TO B**

You can say A에서 B까지 and A부터 B까지 but when you’re talking about time, you can only use 부터, not 에서.

For a location, both are interchangeable.
