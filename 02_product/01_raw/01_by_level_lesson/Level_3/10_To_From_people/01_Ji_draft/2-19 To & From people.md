Now, let’s talk about how to say **“to someone”** in Korean.

In English, you can say:
“I gave John a candy”

And people always say things like “존을 줬어요” and this is wrong because it means you gave “John” TO someone,

So in Korean, we need to change the phrasing to “I gave a candy to John.” In Korean, we use a particle to mark the recipient.

So, you’d say:

* 존**에게** 사탕을 줬어요
* 존**한테** 사탕을 줬어요

에게 and 한테 both mean: *“I gave a candy to John.”*

They’re interchangeable but:

* **한테** is much more common in spoken Korean.
* **에게** is more formal and usually appears in written Korean — in essays, news articles, or formal documents.

There’s also an **honorific** version:

* **께**

**께** is used when you’re speaking respectfully to or about someone higher in status — like a teacher, boss, or elder.

For example:

* 선생님**께** 사탕을 드렸어요
  (*I gave a candy to the teacher.*)

But you could also say:

* 선생님**에게** 사탕을 드렸어요
* 선생님**한테** 사탕을 드렸어요

The point is as long as you use the right kind of verb whether it’d be honorific or humble, 께 is not always necessary.

So in the example sentence, we switched the verb **주다 → 드리다** to keep the humble tone consistent.

Remember:

* 한테 → informal/conversational
* 에게 → formal/written
* 께 → honorific

**From people**

Now let’s look at how to say **“from someone”** in Korean — like when you receive something **from a person.**

You’ll use: **에게서**, **한테서**, or **께**. The 서 here adds the meaning of "from" — but it's **optional**, so you might hear people say it with or without 서.

For example:

* 지수**에게(서)** 사탕을 받았어요
* 지수**한테(서)** 사탕을 받았어요
  Both mean: *“I received a candy from Jisoo.”*

Again, **한테서** is more common in spoken Korean, and **에게서** is more formal or written.

Now, the honorific form is a little different:
When receiving from someone you respect — like a teacher or parents — use **께**.
So:

* 부모님**께** 선물을 받았어요
  *“I received a gift from my parents.”*

But here’s something important:
**께서** and **께** are not the same thing.
**께서** is actually the **honorific version of the subject marker** — it replaces **이/가** when the subject is someone of higher status.

For example:

* 선생님**께서** 저에게 사탕을 주셨어요
  (*The teacher gave me a candy.*)
  Here, **께서** is marking *“the teacher”* as the subject in an honorific way.

You can also say:

* 선생님이 저에게 사탕을 주셨어요
  (*Same meaning, just less honorific*)

But when you’re saying *you received* something from the teacher, it becomes:

* 선생님**께** 사탕을 받았어요
  (“I received a candy from the teacher”) — and **not** 께서.

So quick recap:

* **에게(서)** / **한테(서)** → from someone
* **께** → from someone respectfully

**께서** → honorific subject marker (not "from")
