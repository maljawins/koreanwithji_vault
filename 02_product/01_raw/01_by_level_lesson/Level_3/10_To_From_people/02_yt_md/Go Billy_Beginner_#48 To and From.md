### Overview
This lesson focuses on the particles used to indicate directionality—specifically "to" and "from"—when dealing with both people and locations. The instructor breaks down the formal and informal versions of these particles, explains how context allows for simplification, and provides a detailed guide on the pronunciation of the consonant ㅎ (hieut), which often softens or disappears in natural speech. The lesson concludes with a conversation practice that integrates all the particles taught.

---

### To and From a Person: 에게 and 에게서
To say "to" a person in a formal or written context, the particle 에게 is attached directly after the person. For example, "I gave it to Billy" is translated as 빌리 씨에게 주었어요. Other examples include 지민 씨에게 사탕을 주세요 (Please give the candy to Jimin) and 저에게 한국어를 가르쳐 주세요 (Please teach Korean to me). The instructor also introduces the verb 보여주다 (to show) in the sentence 우리에게 보여 주세요 (Please show it to us).

To express "from" a person, the particle 에게서 is used. Examples provided are 빌리 씨에게서 선물을 받았어요 (I got a present from Billy) and 선생님에게서 들었어요 (I heard it from the teacher). The instructor notes a conjugation exception for the verb 듣다 (to listen/hear): the ㄷ at the bottom of the stem changes to ㄹ when followed by a vowel, resulting in 들어요 or 들었어요. Another example is 지민 씨에게서 전화가 왔어요 (I got a call from Jimin), where the instructor explains that 전화가 오다 literally means "a phone call comes."

A critical point of simplification is that the 서 in 에게서 is optional. In practice, 에게 can mean both "to" and "from." The intended meaning is almost always clear from the context of the sentence. For instance, in 선생님에게 들었어요, it would not make sense to "hear to" a teacher, so it must mean "heard from."

---

### To and From a Location: 에서 and 까지
When talking about locations rather than people, different particles are required. 에서 is used to mean "from" a location, while 까지 means "to," "as far as," or "until." A common sentence structure combining both is 집에서 학교까지 갔어요 (I went from home to school). The instructor highlights that 까지 implies going up to a certain point but no further. A practical application is telling a taxi driver where to go: 은행까지 가 주세요 (Please go to the bank / as far as the bank).

---

### Informal Alternatives: 한테 and 한테서
While 에게 and 에게서 are more formal and common in writing, 한테 and 한테서 are the preferred versions for everyday spoken Korean. They function identically to their formal counterparts. To say "to" a person informally, use 한테, as in 아빠한테 주고 싶어요 (I want to give it to dad). To say "from," use 한테서 or simply 한테. For example, 누구한테 받았어요? (Who did you get that from?). While these are less formal, the instructor clarifies that they are not impolite and are suitable for most social situations.

---

### Pronunciation of the Consonant ㅎ
The instructor provides a deep dive into how the ㅎ (hieut) sound changes in speech. Generally, ㅎ is much softer than the English "H" and often disappears or blends with surrounding sounds. There are three main rules demonstrated. First, ㅎ disappears before the silent consonant ㅇ, so 좋아요 is pronounced as 조아요. Second, when a regular consonant (like ㄷ) comes before or after ㅎ, they combine into a strong consonant, and the ㅎ sound vanishes; thus, 좋다 is pronounced as 조타. Third, in many common words, the ㅎ simply softens to the point of being silent: 전혀 (totally) sounds like 저녀, 올해 (this year) sounds like 오래, 감히 (daringly) sounds like 가미, and 전화 (phone call) sounds like 저나. This explains why 한테 and 한테서 often sound like 안테 and 안테서.

---

### Communication Verbs and Directional Particles
A specific rule for communication is introduced: when using verbs like "to call," "to tell," or "to ask," you must use the "to/from" particles (에게/한테) rather than the object marker (을/를). This is because you are communicating "to" a person.
- 빌리 씨한테 전화를 했어요 (I called Billy / literally: did a phone call to Billy)
- 빌리 씨한테 말하고 싶지 않아요 (I don't want to tell Billy / literally: speak to Billy)
- 빌리 씨한테 물어봤어요 (I asked Billy / literally: asked to Billy)
