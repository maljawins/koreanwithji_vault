## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor provides a comprehensive guide to expressing "to" and "from" a person in Korean, covering three distinct levels of formality and politeness. The lesson is framed around a humorous scenario where the instructor is stranded on a roadside with a broken bike, using a portable whiteboard to illustrate grammar points while waiting for help.

The learning arc begins with the standard particles 에게 and 에게서, moves to the more common conversational forms 한테 and 한테서, and concludes with the honorific particle 께. A recurring theme throughout the lesson is that the "from" markers (에게서, 한테서) often drop the final syllable 서 in natural speech, meaning the context of the verb is the primary way to distinguish whether a particle indicates the direction "to" or the source "from."

---

### Expressing "To" a Person with 에게
The most basic way to say "to" a person is by attaching the particle 에게 directly to the noun representing the person. The instructor demonstrates this on the whiteboard using the name 철수. By writing **철수에게**, the meaning becomes "to Chalsu."

A critical word order rule is emphasized: the recipient marked with 에게 should appear before the verb, typically following the subject. For example, in the sentence "I said it to Chalsu," the Korean structure is "I to-Chalsu said-it."

> **저는 철수에게 말했어요.** / I said it to Chalsu.

The instructor notes that 에게 must be used with communication verbs such as 말하다 (to say), 전화하다 (to call), 물어보다 (to ask), 알리다 (to inform), and 전하다 (to convey). In English, some of these might take a direct object, but in Korean, you are communicating "to" that person, so the object marker 을/를 is incorrect for the person in these instances.

---

### Expressing "From" a Person with 에게서
To express "from" a person, the particle 에게서 is used. However, the instructor points out that the syllable 서 is completely optional. This means that 에게 can mean both "to" and "from." The listener determines the meaning based on the verb that follows. If the verb is a "receiving" or "hearing" verb, 에게 indicates the source.

> **저는 철수에게(서) 들었어요.** / I heard it from Chalsu.

The instructor provides further examples to show how the verb changes the particle's function. With 받다 (to receive) or 배우다 (to learn), the particle indicates "from." Conversely, with 가르치다 (to teach), the same particle indicates "to."

> **친구에게(서) 받았어요.** / I received it from a friend.
> **저는 빌리 씨에게 한국어를 배웠어요.** / I learned Korean from Billy.
> **저는 빌리 씨에게 한국어를 가르쳤어요.** / I taught Korean to Billy.

---

### Conversational "To" and "From" with 한테
While 에게 is slightly formal, the particle 한테 is the standard choice for everyday spoken Korean. Similar to the previous pattern, 한테 means "to," and 한테서 means "from," but the 서 is frequently dropped in conversation. The instructor advises learners to default to 한테 for most speaking situations.

> **빌리 씨한테 선물을 받았어요.** / I received a present from Billy.
> **그거 누구한테 들었어?** / Who did you hear that from?

The instructor also highlights a specific usage of **저한테는** (to me / for me) as a way to express a personal opinion. He demonstrates the pronunciation, noting that the 하 sound in 한테 is often very soft and breathy.

> **미국 사람들한테는 한국어가 어려워요.** / To Americans, Korean is difficult.

An interesting example is provided for the source of a smell, where 한테 indicates the person from whom the smell originates.

> **어떤 사람한테 냄새가 나요.** / Someone smells. (Literally: From some person, a smell comes out.)

---

### Honorific and Humble "To" and "From" with 께
When speaking to or about someone who requires a high level of respect, such as a teacher or a grandfather, the particle 께 must be used. Like the previous particles, 께 functions as both "to" and "from" depending on the context.

The instructor stresses that 께 is used to show respect to the recipient or source, never to oneself. When using 께 to mean "to," it is often paired with humble verbs like 드리다 (the humble version of 주다, to give).

> **선생님께 선물을 드렸어요.** / I gave a present to the teacher.
> **선생님께 선물을 받았어요.** / I received a present from the teacher.

The lesson concludes with a final comparison using the word grandfather to show how the verb dictates the direction of 께.

> **저는 할아버지께 들었어요.** / I heard it from my grandfather.
> **저는 할아버지께 말했어요.** / I said it to my grandfather.
