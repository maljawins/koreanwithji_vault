## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the particles used to indicate the direction of an action involving people or animals: 에게, 한테, 께, and 께서. The instructor explains that while 에게, 한테, and 께 all share the meanings of "to" and "from," they differ in formality and usage. 께서 is unique among the four as it functions as an honorific subject marker. The video provides a comprehensive guide on how to distinguish between "to" and "from" based on the verb used, how to apply honorifics correctly, and the important distinction between addressing individuals versus organizations.

---

### 에게 and 한테: "To" and "From" (Non-Honorific)

The particles 에게 and 한테 are used after a noun representing a person or an animal. They are essentially interchangeable, but 한테 is significantly more common in spoken Korean because it sounds more casual, whereas 에게 can sometimes feel more like written language or "bookish." Both particles carry two primary meanings: "to" (the recipient of an action) and "from" (the source of an action).

When these particles mean "to," they are typically paired with verbs of giving or communicating. The instructor demonstrates this with several examples:
- **제프에게 선물을 줬어요.** / I gave a present to Jeff.
- **누구한테 말했어요?** / To whom did you tell?
- **고양이한테 밥을 줬어요.** / I fed the cat (literally: gave a meal to the cat).
- **거지에게 돈을 줬어요.** / I gave money to a beggar.

When used to mean "from," they are paired with verbs of receiving, hearing, or borrowing. The meaning is determined entirely by the context and the verb that follows.
- **제프에게 선물을 받았어요.** / I received a present from Jeff.
- **누구한테 들었어요?** / From whom did you hear it?
- **고양이한테 맞았어요.** / I was hit by my cat (literally: received a hitting from the cat).
- **누구에게 돈을 빌렸어요?** / From whom did you borrow money?

The instructor emphasizes that you cannot "receive to" a person or "give from" a person in this grammatical structure, so looking at the verb is the foolproof way to identify the particle's function.

---

### 께: The Honorific Version of "To/From"

께 is the honorific equivalent of 에게 and 한테. It is used when the person being addressed or referred to is someone to whom you must show respect, such as a teacher, a grandparent, or a boss. Like its non-honorific counterparts, 께 can mean both "to" and "from" depending on the verb.

When using 께 to mean "to," it is often accompanied by honorific verbs. For example, instead of using 주다 (to give), one should use 드리다 (the humble/honorific form of give).
- **선생님께 드렸어요.** / I gave (it) to the teacher.
- **할머니께 전화했어요.** / I called grandma (literally: called to grandma).

When 께 means "from," it is used with verbs like 받다 (to receive).
- **선생님께 받았어요.** / I received (it) from the teacher.
- **할머니께 전화를 받았어요.** / I received a call from grandma.

---

### 께서: The Honorific Subject Marker

께서 is often confused with 께 because they look similar and are both honorific, but their functions are entirely different. 께서 is an honorific subject marking particle, used in place of 이 or 가 when the subject of the sentence is a person of high status.

The instructor provides examples where 께서 marks the subject, often paired with honorific verb endings like -(으)세요:
- **할아버지께서 편찮으세요.** / Grandpa is sick. (편찮으시다 is the honorific for "to be sick").
- **선생님께서 예쁘세요.** / The teacher is pretty.
- **교수님께서 저에게 주셨어요.** / The professor gave (it) to me.

In the final example, the instructor notes the coexistence of 께서 (marking the respected professor as the subject) and 에게 (marking "me" as the non-honorific recipient).

---

### Addressing Organizations: Using 에 instead of 에게

A common mistake for learners is using 에게 or 한테 when talking about organizations or places (like a school, company, or library). The instructor clarifies that 에게/한테/께 are strictly for "animated" nouns (people and animals). For "inanimate" organizations, the particle 에 must be used to mean "to."

- **학교에 말했어요.** / I told the school. (Not 학교에게).
- **회사에 이메일을 보냈어요.** / I sent an email to the company.
- **유튜브에 이메일을 보냈어요.** / I sent an email to YouTube.
- **경찰에 신고했어요.** / I reported (it) to the police.

If you are referring to a specific person within that organization (e.g., a police officer), you can use 에게/한테, but when referring to the entity itself, 에 is the correct choice.
