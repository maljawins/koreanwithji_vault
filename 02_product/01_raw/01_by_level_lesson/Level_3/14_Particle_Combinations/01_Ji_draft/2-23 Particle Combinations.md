**에 & 에서 + 도, 만, 밖에**

Let’s go over some example sentences to see how particles like **도**, **만**, and **밖에** combine with location/time particles like **에** and **에서**. These combinations help express more precise meanings such as *also at*, *only at*, or *nowhere else but*.

**1. 이 가게에도 사람이 많아요**

**“There’s a lot of people also at this store.”**

* Here, **에도** is a combination of **에** (location) + **도** (also).
* **Other places** also have many people, and this store is **one of them**.
* It’s like saying: "This store, too, is crowded—just like the others."

**2. 이 공원에만 나무가 하나도 없어요**

**“There’s not even one tree only at this park.”**
*(This is the only park that has no trees.)*

* **에만** = **only at**
* The nuance is that **all other parks have trees**, but **this park is the exception.**

**Structure breakdown**:

* **에만** restricts the statement to *this location only*
* **하나도 없어요** adds the idea of *not even one*, emphasizing total absence

💡 This is a great example of using **에만** to highlight contrast or exception.

**3. 코알라는 호주에 밖에 안 살아요**

**“Koalas live only in Australia.”**

* **에 밖에** = nowhere else but this place
* **안 살아요** = do not live
* The entire sentence structure shows **exclusive location** with a **negative verb**, which is required when using **밖에**.

**Key grammar note**:

* **밖에** must be used with a **negative verb** like **안 살아요**, **없어요**, or **못 해요**.
* So this literally means: **“Except for Australia, they don’t live anywhere else.”**

✅ This gives a stronger sense of limitation than just saying **호주에만 살아요**.

**4. 여기(에)서도 빵을 팔아요?**

**“Do they sell bread also here?”**
*(Do they have bread here too?)*

* **에서도** = **also at (this location where action happens)**
* **빵을 팔아요** = sell bread
* They sell bread in other places and it’s sold **here, too**.

**Note**: **여기서** or **여기에서** are both fine. **에서도** adds “also” to the location.

**5.저는 노래방에서만 노래를 해요**

**“I only sing at karaoke.”**

* **에서만** = only at (this location)
* **노래를 해요** = sing
* I never sing at home, in the car, etc.—only in karaoke rooms.

**Structure breakdown**:

* **노래방에서만** = *only at karaoke*

**6. 이 음식은 한국에서 밖에 못 먹어요**

**“You can only eat this food in Korea.”**

* **에서 밖에** = nowhere else but this location
* **못 먹어요** = can’t eat
* This literally means: *Except in Korea, you can’t eat this food.*

**Slide 76~77 Particle Combinations with 부터 and 까지**

In this slide, we’re looking at how **부터** (starting from) and **까지** (up to/until) combine with **도**, **만**, and **밖에** to express more precise meaning around time or range.

Let’s go through the examples one by one.

**1. 월요일부터도 일이 많았어요**

**“I had a lot of work even from Monday.”**

* **부터도** = *even from*
* The nuance here is: not only Tuesday or Wednesday—**even from Monday**, things were already busy.

✅ This implies that the busy schedule started earlier than expected.

**2. 지금부터만 열심히 하세요**

**“Just work hard starting from now.”**

* **부터만** = *only starting from*
* This suggests: “Don’t worry about before, just focus from now on.”
* It implies that **only the present and future matter** for your effort.

✅ Great expression for motivation or redirection.

**Structure breakdown**:

* **지금부터** = from now
* **만** = only → **지금부터만** = just starting now

**3. 어제부터 밖에 시간이 없었어요**

**“I only had time starting from yesterday.”**

* **부터 밖에** = *not before — only starting from*
* This sentence implies you didn’t have time **until yesterday**; yesterday was the **earliest** you could start.

**Important note**:

* **밖에** must pair with a **negative verb**: **없었어요** = didn't have

✅ This structure is useful when explaining a delay or limited availability.

**4. 밤 열 시까지도 공부했어요**

**“I studied even until 10 p.m.”**

* **까지도** = *even up to*
* Emphasizes how **late** you studied. Maybe people didn’t expect you to study that long, but you did — **even until 10pm**.

✅ Useful for highlighting unexpected effort or dedication.

**Structure breakdown**:

* **밤 열 시까지** = until 10 p.m.
* **도** = even → **까지도** = even up to this time

**5. 여기까지만 말할게요**

**“I’ll talk only up to here.”**

* **까지만** = *only until*
* You’re setting a clear boundary — not going beyond this point in what you say.

**6. 3(삼) 페이지까지 밖에 못 읽었어요**

**“I could only read up to page 3.”**

* **까지 밖에** = *up to… and no more than that*
* Indicates a limitation in **extent** — you didn’t go beyond page 3.

**Again**:

* **밖에** requires a **negative** verb. . **못 읽었어요** = couldn’t read

✅ Use this when expressing limitation in time, amount, distance, or range.

**Slide 78 – Script: 한테 + 도, 만, 밖에**

Now let’s take a look at how the particle **한테** (to someone) combines with **도**, **만**, and **밖에** to express different nuances.

These combinations allow you to add emotional or logical emphasis when talking about giving or saying something **to someone**.

**1. 친한 친구한테도 말 안 했어요**

**“I didn’t even tell my close friend.”**

* **한테도** = *even to someone*
* This emphasizes that you didn’t share it **even with** someone close—so likely, you didn’t tell *anyone*.

✅ Use this when expressing secrecy or how private something is.

🧠 Emotional nuance: “If I didn’t even tell my close friend, then I definitely didn’t tell others.”

**2. 그 얘기는 너한테만 말했어**

**“I told that story only to you.”**

* **한테만** = *only to someone*
* You’re emphasizing exclusivity — nobody else heard that story.

✅ Commonly used to express trust or to imply someone shouldn’t spread the information.

🧠 Emotional nuance: “I trusted you with this. Don’t share it.”

**3. 선생님한테밖에 질문을 못 했어요**

**“I couldn’t ask anyone but the teacher.”**
(= I didn’t/couldn’t ask anyone else.)

* **한테 밖에** = *to no one but…*
* You’re saying you had **no option** but to ask the teacher.

✅ Note: **밖에** is always paired with a **negative verb**, like **못 했어요**.

🧠 Contextual nuance: “I didn’t have the confidence or chance to ask anyone else but the teacher.”

**Slide 79 – Script: 만 + 을, 은, 이**

Now let’s take a look at how **만** combines with other particles like **을**, **은**, and **이**.

This helps us add emphasis or highlight contrast, depending on the sentence structure.

**1. 진실만을 말했습니다**

**“I will speak only the truth.”**

* **만을** = Only \_\_\_ + object marker **을**
* This combination emphasizes that you will say **nothing but** the truth.
* It’s more formal or dramatic than just **진실만 말해요.**

🧠 Nuance: You’re highlighting that **truth alone** is the object of your action. This is often used in public speaking, news, or formal pledges.

**2. 이것만은 꼭 기억해요**

**“At least this, make sure to remember.”**

* **만은** = Only \_\_\_ + topic marker **은**
* When **만** is combined with **은**, it puts contrastive or emphatic focus on the topic.

✅ Use it when you want to say, “Even if you forget everything else, **at least remember this.**”

🧠 Nuance: This structure is good for highlighting **the minimum** someone should do.

**3. 너만이 날 이해할 수 있어**

**“Only you can understand me.”**

* **만이** = Only \_\_\_ + subject marker **이**
* It emphasizes that the **subject** is the one and only person capable of doing something.

**Structure breakdown**:

* **너만이** = only you (as subject)
* **날** = 나 + 를 = me
* **이해할 수 있어** = can understand

🧠 Nuance: Strong emotional emphasis. Use this to highlight a unique bond or exclusive ability.

는 + Other Particles = Contrast

**Slide 77 는 + Other Particles = Contrast**

Okay, now, you had a lot of questions about 은는. Here, I’ll show you more examples on how it can imply contrast—this part should help make that clearer.

When you attach the **topic marker 는** after other particles like **에, 에서, 부터, 까지, 한테**, you’re not just stating a fact— You’re **highlighting contrast**.

You're basically saying, “As for *this* specific time/place/person, [Well, about that]… something is true—but not necessarily for others.”

Let’s go through each one slowly.

**1. 에는 → As for this time/location…**

🧠 **Used with time or place** to highlight **a specific moment** compared to others.

📍 **12시에는 수업이 있어요**

“At 12 o'clock, I have class.”
💡 Implied: “But I might be free at other times.”

* **12시** = 12 o’clock
* **에** = time particle
* **는** = topic marker → shows contrast
* You're marking **this** time as different from others.

Someone asks if you're free at 12. And you want to meet them—you're just busy at that time. So, it subtly suggests: 'I'm busy at 12, but maybe try asking about some other time.

**2. 에서는 → As for this place…
📍 이 카페에서는 커피를 안 마셔요
“I don’t drink coffee at this café.”**

This sentence can imply two slightly different contrasts:

1. “I don’t drink coffee *here* (but maybe elsewhere).”
2. “I don’t drink *coffee* here (but I drink something else).”

💡 The contrast can be:

* Location-based: “I drink coffee, just not *at this café*.”
* Item-based: “At this café, I don’t drink *coffee*, but I drink something else.”

This is possible because:

* 에서 = location particle (where the action happens)
* 는 = topic particle that introduces contrast

🧠 Use this when comparing:

* Your actions across places (“Here I do X, but not there”)
* Your choices within the same place (“At this place, I do A but not B”)

**You're subtly drawing a boundary: “In this context (this place), this action doesn't happen.”**

**3. 부터는 → As for from here/time on…**

📍 **여기부터는 제 자리입니다**

“From here on out, it’s my seat (my space).”
💡 Implied: “Before this point, it wasn’t—but *this* is my boundary.”

* **여기부터** = from here
* **는** = marks the contrast → “from here is different”
* You’re setting a boundary for your personal space.

Another example:

“From this chapter on, things get harder.”
That’s the kind of contrast you're expressing with **부터는**.

**4. 까지는 → As for until this point…**

📍 **지금까지는 많이 참았습니다**

“Until now, I’ve tolerated a lot.”
💡 Implied: “But not anymore. I’ve had enough.”

* **지금까지** = until now
* **는** = contrast → “That was then; now is different.”
* You're clearly drawing a line in time.

🧠 Use this when showing a **change of state**:

“I was patient—up to now. But now, something’s changing.”

**5. 한테는 / 에게는 → As for this person…**

📍 **왜 나한테는 과자밖에 안 줬어?**

“Why did you give *me* only snacks?”
💡 Implied: “You gave others better stuff!”

* **한테** = to (someone)
* **는** = contrast → me vs. others
* **과자밖에** = only snacks

You’re not just stating what you got—you’re comparing it to what **others got**.

This use of **한테는** expresses a **feeling of unfairness** or unequal treatment.
