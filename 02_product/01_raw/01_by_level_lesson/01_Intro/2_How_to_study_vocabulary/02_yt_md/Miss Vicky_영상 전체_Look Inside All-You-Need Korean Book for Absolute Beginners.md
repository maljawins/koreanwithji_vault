### Chapter 1: Mastering Hangul
The first chapter is dedicated entirely to the Korean writing system. It covers basic consonants `기본 자음`, single vowels `단모음`, aspirated consonants `격음 자음`, double consonants `쌍자음`, diphthongs `이중 모음`, and final consonants `받침`. Crucially, it also includes a section on consonant assimilation `자음 동화`, which explains how sounds change when certain characters meet. The instructor notes that while her YouTube video on this topic is nearly two hours long and highly detailed, the book focuses on the most essential rules for beginners to prevent overwhelm.

Visually, the book utilizes engaging illustrations, such as a four-panel comic showing King Sejong inventing Hangul to help his people who found Chinese characters too difficult. The instructor points out "Consonant Sounds Comparisons" pages where similar-sounding characters like `ㄱ` vs `ㅋ` or `ㄴ` vs `ㄷ` are contrasted with example words like `고구마 (sweet potato)` and `나비 (butterfly)`. These sections are supported by audio files recorded by the instructor to ensure accurate pronunciation from the start.

### Chapters 2 and 3: Beyond the Alphabet
Chapter 2, titled "존댓말 vs 반말 (Polite vs. Casual Language)," introduces the social hierarchy reflected in the Korean language. It covers politeness levels, basic expressions, and the fundamentals of sentence construction. Chapter 3 focuses on "숫자 (Numbers)," specifically the distinction between Sino-Korean and Native Korean number systems. The instructor highlights that these chapters provide practical skills that can be used immediately, such as learning how to say "Thank you" in four different ways ranging from the most formal `감사합니다` to the least formal `고마워요`.

The book includes "Reference Boxes" (highlighted in yellow) throughout these chapters. These boxes address frequently asked questions and common mistakes the instructor has observed in her students over the years. For example, one box explains the nuances of the word `저` as a demonstrative meaning "that (over there)" versus its use as a humble first-person pronoun. Another reference box clarifies why `십육 (sixteen)` is pronounced as `심뉵` due to pronunciation preferences of native speakers, even if it doesn't follow a standard rule perfectly.

---

### Effective Study Strategies and the Role of Romanization
The instructor outlines two primary methods for using the book alongside her YouTube channel. Method 1 involves studying the book's detailed explanations and exercises first, then using videos for review and listening practice. Method 2 suggests watching the videos first to build a general framework—specifically using the "Hangul Overview" on page 18 of the book—before diving into the granular details of the text. She explicitly mentions that any minor inaccuracies in older videos have been corrected in the book's text and the video comment sections.

A significant portion of the advice centers on the use of romanization. While the book follows standardized romanization for consistency, the instructor warns that it is merely a temporary bridge. Learners are encouraged to associate Roman letters with the specific Korean sounds they represent rather than reading them with English phonetic values. The ultimate goal is to drop the reliance on romanization entirely as one becomes accustomed to Hangul, as the two languages have fundamentally different phonetic systems.
