The Structure

The course is divided into 9 levels. Each level consists of 12 to 20 video lessons.

And I've designed the curriculum in such a way that what you learned from earlier lessons will keep

showing up again and again in the examples in later lessons. This is called linear scaffolding.

Imagine building a skyscraper. You cannot install the windows on the 10th floor if you haven't built the

frame for the 10th floor, and you can't build that frame if the foundation isn't set.

For example, sentences shown in level 2 will contain grammar points you learned in level 1. This will

help with retaining and solidifying your learnings by constant exposure to previous learnings as you go.

Speaking of grammar, if the word “grammar” still feels intimidating, despite what I talked about in the

previous video, here's a little something that might change your perspective.

How you should think about Grammar

Grammar is just a tool like a spoon or a pen. It’s an “important” tool the same way a pen is an

essential tool that lets you write on a piece of paper.

Would you be intimidated by a pen? Of course not.

Once you truly embrace this idea, everything changes.

K-dramas. Movies. K-pop songs. Whatever Korean content you enjoy— you name it. You'll start

recognizing patterns and understanding the framework of the sentences you see.

Simply put, grammar is just a means to an end. It’s an important tool to build your language core but

nonetheless, a tool. That’s all it is.

So I urge you to keep reminding yourself as you go about the WHY of learning grammar and HOW

you’re going to USE grammar to your advantage.

It isn’t “to NOT make a mistake” or “to sound perfect”. It’s to “understand”.

Structure of each lesson

I also talked about these three categories in the previous video: Input. Process. Output.

This is the learning model we'll be using throughout the entire course.

Each video lesson will be the Input and Process stage where you learn new information.

And we need to talk a little more about the output stage.

In each page, you will have access to 2 attached PDF documents: a lesson summary and a worksheet.

And the worksheet is where you USE what you learned to produce your own output.

The first section of the worksheet will have more example sentences relevant to that lesson, along with

sentence breakdowns.

The second section will have a list of words—not full sentences – which you’ll then use to complete the

sentences like a puzzle. This is your exercise section.

The third and final section is where you create your own sentences.

It also includes clear, step-by-step instructions on how to naturally internalize the grammar point you

just learned and the sentences you created.

I encourage you to carefully read the instructions because this is the essence of how to USE grammar

to your advantage instead of blindly memorizing it.

Also, I recommend you create your own lesson notes and use the lesson summaries provided just for

reference.

When you write notes in your own words, you do it in a way that makes sense to you. This will help

you remember things much better.
