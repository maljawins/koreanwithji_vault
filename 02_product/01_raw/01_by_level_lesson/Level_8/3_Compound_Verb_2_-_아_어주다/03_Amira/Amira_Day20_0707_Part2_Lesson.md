## Teaching Points

*   **The Versatile ~아/어지다 (To become / Passive / Inability):**
    *   **To become/get:** When attached to Descriptive Verbs (D.V), it indicates a change in state. Ji notes that once ~아/어지다 is attached, the whole unit functions as an action verb. Examples: 피곤하다 (to be tired) → 피곤해지다 (to get tired); 무겁다 (to be heavy) → 무거워지다 (to get heavy).
    *   **Passive Voice:** For verbs that do not take the standard 이/히/리/기 suffixes, ~아/어지다 acts as the passive marker. Example: 만들다 (to make) → 만들어지다 (to be made).
    *   **External Inability (Can't):** Used to express that an action cannot be performed due to external or natural circumstances rather than a lack of will. Ji uses the example of a hangover: 숙취 때문에 안 일어나져요 (I can't get up/am not gotten up because of a hangover).
*   **있다/없다 + 어지다:**
    *   없다 (to not exist) becomes 없어지다 (to disappear).
    *   Ji clarifies that 있어지다 does not exist; instead, 생기다 (to appear/emerge) is used.
    *   However, ~어지다 works with descriptive verbs ending in 있다, such as 재미있어지다 (to become fun) or 맛있어지다 (to become delicious).
*   **~고 싶어지다 (To start wanting to):**
    *   Combines "wanting" with "becoming" to show a growing desire. Example: 맥주가 마시고 싶어지네 (I feel like having a beer now/I've started wanting a beer).
    *   Ji introduces the slang 땡기다 (to crave/pull) as a synonymous expression for this feeling.
*   **Noun Modifiers (Timeline Nuances):**
    *   **Present:** D.V + 은/ㄴ (작은 고양이); A.V + 는 (가는 남자).
    *   **Future:** 을/ㄹ (할 것, 먹을 것) indicates possibility or things yet to happen.
    *   **Past (~은/ㄴ vs. ~던 vs. ~았던/었던):**
        *   **~은/ㄴ:** Indicates a simple completed action. Context determines if it still affects the present (e.g., "the clothes I put on" vs. "the clothes I wore yesterday").
        *   **~던:** Indicates a past action that was habitual or was in progress but not completed (e.g., 먹던 피자 - the pizza I was eating/leftovers).
        *   **~았던/었던:** Indicates a past state or action that is completely finished and disconnected from the present (e.g., 작았던 강아지 - the puppy that was small but is now big).
*   **Comparison Markers (Self-Study Intro):**
    *   **보다 (Than):** Used after a noun to mean "than [noun]." Example: 저보다 키가 커요 (Taller than me).
    *   **만큼 (As much as):** Attached after modifiers to show equality in degree. Example: 먹는 만큼 (as much as [one] eats).

## Lesson Flow

*   **Introduction to ~아/어지다:** Ji introduces the form, explaining that it works with over 90% of Korean verbs. He starts with the "to become" usage for descriptive verbs, using the example of getting tired without coffee.
*   **Passive and Inability Nuances:** Ji explains the passive usage (만들어지다) and then moves to the "Can/Can't" nuance. He explains that saying 안 일어나져요 (can't get up) subtly blames the hangover rather than the person's willpower. Amira finds this distinction interesting.
*   **있다/없다 and Slang:** Ji clarifies the difference between 없어지다 and the non-existent 있어지다 (using 생기다 instead). He then introduces ~고 싶어지다 and the common slang 땡기다, explaining its origin from the word "to pull" (당기다).
*   **Modifier Recap and Past Tense Deep Dive:** Ji briefly recaps present and future modifiers before tackling the three past tense forms. He uses a timeline on the screen to show how ~은/ㄴ is a simple completion, while ~던 implies a "recall" or "retrospective" (회상) vibe.
*   **Timeline Comparisons:** Ji uses the example of "leftover pizza" (먹던 피자) to explain ~던 and "clothes that used to fit" (맞았던 옷) to explain ~았던/었던. He notes that for descriptive verbs, Koreans usually prefer the past stem (~았던/었던) to show a clear break from the past state.
*   **Student Feedback and Homework:** Amira admits it is a lot of information but says she understands the logic. They discuss her listening practice with the SDBD podcast. Ji encourages her, noting that her processing speed will increase with more vocabulary.
*   **Self-Study and Scheduling:** Ji assigns self-study on 나다 vs 내다 and comparison markers. They coordinate their next lesson for the following Saturday.
