## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces a more advanced way to ask for favors by combining action verbs with the polite request form 주세요. While a previous lesson covered using 주세요 to ask for physical items, this lesson focuses on the grammar structure used to ask someone to perform an action for the speaker's benefit. The lesson covers the conjugation rules, the nuanced difference between a standard request and a favor, and specific adverbs that modify the tone of the request.

---

### The Favor Form: Conjugated Action Verb + 주세요

The core grammar taught is how to transform a simple command into a polite favor. The instructor explains that while the standard polite request form (으)세요 means "do [verb]," adding 주세요 after a conjugated verb changes the meaning to "please do [verb] for me." To create this form, one must first conjugate an action verb into its "요 form" but drop the 요 before adding 주세요.

The instructor provides a visual comparison using the verb 하다 (to do). The standard request is 하세요 (do it), whereas the favor form is 해주세요 (please do it for me). This works because 주세요 is derived from the verb 주다 (to give). By attaching it to another verb, the speaker is literally asking the listener to "give the action" of doing that verb to the speaker.

---

### Conjugation Practice and Irregularities

The lesson includes a practice segment where several verbs are converted into the favor form. The instructor highlights a specific consonant shift that occurs with certain verbs. For the verb 듣다 (to listen), the final consonant 디귿 (ㄷ) changes to 리을 (ㄹ) when followed by a vowel during conjugation. Therefore, 듣다 becomes 들어주세요 (please listen). Other examples provided include:

- **피자를 만들다 (to make pizza)** → 피자를 만들어 주세요 (Please make me a pizza)
- **한국어를 배우다 (to learn Korean)** → 한국어를 배워 주세요 (Please learn Korean for me)
- **이것을 보다 (to look at this)** → 이것을 봐 주세요 (Please look at this for me)
- **이해(를) 하다 (to understand)** → 이해(를) 해 주세요 (Please understand)
- **돈을 빌리다 (to borrow/lend)** → 돈을 빌려 주세요 (Please loan me money)

---

### Adverbs for Requests: 꼭 and 제발

The instructor introduces two adverbs that are frequently used when asking favors. The first is 꼭, which means "make sure to" or "definitely." When used with a request, it adds emphasis that the action is important. For example, 꼭 보세요 means "make sure to see it."

The second adverb is 제발, which translates to "please." However, the instructor strongly cautions that 제발 is much more intense than the English "please." It is used for begging or pleading when the speaker is in a desperate situation. Using 제발 in a normal, everyday request can sound overly dramatic or strange because the 주세요 form itself already carries the polite "please" sentiment. An example of its strong usage is 제발 숙제를 해주세요 (Please, I beg you, do the homework for me).

---

### Usage Notes and Casual Speech

The instructor concludes with two important usage notes. First, he clarifies that because 주세요 already means "give me," it is redundant and incorrect to conjugate 주다 into the favor form as 주어 주세요. One should simply say 주세요.

Second, the instructor explains how to use this favor form with close friends or people of a lower social rank. In these casual (반말) situations, the polite ending 주세요 is replaced with the casual ending 줘 or 주어. For example, a speaker might say 이해를 해줘 (Please understand) to a close friend. This casual version must never be used with strangers or people requiring honorifics.
