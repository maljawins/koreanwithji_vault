### Making Polite Requests with (으)세요
The instructor explains that 주세요 is actually a combination of the verb 주다 (to give) and the polite ending 세요. To make requests with other action verbs, one must follow a simple two-step conjugation process based on the verb stem. First, identify the verb stem of an action verb. Second, attach the appropriate ending: use 으세요 if the verb stem ends in a consonant, or attach just 세요 if the verb stem ends in a vowel.

This grammar form is a polite way to command or request that someone perform the action of the verb. It is essential for daily interactions where you need to ask someone to "do" something rather than just "give" something.

### Conversation Practice: Asking for Help
A practice dialogue demonstrates the grammar in a realistic scenario involving a difficult homework assignment. The character 영애 starts by saying:
> **저를 좀 도와주세요.** / Please help me a bit.

The instructor notes that 도와주세요 comes from 도와주다 (to help), and the word 좀 (a little bit) is used here to soften the request, making it sound more natural and polite. When the second character, 병헌, responds with **네?**, the instructor explains that 네 can be used as a question to ask for clarification or to ask someone to repeat themselves.

The dialogue continues with 영애 explaining that the homework is too difficult (**이 숙제가 너무 어려워요**). 병헌 apologizes and explains that he also cannot do that homework:
> **미안해요. 저도 그 숙제를 할 수 없어요.** / I'm sorry. I also cannot do that homework.

A key grammatical nuance is highlighted here: while 도 usually means "too" or "also," when it is used with a negative verb like 수 없다 (cannot), it can be translated as "either" or "neither." Thus, the sentence means "I can't do that homework either."

### Conjugation Practice
The instructor provides six examples for the viewer to practice conjugating into the (으)세요 form to create polite requests:

- **이 펜을 쓰다** (to use this pen) → **이 펜을 쓰세요.** (Use this pen.)
- **이메일을 보내다** (to send an email) → **이메일을 보내세요.** (Send an email.)
- **티비를 보다** (to watch TV) → **TV를 보세요.** (Watch TV.)
- **책을 읽다** (to read a book) → **책을 읽으세요.** (Read a book.)
- **도서관에 가다** (to go to the library) → **도서관에 가세요.** (Go to the library.)
- **빨리 앉다** (to sit down quickly) → **빨리 앉으세요.** (Sit down quickly.)

---

### The ㄹ Irregular Rule and a Common Expression
The lesson concludes with a specific rule for verb stems ending in the consonant ㄹ. When attaching the 세요 ending to a stem ending in ㄹ, such as 팔다 (to sell), the ㄹ must be removed before adding 세요. This results in the conjugation **파세요**.

This rule is famously used in the common Korean parting expression:
> **많이 파세요.** / Sell a lot.

While it literally means "sell a lot," it is used as a polite way to say goodbye to a shopkeeper when leaving their store. It expresses a well-wish that the business owner will be successful and sell many products.
