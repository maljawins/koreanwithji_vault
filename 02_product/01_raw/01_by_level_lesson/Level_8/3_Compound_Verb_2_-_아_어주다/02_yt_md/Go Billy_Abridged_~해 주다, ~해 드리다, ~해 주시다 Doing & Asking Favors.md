## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the essential grammar for performing actions as favors or requesting them from others. The core concept involves combining an action verb with a verb meaning "to give." By doing so, the speaker expresses that an action is being "given" to someone else as a helpful gesture or service.

The lesson focuses on three specific versions of the verb "to give": **주다**, **드리다**, and **주시다**. While all three share the basic meaning of giving, they differ significantly in their social application based on the relationship between the giver and the receiver. The instructor breaks down the conjugation mechanics, the nuances of humble and honorific speech, and provides practical examples for everyday requests and statements.

---

### The Basic Form: Action Verb + 주다
To express doing something for someone, you must first conjugate an action verb into its informal polite (요) form. Once you have that base, you remove the 요 and attach the verb **주다 (to give)**. For example, the verb **하다 (to do)** becomes **해**, which then combines to form **해 주다 (to do as a favor)**. Similarly, **보다 (to see/look)** becomes **봐 주다**, and **만들다 (to make)** becomes **만들어 주다**.

Visually, the instructor notes that there is a space between the conjugated action verb and the verb **주다**. This construction literally means "to give the act of [verb]." When you say you will make something using this form, you aren't just saying you will perform the action; you are saying you will give the effort of that action to someone else. **주다** can be conjugated into any tense or mood, such as **주세요 (please give)**, **줄게요 (I will give)**, or **줬어요 (gave)**.

Examples of **주다** in context:
> **사진을 찍어주세요.** / Please take a photo (for me).
> **파스타를 만들어주세요.** / Please make me pasta.
> **친구가 점심을 사줬어요.** / A friend bought lunch (for me).
> **한국어를 가르쳐 줄게요.** / I will teach you Korean (as a favor).
> **친구가 매일 요리를 해줘요.** / My friend cooks for me every day.

---

### The Humble Form: Action Verb + 드리다
When the person performing the favor considers themselves to be in a lower social position than the receiver, the humble verb **드리다** is used instead of **주다**. This is common when the speaker is doing something for an older person, a teacher, a grandparent, or someone in a higher professional rank. The instructor describes this visually as "giving up" to someone while lowering oneself.

The conjugation process remains the same: take the action verb, conjugate it to the 요 form, remove the 요, and attach **드리다**. Common endings include **드리겠습니다**, **드릴게요**, or **드려요**. It is important to note that **드리다** is not strictly about "formal" speech; it is about the relationship of giving. You could speak casually to a friend about a favor you did for a teacher and still use **드려** because the recipient of the action is the one being respected.

Examples of **드리다** in context:
> **사진을 찍어드리겠습니다.** / I will take a photo for you (very formal/polite).
> **한국어를 가르쳐드릴게요.** / I'll teach you Korean (as a favor to a superior).
> **할머니를 위해 점심을 만들어 드렸어요.** / I made lunch for my grandmother.

---

### The Honorific Form: Action Verb + 주시다
The third variation uses **주시다**, which is the honorific form of **주다**. This is used when the person performing the action is someone higher in status than the receiver. By using **주시다**, the speaker "raises" the person who is doing the favor. The instructor points out that learners are already familiar with this through the common request ending **~해주세요**, which uses the honorific **주시다** to show respect to the person being asked to do something.

A particularly useful way to ask for favors from strangers or in polite settings is the question form **주시겠어요?**, which translates to "Could you please...?" or "Would you please...?" This sounds much more polite than a standard command.

Examples of **주시다** in context:
> **아빠가 핸드폰을 사주셨어요.** / My dad (honorifically) bought me a cell phone.
> **사진을 찍어주시겠어요?** / Could you please take a photo for me?
