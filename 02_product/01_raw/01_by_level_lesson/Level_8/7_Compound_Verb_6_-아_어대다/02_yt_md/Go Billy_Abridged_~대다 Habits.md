## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form -아/어대다, which is used to describe an action that happens continuously, repeatedly, or excessively. While it shares some similarities with other "habitual" or "frequent" markers, this specific form carries a distinct negative nuance. It is used when the speaker feels that an action is "too much" or "extreme," often expressing annoyance, frustration, or disapproval regarding the behavior being described.

The lesson covers the mechanical conjugation of the form, its specific pragmatic constraints—namely its casual and somewhat "slangy" nature—and provides several contextual examples ranging from noisy pets to excessive spending. Finally, the instructor compares -아/어대다 with two related forms, -곤 하다 and -기가 일쑤이다, to clarify when and why a speaker would choose one over the others.

---

### Construction and Meaning of -아/어대다
To create this form, you take an action verb stem and conjugate it into the standard -아/어 form (the same way you would for the polite present tense) and then attach the auxiliary verb 대다. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 해대다, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹어대다. 

The primary meaning of this form is that an action is happening continuously or keeps happening. However, the instructor emphasizes that it is almost always used to talk about something negative. It implies that the action is being done to an extreme or excessive degree. To help students grasp the nuance, the instructor suggests two mental substitutions: 심하게 하다 (to do seriously/extremely) or 너무 많이 하다 (to do too much). If a sentence with -아/어대다 feels difficult to translate, replacing the verb with one of these two phrases can help clarify the speaker's intent. Because it inherently expresses the speaker's negative feelings, it is considered a casual or even "slangy" expression and should never be used in situations where the speaker needs to sound polite or formal.

---

### Example Sentences and Context
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate how -아/어대다 functions in natural conversation. In the first example, "철수는 요즘 잠도 안 자고 게임만 해대요" (Lately, Chul-soo doesn't even sleep and only keeps playing games), the use of 해대다 shows that the speaker thinks Chul-soo's gaming habit is excessive and problematic. 

Other examples focus on external annoyances or bad habits:
- **밖에서 고양이가 밤마다 울어대서 잠을 자기 힘들어요.** / It's hard to sleep because the cat keeps crying (meowing) outside every single night. (Here, 울어대다 emphasizes that the crying is constant and annoying.)
- **새벽에 옆집 강아지가 짖어대서 일찍 깼어요.** / I woke up early because the neighbor's dog kept barking late at night. (짖어대다 implies excessive, bothersome barking.)
- **아이가 자면서 저를 발로 차대서 멍이 들었어요.** / I got a bruise because my child kept kicking me while they were asleep. (차대다 indicates repeated, forceful kicking.)
- **캐러멜을 그렇게 먹어대면 이빨이 다 망가질 거예요.** / If you keep scarfing down caramel like that, your teeth are all going to get ruined. (먹어대다 suggests eating greedily or excessively.)
- **돈을 그렇게 써대면 금방 다 써버릴 거예요.** / If you keep wasting your money like that, you'll soon spend all of it. (써대다 implies reckless or excessive spending.)
- **일찍 자야 한다면서 계속 커피를 마셔대면 어떡해요?** / While saying you have to go to bed early, if you keep drinking coffee like that, what are you going to do? (마셔대다 highlights the contradiction of drinking too much coffee.)

---

### Comparisons: -곤 하다 and -기가 일쑤이다
The instructor distinguishes -아/어대다 from two other forms that describe frequent actions. The first is -곤 하다 (or -고는 하다), which is used to show that something frequently or repeatedly happens. Unlike -아/어대다, this form does not have to be negative; it simply describes a habit. It is often used in the past tense to mean "used to," and it is perfectly acceptable in formal or polite speech.

The second comparison is with the form -기가 일쑤이다. Like -아/어대다, this form is used to show that something often happens and it usually carries a negative nuance. However, the instructor notes a key difference: -기가 일쑤이다 does not necessarily imply that the action is "too much" or "extreme" in the way -아/어대다 does. Furthermore, -기가 일쑤이다 is much less common in daily conversation and is more likely to be encountered in writing or specific formal contexts, whereas -아/어대다 is strictly for casual, informal speech.
