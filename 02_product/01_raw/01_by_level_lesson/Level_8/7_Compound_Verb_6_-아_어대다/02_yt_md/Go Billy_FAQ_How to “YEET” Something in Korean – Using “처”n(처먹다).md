## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces an advanced-level linguistic feature: the single-syllable prefix **처**. This prefix can be attached to the beginning of various action verbs to significantly alter their emotional weight and nuance without changing the core meaning of the action itself. While the base verb remains the same, the addition of **처** adds a layer of violence, recklessness, or wild exaggeration to the description of the act.

The lesson emphasizes that while these are not technically "swear words," they carry an extremely strong, harsh, and often negative feeling. Because of this, they are strictly reserved for very casual situations among close friends. The instructor warns that these forms should never be used in formal writing, essays, or when speaking to teachers or strangers, as they can sound quite rude or aggressive depending on the context.

---

### The Meaning and Nuance of 처
The prefix **처** functions as an intensifier that describes an action being done wildly, violently, recklessly, or in an exaggeratedly large amount. It is often used to express annoyance or to describe someone doing something in a way that lacks grace or care. For example, the instructor compares the difference between the standard verb **먹다 (to eat)** and the prefixed version **처먹다**. While both mean to eat, **처먹다** carries the nuance of "pigging out," "shoving food into one's mouth," or "scarfing food down your pie hole." It transforms a neutral action into one that feels much harsher and more judgmental.

---

### Common Verbs Using the 처 Prefix
The instructor lists several common verbs that frequently take this prefix to create slangy, high-intensity expressions. Many of these can be found in a dictionary, but many others are part of living slang.

- **쳐던지다** — Derived from **던지다 (to throw)**. This means to throw something violently, recklessly, or wildly. The instructor equates this to the English slang "to yeet" or "to chuck" something with great force.
- **처입다** and **처신다** — Derived from **입다 (to wear clothes)** and **신다 (to wear shoes)**. These mean to "throw on" clothes or shoes carelessly or hurriedly.
- **처넣다** — Derived from **넣다 (to put in/insert)**. This carries the nuance of "shoving" something in, such as recklessly forcing a USB drive into a computer.
- **처때리다** — Derived from **때리다 (to hit)**. This describes hitting someone or something violently, wildly, or "smacking" them.
- **처마시다** — Derived from **마시다 (to drink)**. This means to "down" a drink or drink something in an exaggerated or reckless manner.
- **처바르다** — Derived from **바르다 (to apply/spread)**. While the base verb is used for applying makeup or spreading cream, **처바르다** implies "caking on" makeup or applying something much too heavily and recklessly.

---

### Spelling and False Friends
A critical note is provided regarding the spelling and potential confusion with other verbs. The prefix is spelled as **처** (or **쳐** depending on the following vowel's historical conjugation rules, though often simplified in modern slang). The instructor points out that the verb **쳐들다 (to lift/raise up)** is a "false friend" in this context. Although it starts with the same sound, it is a standard verb that does not carry the negative, violent, or reckless nuance associated with the **처** prefix discussed in this lesson. It is simply a neutral word for lifting something.
