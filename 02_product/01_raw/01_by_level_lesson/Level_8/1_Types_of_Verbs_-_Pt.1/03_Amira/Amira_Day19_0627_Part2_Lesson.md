## Teaching Points

*   **Transitive vs. Intransitive Verbs:** Ji explains that transitive verbs (`타동사`) can take an object, while intransitive verbs (`자동사`) cannot. He uses the English examples "raise" (transitive) vs. "rise" (intransitive) to illustrate this. Crucially, he notes that all descriptive verbs in Korean are intransitive.
*   **Passive Voice (`피동사`):** Unlike English, which uses "be + past participle," Korean verbs change their dictionary form (e.g., `물다` to bite → `물리다` to be bitten). 
    *   **Particle Changes:** In passive voice, the receiver of the action becomes the subject (`이/가`). The doer of the action is marked by `에게/한테` (for people/animals) or `에` (for things/organizations/insects).
    *   **Formal "By":** Ji introduces `에 의해(서)` as a formal, written version of "by," though he advises Amira it is rarely used in speech.
    *   **Logical Constraint:** Only transitive verbs can become passive because an intransitive verb has no object to become the new subject.
*   **Passive for "Can/Can't":** A unique Korean usage where passive voice expresses inability due to external factors rather than personal lack of skill.
    *   `안 보여요` (It's not visible / I can't see it because something is blocking me) vs. `볼 수 없어요` (I don't have the ability to see).
    *   `들리다` (To be audible), `믿기다` (To be believable).
*   **Passive Verb Formation Patterns:**
    *   **Pattern 1 (`이/리/히/기`):** Added to pure Korean transitive non-`하다` verbs.
        *   `이`: Often for stems with no batchim (`보다` → `보이다`).
        *   `리`: Often for stems ending in `ㄹ`, `ㄷ` (irregular), or `르` (`듣다` → `들리다`).
        *   `히`: Often for stems ending in `ㄱ`, `ㅈ`, `ㅂ`, `ㄷ` (regular) (`먹다` → `먹히다`).
        *   `기`: For various other batchim (`믿다` → `믿기다`).
    *   **Pattern 2 (`Noun + 하다`):**
        *   `되다`: Used when the noun cannot be "received" (`준비되다` - to be prepared).
        *   `받다`: Used when the noun is something one can receive (`사랑받다` - to be loved).
        *   `당하다`: Used for negative actions against one's will (`해고당하다` - to be fired).
*   **Causative Verbs (`사동사`):** Verbs that mean "to cause/make someone do something." They use similar suffixes to passive verbs (`이/리/히/기/우/추`). Ji introduces this as an advanced topic to be revisited later.
*   **Compound Verbs (`Stem + 아/어 + Verb`):** Combining two verbs to create a new meaning (e.g., `알다` + `보다` = `알아보다` - to recognize/research).
*   **Describing Others' Feelings (`~아/어 하다`):** In Korean, one cannot definitively state how another person feels using a descriptive verb. To say "He is sad," one must use `슬퍼하다` (He acts sad/is behaving sadly) rather than `슬퍼요`. This turns a descriptive verb into an action verb.

## Lesson Flow

*   **Introduction to Transitivity:** Ji starts by defining transitive and intransitive verbs. He shows a slide with the Naver Dictionary to demonstrate how to check if a verb is `타동사` or `자동사`. Amira confirms she understands the concept.
*   **Passive Voice Mechanics:** Ji explains the shift from "A dog bit the kid" (`개가 애를 물었어요`) to "The kid was bitten by a dog" (`애가 개한테 물렸어요`). He highlights that the word order can change to emphasize the receiver.
*   **The "By" Particles:** Ji breaks down the use of `에게/한테` for animate doers and `에` for inanimate doers (including insects). He gives the example of being stung by a bee (`벌에 쏘이다`).
*   **The Logic of Passive Inability:** Ji explains the nuance of `안 보여요` (I can't see). He uses a visual of a person with a giant cone to explain `들리세요?` (Can you hear me?). Amira finds the distinction between personal ability and external factors interesting.
*   **Formation Drills:** Ji goes through the `이/리/히/기` patterns. He admits these are not "rules" but "patterns" with many exceptions. He advises Amira not to memorize them but to recognize them.
*   **`하다` Verb Transformations:** Ji explains the logic of `되다`, `받다`, and `당하다`. He asks Amira if one can "receive" preparation; when she says no, he explains why it becomes `준비되다`.
*   **Brief Intro to Causatives:** Ji introduces `웃기다` (to make someone laugh) to show that causative verbs, unlike passive verbs, can take an object (`나를 웃겨서`). He tells Amira they will study this in depth in 7-8 lessons.
*   **Compound Verbs & Cultural Nuance:** Ji explains `알아보다` and how context determines if it means "recognize" or "research." He then moves to the final point about `~아/어 하다`, explaining the Korean linguistic assumption that we can never truly "know" another's internal state.
