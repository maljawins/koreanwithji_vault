## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the compound verb ending `-아/어내다`, which is used to express the completion of an action. While the standalone verb `내다` can mean "to produce," "to supply," or "to put forth," its function as an auxiliary verb attached to an action verb is more specific. It conveys that the subject of the sentence successfully completed a task through their own significant effort, often after overcoming obstacles or facing some level of difficulty.

The lesson focuses on the nuance of "managing to do" or "finally being able to do" something. The instructor emphasizes that this form is not just about finishing an action, but about the struggle and personal agency involved in that completion. Because it describes a completed result, it is most frequently used in the past tense, though it can appear in other forms like `~수 있다` (can) to express the potential to overcome a challenge.

---

### Conjugation and Usage Constraints
To form this structure, one takes an action verb and conjugates it into the `-아/어` form (just like the polite present tense without `요`), then attaches `내다`. For example, `하다 (to do)` becomes `해내다`. The instructor clarifies that this is distinct from expressions like `화를 내다 (to get angry/show anger)`, where `내다` is part of a fixed idiom.

There are strict constraints on which verbs can be used with this form. It can only be used with action verbs that require a conscious expenditure of effort. Verbs describing natural phenomena or passive states are incompatible. For instance, one cannot say `비가 와내다` because `비가 오다 (to rain)` requires no effort from a subject. Similarly, `가지다 (to have)` is generally not used because it describes a state of possession rather than the active process of acquiring something through difficulty. The action must be performed by the subject themselves to show their personal achievement.

---

### The Nuance of Effort and Difficulty
The core meaning of `-아/어내다` is best translated into English as "finally managed to" or "was finally able to." It emphasizes that the action was not easy and that the subject might have almost failed. By using this form, the speaker highlights the difficulty of the task and the persistence required to finish it. 

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this "managed to" feeling. `찾다 (to look for/find)` becomes `찾아내다 (to manage to find/track down)`, implying the search was difficult. `알다 (to know)` becomes `알아내다 (to find out/figure out)`, suggesting that the information was discovered through investigation or effort rather than just being told. `견디다 (to endure)` becomes `견뎌내다 (to bear through/withstand)`, emphasizing the strength required to survive a hard situation.

---

### Example Sentences and Analysis
The instructor walks through several sentences to show how the nuance of effort changes the meaning of the base verb:

> **제가 이걸 해낼 수 있을까요?** / Do you think I can manage to do this?
This uses `해내다` to ask if the speaker has the capability to complete a difficult task by themselves.

> **강아지가 간식을 찾아내서 먹었어요.** / The dog managed to find the snack and ate it.
This implies the snack was hidden or hard to find, and the dog had to work to get it.

> **드디어 범인이 누군지 알아냈어요.** / I finally figured out who the culprit is.
The use of `드디어 (finally)` often pairs naturally with this form to emphasize the successful conclusion of a difficult process.

> **저도 이것과 똑같이 만들어낼 수 있을 것 같아요.** / I think I can manage to make it exactly like this too.
The speaker acknowledges that making the item is hard, but believes they can do it through effort.

> **장발장은 배고픔을 견뎌내지 못하고 빵을 훔쳤어요.** / Jean Valjean was unable to endure his hunger and stole bread.
Here, `견뎌내다` shows the extreme difficulty of resisting hunger.

> **철수는 잠의 유혹을 이겨내지 못하고 결국 잠이 들었어요.** / Chul-soo was unable to win over the temptation of sleep and eventually fell asleep.
`이겨내다` (from `이기다`, to win) emphasizes the struggle against the "temptation" (`유혹`).

> **그런 태도로는 누구의 마음도 얻어낼 수 없어요.** / With that kind of attitude, you can't win over anyone's heart.
`얻어내다` (from `얻다`, to get/receive) implies that winning someone's heart is a difficult goal requiring effort.

---

### Adding Further Emphasis
To add even more weight to the feeling of completion or the finality of the result, two other grammar forms can be attached to the end of `내다`:
1. **~버리다**: Adds a feeling of "completely" or "done with."
2. **~고 말다**: Adds a feeling of "ending up" doing something, often emphasizing strong will or an inevitable result.

The instructor demonstrates this with a high-stakes example:
> **난 이 시험을 일등으로 통과해내고 말 거야.** / I am going to end up passing this test in first place (no matter what).
By combining `통과하다 (to pass)`, `내다 (effort/difficulty)`, and `고 말다 (strong determination/finality)`, the speaker expresses an intense resolve to overcome a hard challenge.
