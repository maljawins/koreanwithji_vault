## Teaching Points

### Overview

This live lesson focuses on the auxiliary verbs 보다 and 보이다 when combined with other verbs and adjectives. The instructor explains that while 보다 independently means "to see" or "to watch," its meaning shifts significantly when it functions as a compound verb ending. The lesson is structured around three primary functional meanings of the `Present Verb + 보다` pattern: attempting an action to see a result, expressing past experience, and softening commands or suggestions. Additionally, the instructor covers the `Present Adjective + 보이다` pattern, which is used to describe how something or someone appears (e.g., "to look happy" or "to look difficult").

---

### Meaning 1: Attempting or Trying an Action

The first meaning of the `Present Verb + 보다` pattern is to tell someone to try or attempt something to see the result. In English, this is equivalent to "try doing [verb]." To form this, the main verb is conjugated into its present form (often represented in textbooks as the 아/어/여 form) followed by 보다. The instructor demonstrates this with several common verbs: 하다 (to do) becomes 해보다 (to try doing), 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹어보다 (to try eating), and 가다 (to go) becomes 가보다 (to try going).

The instructor provides several contextual examples to illustrate this usage. For instance, when suggesting someone play a game because it is fun, one would say:
> **이 게임 재미있으니까 너도 해봐.** / This game is fun, so you try it too.

Similarly, when offering food:
> **이 빵 내가 만들었어. 먹어봐.** / I made this bread. Try it.

A common addition to this pattern is the adverb 한 번 (one time/once). While it literally means "once," in this context, it is used to emphasize the "trying" aspect or to make the suggestion sound softer and more natural. It functions similarly to the Chinese expression "一下" (yīxià). For example, "한 번 먹어봐" sounds more natural than just "먹어봐" in many social situations.

---

### Meaning 2: Expressing Past Experience

The second meaning of the `Present Verb + 보다` pattern is to indicate that someone has ever done or tried something before. It is also used to ask questions about past experiences. When used this way, the auxiliary verb 보다 is almost always conjugated into the past tense: 봤어 or 봤어요.

The instructor emphasizes that this pattern inherently contains the meaning of "before," so adding a separate word for "before" is usually unnecessary. Examples include:
> **미국에 가봤어요.** / I have been to the US.
> **한국에 두 번 가봤어요.** / I have been to Korea twice.
> **말을 타 봤어요.** / I have ridden a horse before.

When asking a question, the intonation is simply raised: "미국에 가봤어요?" (Have you ever been to the US?). The instructor notes that while other patterns like `ㄴ/은 적이 있다` also express past experience, the `봤어요` form is the focus of this lesson for consistency.

---

### Meaning 3: Softening Commands and Suggestions

The third meaning of the `Present Verb + 보다` pattern is to make a command or suggestion sound less strong, demanding, or blunt. It functions as a "soft command" or encouragement. This is particularly useful in social interactions where a direct command might feel rude.

The instructor provides a dialogue example involving waiting:
> **A: 아직도 사장님이 안 오시네요.** / The boss isn't coming yet.
> **B: 조금만 더 기다려보세요.** / Please wait just a bit more.

In this case, B is not asking A for a favor (which would require `기다려 주세요`), but is instead encouraging A to wait a little longer. Adding 보세요 makes the suggestion sound much softer than the plain imperative `기다리세요`.

This softening effect also applies to the present progressive form `고 있다`. By adding 봐 to the end, the speaker makes a suggestion to "stay" or "keep" doing something sound more natural:
> **기다리고 있어봐. 곧 갈게.** / Stay waiting. I'll be there soon.
> **먼저 먹고 있어봐. 음료수 가져올게.** / Go ahead and keep eating. I'll bring a beverage.

---

### The 보이다 Pattern: Describing Appearance

The final section of the lesson covers the `Present Adjective + 보이다` pattern. Unlike the 보다 pattern which uses verbs, this pattern uses adjectives to express how something "looks" or "appears." The adjective is conjugated to its present form and combined with 보여 or 보여요.

The instructor provides a comprehensive list of adjective transformations:
- **많다 (to be many) → 많아 보여** (looks like a lot)
- **어렵다 (to be difficult) → 어려워 보여** (looks difficult)
- **행복하다 (to be happy) → 행복해 보여** (looks happy)
- **슬프다 (to be sad) → 슬퍼 보여** (looks sad)
- **피곤하다 (to be tired) → 피곤해 보여요** (you look tired)

Special attention is given to the ㅂ-irregular adjectives like 어렵다 (difficult) and 쉽다 (easy), where the ㅂ changes to 워 (어려워, 쉬워). The instructor also distinguishes between similar adjectives like 깨끗하다 (to be clean) and 깔끔하다 (to be neat), as well as 더럽다 (to be dirty) and 지저분하다 (to be messy).
