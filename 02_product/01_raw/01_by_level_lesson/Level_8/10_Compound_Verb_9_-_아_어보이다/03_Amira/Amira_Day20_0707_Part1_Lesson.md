## Teaching Points

- **The ~아/어 있다 Restriction:**
    - This form describes a continuing state resulting from a completed action.
    - **Rule:** It is primarily used with **intransitive** verbs. For example, Ji explains that while 닫다 (to close) is transitive, you must use the passive/intransitive version 닫히다 to say "the door is [in the state of being] closed" (문이 닫혀 있다).
    - **Transitive Exception:** For transitive verbs like 입다 (to wear), Korean uses the ~고 있다 form to describe the state of wearing something (옷을 입고 있다), which can mean both "currently putting on" or "staying dressed."

- **Compound Verbs with 주다 (to give):**
    - **~아/어 주다:** Indicates doing something as a favor for someone.
    - **Polite Requests:** Ji highlights that using ~아/어 주세요 is much softer and more polite than the standard command form ~세요. For example, "공항으로 가 주세요" (Please go to the airport) is preferred over "공항으로 가세요" (Go to the airport).
    - **Common Units:** 도와주다 (to help), 알려주다 (to let know/inform), 빌려주다 (to lend), 보내주다 (to send/let go), and 보여주다 (to show).

- **Compound Verbs with 보다 (to see/try):**
    - **~아/어 보다:** Means "to try doing something to see what happens" or "to experience." It is distinct from 노력하다 (to put in effort).
    - **Experience:** The form ~아/어 본 적(이) 있다/없다 is used to ask if someone has "ever" had a specific experience.
    - **Common Units:** 물어보다 (to ask) and 여쭤보다 (humble version of ask). Ji notes that 묻다 (to ask) is a ㄷ-irregular verb, changing to 물어.

- **Appearance and Becoming (~아/어 보이다 & ~아/어지다):**
    - **~아/어 보이다:** Used with Descriptive Verbs to mean "to look" or "to seem" (e.g., 행복해 보이다 - to look happy).
    - **~아/어지다:** Used to mean "to become" (e.g., 피곤해지다 - to get tired) or as a passive voice.
    - **External Factors:** Ji introduces a unique usage of ~아/어지다 to express "Can/Can't" due to external factors, such as "안 일어나져요" (I can't get up [because my body won't let me due to a hangover]).

## Lesson Flow

- **State of Being (~아/어 있다):** The recording starts with Ji explaining why "문을 닫아 있다" is incorrect. He explains that because 닫다 is transitive, it doesn't work with the state-of-being form. He corrects it to "문이 닫혀 있다" using the passive form. He then explains the "wearing" quirk where "옷을 입고 있다" is used for the state of being dressed.

- **The Favor Form (~아/어 주다):** Ji transitions to 주다, explaining it as "giving the act of doing." He provides examples like "사줬어요" (bought for me) and "만들어 주셨어요" (made for me). He emphasizes the "softening" effect of this form in requests, comparing a taxi driver command to a polite request.

- **Common 주다 Verbs:** Ji goes through a list of essential compound verbs. He explains the ㅂ-irregularity in 도와주다 (help) and the causative nature of 알려주다 (inform). He introduces the word "좀" as a way to further soften requests (e.g., "물 좀 주세요"). He also covers 빌려주다 (lend), 보내주다 (send/let go), and 보여주다 (show).

- **The Experience Form (~아/어 보다):** Ji clarifies that 보다 in this context doesn't mean "to see physically" but "to try and see the result." He distinguishes this from "effort" (노력하다). He uses the example of trying on a jacket (입어보다) and asking directions (물어보다).

- **Experience and Appearance:** Ji explains the "Have you ever..." structure (~아/어 본 적 있다) and how it differs slightly from the simple past experience form. He then moves to ~아/어 보이다, correcting a common mistake where students use ~아/어 하다 to mean "to seem." He uses social media (SNS) as a context for "looking happy" (행복해 보이다).

- **Becoming and Passive (~아/어지다):** The recording ends with Ji introducing the three usages of ~아/어지다: becoming, passive voice, and the "Can/Can't" nuance caused by external factors like a hangover or being too full to breathe properly.
