## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammatical structure **~아/어 있다**, which is used to describe a continuing state resulting from a completed action. While many learners are familiar with the progressive form (~고 있다) to describe an action currently in progress, this form focuses on the aftermath: the action has finished, and the subject remains in that resulting state.

The lesson covers the construction of the form, the critical distinction between "action in progress" and "continuing state," and the specific categories of verbs that are compatible with this structure. A significant portion of the video is dedicated to explaining why passive verbs are frequently required and why verbs that take direct objects are generally excluded from this particular grammar pattern.

---

### Construction and Meaning
To create this form, an action verb stem is conjugated using the standard **아/어/여** rules (the same used for the polite informal 요 form), followed by a space and the verb **있다 (to exist/to be)**. The verb 있다 can then be conjugated into various tenses or speech levels, such as 있어요 or 있었습니다.

The core meaning is that an action has reached completion, and the resulting state of that action continues to exist. The instructor defines a "state" as "how something is." For example, in the English sentence "The door is open," the action of opening is finished, but the "openness" continues. This is distinct from simply saying "The door opened," which focuses only on the past event without necessarily implying the current state.

---

### Continuing State vs. Progressive Action
The instructor highlights the difference between **~아/어 있다** (continuing state) and **~고 있다** (progressive action) using the verb **서다 (to stand)**. 

- **서고 있다** describes the physical process of moving from a sitting or lying position to a standing one. It is an action in progress.
- **서 있다** describes the state of already being in a standing position after the action of standing up has finished.

This distinction is vital for verbs of posture and movement. While one might say a slow-moving giant robot is **서고 있어요** (standing up right now), a person waiting at a bus stop is **서 있어요** (in the state of standing).

---

### The Role of Passive Verbs
A common mistake for learners is using active transitive verbs with this form. The instructor uses **닫다 (to close)** to illustrate this. Saying **닫아 있어요** is incorrect if you mean "the door is closed," because 닫다 implies an agent performing the action on an object. 

To describe the state of the door, one must use the passive version of the verb: **닫히다 (to be closed)**.
- **닫고 있어요**: I am (currently) closing the door.
- **닫혀 있어요**: The door is (in the state of being) closed.

Similarly, **열다 (to open)** becomes **열리다 (to be opened)** to form **열려 있다** (to be open). The instructor emphasizes that this form is frequently paired with passive verbs because it describes the state of the subject itself.

---

### Compatible Verbs and Restrictions
The instructor provides a list of common verbs used with this form, categorized by their nature:
- **Posture/Position:** 서다 (to stand), 앉다 (to sit), 눕다 (to lie down), 일어나다 (to get up), 깨다 (to awaken), 숨다 (to hide).
- **Movement/Existence:** 가다 (to go), 오다 (to come), 들어오다 (to enter), 위치하다 (to be located).
- **Life/Death/Status:** 죽다 (to die), 살다 (to live), 남다 (to be left over), 비다 (to be empty), 피다 (to bloom).
- **Attachment/Stains:** 붙다 (to be stuck), 묻다 (to be stained), 떨어지다 (to fall).

**The Object Rule:** This form cannot be used with verbs that take a direct object (marked by 을/를). For example, to say "I am wearing a shirt," you cannot use **입어 있어요** because 입다 (to wear) takes an object. Instead, you must use the progressive form **입고 있어요**, which in Korean covers both the act of putting it on and the state of wearing it.

---

### Location Markers: 에 vs. 에서
The instructor clarifies a common point of confusion regarding location markers when using **앉아 있다** or **서 있다**. Although sitting and standing might seem like "actions," in this grammatical context, they are "states." Therefore, the static location marker **에** is used instead of the dynamic action marker **에서**.
- **도서관에서 공부하고 있어요**: Studying (an action) at the library.
- **밖**에 **앉아 계세요**: Sitting (a state) outside.

---

### Honorifics and Tense
The verb **있다** in this structure can be replaced by its honorific equivalent **계시다** when referring to someone deserving of respect, such as a grandfather.
- **앉아 있어요** → **앉아 계세요** (Honorific)

Additionally, the tense of the sentence is determined by the conjugation of 있다.
- **누워 있어요**: I am lying down (now).
- **누워 있었어요**: I was lying down (past state).
