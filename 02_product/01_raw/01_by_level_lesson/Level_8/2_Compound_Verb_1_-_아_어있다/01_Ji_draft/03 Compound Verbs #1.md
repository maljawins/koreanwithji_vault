
**아/어 있다**

Alright, let's move on to our next compound verb: **아/어 있다**.

The idea behind this form is that **an action has been completed, and it continues to "exist" in that state.** It's kind of like how we use the "-en" ending in English like in "broken".

Let’s look at this movie. “Alive”. The Korean title of this movie is 살아있다 – a combination of 살다 to live and 있다 to be. So altogether 살아있다 means “to be alive” – the action of living is complete, and that state continues.

예문: 저희 할머니는 아직 살아 계세요.

By the way, 살아남다 (to survive) is also a compound verb, which means "to live and remain," or "to survive." So, 꼭 살아남아야 한다 means "You MUST survive!"

**~고 있다 VS 아/어있다**

Now, do you remember ~고 있다? We learned that it's used for something that's *currently going on*, like an action in progress. It uses the *same* verb 있다.

Let’s compare the two forms.

창문이 깨지고 있어요 – The glass is breaking. (right now)

창문이 깨져 있어요 – The glass is broken.

Let’s look at another example 앉다, to sit.

아기가 앉고 있어요 – The baby is sitting down. (Currently in action)

아기가 앉아 있어요 – The baby is sitting down (He is seated)

In English, you’d say the baby is sitting down for both cases, but it’s different in Korean

**아/어있다 restriction**

Here's an important restriction for **아/어 있다**: it **can only be used with verbs that can remain in that state** (i.e., verbs that describe a lasting state), and most of the time, those verbs are **intransitive verbs.**

For example, 닫다 (to close) is a **transitive verb - to** *close* something.

So, 닫아있다 would be wrong; you can't "be in the state of having closed the door" yourself. This makes no logical sense in Korean.

Therefore, you need to use **닫히다**, which is **intransitive** – “To close on its own” or “To BE closed” to be precise. This is the passive form of 닫다. We’ll learn about passive verbs in level 5.

So, you'd have to say **문이 닫혀있다** (O) (The door is [in the state of having been] closed).

In English, you use “close” for both cases like I “close” the door and the door “closes”. But in Korean, it’s different.

어제 약국에 갔는데 문이 닫혀있더라고요. (I went to the pharmacy yesterday, but I recall the door was closed.) Here, 닫혀있더라고요 clearly indicates the *state* of the door.

If you want to express this idea using a **transitive verb**, you would typically use the ~고 있다 form. For instance, 입다 (to wear) is a transitive verb. Even though you can *stay dressed*, 옷을 입어있다 (X) sounds awkward.

The natural way to say "I'm wearing clothes" is **옷을 입고 있다** (O). This form can mean both "currently wearing" (the action in progress) and "stay dressed" (the resulting state).

**아/어 주다**

Next, we have 아/어 주다. This means is you’re giving the act of doing “FOR SOMEONE” as a favor.

And you know that 주다 becomes 주시다 in honorific, and 드리다 in humble.

For example,

남자친구가 점심을 사줬어요 – My boyfriend bought me lunch

엄마가 파스타를 만들어 주셨어요. – Mom made pasta for me, for the family, or someone else.

And 아/어 주세요 is often translated as “please” because it’s used for polite requests.

For example, 가세요 like in 공항으로 가세요 sounds like “GO to the airport,” like you’re giving an order because it’s still a “command” even though you’re saying it honorifically.

So, we say “공항으로 가 주세요” instead like “kindly give the act of going to the airport for me.”

Let’s look at some common 주다 verbs.

**도와주다**

You actually had this word on your notes for today’s assignment.

Now, 도와주다 comes from 돕다 to help. But 돕다 is rarely used on its own because helping, by nature, is giving, so just memorize 도와주다 as is.

돕다 is an irregular verb which ends in ㅂ. So you have to replace ㅂ with 우 then add 아.

우아 when said fast, sounds like 와, this is how you get 도와주다

저 좀 도와주세요, please help me.

도와드릴까요? Can I help you?

What is 좀? 좀 is a shortened version of 조금 which means “a little”. So, it’s informal and used mostly in spoken Korean.

But 좀 itself has one usage that 조금 doesn’t. It softens the tone of requests and commands. Kind of like the word “some” in English.

So, if you said, 물 주세요 it sounds like “Give . me . water, please”, a bit too stiff and a bit rude.

So, 물 좀 주세요 would be much more polite and natural like “Could you give me some water, please?

**알려주다**

Another common verb, 알려주다. 알리다 derives from 알다, which means to know. This is something we call a causative verb. And causative verbs basically CAUSE the action. And 알리다 means to inform or to notify. We’ll learn all about causative verbs in level 5.

Notably, notification is 알림 in Korean.

And again, informing, by nature, is “giving” information to someone.

Example

이건 비밀이에요. 아무도 알려주지 마세요.

Now, 이건 is short of 이것”은”, the topic marker. And 비밀 means secret.

I’m sure you now have a better understanding about 은는 and 이가 but I figured I’d use this example to touch on it again.

If you used a subject marker and you said, 이”게” 비밀이에요, this would mean this is THE secret. So, there definitely needs to be some context behind this sentence.

Maybe it’s the secret that they’ve been hiding, that you wanted to talk about or something.

**빌려주다**

빌리다 means to borrow, now if you combine it with 주다, giving the act of borrowing, that means to lend.

예문 설명

* **나 방금 핸드폰이 꺼졌거든. 네 것 좀 빌려줄 수 있어?**

**– (Just so you know) My phone just turned off (died). Could you lend (me) yours?**

* **제가 지금 현금이 없네요. 이천원만 빌려주시면 내일 갚겠습니다.**

**– (I just realized) I don’t have cash. If you (could) lend me 2000 won, I’ll pay you back tomorrow**

**보내주다**

보내주다 comes from 보내다, which means to send.

And you send something “TO” someone.

And if you look at this example, it can also translate as “to let someone go”.

너무 피곤해요. 이제 집에 보내주세요. Send me home as in let me go home.

**보여주다**

Now, 보이다 is also the causative of 보다. So it also means “to show”

But 보이다 is rarely used on its own just like in 내보이다 to reveal and 선보이다 to present.

Now, showing something to someone is an act of giving. So, 그냥 “보이다” 보다 “보여주다”가 훨씬 더 자주 쓰인다. 그렇기 때문에 보여주다도 마찬가지로 그냥 하나의 동사로 생각하고 배우는 게 좋다.

예문 설명

우리의 능력을 보여주자

샘플은 아직 안 보여주셨습니다.

Let’s say you came back from a business meeting and your boss asks you, how did it go? Did you get the sample?

And you would go “샘플은” – Well, about the sample 아직 안 보여주셨습니다, using 은 to imply contrast. So you might follow up with something like but they did show me the photo or the price.
