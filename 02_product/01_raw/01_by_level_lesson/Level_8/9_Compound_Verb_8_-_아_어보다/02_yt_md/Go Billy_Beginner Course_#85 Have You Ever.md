## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces a grammar structure used to express past experiences, equivalent to the English phrase "Have you ever...?" or "I have done... before." This lesson builds upon the previously learned `아/어보다` form, which means "to try doing something." By combining this "try" form with a specific noun meaning "time" or "experience," learners can discuss their history with various activities. The lesson also covers two important comparative and quantifying words, `정도` (about) and `만큼` (as much as), which frequently appear when discussing the frequency or scale of one's experiences.

---

### Expressing Past Experience: 아/어본 적이 있다/없다

To say that you have the experience of doing something, you use the structure: **Action Verb Stem (conjugated) + 본 적이 있다**. To say you have never done something, you use **본 적이 없다**. The instructor explains the literal breakdown: `본` comes from the verb `보다` (to try/see), and `적` is a noun meaning a "time" or an "experience." Therefore, the form literally translates to "having the experience of trying to do something."

The conjugation process involves two steps. First, conjugate the action verb into the standard `아/어` form as if you were adding `요`, but do not attach the `요`. Second, add `본 적이 있다` or `본 적이 없다` and then conjugate the final `있다/없다` for the desired politeness level.

> **한국에 가본 적이 있어요?** / Have you ever been to Korea before?
> **그걸 해본 적이 없어요.** / I've never tried doing that before.

A special rule applies when the main verb is `보다` (to see/watch). Instead of saying `봐본 적이 있다`, which sounds redundant, you simply use `본 적이 있다`. The `본` already carries the meaning of "seen" in this specific context.

---

### Quantifying Experiences with 정도 (About/Approximately)

The word `정도` is used to express an approximate amount or a guess. It is a noun that follows another noun, separated by a space. It is commonly used when discussing how many times an experience has occurred or how long an activity lasted.

> **십 분 정도 기다렸어요.** / I waited about ten minutes.
> **세 번 정도 해본 적 있어요.** / I have tried it about three times before.

The instructor provides further examples of its versatility with different units of measurement:
- **오백 원 정도예요.** / It's about five hundred won.
- **두 시간 정도 일기를 쓰고 있었어요.** / I was writing a journal for about two hours.
- **십 그램 정도 추가를 했어요.** / I added about ten grams.

---

### Comparing Experiences with 만큼 (As Much As)

The word `만큼` means "as much as" or "to the extent of." Unlike `정도`, it is attached directly to the noun it follows without a space. It is used to compare the degree or amount of a quality or action between two things.

> **윤소만큼 예의 바른 사람은 없어요.** / There's nobody as polite as Yunso. (Literally: There is no person who is polite as much as Yunso.)
> **형만큼 운전을 잘 하지 못해요.** / I cannot drive as well as you (older brother).

The instructor highlights its use in expressing high praise or absolute comparisons:
- **한식만큼 맛있는 음식이 없어요.** / There's no food as delicious as Korean food.
- **이 차만큼 빠른 차가 없어요.** / There's no car as fast as this car.
- **저는 선생님만큼 한국말을 잘 하지 못해요.** / I can't speak Korean as well as the teacher.

---

### Nuance Note: Experience vs. Attempt

The instructor clarifies the difference between the long form `아/어본 적이 있다` and the shorter `아/어보다`. While both involve the concept of "trying," the `본 적이 있다` form specifically emphasizes that the action is a part of one's life experience or history. If you simply want to say you "tried" something without emphasizing it as a significant life experience, you can use the standard `아/어보다` past tense conjugation (e.g., `해 봤어요` vs `해 본 적이 있어요`).
