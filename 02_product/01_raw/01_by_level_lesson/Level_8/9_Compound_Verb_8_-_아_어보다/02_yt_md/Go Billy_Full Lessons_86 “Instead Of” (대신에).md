## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Korean grammar form used to express the concept of "instead of." This is a versatile and essential structure for comparing choices or explaining substitutions in daily conversation. The lesson is divided into two primary parts: using the form with nouns and using it with action verbs.

The instructor emphasizes a key word order difference between English and Korean. While English places "instead of" before the item being replaced (e.g., "instead of a movie"), Korean attaches the grammar marker after the noun (e.g., "movie instead of"). The video uses clear on-screen text and practical examples to demonstrate these transformations and concludes with a series of practice sentences to reinforce the rules.

---

### Using 대신에 with Nouns
To express "instead of [Noun]" in Korean, you simply take the noun and attach the word 대신 followed by the particle 에. This results in the construction: **Noun + 대신에**. The instructor notes that while the 에 is often included, it can sometimes be omitted in casual speech, though using the full 대신에 is the standard way to learn the form.

A critical point of the lesson is the reversal of word order compared to English. In the English sentence "I watched a drama instead of a movie," the word "movie" follows "instead of." In the Korean translation, the word for movie (영화) comes first, followed by 대신에. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this:

- **그거 대신에 이거 하세요.** / Do this instead of that.
- **사과 대신에 오렌지를 먹었어요.** / I ate an orange instead of an apple.
- **저 대신에 해주세요.** / Please do it instead of me.
- **치마 대신에 바지를 샀어요.** / I bought pants instead of a skirt.

In each case, the item that is being replaced or substituted is the one that appears immediately before 대신에.

---

### Using 대신에 with Verbs
When you want to say "instead of [Doing something]," the construction changes slightly to accommodate a verb. To use this form with an action verb, you take the verb stem and attach the suffix 는, followed by 대신에. This creates the construction: **Verb Stem + 는 대신에**.

The instructor demonstrates this by expanding on the previous movie example. If the sentence is "I watched a drama instead of going to see a movie," the verb "to go" (가다) must be conjugated. The stem 가 is combined with 는 to become 가는, resulting in the phrase 가는 대신에.

The instructor provides further examples of this verbal construction:

- **뭘 먹는 대신에 물을 마셔요.** / Drink water instead of eating something.
- **도서관으로 가는 대신에 친구들이랑 놀러 갔어요.** / I went to play with friends instead of going to the library.
- **잠을 자는 대신에 저를 도와주세요.** / Help me instead of sleeping.
- **컴퓨터 게임을 하는 대신에 한국어를 공부하세요.** / Study Korean instead of playing games.

The instructor notes that this form allows for more complex sentences where one action is substituted for another, following the same logic as the noun-based form where the action being replaced precedes the grammar marker.
