## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the compound verb form 아/어보다, which is used to express the idea of "trying" to do something. This form is specifically used with action verbs to indicate that someone is attempting an action to see what the result or experience will be like. The lesson covers the conjugation rules, provides numerous practical examples ranging from food to clothing, and explores related linguistic nuances such as the dual meanings of question words and how to express "should" and "shouldn't" in Korean.

---

### The "Try Doing" Form: 아/어보다
To say that you are trying an action, you must use the action verb 보다 (to see) in combination with another action verb. The construction literally translates to "doing something and seeing how it goes." The instructor explains the two-step conjugation process: first, conjugate the main action verb into its standard polite form but remove the 요 ending; second, attach the verb 보다 and conjugate it into the desired tense or politeness level. For example, the instructor demonstrates how 김치를 먹다 (to eat kimchi) becomes 먹어봤어요 (tried eating kimchi), and how making side dishes is expressed as 반찬을 만들어 보세요 (please try making side dishes).

---

### Examples and Usage Nuances
The instructor provides a variety of sentences to illustrate the versatility of the 아/어보다 form. When used with the counter 번 (times), it can describe how many times someone has tried an action, such as 두 번 먹어봤어요 (I tried eating it twice). The form is also frequently paired with the ~고 싶다 (want to) ending to express a desire to try something, like 이 드레스를 입어보고 싶어요 (I want to try on this dress). 

A significant portion of the lesson is dedicated to the specific verbs for "wearing" items, as they change based on the body part involved. For clothing worn over the body like shirts or pants, 입다 is used (입어보다). For items worn on the feet like socks or shoes, 신다 is used (신어보세요). For items worn on the head or face like glasses or hats, 쓰다 is used (써봤어요). The instructor also notes that the 아/어보다 form implies the action is actually being performed to gain experience, rather than just making a mental effort to do it.

---

### Question Words with Indefinite Meanings
The instructor takes a brief detour to explain that certain question words in Korean have second meanings as indefinite pronouns. 누구 (who) can mean "someone," 뭐 (what) can mean "something," and 어디 (where) can mean "somewhere." The distinction between the question and the indefinite meaning is determined by context and intonation. To emphasize the indefinite meaning (someone, something, somewhere), a speaker should place a higher intonation on the verb rather than on the question word itself. For instance, putting the stress on 뭐 in 뭐 먹었어요? makes it "What did you eat?", while stressing the verb makes it "Did you eat something?" The instructor also introduces alternative forms for these indefinite pronouns: 누군가 (someone), 뭔가 (something), and 어딘가 (somewhere), noting that both sets are interchangeable.

---

### Expressing "Should" and "Shouldn't"
As an extension of making suggestions, the instructor teaches how to say "should" and "shouldn't" using the ~(으)면 form. To say "should," one combines the ~(으)면 form with 좋다 (to be good), literally meaning "it is good if..." For example, 지금 떠나면 좋아요 translates to "You should depart now." Conversely, to say "shouldn't," the ~(으)면 form is combined with 안 되다 (to not be okay), literally meaning "it is not okay if..." This results in sentences like 지금 떠나면 안 돼요 (You shouldn't depart now).

---

### Conversation Practice and Spacing Notes
The lesson concludes with a dialogue between characters 지수 and 제니, where they discuss trying to make sushi (초밥). This practice highlights the use of the progressive tense with the "try" form: 만들어 보고 있어 (I am trying to make). The instructor points out a subtle spacing rule: 한 번 (with a space) means "one time," whereas 한번 (without a space) is often used with the 보다 form to mean "a try" or "giving it a go." The dialogue also reinforces the use of the past progressive tense, 보고 있었다 (was trying), to describe a past attempt.
