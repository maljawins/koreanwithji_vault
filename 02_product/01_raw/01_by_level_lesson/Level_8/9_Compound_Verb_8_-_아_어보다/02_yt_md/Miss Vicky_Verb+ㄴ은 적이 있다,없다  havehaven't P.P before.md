## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson builds upon a previous class where students learned the `아/어 보다` pattern, which also conveys trying or experiencing an action. The instructor explains that while both patterns are used to talk about past experiences, the `ㄴ/은 적이 있다` structure specifically highlights the existence of that experience as a fact in one's history.

The learning arc begins with a review of the `아/어 봤다` pattern to establish a foundation. From there, the instructor transitions into the mechanics of `ㄴ/은 적이 있다`, covering conjugation rules for various verb stems, including irregulars. The lesson then moves into the negative form, the use of emphatic adverbs like `한 번도` (not even once), and how to handle complex verbs that already contain the `주다` (to do for someone) auxiliary verb. ---

### Expressing Past Experience with ㄴ/은 적이 있다

The instructor explains that `ㄴ/은 적이 있다` literally translates to "the experience of [verb] exists." To form this expression, you take the base stem of a verb. If the stem ends in a vowel (no batchim), you add `ㄴ` as a bottom consonant followed by `적이 있다`. If the stem ends in a consonant (batchim), you add `은 적이 있다`. For example, the verb `먹다 (to eat)` has the stem `먹`, which ends in a batchim, so it becomes `먹은 적이 있어요 (I have eaten [it] before)`.

An important nuance the instructor highlights is that you can combine this pattern with the `보다` auxiliary verb. Instead of just using the base verb, you can use the `아/어 보다` form as the stem. For instance, `먹어보다 (to try eating)` becomes `먹어본 적이 있어요`. The instructor notes that while this might seem redundant because both parts imply "experience," it is extremely common in native speech. Both `먹은 적이 있어요` and `먹어본 적이 있어요` are correct and interchangeable.

---

### Handling Irregular Verbs

The instructor demonstrates how irregular verbs behave with this pattern. For `ㄷ` irregular verbs like `듣다 (to listen)`, the `ㄷ` changes to `ㄹ` before adding the suffix, resulting in `들은 적이 있어요 (I have heard [that] before)` or `들어본 적이 있어요`. For `ㄹ` irregular verbs like `울다 (to cry)`, the `ㄹ` is dropped and replaced by `ㄴ`, resulting in `운 적이 있어요 (I have cried before)`.

The instructor also clarifies the usage of the verb `보다 (to see/watch)`. Since the grammar pattern itself often uses `보다`, students might wonder if they should say `봐본 적이 있어요`. The instructor explains that for the verb "to see," you typically just use the single verb: `본 적이 있어요`. She provides the example `방탄소년단을 실제로 본 적이 있어요 (I have seen BTS in real life)`. She also introduces the useful adverb `실제로 (in real life/actually)` to add detail to experience-based sentences.

---

### Negation: ㄴ/은 적이 없다

To express that you have never done something, the instructor explains that you simply replace `있다 (to exist/have)` with `없다 (to not exist/not have)`. The conjugation rules for the verb stem remain the same. For example, `가다 (to go)` becomes `간 적이 없어요` or `가본 적이 없어요`, both meaning "I have never been there." This is equivalent to saying `안 가봤어요`.

To emphasize the negation, the instructor introduces the adverb `한 번도 (not even once)`. This can be placed before the verb or before the `없어요` part of the pattern. For example, `한국에 한 번도 간 적이 없어요` emphasizes that the speaker has not been to Korea even a single time. This adds a layer of strong denial or a sense of "never in my life" to the statement.

---

### Usage with 주다 (Benefactive) Verbs

The instructor addresses how to use this pattern with verbs already combined with `주다` (to do something for someone), such as `도와주다 (to help)` or `요리해주다 (to cook for someone)`. She explains that for these verbs, it is not necessary to add an extra `보다` before the `적이 있다` pattern. While `도와줘 본 적이 있어요` is technically possible, it makes the verb "messier."

Instead, it is much more natural to simply attach the pattern to the `주다` stem. For example, `제프는 저를 도와준 적이 있어요 (Jeff has helped me before)` or `아빠는 저를 한 번도 안아준 적이 없어요 (My dad has never given me a hug)`. The instructor also shows how to quantify these experiences using adverbs like `많이 (a lot)`. If someone has done something for you many times, you can say `요리해 준 적이 많이 있어요` or more simply `요리해 준 적이 많아요`. Conversely, if it hasn't happened often, you can say `요리해 준 적이 별로 없어요` or `요리해 준 적이 적어요 (The times [he] cooked for me are few)`.
