## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form ~아/어 보니(까), which is used to express a realization or discovery made after attempting or completing an action. This structure combines the compound verb form ~아/어 보다 (to try doing something) with the causal or sequential ending ~(으)니까 (because/since). While ~(으)니까 often translates to "because," in this specific combination, it functions to show that the first action provided the basis for a new realization.

The lesson covers the construction of the form, the nuance of discovery, and the distinction between spoken and written registers. Through various examples, the instructor demonstrates how this form is typically used by the speaker to describe their own experiences and the resulting insights, often involving a sense of "now that I've tried it, I've realized..."

---

### Construction and Usage
To create this form, the speaker takes an action verb stem and conjugates it into the ~아/어 form (the same way one would create the polite 요 form), then attaches 보니 or 보니까. The instructor notes that in daily conversation, the full form 보니까 is most common, whereas in formal writing, the shortened 보니 is frequently used. 

The core meaning is "after trying to do [verb], I realized [fact]." It implies that the speaker has gained new information or changed their perspective as a result of the action. Because it uses the 보다 (to try) auxiliary, it often suggests that the action was done for the first time or as an experiment. A special rule applies to the verb 보다 (to see/look) itself: rather than doubling it to 봐 보니까, speakers simply use 보니까. Similarly, the verb 알아보다 (to look into/investigate) already contains 보다, so it becomes 알아보니(까) rather than 알아봐 보니(까).

---

### Realizations and Discoveries
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate how the form highlights a discovery. For instance, opening a window might lead to the discovery that it is snowing:

> **창문을 열어보니 눈이 오고 있더라고요.** / I opened the window and realized (saw) it was snowing.

In this case, the speaker uses the ~더라고요 ending to emphasize that they are recalling a past discovery. Another example involves trying food at a famous restaurant:

> **이걸 먹어보니 여기가 왜 유명한지 알겠어요.** / After trying this, I realize why this place is famous.

The discovery can also be about physical comfort or quality. The instructor demonstrates this with examples about sitting in a chair or walking in shoes:

> **직접 앉아보니 생각보다 편했어요.** / After actually sitting down in it, it was more comfortable than I expected.
> **그 신발을 신고 걸어보니 좀 별로였어요.** / After walking around in those shoes, I realized they weren't very good.

---

### Common Expressions and Life Lessons
One of the most frequent ways this grammar appears in natural Korean is the expression 생각해보니(까). This is used when the speaker has a new thought or changes their mind after a moment of reflection.

> **생각해보니 빨간색이 더 예쁜 것 같아요.** / Now that I think about it, I think red is prettier.

The form is also used to describe profound life realizations, such as understanding one's parents only after experiencing parenthood oneself:

> **아이를 키워보니 엄마의 마음을 이해하게 됐어요.** / After raising a child, I came to understand how my mom feels.

Finally, the instructor shows how it can be used to acknowledge the reputation of a place after living there:

> **이 동네에 살아보니 사람들이 왜 여길 좋아하는지 알겠네요.** / After living in this neighborhood, I understand now why people like it here.
