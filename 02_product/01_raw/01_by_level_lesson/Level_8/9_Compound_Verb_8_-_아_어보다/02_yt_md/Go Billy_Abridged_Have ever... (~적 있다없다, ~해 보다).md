## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar structure used to express past experiences, equivalent to the English "have ever done" or "have done before." To build this, the lesson first reviews the compound verb form ~아/어보다, which means "to try doing something" in the sense of doing it to see what happens. The core of the lesson focuses on combining this "try" form with the noun 적 (experience) and the verbs 있다 (to exist/have) or 없다 (to not exist/not have). By the end of the video, learners will understand how to describe their life experiences, contrast those experiences using markers, and distinguish between simply "trying" something once versus having the general "experience" of it.

---

### Review: The ~아/어보다 Form

Before diving into the "have ever" structure, the instructor clarifies the meaning of ~아/어보다. On screen, he writes ~보다 and notes that while 보다 literally means "to see," when attached to another verb, it means "to try" doing that action. He emphasizes that this does not mean putting in a great deal of effort; rather, it means actually performing the action to see how it goes or what the result is. For example, 먹어보다 means to eat something to see if you like it.

---

### Construction of ~본 적(이) 있다/없다

The "have ever" form is built in several steps. First, the main verb is conjugated to the standard 요 form, but the 요 is dropped. For example, 하다 becomes 해, 가다 becomes 가, and 먹다 becomes 먹어. Next, you attach 본, which is the past tense adjective form of 보다. This creates a phrase meaning "having tried."

The next component is the noun 적, which the instructor defines on the whiteboard as "an experience" or "something that happened." Following this, the subject marker 이 is often added (though it is optional and frequently omitted in casual speech). Finally, the sentence ends with either 있다 (to exist/have) or 없다 (to not exist/not have).

The literal translation of a sentence like 해본 적이 있어요 is "The experience of having tried doing [it] exists." In natural English, this simply means "I have done it before." Conversely, using 없다 indicates that you have never had that specific experience.

---

### Examples and Conjugation

The instructor provides several example sentences to demonstrate the form in practice:
- **해본 적이 있어요.** / I have done it before.
- **한국어를 배워 본 적이 없어요.** / I have never tried learning Korean before.
- **저는 김치를 먹어본 적이 없어요.** / I have never tried kimchi before.
- **한국에 가본 적이 있어요?** / Have you ever been to Korea?

He notes that the final verb can be conjugated into any polite or casual level, such as 있습니까 (formal question), 있나요 (polite question), or 있어 (casual). A special rule is mentioned for the verb 보다 (to see/watch) itself. Instead of saying "보고 본 적," which sounds redundant, you simply use the past adjective form of the main verb: **이 영화를 본 적이 있어요** (I have seen this movie before).

---

### Using Markers for Contrast and Emphasis

The instructor explains how changing the marker after 적 can alter the nuance of the sentence. While the subject marker 이 is the default, using the topic marker 은 adds a sense of contrast. For example, **비행기를 타본 적은 없어요** implies "As for riding an airplane, I haven't done that (but I might have ridden a boat or a train)." It sets the experience apart from others.

Similarly, using the marker 도 (also/even) adds emphasis. **영국에 가본 적도 없어요** translates to "I haven't even been to England" or "I don't even have the experience of going to England."

A critical grammatical warning is given: never use the object marker 을/를 before 있다 or 없다 in this structure. You can say 적이, 적은, or 적도, but **적을** is strictly incorrect.

---

### ~본 적 있다 vs. ~아/어 보다

Learners often confuse the "experience" form (~본 적 있다) with the simple "try" form (~아/어 보다). The instructor explains that 적 specifically highlights the general life experience of having done something at some point in the past. If you want to say "I have been to America before" (focusing on the experience), use **미국에 가본 적이 있어요**. If you just want to say "I went to America (to see what it was like)," use **미국에 가봤어요**.

Furthermore, the simple ~아/어 보다 form is more flexible with tenses. You can say **먹어보고 있어요** (I am currently trying it), whereas the 적 form is almost exclusively used for past experiences.

---

### Experience Without "Trying" (~한 적 있다)

Finally, the instructor addresses the difference between using the "try" form (~해본 적 있다) and just using the past adjective form of the verb (~한 적 있다). While both are grammatically possible, ~해본 적 있다 is much more common when discussing experiences. This is because most life experiences are viewed as things one "tried out." Using **한 적이 있어요** (I have done it) is less common than **해본 적이 있어요** (I have tried doing it) in this specific context.
