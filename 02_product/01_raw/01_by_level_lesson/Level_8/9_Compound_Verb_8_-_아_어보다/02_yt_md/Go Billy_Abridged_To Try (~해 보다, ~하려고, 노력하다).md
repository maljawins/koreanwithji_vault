## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson clarifies the distinctions between three primary ways to express "trying" in Korean: the compound verb form ~아/어 보다, the intentional grammar structure ~(으)려고 하다, and the verb 노력하다 (to make an effort). While all three can translate to "try" in English, they carry fundamentally different nuances regarding success, intention, and physical effort. The instructor emphasizes that ~아/어 보다 focuses on experiencing an action to see the result, ~(으)려고 하다 focuses on the internal intention or attempt to do something, and 노력하다 emphasizes the exertion of effort itself.

---

### The Experience Marker: ~아/어 보다

The first method of saying "to try" involves attaching the verb 보다 (to see) to a conjugated verb stem. To form this, you conjugate the main verb into its standard 요 form (without the 요) and then add 보다. For example, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹어 보다, and 하다 (to do) becomes 해 보다. The literal meaning is "to do [verb] and see how it goes." This form implies that the action is actually performed or experienced, rather than just attempted. It is frequently used when suggesting someone try a new food, as in 먹어 봐요 (Try eating it/Try it), or when describing a past experience, such as 선생님을 만나 봤어요 (I tried meeting the teacher / I met the teacher to see how it was).

The instructor highlights that this form does not imply failure or a struggle; it simply means you went ahead and did the action to check the outcome. A common usage is with the verb 입다 (to wear) to create 입어 보다, which is the standard way to say "to try on clothes" in a store. It can also be used with movement verbs like 가다 (to go) to form 가 보다. Saying 한국에 가 보고 싶어요 (I want to try going to Korea) implies a desire to go there and experience what it is like, whereas just saying 가고 싶어요 (I want to go) is a simpler statement of desire. A useful phrase for learners is 해 보면 알 거예요 (If you try it, you will know), suggesting that the experience itself will provide the answer.

---

### The Intentional Attempt: ~(으)려고 하다

The second form, ~(으)려고 하다, shifts the focus from the experience to the intention or attempt. It is often translated as "intend to" or "going to" (in the sense of planning). To construct this, you attach 려고 to verb stems ending in a vowel (하 + 려고 = 하려고) and 으려고 to stems ending in a consonant (먹 + 으려고 = 먹으려고). An exception exists for stems ending in ㄹ, where you simply add 려고 (살다 becomes 살려고). Unlike the 보다 form, this structure does not guarantee that the action was successfully completed; it only states what the speaker is putting effort toward or intending to do.

A critical grammatical constraint of ~(으)려고 하다 is that it is generally restricted to the first and second person (I or You). You cannot use it to state the intentions of a third party (He, She, They) because, in Korean logic, you cannot definitively know the internal intentions of another person. For example, you can say 다이어트를 하려고 해요 (I am trying/intending to diet), but you should avoid using this specific form for someone else like "Chul-soo." The instructor contrasts 친구를 만나려고 해요 (I am intending to meet my friend) with 친구를 만나 볼 거예요 (I am going to try meeting my friend). The former emphasizes the plan and effort to make the meeting happen, while the latter suggests meeting them to see how the encounter goes (perhaps after a long time apart).

---

### Exerting Effort: 노력하다 and Combined Forms

The verb 노력하다 literally means "to put forth effort" or "to exert oneself." It can be used as a standalone verb, such as 지금 노력하고 있어요 (I am trying/putting in effort right now). However, it becomes particularly powerful when combined with the intentional ~(으)려고 form. By saying [Verb Stem]~(으)려고 노력하다, you express that you are actively exerting effort while intending to achieve a specific goal.

The instructor provides the example 한국어를 배우려고 열심히 노력하고 있어요 (I am trying hard to learn Korean). Literally, this translates to "I am trying hard/exerting effort while intending to learn Korean." This combination is the most natural way to express "trying hard to [do something]" in Korean. Another example of using the intentional form as a connector is 여행을 가려고 돈을 모으고 있어요 (I am saving money intending to go on a trip). Here, the first clause provides the purpose or intention behind the action in the second clause. Similarly, 수학 선생님이 되려고 수학을 공부했어요 (I studied math intending to become a math teacher) shows the intention driving the past action of studying.
