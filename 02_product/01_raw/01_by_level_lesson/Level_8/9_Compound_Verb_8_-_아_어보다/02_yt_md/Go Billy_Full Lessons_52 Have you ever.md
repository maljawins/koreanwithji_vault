## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammatical structure used to express past experiences, equivalent to the English phrase "Have you ever...?" or "I have done... before." This lesson builds upon the previously taught compound verb form `아/어보다`, which means "to try doing something." By combining this "try" form with a specific noun and existence verbs, learners can describe their life experiences or ask others about theirs.

---

### Expressing Experience with 적이 있다/없다
To state that one has the experience of doing something, the instructor explains a multi-step construction. First, the verb must be put into the `아/어보다` form. Then, the word `적` is added, which functions as a noun meaning "a time" or "an experience." Following `적`, the subject marker `이` is added, though the instructor notes that this marker is optional in casual conversation. Finally, the sentence is completed with the verb `있다 (to exist/to have)` to indicate you have the experience, or `없다 (to not exist/to not have)` to indicate you do not.

The instructor demonstrates the full combined form: `본 적이 있다` and `본 적이 없다`. While it may appear long to beginners, he emphasizes that it becomes simple once practiced a few times. This structure allows for both affirmative statements about one's history and interrogative sentences to inquire about someone else's past actions.

---

### Example Sentences and Usage
The video provides several practical examples to illustrate the grammar in context. When asking about travel history, the instructor uses:
> **한국에 가본 적이 있어요?** / Have you ever been to Korea before?

To express a lack of experience regarding meeting someone, the example provided is:
> **제 친구를 만나본 적이 없어요?** / Have you never met my friend before?

Another common usage involves media or entertainment:
> **이 영화를 본 적이 있어요?** / Have you ever seen this movie before?

---

### Special Note on the Verb 보다
A critical nuance is highlighted regarding the verb `보다 (to see/watch)`. While most verbs require conjugation into the `아/어보다` form before adding `적`, the instructor explains that when the base verb is already `보다`, it does not need to be conjugated into a compound form (which would redundantly result in `봐 보다`). Instead, it can simply be used in its past participle form `본`. The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence:
> **본 적이 없어요.** / I've never seen it before.

In this specific case, `본` acts as the modifier for `적`, simplifying the expression while maintaining the meaning of having seen or experienced the content.
