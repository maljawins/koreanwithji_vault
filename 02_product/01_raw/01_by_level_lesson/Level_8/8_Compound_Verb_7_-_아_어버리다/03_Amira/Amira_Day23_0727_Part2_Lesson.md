## Teaching Points

*   **The Plain Form (Formal Low):** Ji introduces this as the "Formal Low" level. It is the standard for written Korean (articles, essays, journals) because it is neutral and directed at an unknown audience.
    *   **Spoken Usage:** In speech, it is considered casual (반말) and used in three specific contexts:
        1.  **Announcements:** e.g., "지금부터 회의를 시작한다!" (We start the meeting now!).
        2.  **Self-talk:** e.g., "아, 배고프다." (Ah, I'm hungry).
        3.  **Storytelling:** Using a rising intonation to set the scene, e.g., "어제 이상한 사람 봤다~?" (I saw a strange person yesterday...?).
*   **Plain Form Conjugation (Present Tense):**
    *   **Action Verbs:** Stem + ㄴ다 (vowel ending) or 는다 (consonant ending). For ㄹ irregulars, drop the ㄹ and add ㄴ다 (살다 → 산다).
    *   **Descriptive Verbs:** Stays the same as the dictionary form (좋다 → 좋다).
    *   **이다 (To be):** Becomes (이)다. After a vowel, the 이 is often dropped (의자다).
*   **Plain Form Conjugation (Past & Future):**
    *   **Past:** Past stem + 다 (했다, 먹었다).
    *   **Future:** Stem + ㄹ/을 거다 (갈 거다, 먹을 거다). Ji notes that 거다 is a shortened version of 것이다.
*   **Plain Form Questions:** Ji explains that while written questions often use ㄴ가?, spoken self-talk questions typically use the 나? ending (e.g., "벌써 시간이 이렇게 됐나?" / Has it already gotten this late?).
*   **Commands and Suggestions:**
    *   **Suggestions:** Stem + 자 (same as regular 반말).
    *   **Commands:** Stem + (으)시오. Ji emphasizes that this is *never* used in speech and is only found in formal written instructions, like exam papers (e.g., "문제를 푸시오" / Solve the problem).
*   **The Quoting Form Prerequisite:** Ji reveals that the Plain Form is essential because the Quoting Form for statements is constructed by simply adding 고 to the Plain Form (e.g., 한다 + 고 = 한다고).
*   **Grammar Nuance - 기는 하다 (긴 하다):** During note feedback, Ji corrects "재미있을 줄 알았는데" (I thought it would be fun [but it wasn't]) to "재미있을 줄 알긴 했는데" (I *did* know it would be fun [but it was even more so]).
