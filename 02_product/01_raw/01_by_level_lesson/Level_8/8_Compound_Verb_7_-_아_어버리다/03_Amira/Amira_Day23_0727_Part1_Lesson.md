## Teaching Points

*   **Future Tense Nuances:** Ji clarifies that while (으)려나 보다 (looks like it's about to) and ㄹ/을 건가 보다 (looks like it will) are often used interchangeably, (으)려나 보다 is technically a shortened form of 려고 하나 보다 and is not strictly a future tense grammatically, whereas ㄹ/을 건가 보다 (from 것이다 + ㄴ가 보다) is.
*   **아/어 버리다 (Completion/Impulse):** This form adds the nuance of "throwing away" an action. Ji teaches two main keywords: "Away" and "Screw it."
    *   **Positive/Relief:** 숙제를 다 해 버렸어요! (I finished all my homework!) — implies the task is "away" from the to-do list.
    *   **Neutral/Regret:** 결국 다 먹어 버렸어요. (In the end, I ate it all.) — implies complete consumption.
    *   **Negative/Finality:** 그냥 포기해 버렸어요. (I just gave up completely.) — implies the effort was "thrown away."
*   **Slang Usage (지르다):** Ji introduces the slang 지르다, which means to buy something impulsively. When combined with 버리다, it becomes 질러버려! (Just buy it! / Screw it, buy it!).
*   **Binary Verbs (잊어버리다 & 잃어버리다):** Ji explains that certain actions are binary—you either do them or you don't. Because you cannot "partially" forget or "partially" lose an object, the verbs 잊다 (to forget) and 잃다 (to lose) are almost always used with 버리다 to show the "complete" nature of the action.
*   **Lose (잃다 vs. 지다):** Ji distinguishes between 잃다 (losing an object) and 지다 (losing a game or match). To say a team lost a match with the "away" nuance, one uses 져버렸어요.
