## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the verb 버리다, which literally means "to throw away." While it functions as a standalone active verb, its primary focus in this lesson is its role as a grammatical ending attached to other verbs. When used as an auxiliary verb in the form ~아/어 버리다, it does not change the literal action of the preceding verb but instead infuses the sentence with specific emotional nuances.

The learning arc begins with the literal usage and passive forms of 버리다, moves into the mechanics of conjugation, and then explores the "totally" or "completely" sentiment it conveys. The instructor emphasizes that while this form can express relief or positive completion, it is most frequently used to convey negative emotions such as disappointment, sadness, or regret. The lesson concludes by distinguishing between various ways to say "to forget" in Korean, depending on whether one is forgetting information or a physical object.

---

### Literal Meaning and Passive Form
As a standalone verb, 버리다 means `to throw away`. The instructor provides the example sentence `쓰레기를 버렸어요 (I threw away the trash)` to demonstrate its standard active use. He also notes that the passive form is `버림(을) 받다`, which literally translates to "to receive throwing away" or "to be thrown away." However, he advises learners that the active voice is far more common in daily conversation; one would typically say "he threw away the trash" rather than "the trash was thrown away."

---

### Conjugation Mechanics of ~아/어 버리다
To use 버리다 as a verb ending, the main action verb must be conjugated into its `요` form, with the `요` then removed. For example, the verb `하다 (to do)` becomes `해`, to which `버리다` is attached to form `해 버리다`. The instructor highlights a critical rule: the first verb in this compound structure always remains in its plain present-tense base (`해`, `잊어`, `가`, etc.). Any tense markers, such as past tense or future intentions, are applied only to the final `버리다` part of the construction. Therefore, "I did it (completely)" becomes `해 버렸어요`, not `했어 버렸어요`.

---

### Emotional Nuance: The "Totally" Effect
The instructor explains that `해 버렸어요` and `했어요` share the same literal meaning ("I did it"), but the former adds a layer of emotion. This emotion is roughly equivalent to the English words "totally" or "completely." While it can be used for positive or neutral completion—such as finally finishing homework or being glad someone has finally left—it carries a negative connotation roughly 90% of the time. It is the go-to form for complaining, expressing sadness, or showing disappointment. The instructor suggests thinking of the negative usage as having an "ugh, whatever" or "I can't believe it" feeling attached to the action.

---

### Examples of ~아/어 버리다 in Context
The instructor provides a wide variety of sentences to illustrate the range of emotions this form can express:
- **Disappointment/Regret:** `잊어 버렸어요 (I totally forgot)`, `지갑을 잃어버려서 너무 슬퍼해요 (I'm so sad because I [totally] lost my wallet)`, `메이크업을 망쳐버렸어요 (I totally messed up my makeup)`.
- **Impulsiveness/Finality:** `전화를 그냥 끊어버렸어 (I just [totally] hung up the phone)`, `선생님이 시험지를 가져가 버렸어요 (The teacher [totally] took away the exam papers)`.
- **Unintentional Actions:** `TV를 보다가 잠들어버렸어요 (I totally fell asleep while watching TV)`.
- **Completion/Relief:** `철수가 드디어 가버렸어요 (Chul-soo finally left [at last])`, `숙제를 먼저 끝내버렸어요 (I just finished my homework first)`.
- **Extreme States:** `정말 미쳐버릴 거야 (I'm totally going to go crazy)`, `질려버렸어 (I'm [totally] sick of it)`.

---

### Special Focus: Ways to Say "To Forget"
The lesson concludes with a detailed breakdown of "forgetting," as `잊다` is almost always paired with `버리다`.
- **Forgetting Information:** `잊어버리다` is the standard way to talk about forgetting knowledge, names, or tasks. The instructor introduces more casual or slang alternatives: `잊어먹다` (casual/slangy), `까먹다` (very common slang among friends), and `깜빡하다`. He distinguishes `깜빡하다` as meaning "to have a brain fart" or to forget something momentarily (like a light flashing), rather than forgetting it completely.
- **Forgetting Objects:** One cannot use `잊어버리다` for physical items. If you leave your umbrella at home, you must use `놓고 오다` (to put down and come) or `놓고 가다` (to put down and go). Alternatively, you can use `안 가져오다` (to not bring) or `안 가져가다` (to not take). While both translate to "I forgot my umbrella" in English, `놓고 왔다` implies you left it somewhere specific, while `안 가져왔다` simply means you don't have it with you.
