## Teaching Points

**1. The Context Marker ~을/ㄹ까요? (Wondering about the Listener's Opinion)**
Ji concludes the grammar portion by explaining the nuances and irregularities of ~을까요. This marker is used to ask for the listener's opinion with a "soft wondering" or "mild suggestion" tone.
*   **Meaning:** Depending on the context, it can mean "Shall I?", "Should we?", "Do you want me to?", or "Do you think...?"
*   **Irregular ㄹ:** For verbs ending in ㄹ, just add 까요.
    *   알다 (to know) → 알까요? (Does he/she know? / Would he/she know?)
*   **Irregular ㅂ:** Replace ㅂ with ㅜ, then add ㄹ까요.
    *   맵다 (to be spicy) → 매울까요? (Do you think it will be spicy?)
*   **Irregular ㅎ:** Remove ㅎ, then add ㄹ까요.
    *   어떻다 (to be how) → 어떨까요? (How would it be?)
*   **Example Sentence:**
    > **집에 사람이 없을까요?** / Do you think there will be no one at home?

**2. The "Future Branch" (을/ㄹ Suffix)**
Ji clarifies that while 을/ㄹ is often associated with the future tense (as in ~ㄹ 거예요), it is more accurately described as a suffix for things that "haven't happened yet." He calls it the "future branch" to help Amira recognize it in various structures like ~ㄹ 수 있어요 (can do) or modifying nouns like 할 것 (thing to do).

**3. Comprehensible Input Training Methodology**
Ji introduces a specific four-step listening training process designed to bridge the gap between reading speed and natural speaking speed:
*   **Step 1: Listen Straight Through.** Listen to an entire episode (from a recommended gaming channel or the SDBD podcast) without translating. Only pause to note unfamiliar words or grammar.
*   **Step 2: Research & Share.** Research the noted words individually (not full sentences in a translator) and share the list with Ji for feedback.
*   **Step 3: Listen Again.** Re-listen to the same episode, focusing on how the studied words sound in context, paying attention to intonation and tone.
*   **Step 4: Repeat Out Loud.** Mimic the rhythm and tone of the sentences as accurately as possible (shadowing).
