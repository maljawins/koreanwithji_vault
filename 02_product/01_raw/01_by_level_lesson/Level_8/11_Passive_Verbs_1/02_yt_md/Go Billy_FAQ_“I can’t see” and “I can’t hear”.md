## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor explains the nuanced difference between saying "I can't see/hear" using active verbs versus passive verbs in Korean. While many learners initially reach for the negative forms of "to see" and "to listen," these often carry a connotation of personal fault or a physical inability to perform the action. To sound more natural and accurately describe situations where something is simply not visible or audible, speakers should use passive verb forms.

The lesson focuses on the transition from active verbs like 보다 (to see) and 듣다 (to listen) to their passive counterparts, 보이다 (to be visible) and 들리다 (to be heard). By understanding the "blame" or "fault" associated with each form, learners can choose the correct expression for common daily situations, such as a TV screen being blocked or a volume level being too low.

---

### Active vs. Passive: The Concept of Fault
The instructor introduces the common active verbs 못 보다 (cannot see) and 못 듣다 (cannot listen/hear). On screen, these are equated with the ability-based structures 볼 수 없다 (to not have the ability to see) and 들을 수 없다 (to not have the ability to hear). Using these active forms implies that the speaker is at fault for the failure to see or hear. For example, saying 못 들었어요 (I couldn't hear) might suggest a personal physical limitation or that the speaker wasn't paying attention.

To avoid placing the blame on oneself, the instructor introduces the passive verbs 보이다 (to be visible) and 들리다 (to be audible). Using these forms shifts the "fault" to the object or the environment. If a speaker says something is not visible, they are implying that the object itself is hard to see, perhaps because it is too small, the lights are off, or someone is standing in the way.

---

### Using 안 with Passive Verbs
A critical grammatical rule highlighted in the video is the choice of negation. While active verbs often use 못 (cannot) to show inability, passive verbs like 보이다 and 들리다 are almost exclusively paired with the general negative adverb 안 (not). The instructor explicitly warns against saying "못 보여요," noting that the correct, natural expression is 안 보여요 (it is not visible). This applies to hearing as well: 안 들려요 (it is not audible) is the standard way to say "I can't hear it" when the sound is too faint or distant.

---

### Practical Examples and Comparisons
The instructor provides two sets of comparisons to illustrate these points in context. In the first example, TV를 못 봐요 (I cannot watch TV) suggests a personal reason, such as being too busy or a religious prohibition. In contrast, TV가 안 보여요 (The TV is not visible) suggests an external problem, like the TV being off or someone blocking the view. Note the change in particles: the active verb takes the object marker 를, while the passive verb takes the subject marker 가.

The second example compares 그 소리를 못 들어요 (I cannot hear/listen to that sound) with 그 소리가 안 들려요 (That sound is not audible). The first version implies the speaker is choosing not to listen or has a personal sensitivity to the sound. The second version, using the passive 들리다, indicates that the sound is physically impossible to hear because it is too quiet or too far away.
