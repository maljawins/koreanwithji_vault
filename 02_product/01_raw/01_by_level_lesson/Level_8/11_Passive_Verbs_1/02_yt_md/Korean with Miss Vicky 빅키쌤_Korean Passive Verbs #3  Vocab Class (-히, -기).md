## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the vocabulary and usage of Korean passive verbs, specifically those formed with the suffixes `-히-` and `-기-`. The instructor covers 20 essential verb pairs, explaining the nuances between active and passive forms, common collocations, and idiomatic expressions. The class is structured around prose explanations of each word's meaning, followed by interactive sentence-building exercises and quizzes to reinforce the material. Key distinctions are made between similar verbs, such as the difference between handwriting and creative writing, or different types of scratching.

---

### 적다 and 적히다: Handwriting vs. Creative Writing

The verb `적다 (to write)` refers specifically to the act of writing itself, usually handwriting or jotting something down. Its passive form is `적히다 (to be written)`. The instructor contrasts this with `쓰다 (to write)`, which has a broader meaning. While `쓰다` includes all types of writing, including creative works like novels or news articles, `적다` is limited to simple recording. For example, one would say `소설을 쓰다 (to write a novel)` or `기사를 쓰다 (to write an article)`, but never use `적다` for these creative processes. However, for simple tasks like writing a name on an exam sheet, both `시험지에 이름을 쓰세요` and `시험지에 이름을 적으세요` are correct. In the passive voice, `칠판에 적힌 글자 (letters written on a blackboard)` is a natural use of `적히다` because it describes plain text rather than a creative composition.

---

### 막다 and 막히다: Blocking and Traffic

`막다` means to block, stop, or prevent something, and its passive form is `막히다 (to be blocked)`. Common physical uses include `구멍을 막다 (to block a hole)` to prevent leaks, or `길을 막다 (to block the road)`. The instructor introduces the slang term `길막하다`, which is an abbreviation for blocking the road or standing in someone's way. Metaphorically, `막다` can mean stopping a person, as in `아무도 나를 막을 수 없어 (no one can stop me)`, or preventing accidents and fires. The passive form `막히다` is most frequently used in the context of traffic: `차가 막히다`, `도로가 막히다`, or `길이 막히다` all describe traffic congestion. A common phrase for rush hour is `출퇴근 시간`, the time when people go to and get off from work.

---

### 찍다 and 찍히다: Dipping, Photos, and Guessing

`찍다` is a versatile verb with several distinct meanings. First, it refers to dipping food into sauce, leading to the popular "Would you rather" debate between `찍먹 (dipping)` and `부먹 (pouring sauce over)`. Second, it is the standard verb for taking photos: `사진을 찍다` or `셀카를 찍다 (to take a selfie)`. The passive form `찍히다` is used for the photos themselves, such as `어렸을 때 찍힌 사진 (photos taken when I was younger)`. Third, it can mean chopping or stamping, as in `나무를 찍다 (to chop a tree)` or `도장을 찍다 (to stamp a seal)`. The instructor mentions the proverb `열 번 찍어 안 넘어가는 나무 없다`, which literally means no tree won't fall after ten chops, encouraging persistence. Finally, in a testing context, `찍다` means to take a random guess on a multiple-choice question when the answer is unknown.

---

### 긁다, 할퀴다, and 긁히다: Itchiness vs. Attack

The verb `긁다` means to scratch, typically due to itchiness, such as `모기에 물린 부분을 긁다 (to scratch a mosquito bite)`. Its passive form is `긁히다 (to be scratched)`. The instructor introduces a specialized tool called `효자손 (back scratcher)`, literally "devoted son's hand," used to scratch hard-to-reach spots on the back. In contrast, `할퀴다` refers to scratching as an attack, usually by animals with claws like cats. The passive form is `할퀴다` (or `할키다`), used in sentences like `고양이한테 팔을 할퀴었어요 (I got my arm scratched by a cat)`. For cars, `긁히다` is used to describe surface scratches, as in `차가 벽에 긁혔어요 (the car got scratched by the wall)`.

---

### 박다 and 박히다: Nailing, Crashing, and Splinters

`박다` means to nail something up or to crash into something. It is commonly used with `못 (nail)`, as in `망치로 못을 박다 (to drive a nail with a hammer)`. The passive form `박히다` is used for being nailed, such as the religious expression `십자가에 못 박히다 (to be nailed to a cross)`. Figuratively, `가슴에 못을 박다` means to deeply hurt someone's feelings, often used regarding parents. `박히다` also describes getting a splinter or thorn: `손가락에 가시가 박혔어요 (I got a splinter in my finger)`. In driving, `박다` is used when a car crashes into an object or another car, whereas `치다` is used specifically for hitting a person.

---

### 먹다 and 먹히다: Being Eaten and "Working"

While `먹다` means to eat, its passive form `먹히다` means to be eaten. For animals hunting prey, the compound verb `잡아먹히다 (to be caught and eaten)` is more natural, such as `물고기는 고양이한테 잡아먹혀요 (fish are eaten by cats)`. In a social context, `먹히다` is an idiomatic way to say something "works" or is effective, such as a strategy, a tip, or a pick-up line (`작업 멘트`). For example, `선생님이 알려주신 팁이 잘 먹히네요 (the tip the teacher taught me works well)`.

---

### 잊다 and 잊히다: Forgetting and Being Forgotten

`잊다` means to forget a person, event, or fact, and its passive form is `잊히다 (to be forgotten)`. In daily conversation, Koreans more frequently use `잊어버리다` or the informal `까먹다` to describe forgetting something. The verb `깜빡하다` is specifically used for forgetting to perform a task, such as `문을 잠그는 걸 깜빡했어요 (I forgot to lock the door)`. The passive `잊히다` is often used in the context of being forgotten by the public or a specific group of people.

---

### 꽂다 and 꽂히다: Plugging In and Being Hooked

`꽂다` means to stick, plug, or put something into a small space. Common objects include `책 (books)` on a shelf, `머리핀 (hairpins)` in hair, or `충전기 (chargers)` and `이어폰 (earphones)` into sockets. The passive form `꽂히다` is used physically, but also metaphorically to mean being "hooked" on something. If you are obsessed with a song or a hobby, you can say `그 노래에 꽂혔어요 (I'm hooked on that song)`.

---

### 읽다 and 읽히다: Reading and Readability

`읽다` means to read, and `읽히다` is its passive form. Beyond simply being read, `읽히다` often refers to the readability of a text. If a book is difficult, you might say `잘 안 읽혀요 (it doesn't read well/easily)`. The instructor also mentions `팔리다 (to be sold)`, the passive of `팔다`, noting that a bestseller is a book that is both `많이 읽히고 팔리는 책 (a book that is read and sold a lot)`.

---

### 잡다 and 잡히다: Catching and Connections

`잡다` means to grab, hold, or catch. It applies to objects, hands, or criminals (`범인을 잡다`). The passive `잡히다` is used when a criminal is caught by the police. A very common modern usage of `잡히다` is for electronic signals: `와이파이가 잡히다` means the Wi-Fi is connected or "caught." If the connection is poor, you say `와이파이가 안 잡혀요`.

---

### 뽑다 and 뽑히다: Selecting, Hiring, and New Purchases

`뽑다` means to pluck out (like weeds or gray hair), to elect (like a president), or to hire (like an employee). Its passive form is `뽑히다 (to be selected/hired)`. A native-level idiomatic use of `뽑다` is for buying a new car: `차를 뽑다`. It can also mean withdrawing money from an ATM: `돈을 뽑다`. `제비뽑기` refers to drawing straws or randomly selecting something.

---

### 씹다 and 씹히다: Chewing and Ignoring

`씹다` means to chew food, but it is also a common slang term for ignoring someone's words or messages. The passive `씹히다` means to be ignored. The instructor introduces the slang terms `읽씹 (reading a message but not replying)` and `안 읽씹 (not even reading the message to avoid replying)`.

---

### 꼬집다 and 꼬집히다: Pinching

`꼬집다` means to pinch, and `꼬집히다` is the passive form. It is most commonly used with `볼살 (cheek fat)`, especially when someone has chubby cheeks (`통통한 볼살`) that others find cute and want to pinch.

---

### 뜯다, 떼다, and 벗다: Different Ways of Taking Off

The instructor distinguishes between three verbs for removing things. `뜯다` means to break open or unseal something like an envelope (`편지 봉투`) or a parcel (`택배`). Its passive is `뜯기다`. `떼다` means to take off something attached with adhesive or fasteners, like a price tag (`가격표`) or a sticker. Its passive is `떼이다`. `벗다` is used for taking off items from the body, such as clothes, glasses, or shoes. Its passive is `벗기다`.

---

### 빼앗다 and 빼앗기다: Taking Away

`빼앗다` (often contracted to `뺐다`) means to take something away by force or authority, such as a parent taking a child's phone. The passive `빼앗기다` means to have something taken away. Metaphorically, it is used for having one's heart stolen: `마음을 빼앗기다`. Slang versions for being mugged or having money taken include `돈을 뜯기다` or `삥 뜯기다`.

---

### 담다 and 담기다: Putting In and Memories

`담다` means to put something inside a container. While physically interchangeable with `넣다`, `담다` is preferred for metaphorical contexts, such as putting memories into a photo (`사진에 담다`) or keeping something in one's heart (`마음에 담다`). The passive form is `담기다`.

---

### 믿다, 믿기다, and 믿어지다: Belief

`믿다` means to believe. It has two passive forms: `믿기다` and `믿어지다`. These are almost always used in the negative to express disbelief: `안 믿겨요` or `안 믿어져요 (I can't believe it)`. While `믿을 수 없어요` is also common, the passive forms are very natural for expressing that something is unbelievable.

---

### 끊다 and 끊기다: Cutting and Hanging Up

`끊다` means to cut a physical string or line, but also to cut off a conversation, relationship, or phone call. `전화를 끊다` means to hang up the phone. `연락을 끊다` means to cut off contact. The passive form `끊기다` is used when a connection is lost or a relationship is severed.

---

### 찢다 and 찢어지다: Ripping and Slaying

`찢다` means to rip or tear paper or fabric. It has two passive forms, `찢기다` and `찢어지다`, with the latter being more common in daily speech. It is used for ripped jeans (`찢어진 청바지`) or torn skin. In modern slang, `찢었다` is used to mean someone "slayed" or did an amazing job, particularly on stage.
