## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of active and passive voice, noting that while both exist in English and Korean, Korean learners often encounter a specific phenomenon called "double passive verbs" (이중 피동사). Active voice emphasizes the doer of an action, such as "I kicked the ball," while passive voice emphasizes the object being affected, as in "The ball was kicked." In Korean, passive verbs are often formed by attaching the suffix ~지다 to a conjugated verb stem.

The core of the lesson focuses on the fact that double passive verbs—where a verb that is already in its passive form is made passive a second time—are technically grammatically incorrect. However, like common mistakes in English (such as writing "alright" as one word), these double passives are extremely common in everyday spoken Korean. The instructor aims to help students recognize these forms so they can understand native speakers, even while advising that students should strive to use the correct single passive forms themselves.

---

### Understanding Passive Formation and ~지다
In Korean, many action verbs become passive by attaching ~지다. The instructor provides examples on screen: 주다 (to give) becomes 주어지다 (to be given), and 만들다 (to make) becomes 만들어지다 (to be made). He notes that while ~지다 creates a passive meaning for action verbs, it carries a different meaning when attached to descriptive verbs. For example, 춥다 (to be cold) becomes 추워지다, which means "to become cold" or "to get cold," rather than a passive "to be colded."

---

### The Origin of Double Passives
Double passives occur when a speaker takes a verb that already has a dedicated passive form and adds ~지다 to it anyway. This often happens because speakers may not be fully familiar with the specific passive conjugation of a verb and assume it needs the ~지다 ending to "sound" passive. 

A prime example shown is the verb 쓰다 (to write/use). Its correct passive form is 쓰이다 (to be written/used). However, many Koreans mistakenly attach ~지다 to the passive form, resulting in 쓰여지다. The instructor points out that 쓰여지다 is actually used more frequently than the correct 쓰이다 in common speech, despite being a double passive and technically wrong.

---

### Common Double Passive Examples
The instructor lists several common verbs where the double passive form is frequently heard. These are presented in a three-part transformation: [Active] → [Correct Passive] → [Common Double Passive].

- **찢다 (to rip/tear)**: The correct passive is 찢기다 (to be torn). The common double passive mistake is 찢겨지다.
- **보다 (to see/look)**: The correct passive is 보이다 (to be seen). The double passive form is 보여지다.
- **하다 (to do)**: This becomes 되다 (to become/be done). The double passive form often heard is 되어지다.
- **믿다 (to believe/trust)**: The correct passive is 믿기다 (to be believed). The double passive mistake is 믿겨지다.
- **잊다 (to forget)**: The correct passive is 잊히다 (to be forgotten). The double passive form is 잊혀지다.

The instructor concludes by emphasizing that while these double passive forms are technically errors, they are so prevalent that learners must be aware of them to navigate natural Korean conversations.
