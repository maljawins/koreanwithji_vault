## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the concept of passive verbs by first contrasting active and passive voice. In an active sentence, the subject performs the action, whereas in a passive sentence, the subject is the recipient of the action. While passive voice is less common in both English and Korean, it is essential for situations where the speaker wants to focus on the object being affected or when the performer of the action is unknown or unimportant. The lesson covers several ways to form passive verbs in Korean, including using specific passive counterparts, the verb 되다, the verb 받다, and the grammatical suffix -어지다.

---

### Active Voice vs. Passive Voice

Active voice is the most direct and common way of speaking. For example, in the sentence "The lion ate the rabbit," the lion is the subject doing the eating. In the passive version, "The rabbit was eaten by the lion," the rabbit becomes the subject because it is the thing being affected by the action. The instructor notes that passive voice can sometimes sound vague or indirect, similar to how Yoda speaks in Star Wars: "The rabbit, eaten it was." In Korean, changing a sentence to passive voice involves changing the verb itself and adjusting the subject marker.

> **사자가 토끼를 먹었어요.** / The lion ate the rabbit. (Active)
> **토끼가 먹혔어요.** / The rabbit was eaten. (Passive)

To specify who performed the action in a passive sentence, the marker 에게 is used, such as **사자에게** (by the lion).

---

### The Passive Verb 되다

The verb 되다 is one of the most common passive forms in Korean. It serves as the passive counterpart to the active verb 하다 (to do). While 되다 has several meanings—including "to become," "to be okay," or "to function"—it is frequently used to transform active 하다 verbs into passive ones. For instance, 준비를 하다 (to prepare) becomes 준비가 되다 (to be prepared).

The instructor provides several comparative examples:
- **다 준비했어요.** (I prepared it all) vs. **다 준비됐어요.** (It was all prepared.)
- **수업은 8시에 시작했어요.** (Class started at 8:00) vs. **수업은 8시에 시작됐어요.** (Class was started at 8:00.)
- **숙제를 이해했어요?** (Did you understand the homework?) vs. **숙제가 이해됐어요?** (Was the homework understood?)
- **제가 주문한 음식이 도착했어요.** (The food I ordered arrived) vs. **주문된 음식이 도착했어요.** (The food that was ordered arrived.)

---

### Unique Passive Verb Pairs

Many Korean verbs have unique passive forms that must be memorized individually. These do not follow a single universal rule but are very common in daily speech.

- **먹다 (to eat) → 먹히다 (to be eaten)**
- **쓰다 (to write/use) → 쓰이다 (to be written/used)**
- **바꾸다 (to change) → 바뀌다 (to be changed)**

The instructor highlights two specific verbs that are often translated more naturally as "ability" in English:
- **보이다 (to be seen / to be visible):** Derived from 보다 (to see). Instead of saying "Chul-soo's face is not seen," Koreans say **철수의 얼굴이 안 보여요**, which translates naturally as "I can't see Chul-soo's face."
- **들리다 (to be heard / to be audible):** Derived from 듣다 (to hear). **철수의 목소리가 안 들려요** translates naturally as "I can't hear Chul-soo's voice."

While active forms like 볼 수 있다 (can see) and 들을 수 있다 (can hear) exist and carry similar meanings, the passive forms 보이다 and 들리다 are extremely common for describing what is currently visible or audible to the speaker.

---

### Passive Voice with 받다

Some verbs become passive by replacing the active 하다 with the verb 받다 (to receive/get). This is common with verbs involving emotions or social interactions.

- **사랑을 하다 (to love) → 사랑을 받다 (to be loved)**
- **존경을 하다 (to respect) → 존경을 받다 (to be respected)**

---

### The -어지다 Construction

For verbs that do not have a dedicated passive counterpart or don't use 되다/받다, the suffix -어지다 can be attached to the verb stem. This is formed by taking the infinitive (요) form of the verb, removing 요, and adding 지다.

- **주다 (to give) → 주어지다 (to be given)**
- **만들다 (to make) → 만들어지다 (to be made)**
- **정하다 (to decide) → 정해지다 (to be decided)**

Example sentences:
> **선물이 주어졌어요.** / A present was given.
> **나무로 만들어진 책상을 샀어요.** / I bought a desk made of wood.
> **정해진 날짜에 만났어요.** / We met on the decided date.

---

### Practical Usage and Advice

Passive voice is particularly useful when you want to describe an event without specifying who caused it. For example, if you want to say "I want to buy a book written in Korean," the focus is on the book's state, not the author. Using the passive 쓰이다 is more natural here:

> **한국말로 쓰인 책을 사고 싶어요.** / I want to buy a book written in Korean.

The instructor concludes by advising students not to stress over memorizing every passive verb immediately. Since passive voice is less frequent than active voice, one can often get by using active sentences. Over time, through listening and practice, these passive forms will become more recognizable and easier to use.
