### Overview

This lesson concludes the comprehensive session on Korean passive verbs, focusing on specific vocabulary groups that form passive versions using four primary methods: adding `-아/어지다`, changing `-하다` to `-되다`, changing `-하다` to `-받다`, and changing `-하다` to `-당하다`. The instructor emphasizes that while some verbs follow predictable patterns, others are independent intransitive verbs that simply resemble passive forms.

### Passive Verbs with -아/어지다

The first group consists of verbs that become passive by conjugating the base verb into the present tense and adding `지다`.

The verb `정하다 (to decide, to set)` becomes `정해지다 (to be decided, to be set)`. This is commonly used with nouns like `날짜 (date)`, `가격 (price)`, `규칙 (rule)`, and `메뉴 (menu)`. For example, `날짜를 정하다 (to set a date)` becomes `날짜가 정해지다 (the date is set)`. When choosing a specific item, the particle `로` is used to mark the choice: `김치찌개로 정했어요 (I decided on Kimchi stew)`.

The pair `켜다 (to turn on)` and `끄다 (to turn off)` become `켜지다 (to be turned on)` and `꺼지다 (to be turned off)`. These apply to electronic devices like `핸드폰 (cell phone)`, `에어컨 (air conditioner)`, `전자레인지 (microwave)`, and `컴퓨터 (computer)`, as well as `불 (light/fire)` and `전원 (power)`.

The verb `깨다 (to break/shatter)` becomes `깨지다 (to be broken/shattered)`. This specifically refers to things that shatter into pieces, such as `창문 (window)` or `컵 (cup)`. It is also used figuratively for `약속 (promise/plan)` and `규칙 (rule)`. For instance, `약속이 깨지다` can mean a plan was called off or a promise was broken.

The verb `이루다 (to realize/achieve)` becomes `이루어지다 (to come true/be realized)`. The most common noun used here is `꿈 (dream)`. The instructor notes that `이루어지다` is often abbreviated to `이뤄지다`. A famous expression is `꿈은 이루어진다 (Dreams come true)`.

The verb `지키다 (to observe/abide by)` becomes `지켜지다 (to be observed/kept)`. This is used with `법 (law)` and `약속 (promise)`. For example, `법이 지켜지다 (the law is observed)`.

### Independent Intransitive Verbs Ending in -지다

Some verbs end in `지다` but are independent intransitive verbs, meaning they do not stem from a transitive counterpart.

The verb `떨어지다 (to fall, drop, fail)` is used for physical objects like `빗방울 (raindrops)` or `눈물 (teardrops)`. It also applies to numerical values like `점수 (score)` or `성적 (grade)`. In the context of exams, `시험에 떨어지다` means to fail the test, while the antonym `시험에 붙다` means to pass (literally "to stick to"). It can also mean to fall from a height, distinct from `넘어지다 (to trip over)`.

The instructor compares `떨어지다` with `늘다 (to increase/improve)` and `줄다 (to decrease/get rusty)`. While `성적이 떨어지다` is common for grades, `실력이 줄다` is more natural for skills getting rusty, and `실력이 늘다` is used for skills improving.

The verb `터지다 (to burst, explode, pop)` is used for `풍선 (balloon)`, `웃음 (laughter)`, and `울음 (crying)`. Figuratively, `배가 터지다` (stomach bursts) means to be extremely full.

### Passive Verbs with -되다

Many verbs formed by `Noun + 하다` become passive by changing `하다` to `되다`. This is typically used for inanimate subjects.

- `취소하다 (to cancel)` → `취소되다 (to be canceled)`: Used for `예약 (reservation)`, `일정 (schedule)`, and `행사 (event)`.
- `수정하다 (to revise/edit)` → `수정되다 (to be revised)`: Specifically used for fixing errors in writing, like `오타 (typo)`, `글 (article)`, or `댓글 (comment)`.
- `삭제하다 (to delete)` → `삭제되다 (to be deleted)`: Used for `계정 (account)`, `사진 (photo)`, or `파일 (file)`.
- `사용하다 (to use)` → `사용되다 (to be used)`: An advanced version of `쓰다`.
- `준비하다 (to prepare)` → `준비되다 (to be prepared/ready)`: The phrase `준비됐어요` is the standard way to say "I am ready."

The instructor notes the difference between `수정하다 (to fix/make right)`, `편집하다 (to edit video/photos/books)`, and `변경하다 (to alter/change to an alternative)`.

---

### Passive Verbs with -받다 and -당하다

When the subject is a person, passive forms often use `-받다` (for positive/neutral situations) or `-당하다` (for negative situations).

**Using -받다 (to receive):**
- `사랑하다 (to love)` → `사랑받다 (to be loved)`
- `환영하다 (to welcome)` → `환영받다 (to be welcomed)`
- `초대하다 (to invite)` → `초대받다 (to be invited)`
- `추천하다 (to recommend)` → `추천받다 (to be recommended)`
- `오해하다 (to misunderstand)` → `오해받다 (to be misunderstood)`: Note that even though misunderstanding is negative, `받다` is used instead of `당하다`.

**Using -당하다 (to suffer/undergo):**
- `거절하다 (to reject)` → `거절당하다 (to be rejected)`
- `사기치다 (to scam)` → `사기당하다 (to be scammed)`
- `무시하다 (to ignore/look down on)` → `무시당하다 (to be ignored/looked down upon)`
- `차별하다 (to discriminate)` → `차별당하다 (to be discriminated against)`: Includes `인종차별 (racism)` and `성차별 (sexism)`.

The instructor clarifies that `사기` (fraud) must be used with the verb `치다` (to hit), resulting in `사기치다` (to scam) and its passive `사기당하다`.
