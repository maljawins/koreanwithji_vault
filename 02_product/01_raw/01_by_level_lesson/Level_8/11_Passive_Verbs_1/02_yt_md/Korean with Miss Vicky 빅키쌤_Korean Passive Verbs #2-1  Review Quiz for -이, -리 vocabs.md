## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson is a comprehensive review session focusing on Korean passive verbs, specifically those formed with the suffixes `-이` and `-리`. The instructor uses a 30-question quiz format to reinforce the distinction between transitive (active) verbs and their passive counterparts. The session is designed to help students identify when to use subject markers versus object markers based on the nature of the verb, and how to describe the state of an object using specific grammar patterns.

---

### Transitive vs. Passive Verb Particles

The instructor emphasizes that the choice between the subject marker `이/가` and the object marker `을/를` depends entirely on whether the verb is transitive or passive. A transitive verb like `열다 (to open)` requires an object, so one says `문을 열었어요 (opened the door)`. Conversely, a passive verb like `열리다 (to be opened)` focuses on the subject's state, leading to `문이 열렸어요 (the door is opened)`. The instructor warns that saying `문이 열었어요` is awkward because an inanimate object like a door cannot perform the action of opening itself.

---

### Describing States with -아/어 있다

A critical grammar point covered is the use of `Verb -아/어 있다` to describe a continuing state. This pattern is almost exclusively combined with passive verbs. For example, to say "the door is locked" (and remains in that state), one uses the passive verb `잠기다 (to be locked)` to form `잠겨 있어요`. The instructor provides several examples of this state-descriptive pattern:
- `열려 있어요` / It is (staying) open.
- `닫혀 있어요` / It is (staying) closed.
- `놓여 있어요` / It is (staying) placed (on something).

---

### Distinguishing Similar Verbs: 놓다 vs. 넣다

The instructor clarifies the difference between `놓다` and `넣다`, which are often confused by learners. `놓다` means to put or place something *on* a surface, while `넣다` means to put something *inside* a container. This distinction carries over to their usage in sentences: `책상에 상자가 놓여 있어요 (The box is placed on the desk)` uses the passive `놓이다`, whereas `가방 안에 노트북을 넣었어요 (I put the laptop inside the bag)` uses the transitive `넣다` because the laptop is the object of the action.

---

### Psychological States and Passive Verbs

Passive verbs are also used to describe emotions and psychological shifts. The expression `마음이 놓이다` literally means "the mind is placed down," which translates to "to feel relieved." Similarly, `끌리다 (to be pulled/drawn)` is used to express being attracted to someone or something, as in `내향적인 사람에게 끌려요 (I am attracted to introverted people)`. The instructor notes that `끌리다` is used for abstract or psychological attraction, whereas `당기다` is typically reserved for physical pulling.

---

### Physical Actions and Passive Forms

The quiz covers various physical actions where the passive form is the standard way to express an experience:
- **Stinging/Biting:** `쏘다 (to sting/shoot)` becomes `쏘이다 (to be stung)`. `물다 (to bite)` becomes `물리다 (to be bitten)`.
- **Cutting:** `베다 (to cut/slash)` is used for accidental cuts, such as `손을 베다 (to cut one's finger)`. The passive is `베이다`.
- **Hitting:** `치다 (to hit)` becomes `치이다 (to be run over/hit by a vehicle)`.
- **Shaking:** `흔들다` is used for willful shaking (waving a hand), while `떨다` is for unwillful shaking (trembling from caffeine or nerves). The passive `떨리다` is a common synonym for being nervous.

---

### Vocabulary Nuances: 바뀌다 vs. 변하다

The instructor explains the subtle difference between `바뀌다` and `변하다`, both meaning "to change." `바뀌다` refers to a general change in appearance or a replacement (like changing a plan or a personality), while `변하다` refers to a fundamental change in inner quality or nature. Therefore, `계획이 바뀌다 (the plan changed)` is correct, while `계획이 변하다` would sound awkward.

---

### Miscellaneous Passive and Transitive Pairs

The lesson concludes with several specific vocabulary pairs and their usage:
- **Solving:** `풀다 (to solve/untie)` and `풀리다 (to be solved/untied)`. Used for problems (`문제가 안 풀려요`).
- **Tying:** `묶다 (to tie)`. Used for shoelaces (`신발끈`) or hair.
- **Mixing:** `섞다 (to mix)`. Used for mixing languages or ingredients.
- **Shaving:** Both `밀다 (to push/shave)` and `깎다 (to cut/shave)` can be used for body hair like a beard (`수염`).
- **Setting Plans:** `계획을 짜다` or `계획을 세우다` are the standard transitive expressions for "to set/establish a plan."
