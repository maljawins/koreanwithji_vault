## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces a nuanced usage of Korean passive verbs that often eludes beginners: their ability to express "can" or "can't." While most learners first encounter passive verbs as a way to describe an action happening to a subject (e.g., "the book was written"), this video focuses on how the passive voice shifts the focus away from the speaker's ability and onto the state of the object itself.

The lesson frames this as a matter of "fault" or "control." By using a passive verb instead of a standard "can't" construction like `못` + active verb, a speaker implies that the inability to perform an action is due to external factors rather than a personal lack of skill or effort. The learning arc moves from a review of active vs. passive voice to specific common verb pairs, demonstrating how the passive form naturally translates to "can" or "can't" in specific contexts.

---

### Active Voice vs. Passive Voice Review
Active voice occurs when the subject of the sentence is the one performing the action, such as "Billy eats the cheeseburger." Passive voice occurs when the subject is the one being affected by the action, such as "The hamburger was eaten by Billy." The instructor notes that while passive voice can sometimes feel awkward in both English and Korean, it is essential when using verbs as adjectives. For example, to say "a book written in Korean," the passive form is necessary. However, the most practical daily use for passive verbs in Korean is to express capability or the lack thereof.

---

### 보이다 vs. 못 보다: Visibility and Control
The instructor uses the pair `보다 (to see)` and its passive counterpart `보이다 (to be seen/to be visible)` to illustrate the "can't" nuance. If someone is blocking your view of the TV, using `못 보다 (cannot see)` implies that you are simply unable to do the action of looking. While it doesn't explicitly blame the speaker, it keeps the focus on the speaker's inability. 

In contrast, using `보이다` shifts the focus to the TV. If you say the TV `안 보여요 (is not visible)`, you are expressing "I can't see the TV" while implying it is not your fault; the TV itself cannot be seen due to an external obstruction. This makes the passive form the more natural choice when the cause is outside the speaker's control.

---

### 되다 as the Passive of 하다
The verb `되다 (to become)` often functions as the passive version of `하다 (to do)`. The instructor compares `못해요 (I can't do it)` with `안 돼요 (It doesn't work/It can't be done)`. While `못해요` is a general statement of inability, `안 돼요` implies that the thing itself is not working or the situation is out of the speaker's control. This usage is distinct from the common expression `안 돼요` meaning "No" or "You may not"; when attached to other verbs, it specifically indicates a functional "can't."

---

### Door Example: 열다 vs. 열리다
A practical demonstration is provided using a locked door. To tell a friend you cannot open the door, you have two choices:
- **못 열어요** / I can't open it. (Focuses on the speaker; perhaps they aren't strong enough or are too tired.)
- **안 열려요** / It won't open. (Focuses on the door; it implies the door is stuck or locked, and no amount of effort from the speaker would change the outcome.)

The instructor also shows these in the past tense to highlight the difference in agency. `열었어요 (I/someone opened it)` is active and direct. `열렸어요 (The door opened)` is passive; it describes the event of the door opening without emphasizing who did it.

---

### Photo and Concentration Examples
The instructor provides two final comparisons to solidify the concept:
- **찍다 (to take a photo) vs. 찍히다 (to be taken):** `안 찍어요` means "I am not taking a photo" (choice/active). `안 찍혀요` means "The photo isn't being taken" (passive), which translates to "I can't take the photo," likely because the camera is malfunctioning.
- **집중하다 (to concentrate) vs. 집중이 되다 (to be concentrated):** This is a case where the passive voice is almost always translated as "can" or "can't" in English. `집중이 안 돼요` means "I can't focus," specifically implying that external factors (like loud music or people talking) are preventing the concentration. `집중 못해요` would imply a personal inability to focus regardless of the environment.

The lesson concludes with a reminder that while "can/can't" is a common translation for these passive forms, they still retain their literal passive meanings in other contexts, such as `찍히다` meaning "to be caught/taken" by a speed camera.
