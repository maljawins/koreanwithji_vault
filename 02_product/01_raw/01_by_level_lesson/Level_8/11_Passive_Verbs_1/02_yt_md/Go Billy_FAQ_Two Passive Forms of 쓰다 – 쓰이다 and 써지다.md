## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners regarding the verb `쓰다 (to write; to use)`. While many verbs have a single passive form, `쓰다` has two: `쓰이다` and `써지다`. The lesson aims to clarify the specific nuances and situational requirements for each, ensuring students can choose the correct form based on whether they are discussing general usage, the physical act of writing, or the flow of ideas from the mind.

---

### The Common Error: 쓰여지다
Before diving into the correct forms, the instructor warns against a very common mistake: `쓰여지다`. This is a "double passive" error, combining the passive suffix `-이-` with the `-어지다` passive construction. While native speakers frequently use this in casual speech, it is grammatically incorrect. Learners are advised to avoid it and instead choose between the two legitimate forms: `쓰이다` or `써지다`.

---

### The Standard Passive: 쓰이다
For the vast majority of situations—roughly 99% of the time—`쓰이다` is the correct passive form to use. It functions as the general equivalent of "to be used" or "to be written" in the passive voice. The instructor provides a visual example of a book: `한글로 쓰인 책 (a book written in Hangeul)`. In a full sentence, this becomes:
> **한글로 쓰인 책을 샀어요.** / I bought a book that was written in Hangeul.

Another common usage involves the expression `신경을 쓰다 (to worry; to pay attention; literally "to use one's nerves")`. When this is turned into a passive state, it conjugates as:
> **자꾸 신경이 쓰여요.** / It keeps weighing on my mind. / I can't help but worry about it.

In this context, `쓰이다` becomes `쓰여요` in the polite `-요` form. This remains the most versatile and common way to form the passive of `쓰다`.

---

### The Specific Passive: 써지다
The form `써지다` is derived by conjugating `쓰다` to `써` and attaching the `-지다` ending. Unlike `쓰이다`, this form does specifically **not** mean "to be used" in a general sense. Instead, it is used to describe the physical or mental process of writing. The instructor identifies three specific nuances for `써지다`:

First, it is used to specify that something is being written with a physical instrument, such as a pen, pencil, or brush. It often describes whether that instrument is functioning correctly. For example, if a pen is out of ink, a speaker would say:
> **안 써지네요.** / It's not writing. / (The pen) isn't working.

Using `안 쓰여요` in this case would sound indirect and strange, as it would imply "it is not being written" rather than " the pen fails to produce marks."

Second, it describes the flow of words or ideas from the mind onto paper. If a writer is struggling to get their thoughts down, they use `써지다`. The instructor demonstrates this with a comparison:
> **소설을 써보고 싶었지만 글이 잘 안 써지더라고요.** / I wanted to try writing a novel, but the words wouldn't come out well.

Third, it describes the physical "flow" or quality of a writing tool. If a calligraphy brush moves smoothly across the paper, the instructor notes that `써지다` is the natural choice:
> **이 붓이 잘 써져요.** / This brush writes well. / It flows nicely.

If one were to say `이 붓이 잘 쓰여요`, it would mistakenly imply that the brush is "used well" or "used often," which is not the intended meaning when discussing the quality of the writing experience.

---

### Summary of Differences
The instructor concludes by reinforcing the core distinction. `쓰이다` is the general-purpose passive for "to be used" or "to be written." `써지다` is reserved for the physical act of writing, the functioning of writing instruments, and the process of ideas being put down on paper. Choosing the wrong one results in awkward phrasing or a shift in meaning that may confuse the listener.
