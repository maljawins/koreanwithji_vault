**Intro**

Welcome to the last part of compound verbs and in this lesson, we’re going to be learning about 놓다 and 두다.

놓다 and 두다 can both be translated to “to put” or “to place” something. So, they’re mostly interchangeable but there are some subtle differences.

First, 놓다 literally means to put something down. By that I mean, you take an object and put it down and let it go.

And 두다 ALSO means to put something down but it has one more meaning that 놓다 doesn’t. And that is to “Leave something” sort of just “Keeping it there”.

So, since 놓다 has a feeling of “letting something go”, it’s more like putting something temporarily.

For example, in this situation (멱살 잡고 있는 사진 삽입) you would say “이거 놔” - “Let this go”. In English, you’d say “Let me go.”

But 두다는 “letting go” 보다는 “Leaving or keeping it there”의 느낌이 강하기 때문에, it feels more like “stashing” like putting a box up into your basement.

For example, “나 좀 내버려둬” would translate to “Leave me alone”. 내버려두다 is “technically” a compound verb which is 내버리다 and 두다 combined. But nobody uses 내버리다 on its own, so it’s much better to learn this word as a whole chunk – To leave something be.

That said, 놓다 is used more commonly than 두다 just in general. So you’ll be good with just 놓다 most of the time.

**So, what does it mean when they’re used at the end of a compound verb?**

**아/어 놓다**

These forms show a completed action in preparation for something. Now how does “putting something down” relate to a completed action?

Well, we need to use a bit of imagination here. Think of it this way.

What you're saying is do something and then putting it down like that. You put that action down ok now I'm done with this and now there it is.

We can say that “completing an action” is like “putting down the outcome of said action somewhere” so you can use that outcome for something you need later.

For example, let’s say you have a party tonight so you’re going to have some friends over. And you’ve just ordered some food. Now here, you’ve just completed the act of “ordering food” IN PREPARATION for the party. In other words, you completed “ordering food” and put that completed action down so you can enjoy it with your friends later.

THIS is how this form is used.

Example

음식을 주문해 놓았어요 / 놨어요.

음식 means food, and 주문하다 means “to order”

On a sidenote, most of the time 놓아 is shortened to 놔. Namely, the past stem 놓았 is also just shortened to 놨.

So, this sentence means I ordered food (so we can have it together.)

Note that what’s in the brackets is IMPLIED through context. If you just looked at this sentence by itself, we don’t know WHAT exactly this person ordered food FOR. But we can guess that it was to EAT together.

However, if you JUST said “음식을 주문했어요”, this wouldn’t have ANY extra meaning of ordering the food for something later.

3 More examples

**아/어 두다**

As I mentioned earlier in the beginning of this lesson, 두다 feels less temporary than 놓다. So, if you want to emphasize that you completed an action and you intend for it to stay that way long-term, then you can use 두다.

Here’s an example. I’ll just assume you’re a girl and you have new boyfriend. You just started dating him. And he told you his birthday. You want to remember his birthday so you say

“기억해 둘게”

**In this example, 두다 is actually better than 놓다. It's like I will remember it and I will keep that in my mind try to keep it there for as long as I can. However, “기억해 놓을게” is still fine, too.**

Another example.

한국어를 배워두고 싶어요 - **I want to learn Korean (for later)**

**Learning a new language, or any kind of learning for that matter, is intended to have that knowledge stay with you forever, or at least long-term.**

**Other than this, the way you use 아/어 두다 is pretty much the same as 아/어 놓다.**
