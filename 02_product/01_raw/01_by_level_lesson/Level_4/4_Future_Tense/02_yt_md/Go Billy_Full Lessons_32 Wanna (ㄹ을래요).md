## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson introduces a casual way to express desire in Korean, specifically focusing on the ending ~(으)ㄹ래(요). While previous lessons covered the standard ways to say "want" using 원하다 (to want) or ~고 싶다 (to want to), this episode focuses on a more colloquial form similar to the English contraction "wanna." 

The instructor explains the grammatical construction, the nuances of politeness levels associated with this ending, and how to apply it to various verb stems. The lesson concludes with several practical examples, including how to use the form in questions and negative sentences.

---

### The ~(으)ㄹ래(요) Ending
The instructor describes ~(으)ㄹ래(요) as a casual version of "want to." To conjugate a verb into this form, you first take the verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you add 을래. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you simply attach the ㄹ patchim to the stem followed by 래. 

While this is primarily a casual form, adding the politeness marker 요 to the end makes it acceptable to use with people who are older than you. However, the instructor cautions that even with 요 attached, this ending should be avoided in formal situations or when one needs to be extra polite. It is best reserved for friendly, everyday interactions.

---

### Examples and Special Cases
The video provides several sentences to demonstrate the grammar in action. For a standard consonant-ending verb like 먹다 (to eat), the stem 먹 combines with 을래요 to become 먹을래요.

> **김치를 먹을래요.** / I wanna eat kimchi.

For vowel-ending stems like 가다 (to go) or 시작하다 (to start a diet), the ㄹ is attached directly to the stem.

> **집에 갈래요.** / I wanna go home.
> **다이어트를 시작할래.** / I wanna start a diet. (Casual/반말 version without 요)

A special case is noted for the verb 놀다 (to play/hang out). Since the stem already ends in ㄹ, you do not add another consonant; you simply attach 래(요) to the existing stem, resulting in 놀래요. This is demonstrated in a question format:

> **내일 우리 집에 와서 놀래요?** / Do you wanna come to our house tomorrow and play?

---

### Negative Forms
The ~(으)ㄹ래(요) ending can also be used in the negative to express that one does "not wanna" do something. This can be achieved by adding the negative prefix 안 before the verb or by using the ~지 않다 construction.

> **안 할래.** / I don't wanna do it. (Casual/반말)
> **같이 여행 가지 않을래?** / Don't you wanna go on a trip together?
