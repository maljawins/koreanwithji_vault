## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces a specific Korean grammatical form used when performing an action as a favor for someone else. While English often uses the phrase "I'll do it for you," Korean utilizes a dedicated conjugation pattern to convey this helpful intent. The lesson covers the basic construction of this form, its usage in casual and polite contexts, and the honorific version used when speaking to social superiors.

---

### Basic Construction: -(으)ㄹ게(요)

To express that you will do something as a favor, you begin with the verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you add **을**; if it ends in a vowel, you simply add the consonant **ㄹ**. Following this, you attach **게**. To make the expression polite, you add the particle **요** at the end. The instructor demonstrates this with the verb **하다 (to do)**, which becomes **할게요 (I'll do it)**.

The instructor clarifies that while this form implies a favor, the favor does not need to be a grand gesture. It is a natural way to offer help or state an intention that benefits the listener. Common examples include:

- **숙제를 도와줄게요** / I'll help you with your homework.
- **내가 밥 살게** / I'll treat (literally: I'll buy food).

Regarding the second example, the instructor notes that **살게** is the casual version (without **요**). He jokingly warns that if you use this phrase with Korean friends, they will be very happy, but you must be prepared to actually pay the bill.

---

### Honorific Form: -아/어 드릴게요

When offering a favor to someone older or in a higher position of authority, such as a teacher or a parent, a more respectful form is required. This is constructed by first conjugating the main verb into the **아/어** form (the same base used for the standard polite **요** form). Instead of adding **요**, you attach **드릴게요**.

This form is derived from the honorific verb **드리다**, which means "to give" (used when giving to a superior). Using this shows proper respect to the recipient of the favor. The instructor provides several practical examples of this honorific helpfulness:

- **제가 해 드릴게요** / I'll do it for you (honorific).
- **포장해 드릴게요** / I'll wrap it up for you.
- **갖다 드릴게요** / I'll take it to you / I'll bring it to you.

The instructor mentions that **포장해 드릴게요** is a phrase you are likely to hear from employees at Korean restaurants if you order food to go. These honorific versions maintain the "favor" nuance while elevating the level of politeness to suit the social context.
