### Conjugation Rules for -(으)ㄹ래요
The `-(으)ㄹ래요` form is attached to action verb stems. The specific attachment depends on whether the verb stem ends in a consonant or a vowel. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach `을래요`. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach a `ㄹ` patchim to the bottom of the stem followed by `래요`. In casual speech (반말), the final `요` can be removed to leave just `-(으)ㄹ래`.

The instructor provides specific examples for different stem endings. For a standard vowel-ending stem like `가다 (to go)`, it becomes `갈래요`. For a consonant-ending stem like `먹다 (to eat)`, it becomes `먹을래요`. The instructor also highlights two important irregular cases. First, if a verb stem already ends with a `ㄹ` patchim, such as `팔다 (to sell)`, you simply attach `래요` without adding another `ㄹ`, resulting in `팔래요`. Second, for `ㅂ` irregular verbs like `눕다 (to lie down)`, the `ㅂ` is removed and replaced with `우`, to which the `ㄹ` patchim and `래요` are then attached, resulting in `누울래요`.

---

### Usage and Nuance
The `-(으)ㄹ래요` form is categorized as informal speech. While it is not considered rude, it is best suited for situations where you are speaking with people you know well or are on friendly terms with. A key grammatical restriction is that this form is only used in the first person (to state your own desire/intent) or the second person (to ask about the listener's desire/intent). It cannot be used to talk about what a third party wants to do.

The primary difference between `-(으)ㄹ래요` and `-고 싶다` lies in the level of intention. While `-고 싶다` expresses a general desire ("I want to"), `-(으)ㄹ래요` functions as a combination of `-고 싶다` and the intentional form `-(으)려고 하다`. It signals that the speaker not only wants to do the action but also intends or plans to do it. When used as a question, it asks the listener about their preference or intention, effectively saying, "Do you want to (and are you going to) do this?"

### Vocabulary and Idioms
Several useful vocabulary items and expressions are introduced through the examples. The instructor notes that both `드시다` and `먹다` can be used to mean "to drink" in certain contexts, similar to `마시다`. The adjective `어느` is explained as meaning "which," and it must be paired with a noun like `쪽 (direction)` or `것 (thing)` to function properly in a sentence.

An important idiom featured in the conversation practice is `마음에 들다`, which literally means "to enter the heart." It is used to express that you like someone or something based on a first impression or a specific experience. The instructor also distinguishes between the adverbs `또` and `다시`, both meaning "again," noting that `또` often carries a nuance of something being slightly unexpected or repetitive.
