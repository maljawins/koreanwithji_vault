## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the two primary methods for expressing the future tense in Korean. He begins with a crucial linguistic nuance: while these forms are called "future tense," they often function more as a "probable future" rather than a definitive one. Depending on the context, these structures can translate to either "will" or "would" in English.

The lesson is structured to first present the grammatical construction of both forms, followed by a comparison of their social politeness levels and their specific pragmatic emphases. The instructor concludes with a variety of practical examples, including how to handle irregular verbs and negative sentences within the future tense framework.

---

### Form 1: Verb Stem + 겠다
The first method for creating the future tense involves taking the verb stem and attaching **겠다**. This form can be used in its plain state as **겠다** or conjugated into various politeness levels. Common conjugations shown on screen include the polite **겠어요**, the formal **겠습니다**, and the formal interrogative **겠습니까**.

The instructor provides two primary examples for this form:
> **언제 하겠어요?** / When will you do it?
> **저는 학교에 가겠습니다.** / I will go to school.

---

### Form 2: Verb Stem + (을/ㄹ) 것이다
The second method is slightly more complex in its construction. You take the verb stem and add **을** if the stem ends in a consonant, or just **ㄹ** if it ends in a vowel. This is followed by **것** and the verb **이다 (to be)**. While the full grammatical form is **것이에요** (polite) or **것입니다** (formal), the instructor notes that it is much more common to hear the shortened versions **거예요** and **겁니다**.

In casual speech or slang, this becomes **거야**. In the plain form, often found in news articles or used when talking to oneself, it appears as **것이다** or the shortened **거다**. The instructor demonstrates this with a roleplay of reading a diary:
> **내일은 아침에 일찍 일어날 거다.** / Tomorrow I will wake up early.
> **그리고 운동을 할 것이다.** / And then I will exercise.
> **그리고 청소도 하겠다.** / And then I will do some cleaning.

Additional examples for this form include:
> **찰 수는 피자를 먹을 거예요.** / Chul-soo will eat pizza.
> **그래서 안 할 거야?** / So you're not going to do it?

---

### Comparing the Two Forms: Politeness and Emphasis
The instructor explains how to choose between the two forms based on social context and desired emphasis. Generally, Form 1 (**하겠다**) sounds slightly more polite and formal than Form 2 (**할 거다**). For beginners, the instructor recommends using Form 2 as it sounds a bit more natural and casual in everyday conversation.

Beyond politeness, there is a difference in emphasis. Form 1 puts more emphasis on the verb (the action itself), while Form 2 puts more emphasis on the subject of the verb. To illustrate this, the instructor uses an exaggerated vocal stress:
- **내가 하겠다** emphasizes the "will do" (the intent/action).
- **내가 할 거다** emphasizes the "I" (the person performing the action).

---

### Advanced Examples and Special Rules
The lesson concludes with several examples that highlight specific grammatical interactions.

**Descriptive Verbs:** Future tense can be used with descriptive verbs (adjectives).
> **한국은 여름에 정말 덥겠어요.** / Korea will be really hot in the summer.
> **한국은 겨울에 정말 추울 거예요.** / Korea will be really cold in the winter.
Note that **춥다 (to be cold)** follows its special conjugation rule (the ㅂ-irregular rule) when combined with **~을 거예요**.

**ㄹ-Ending Stems:** If a verb stem already ends in **ㄹ**, such as **놀다 (to play)**, you do not add another **ㄹ**.
> **어디에서 놀 거예요?** / Where will we play/hang out?

**Formal Contexts:**
> **내일 우리 할머니 댁에서 만날 겁니다.** / Tomorrow we will meet at my grandmother's house.

**Negative Sentences:** The future tense works naturally with negative structures like **안** or **~지 않다**.
> **그가 심심하지 않겠어요?** / Will he not be bored?
> **저는 내일 학교에 안 갈 거예요.** / I will not go to school tomorrow.
> **그 사람에게 아무것도 말하지 않을 거야.** / I will not say anything to that person.
