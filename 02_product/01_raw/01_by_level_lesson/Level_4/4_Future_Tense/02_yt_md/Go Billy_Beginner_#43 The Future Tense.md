## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson introduces the Korean future tense, which is used to describe actions or states that will occur after the present moment. Having already covered the present and past tenses, the instructor explains how to transform simple statements like "I go" into "I will go" or "Billy ate" into "Billy will eat." The primary focus is on the grammar construction involving the form 것이다.

The instructor begins by clarifying the relationship between the present and future tenses in Korean. While the present tense can often imply future intent depending on the context, the dedicated future tense is necessary when the speaker wants to emphasize that an action is specifically planned for the future and is not currently happening. The lesson covers the standard conjugation rules, specific exceptions for certain verb stems, and how to apply honorific endings within a future context.

---

### Present Tense vs. Future Tense
The instructor notes that the future tense is not always strictly required because the present tense can already function as a future tense in many contexts. For example, the sentence 내일 파티에 가요 (Tomorrow I go to the party) is grammatically present tense but naturally translates to "Tomorrow I will go to the party" because of the word 내일 (tomorrow). 

However, the future tense should be used whenever you want to emphasize that you will do something and that you are not already doing it. Without the future tense, a sentence like 파티에 가요 could mean "I go to the party" (habitual/present) or "I will go to the party." Using the future tense removes this ambiguity and clarifies that the event is not occurring today.

---

### Making the Future Tense: 것이다
The standard way to form the future tense is by using the grammar form 것이다. This form is a combination of the word 것 (thing) and the verb 이다 (to be). In spoken Korean, 것 is frequently shortened to 거, making the common ending 거예요. The construction follows three steps: first, identify the verb stem; second, attach 을 if the stem ends in a consonant, or attach ㄹ if it ends in a vowel; third, attach 것이다 and conjugate 이다 (usually to 이에요).

The instructor provides the following conjugation examples:
- **가다 (to go):** The stem 가 ends in a vowel, so attach ㄹ to get 갈, then add 거예요 to result in **갈 거예요**.
- **먹다 (to eat):** The stem 먹 ends in a consonant, so attach 을 to get 먹을, then add 거예요 to result in **먹을 거예요**.

A comparison of tenses is shown through these sentences:
> **어제 선생님을 만났어요.** / I met the teacher yesterday.
> **내일 선생님을 만날 거예요.** / I will meet the teacher tomorrow.

---

### The Verb 이다 in the Future Tense
When using the verb 이다 (to be) itself in the future tense, it simply becomes 일 거예요. This is used to state that a noun will be something or located somewhere in the future.
> **내일의 모임은 여기일 거예요.** / Tomorrow's meeting will be here.

The instructor provides a critical usage warning: 일 거예요 cannot be used to say that a person will "become" a professional or a certain type of person (e.g., "I will be a teacher"). In Korean, the concept of "becoming" requires a different verb which is covered in a separate lesson.

---

### Conjugation Exceptions: ㅂ and ㄹ Stems
There are specific rules for verb stems ending in the consonants ㅂ or ㄹ when forming the future tense. For stems ending in ㅂ, the ㅂ is removed and replaced with 우, followed by ㄹ 거예요.
- **춥다 (to be cold):** The stem 춥 loses ㅂ and adds 우 + ㄹ 거예요 to become **추울 거예요**.

For stems that already end in ㄹ, you do not add another ㄹ. Instead, you simply attach 거예요 directly to the stem.
- **팔다 (to sell):** The stem 팔 already ends in ㄹ, so it becomes **팔 거예요**.

---

### Future Tense with Honorifics
When using the honorific verb ending 으시다 in the future tense, the construction changes slightly. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, it becomes 으실 거예요. If the stem ends in a vowel, it becomes 실 거예요. This allows the speaker to show respect to the subject of the sentence while speaking about the future.

Examples of honorific future tense:
> **우리 할머니도 중국어를 공부하실 거예요.** / My grandmother also will study Chinese.
> **선생님은 나가실 거예요?** / Will the teacher go outside?
