## Teaching Points

### 1. `겠다` in Inquisitive Sentences
Ji explains two primary ways `겠다` functions in questions:
*   **To Confirm/Make Sure:** Used to politely confirm a situation or intention. It is similar to `-(을)ㄹ래요?` but significantly more formal.
    *   **정말 괜찮으시겠어요?** / Are you sure you're really going to be okay?
    *   **상금을 받으시겠습니까?** / Would you like to receive the prize money?
*   **Rhetorical Questions:** Used to express doubt or a "why would..." sentiment.
    *   **전화를 받겠어?** / Would he answer the call? (Implies: Why would he answer? He won't.)
    *   **제가 그 돈을 왜 받겠습니까?** / Why would I receive that money?

### 2. Special Case: `알다` vs. `알겠어요`
Ji introduces the concept that adding `겟` implies the speaker has "thought about" whether they know or not.
*   **알아요 (I certainly know):** Used for binary facts or established skills.
    *   **이 가수를 알아요?** / Do you know this singer?
    *   **기타를 칠 줄 알아요.** / I know how to play the guitar.
*   **알겠어요 (I think I know / I understand):** Associated with new information or responding to commands. It is more respectful because it suggests thoughtfulness.
    *   **이제 알겠어요.** / Now I understand (after being taught).
    *   **네, 알겠습니다.** / Yes, I got it (formal response to a request).

### 3. Special Case: `모르다` vs. `모르겠어요`
Similar to `알다`, the distinction here is often one of politeness and certainty.
*   **몰라요 (I certainly don't know):** Can sound blunt or even rude (like "Why should I know?").
*   **모르겠어요 (I don't think I know / I'm not sure):** The standard "polite" way to say you don't know. It softens the refusal or lack of information.
    *   **아뇨, 모르겠어요.** / No, I'm afraid I don't know (used when someone asks for help).
    *   **잘 모르겠어요.** / I'm not sure (used to be polite even if you definitely didn't like something, like a blind date).

### 4. Subject-Based Nuance
Ji highlights a subtle difference when talking about a third person:
*   **몰라요:** "I am sure she doesn't know."
*   **모르겠어요:** "I don't know if she knows."

## Lesson Flow

*   **Rhetorical Questions with `겠다`:** Ji starts by explaining how `겠다` functions like "would" in English rhetorical questions. He gives the example of someone sleeping at 3 AM and the other person asking, "Would he answer the phone?" (`전화를 받겠어?`).
*   **Honorifics Review:** Ji revisits the "tree" metaphor to show the placement of `-(으)시-` before `겟`. He uses the example `괜찮으시겠어요?` to show how to ask if someone will be okay in a respectful way.
*   **The "Thoughtfulness" of `겟`:** Ji introduces the special cases of `알다` and `모르다`. He explains that `겟` adds an element of "thinking before replying." Amira listens as Ji explains that `알겠어요` is for new concepts, while `알아요` is for things you already knew.
*   **Politeness and `모르겠어요`:** Ji emphasizes that `몰라요` can be rude. He uses a scenario of a mother looking for her daughter. If a stranger says `몰라요`, it sounds dismissive. `모르겠어요` sounds like "I'm sorry, I don't think I've seen her." Amira agrees, noting the importance of the "feeling" behind the words.
*   **Contextual Examples:** Ji provides a scenario about a blind date (`소개팅`). Even if the student hated the date, saying `잘 모르겠어요` (I'm not sure) is the polite way to respond to the friend who set it up.
*   **Wrap-up and Assignment:** Ji summarizes that Korean is a contextual language. He assigns Amira to write 10 sentences for each future tense (`ㄹ 거다`, `ㄹ게요`, `ㄹ래요`) and 20 sentences using `겠다` across its various uses. He also encourages her to use a podcast to find and break down natural sentences.
