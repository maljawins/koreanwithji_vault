## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the progressive tense, which is used to describe actions that are currently in progress or happening continually. While the standard present tense in Korean can often translate to both "I do" and "I am doing," the progressive tense specifically emphasizes the "ing" aspect of an action. This lesson covers the grammatical structure, conjugation rules, negative forms, and the nuanced differences between the present and progressive tenses.

---

### What is the Progressive Tense?

The progressive tense is similar to the present tense in that it can describe something happening right now. However, its use is more restricted: it is only used for actions that are happening currently or continually. When used with an action verb, it translates to the English "-ing" form. It is called the "progressive" because it explicitly shows that an action is in progress at the moment of speaking.

The instructor provides a comparison to illustrate the difference in specificity:
- **한국어를 자주 공부해요.** / I study Korean often. (General habit)
- **한국어를 공부해요.** / I study Korean. OR I am studying Korean. (General or current)
- **한국어를 공부하고 있어요.** / I am studying Korean. (Specifically currently or continually)

---

### Conjugation Rules

To form the progressive tense, the instructor outlines a simple three-step process. First, identify the action verb and get its verb stem by removing the final 다. Second, attach the marker 고 있다 to that stem. Third, conjugate the verb 있다 into the desired politeness level. In most beginner contexts, this results in the ending 고 있어요.

The instructor emphasizes that this form is strictly for action verbs. Because the progressive tense focuses on an ongoing process, it does not apply to descriptive verbs (adjectives). The visual on screen reinforces that the conjugation remains consistent regardless of whether the verb stem ends in a vowel or a consonant.

### Conversation Practice and Vocabulary

The lesson includes a dialogue between two characters, 지효 and 나연, to demonstrate the tense in context. The first conversation shows a continual action:
> **저는 요즘 강아지를 키우고 있어요.** / I am raising a dog these days.
> **강아지요? 전 고양이를 키우고 싶어요.** / A dog? I want to raise a cat.

The second conversation demonstrates a phone call and immediate actions:
> **네? 아직 잠자고 있어요?** / Yes? Are you still sleeping?
> **아니요. 일어났어요. 왜요?** / No. I woke up. Why?
> **오늘 뭐해요?** / What are you doing today?
> **오늘 친구를 만나요. 나연 씨는요?** / I'm meeting a friend today. How about you, Nayeon?
> **전 지금 빌리 씨랑 현우 씨의 집에서 놀고 있어요.** / As for me, right now I am hanging out at Billy and Hyunwoo's house.

The instructor notes that **잠자다** literally means "to sleep a sleep," and one can use either **잠자다** or the simpler **자다**. Additionally, he points out that when answering the phone and knowing who is calling, a simple **네?** is a natural response.

---

### Negative Progressive Tense

To express that one is not currently doing something, the instructor warns against a common mistake: do not change 고 있다 to 고 없다. Instead, there are two correct ways to form a negative. The first method is to change the ending to 고 있지 않다 and then conjugate it, resulting in 고 있지 않아요. The second, more casual method is to place the negative adverb 안 before the entire progressive construction.

Examples of the negative form:
- **지금 하고 있지 않아요.** / I am not doing it now.
- **지금 안 하고 있어요.** / I am not doing it now.

---

### Nuance: Present vs. Progressive

The instructor concludes with a note on when to choose the progressive tense over the standard present tense. Unless there is a specific need to emphasize that an action is currently or continually happening, the regular present tense is often sufficient. The progressive tense is a tool for emphasis. For example, saying **요즘 한국어를 공부해요** implies you study Korean in general, whereas **요즘 한국어를 공부하고 있어요** emphasizes the ongoing, continuous nature of that study right now.
