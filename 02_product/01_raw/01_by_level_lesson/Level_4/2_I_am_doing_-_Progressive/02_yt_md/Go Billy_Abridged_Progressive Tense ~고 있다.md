## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the Korean progressive tense, which is used to describe actions that are currently or continually happening. This form is the equivalent of the "-ing" form in English (e.g., "I am doing"). The lesson covers the basic construction of the tense, its application in different time frames (past, present, and future), and its honorific variations. Additionally, the instructor explains the nuance between the simple present tense and the progressive tense, as well as a more advanced "in the middle of" construction.

---

### Basic Construction: ~고 있다

To form the progressive tense, you take an action verb stem and attach **고** followed by the verb **있다 (to be/to exist)**. The instructor emphasizes that this form is exceptionally regular with no exceptions; the ending of the verb stem does not matter. While **있다** is the base form, it is most commonly conjugated into the polite present form **있어요**.

The instructor demonstrates this with the verb **하다 (to do)**. The verb stem is **하**, to which you add **고**, resulting in **하고 있어요 (I am doing)**. This structure applies to any action verb to indicate that the action is currently in progress.

---

### Current vs. Continual Actions

The progressive tense serves two primary functions. First, it describes an action happening right now (currently). Second, it describes an action that happens over a period of time (continually), even if it isn't happening at the exact moment of speaking. For example, if a friend asks what you have been doing lately, you can say you are studying Korean to mean you are doing it generally these days.

To clarify which meaning is intended, certain adverbs are often used:
- **지금** / right now
- **요즘** / nowadays, lately, these days

The instructor provides the example:
> **저는 지금 운동을 하고 있어요.** / I am exercising right now.

Without the word **지금**, the sentence **운동을 하고 있어요** could mean either "I am lifting weights right now" or "I have been exercising regularly lately."

---

### Progressive Tense in Past and Future

While the progressive tense is a tense itself, it can be combined with past and future markers.
- **Past Progressive:** Attach **고 있었다** (often conjugated as **있었어요**). This means "was doing" and emphasizes that an action was happening continually at a specific point in the past.
- **Future Progressive:** Attach **고 있을 것이다** (often shortened to **있을 거예요**). This means "will be doing."

The instructor notes that these are often unnecessary. In most cases, the regular past tense (**운동을 했어요** / I exercised) or regular future tense (**운동을 할 거예요** / I will exercise) is sufficient. The progressive versions are reserved for situations where you need to emphasize a specific duration or period of time, such as "I was sleeping in my room when you called."
> **저는 방에서 잠을 자고 있었어요.** / I was sleeping in my room.

---

### Negative Progressive Tense

There are two ways to make the progressive tense negative:
1. Placing **안** before the entire construction: **안 하고 있어요** (I am not doing it).
2. Using the negative ending **~지 않다** on the verb **있다**: **하고 있지 않아요** (I am not doing it).

The instructor issues a stern warning against a common beginner mistake: changing **고 있다** to **고 없다**. In Korean, **없다** is the opposite of **있다** in terms of existence, but it cannot be used to form a negative progressive tense. You must use **안** or **~지 않다**.

---

### Honorific Progressive: ~고 계시다

When speaking about someone who requires respect (like a grandfather or a boss), the honorific version of **있다**, which is **계시다**, must be used. In the polite **요** style, this becomes **계세요**.
> **아버지는 지금 운전하고 계세요.** / My father is driving right now.

---

### Progressive Tense vs. Simple Present Tense

The instructor explains that the simple present tense (e.g., **운동해요**) can also mean "I am exercising right now." The difference lies in emphasis. The progressive tense (**하고 있어요**) adds a layer of focus on the fact that the action is currently occupying your time. It is often used as an explanation or an excuse, such as "I'm busy because I'm working right now."

---

### Expressing a Continued State

With specific verbs, **~고 있다** describes a state that resulted from an action and continues to persist. This is most common with clothing and "carrying" verbs.
- **Wearing clothes:** If you put on a coat, you are in the state of "wearing" it.
  > **영희는 반바지를 입고 있어요.** / Yeong-hui is wearing shorts.
- **Carrying items:** The verb **가지다 (to have/carry)** is used this way.
  > **제가 가지고 있어요.** / I have it (I am in the state of carrying it).

Note: The instructor warns not to use **~고 있다** for the physical acts of standing (**서다**), sitting (**앉다**), or lying down (**눕다**) to describe the *state* of being seated or standing. In those cases, a different grammar form (**~어 있다**) is required, though **앉고 있어요** would literally mean the slow-motion physical act of lowering oneself into a chair.

---

### Advanced Form: ~는 중이다 (In the Middle of)

For intermediate learners, the instructor introduces **~는 중이다**. This is formed by taking an action verb stem, adding the modifier **~는**, and following it with **중 (middle)** and **이다 (to be)**.
- If a verb stem ends in **ㄹ**, the **ㄹ** is dropped before adding **~는** (e.g., **살다** becomes **사는**).

This form specifically emphasizes being "in the middle of" an action right now. It can even be combined with the progressive tense for maximum emphasis:
> **한국어를 공부하고 있는 중이에요.** / I am currently in the middle of studying Korean.
