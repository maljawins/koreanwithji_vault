## Teaching Points
- **Irregular Verb Correction:** Clarifying that 다르다 (to be different) conjugates to 달라요 (not 달ㄹ아요) and 다를 거예요 (not 달를 거예요). Reinforcing that ㄹ-irregular verbs like 살다 (to live) drop the ㄹ before ㄴ (사니?).
- **Particle Nuance (은/는 vs 이/가):** Explaining that 은/는 often implies contrast in specific contexts (e.g., "The clothes dried fast [but maybe not the towels]") whereas 이/가 is a neutral subject marker.
- **Exclusivity and Addition (도, 만, 밖에):**
    - **도 (also/even):** Replaces subject/object markers but follows other particles. Placement dictates emphasis (저도 vs. 수학도).
    - **만 (only):** Can be used alone or combined as 만이, 만은, 만을. 만은 adds a "at least" contrastive nuance.
    - **밖에 (nothing but):** Must be used with negative verbs. It implies a sense of "lacking" compared to the neutral 만.
- **Recipient and Source (에게, 한테, 께):**
    - 한테 is spoken; 에게 is formal/written.
    - 께 is the honorific version for "to/from" someone of higher status.
    - 께서 is strictly an honorific subject marker (replacing 이/가).
- **Particle Combinations:** Demonstrating how combining particles (e.g., 에도, 에서만, 한테밖에) allows for highly specific and expressive Korean sentences.

## Student Notes
- **Spelling Rule:** Every Korean syllable block must have a vowel; "ㄹ아" is impossible.
- **Verb Logic:** 조르다 (to pester) takes the *person* as the object, not the item being pestered for.
- **Speech Level:** Always match the speech level across the whole sentence (e.g., don't mix the casual "너" with the polite "달랐어요").
- **Particle Placement:** Remember that 도 and 만 must follow the specific word being emphasized.
- **Negative Requirement:** 밖에 cannot be used with positive verbs or the verb 아니다.
