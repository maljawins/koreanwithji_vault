### Overview

### The ~곤 하다 Structure
To express that one "used to" do something, the instructor explains a simple two-step conjugation process. First, take the verb stem of the action you are describing. Second, attach the marker **곤** followed by the verb **하다 (to do)**. Because this expression refers to past habits, **하다** is almost always conjugated into the past tense as **했어요** or **했다**.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate how this looks in practice:

> **주말에 할머니 집에 가곤 했어요.** / I used to go to my grandmother's house on the weekends.

In this example, the verb **가다 (to go)** becomes **가** (stem) + **곤 했어요**. This implies a recurring weekend habit in the past.

> **어릴 때 술을 많이 마시곤 했어요.** / I used to drink a lot of alcohol when I was young.

Here, **마시다 (to drink)** is transformed into **마시곤 했어요**. The phrase **어릴 때 (when I was young)** provides the temporal context for the habit.

> **혼자서 밥을 먹곤 했어요.** / I used to eat alone.

The verb **먹다 (to eat)** becomes **먹곤 했어요**. The instructor uses this to show how the structure handles verbs ending in consonants.

> **지난 학기에 도서관에서 숙제를 하곤 했어요.** / I used to do my homework at the library last semester.

In this case, the verb **하다 (to do)** is used twice in different roles: once as the main verb **숙제를 하다 (to do homework)** and once as the auxiliary part of the **~곤 하다** structure, resulting in **하곤 했어요**.

---

### Contrasting Past and Present
The instructor concludes by showing how to use this grammar point within a longer sentence to contrast a past habit with a current reality. This is a common way the "used to" expression is used in conversation.

> **일본어를 공부하곤 했지만 지금은 한국어를 공부해요.** / I used to study Japanese but now I study Korean.

The instructor breaks this down: **공부하다 (to study)** becomes **공부하곤 했지만**. The addition of **~지만 (but)** allows the speaker to transition from the past habit to the current action of studying Korean (**한국어를 공부해요**).
