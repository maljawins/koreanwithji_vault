### Overview

The instructor introduces the fundamental rules for conjugating Korean verbs and adjectives into the past tense. The lesson begins with a short diary entry about Valentine's Day to demonstrate the past tense in a natural context. The core of the lesson focuses on the mechanical transformation of verb stems using the double 시옷 (ㅆ) support character. By leveraging existing knowledge of the polite 요 form, learners can easily master past tense conjugation across various speech levels, including plain, polite, and formal honorific styles.

---

### Basic Conjugation Rules

To form the past tense, one must first identify the verb stem by removing the 다 from the dictionary form. Depending on the vowels in the stem, either 아 or 어 is added. The instructor notes that these are the same rules used for general conjugation (like the 요 form). Once the appropriate vowel is attached, a double 시옷 (ㅆ) is added underneath that vowel as a bottom consonant (받침). Finally, the word is finished with a suffix based on the desired speech level. Adding 다 results in the plain form, which is frequently used in writing and diaries.

---

### The 요 Form Shortcut

The instructor highlights a simplified method for those already familiar with the polite 요 form. One can take the standard 요 form of a verb, remove the 요, and then attach the double 시옷 (ㅆ) to the remaining syllable. This creates the base for the past tense. From this base, different endings can be attached: adding 어요 (or just 어 for casual speech) creates the polite past tense, while adding 습니다 for statements or 습니까 for questions creates the formal honorific past tense.

---

### Conjugation Examples

The instructor provides several visual demonstrations of these rules in action. For the verb 공부하다 (to study), the 요 form is 공부해요. Removing 요 leaves 공부해. Adding the double 시옷 results in the base 공부했. This can then become the plain form 공부했다, the polite form 공부했어요, or the formal forms 공부했습니다 and 공부했습니까. 

For the verb 놀다 (to play/hang out), the 요 form is 놀아요. Removing 요 leaves 놀아. Adding the double 시옷 creates the base 놀았, which conjugates to 놀았어요. For the descriptive verb 덥다 (to be hot), which is irregular, the 요 form is 더워요. Removing 요 leaves 더워, and adding the double 시옷 results in the base 더웠, leading to 더웠어요 or 더웠습니다.

---

### Sentence Examples

The following sentences illustrate the past tense in use:
> **저는 집에 갔어요.** / I went home.
> **친구에게서 카드를 받았습니다.** / I received a card from a friend.
> **어제 서울이 아주 추웠습니다.** / Yesterday Seoul was very cold.
> **맛있는 음식을 준비했어요.** / I prepared some delicious food.
> **집에 누가 있었어?** / Who was at home?

---

### The Verb 이다 (To Be) and 아니다 (To Not Be)

The verb 이다 (to be) follows a slightly different pattern depending on whether the preceding noun ends in a consonant or a vowel. If the noun ends in a consonant, 이다 becomes 이었다. If the noun ends in a vowel, it becomes 였다. Examples shown include:
> **나도 옛날에 학생이었다.** / I was also a student a long time ago.
> **정말 재밌는 하루였다.** / It was a really fun day.

In contrast, the negative verb 아니다 (to not be) always conjugates to 아니었다 (or 아니었어요 in the polite form), regardless of the preceding noun's ending.
> **사실 그는 연예인이 아니었어요.** / Actually, he was not a celebrity.
