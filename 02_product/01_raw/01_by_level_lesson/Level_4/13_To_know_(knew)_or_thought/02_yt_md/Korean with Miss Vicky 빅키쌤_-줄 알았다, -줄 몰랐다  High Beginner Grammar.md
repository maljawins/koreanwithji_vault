## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar patterns `-줄 알았다` and `-줄 몰랐다`, which are high-beginner expressions used to describe assumptions, realizations, and past knowledge. While the basic forms `-줄 알다/모르다` are commonly used to express ability (knowing how to do something), the past tense versions `알았다` and `몰랐다` carry nuanced meanings: "I knew it would be so" versus "I thought it would be so (but it wasn't)." The lesson covers how to distinguish these meanings through spoken stress, how to conjugate the patterns across different tenses (past, present, and future), and provides numerous examples to illustrate the difference between initial thoughts and reality.

---

### Ability vs. Assumption: The Basic Distinction

Before diving into the main lesson, the instructor clarifies the difference between the ability-based `-을 줄 알다/모르다` and the assumption-based `-줄 알았다/몰랐다`. When used in the present tense, these phrases typically refer to skills. For example, `피아노 칠 줄 알아요 (I know how to play the piano)` expresses a skill, while `피아노 칠 줄 몰라요 (I don't know how to play the piano)` expresses a lack of skill, equivalent to `피아노 못 쳐요`. Other examples include `한국어 조금 할 줄 알아요 (I can speak a little Korean)` and `요리할 줄 몰라요 (I can't cook)`.

However, when the final verb is conjugated into the past tense as `알았다` or `몰랐다`, the meaning shifts. While it can still mean "I used to know how" or "I didn't know how" in the past, it more commonly functions to express assumptions or realizations about facts.

---

### The Two Meanings of -줄 알았다

The expression `-줄 알았다` has two primary meanings depending on the context and emphasis. The first meaning is "I knew it," used when the speaker correctly predicted an outcome. The second meaning is "I thought," which implies that the speaker's initial assumption was wrong. For example, the phrase `그럴 줄 알았어` can mean either "I knew that would happen" or "I thought that would be the case (but it wasn't)."

In spoken Korean, these are distinguished by stress. To express "I knew it," the speaker stresses the verb `알았어`. To express "I thought (but was wrong)," the stress is placed on the preceding part, `그럴 줄`, while the `알았어` part is pronounced more weakly. The instructor demonstrates this with several examples:
- `지수가 올 줄 알았어요` (Stressing `알았어요`): I knew Jisoo would come.
- `지수가 올 줄 알았어요` (Stressing `올 줄`): I thought Jisoo would come (implying she didn't).
- `한국어가 어려울 줄 알았어요` (Stressing `어려울 줄`): I thought Korean would be difficult (but it's not).
- `한국어가 어려울 줄 알았어요` (Stressing `알았어요`): I knew Korean would be difficult.

---

### Tense Conjugation Rules

The suffix attached to the verb or adjective changes based on the tense of the action being described.

**Present Tense Assumptions:**
Used when you thought something was happening currently or is a general fact.
- **Verbs:** Add `-는 줄 알았다`. Example: `지수가 매일 일찍 일어나는 줄 알았어요 (I thought Jisoo wakes up early every day)`.
- **Adjectives:** Add `-(으)ㄴ 줄 알았다`. Example: `한국어가 쉬운 줄 알았는데... (I thought Korean was easy, but...)`.
- **Nouns (이다):** Add `-인 줄 알았다`. Example: `학생인 줄 알았어요 (I thought you were a student)`.

**Past Tense Assumptions:**
Used when you thought something had already happened in the past.
- **Verbs:** Add `-(으)ㄴ 줄 알았다`. Example: `숙제를 다 끝낸 줄 알았어 (I thought you had finished your homework)`.
- **Adjectives/Nouns:** These use the same `-(으)ㄴ` or `-인` forms as the present tense; the specific time is usually understood through context.

**Future Tense Assumptions:**
Used for predictions or things that were expected to happen.
- **Verbs/Adjectives:** Add `-(으)ㄹ 줄 알았다`. Example: `비가 올 줄 알았어요 (I knew/thought it would rain)`.

The instructor notes a key difference from English: in Korean, if you are talking about a present fact, you use the present suffix even if the main verb `알았다` is in the past. English requires tense agreement (e.g., "I thought she *woke* up early"), but Korean focuses on the timing of the content itself.

---

### Using -줄 몰랐다

The pattern `-줄 몰랐다` means "I didn't know that..." and follows the same tense rules as `-줄 알았다`. It expresses surprise or a lack of awareness regarding a fact.
- **Future:** `오늘 비가 올 줄 몰랐어요 (I didn't know it would rain today)`.
- **Present:** `지금 비가 오는 줄 몰랐어요 (I didn't know it was raining now)`.
- **Past:** `엄마가 벌써 온 줄 몰랐어요 (I didn't know Mom had already come)`.
- **Nouns:** `선생님인 줄 몰랐어요 (I didn't know you were a teacher)`.
- **Adjectives:** `네가 배고픈 줄 몰랐어 (I didn't know you were hungry)`.

---

### Casual Usage and Pop Culture Examples

In very casual speech, the final verb `알았다` or `몰랐다` is sometimes omitted entirely, leaving just the `-줄` ending. The instructor references the K-drama *The Glory*, where the character Yeon-jin frequently uses this shortened form. Examples from the drama include:
- `보고 싶어 죽은 줄 (I thought I was dying to see you)`
- `뉴스는 보는 줄 (I thought you were watching the news)`

This shortened form is highly informal and often used to express sarcasm or strong emotion.
