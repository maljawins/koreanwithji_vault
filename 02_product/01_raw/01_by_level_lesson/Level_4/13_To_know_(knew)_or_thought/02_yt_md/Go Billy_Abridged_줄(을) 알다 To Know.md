## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form 줄 알다 and its negative counterpart 줄 모르다, which are primarily used to express expectations, thoughts, or knowledge about a situation. While often translated as "to know" or "to think," the core concept is about what the speaker expected to happen or be true. The lesson covers the construction of this form using various verb tenses and highlights a secondary, distinct meaning: knowing "how to" perform a specific skill.

The learning arc begins with the structural mechanics of turning verbs into adjectives to attach to 줄, moves into the nuanced interpretation of "expectation" versus "fact," and concludes by comparing this form to similar-looking structures like 것을 알다 and 는지 알다. By the end of the video, learners should understand how the same sentence can translate as "I thought..." or "I knew..." depending entirely on whether the speaker's expectation matched the actual outcome.

---

### Construction and Tense
To create this form, a verb (action or descriptive) is conjugated into an adjective form and followed by 줄, an optional object marker 을, and finally the verb 알다 (to know) or 모르다 (to not know). The instructor demonstrates that the tense of the adjective determines the timing of the expectation. For an action verb like 먹다 (to eat), the adjective forms are 먹는 (present), 먹은 (past), and 먹을 (future). For a descriptive verb like 높다 (to be high), the forms are 높은 (present), 높았던 (past), and 높을 (future).

The verb 이다 (to be) follows standard adjective conjugation rules: 인 for present, 일 for future, and 이였던 or 였던 for past. While 줄 does not have a specific standalone meaning in this context—functioning similarly to the bound noun 것 (thing)—it can also literally mean 방법 (method/way). Most importantly, the final verbs 알다 and 모르다 are typically used in the past tense (알았어요 / 몰랐어요) because the speaker is usually reflecting on an expectation they held prior to the current moment.

---

### The Concept of Expectation
The instructor emphasizes that translating 줄 알다 as "to expect" is the most reliable way to understand its meaning. In English, "I thought" and "I knew" are used differently, but in Korean, they are often the same sentence. The distinction depends on the result. If you say 비가 올 줄 알았어요 (I expected it to rain) and it is actually raining, it translates as "I knew it would rain." If you say the same sentence but it is not raining, it translates as "I thought it would rain."

This form does not express a personal opinion; it strictly expresses an expectation. Examples provided on screen include:
- **비가 올 줄 알았어요.** / I knew/thought it would rain.
- **비가 올 줄 몰랐어요.** / I didn't know/think it would rain.
- **철수가 가는 줄 알았어요.** / I thought/knew Chul-soo was going (present).
- **철수가 간 줄 알았어요.** / I thought/knew Chul-soo went (past).
- **김치를 안 좋아할 줄 알았어요.** / I thought/knew you wouldn't like Kimchi.
- **철수가 미국 사람인 줄 알았어요.** / I thought/knew Chul-soo was an American.

---

### Knowing "How To" (Skills)
A secondary use of this form occurs specifically when an action verb is conjugated into the future tense adjective form (ending in 을/ㄹ) and followed by 줄 알다/모르다. In this specific configuration, the form means "to know how to" or "to not know how to" do something. This usage is restricted to actual skills rather than general methods.

The instructor contrasts this with the general word 방법 (method). While 방법 is a general, more formal word for any "way" to do something (like the way to go to school), 줄 is conversational and reserved for skills like speaking a language, riding a bike, or cooking. On-screen examples include:
- **한국어 할 줄 알아요.** / I know how to speak Korean. (Note: using the present adjective 하는 would change the meaning to "I thought you speak Korean").
- **자전거를 탈 줄 알아요.** / I know how to ride a bike.
- **요리할 줄 몰라요.** / I don't know how to cook.

---

### Comparisons: 것 vs. 줄 and 는지
The instructor clarifies the difference between 줄 알다 and 것을 알다 (specifically the full form 이라는 것을 알다). While both can translate as "to know that," they function differently. 줄 is about expectations and thoughts (e.g., "I thought he was Korean"). In contrast, 것 is used for established facts and information (e.g., "I knew the fact that he is Korean"). 것 is a general word used in many contexts, whereas 줄 is specific to these expectation and skill patterns.

Finally, the lesson touches on the difference between 줄 and 는지 when expressing "how to." A sentence like 자전거를 어떻게 타는지 알아요 (I know how one rides a bike) requires the question word 어떻게 (how) to convey the same meaning as 자전거를 탈 줄 알아요. The 줄 form is more common in daily conversation, while 는지 has a much broader range of uses in other grammar patterns.
