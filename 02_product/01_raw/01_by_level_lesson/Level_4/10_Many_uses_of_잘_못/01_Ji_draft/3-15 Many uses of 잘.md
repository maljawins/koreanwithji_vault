**Intro**

Welcome. In the previous lesson, we learned about “못”.

And today’s topic “잘” would be the antonym of “못”, which means “Can’t”.

The most standard definition of “잘” is “Well”, as in “I sing well”.

How is “well” the opposite of “can’t?”, you might ask.

Here’s an example. “He can’t sing” doesn’t mean “He’s not CAPABLE of singing.” It just means he’s a BAD singer. I mean, anyone can sing it’s just a matter of how **WELL** they can sing.

See the logic? which is why “He CAN sing” implies “He’s a good singer” or as we say in Korean, “he sings WELL”.

By the way, if you were to translate “I’m a good singer” word for word, it would imply that I’m a professional singer.

And this is the case for ALL -er words like player, swimmer

So, we phrase the same message in this format “저는 노래를 잘 해요 - I sing well.”

Now that we got the most standard use out of the way, let’s learn a couple more ways Koreans use “잘”.

**잘생기다**

We talked about this word in the lesson “More about Past tense”

잘생기다 is used only in the past tense because 생기다 means "to be formed/created."

So 잘생기다 is used always in the past in the sense of "was created well" (i.e., was "formed" handsomely) and that state continues.

And the opposite of 잘생기다 would be 못생기다 – ugly.

**잘 – Frequently / Often**

잘 can also mean often or frequently. And in this case, it would 100% depend on the context.

For example, “저는 김치를 잘 먹어요” could mean “I eat Kimchi pretty well” as in “I have no problem eating it” but it can sometimes mean “I Kimchi often”

Another example, “이 카페 잘 오세요?” – 잘 in this sentence doesn’t translate to “well” well. No pun intended. This sentence means “Do you come to this café often?

**Another word you can use if you just wanna directly say “often” is 자주, which ONLY means often.**

**“잘”- Without any problem, Safely, Seamlessly**

“집에 잘 갔어?” – literally, “did you go home well?”

But you can translate this “Did you get home safely?”

There’s one word I’d like to touch on and that is 잘못.

Now, this is a noun that means “mistake”, “error”, or any kind of wrongdoing. When it means “mistake”, this doesn’t necessarily mean an honest mistake. We have another word for that and that would be 실수.

And there is no space in between.

So, 잘못하다 would mean to make a mistake. 잘 못하다 would mean “can’t do it well”

**Closure**

현재까지 배운 내용과 충분한 vocab으로 이제는 웬만한 survival Korean은 가능할 것이다.

What you are DOING, what you WANT to do, what you NEED and what you CAN do를 한국어로 각각의 speech level과 Present, Past, Future tense로 표현할 수 있으니 말이다.

그리고 각각의 particle들을 사용해서 다양한 조합의 문장을 이제 이해하고 구사할 수 있을 것이다.
