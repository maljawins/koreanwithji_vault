## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces two distinct ways to express the concept of "should have" in Korean. While English uses the same grammatical structure regardless of the subject, Korean differentiates based on whether the speaker is referring to their own past actions (first person) or someone else's past actions (second person). This lesson is specifically aimed at intermediate learners due to the nuance and specific grammatical constraints involved in these forms.

The core of both expressions involves the verb 그랬다, which is the past tense of 그렇다 (to be so). The instructor emphasizes that 그랬다 must be conjugated appropriately for the desired politeness level at the end of the sentence. The primary difference between the two forms lies in the connecting particles used: one utilizes 걸 and the other utilizes 지.

---

### First Person: -(을)걸 그랬다
This form is strictly reserved for the first person (I or me). It expresses regret or a feeling that the speaker should have done something differently in the past. To construct this, take an action verb stem and attach 을 if it ends in a consonant, or ㄹ if it ends in a vowel. This is followed by 걸 and then optionally the verb 그랬다. The instructor notes that in casual speech, 그랬다 is often omitted, leaving just the -(을)걸 ending.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate this usage:
- **나도 파티에 갈 걸 그랬어.** / I also should have gone to the party.
- **어제 할 걸 그랬어.** / I should have done it yesterday.
- **더 일찍 잠을 잘 걸.** / I should have slept earlier. (Omitted 그랬다)
- **그냥 기다릴 걸.** / I should have just waited. (Omitted 그랬다)

For negative sentences (expressing "should not have"), the instructor highlights a critical rule: you must use the verb 말다 (to stop/not do) rather than standard negation like 안 or -지 않다. Using 안 먹을 걸 or 먹지 않을 걸 is considered grammatically incorrect in this specific context. An example of the correct negative form is shown:
- **이걸 먹지 말 걸 그랬나?** / Should I not have eaten this?

---

### Second Person: -지 그랬다
This form is used exclusively for the second person (you). It is used to tell someone else what they should have done, often carrying a tone of suggestion or mild reproach. To form this, attach -지 directly to the action verb stem, followed by the conjugated form of 그랬다. Unlike the first-person form, 그랬다 is generally not omitted here.

The instructor uses the following examples to illustrate the second-person "should have":
- **너도 파티에 가지 그랬어.** / You also should have gone to the party.
- **어제 숙제를 하지 그랬어.** / You should have done the homework yesterday.
- **그냥 저녁을 만들지 그랬어.** / You should have just made dinner.
- **내 말대로 우산을 가져오지 그랬어.** / You should have brought an umbrella like I told you.
- **그 여자한테 좋아한다고 말하지 그랬어.** / You should have told that girl that you like her.

The instructor concludes by reminding learners that these two forms are subject-dependent and cannot be swapped; using the 걸 form for "you" or the 지 form for "I" would be incorrect.
