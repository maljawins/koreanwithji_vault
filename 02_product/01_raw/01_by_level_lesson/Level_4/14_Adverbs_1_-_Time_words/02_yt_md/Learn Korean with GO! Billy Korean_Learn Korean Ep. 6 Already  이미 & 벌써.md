## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the two primary ways to say "already" in Korean: **이미** and **벌써**. While both words function as adverbs and share the same basic English translation, they are used in distinct contexts based on the speaker's emotional state. The lesson focuses on the nuance of surprise as the deciding factor between the two terms.

---

### 이미 vs. 벌써: The Nuance of Surprise
The core difference between these two adverbs lies in whether the speaker is expressing shock or surprise. **이미** is described as the regular, neutral way to express that something has already happened. It is used for stating facts or occurrences without any added emotional weight. In contrast, **벌써** is used when the speaker is surprised, shocked, or finds the timing of an event unexpected. The instructor simplifies this into a rule of thumb: use **벌써** for surprise or shock, and use **이미** for every other situation.

---

### Using 이미 in Sentences
The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate the neutral use of **이미**. In the sentence **나는 이미 숙제를 했어** `(I already did the homework)`, the speaker is simply stating a completed action. Another example, **이미 시작했어요** `(It already started)`, shows the adverb modifying a verb in a polite context. A more complex example is **첫 사랑을 찾았지만 이미 결혼한 뒤였다** `(I found my first love but it was after already getting married)`. The instructor notes that because Korean often omits pronouns, sentences like these can refer to "I," "him," "her," or "you" depending on the context.

---

### Using 벌써 in Sentences
To illustrate the element of surprise, the instructor uses the sentence **그분은 벌써 나가셨어요** `(He already left)`. In this scenario, the speaker is expressing surprise that the person has departed so soon. Another common use case is asking a question when something finishes earlier than expected, such as **벌써 끝났어?** `(Is it finished already?)`. This might be said to a friend who finishes a final exam much faster than their peers. Finally, the expression **벌써 졸업할 때가 되었다** `(It's already time to graduate)` conveys the feeling that time has passed surprisingly quickly.

---

### Vocabulary and Politeness Notes
During the examples, the instructor highlights the word **그분** as a polite way to say "that person," "he," or "him." He contrasts this with **그 사람** (casual) or simply **그** (informal). He emphasizes that if you are not close friends with the person you are discussing, it is best to use the polite form **그분**. Additionally, the verb **끝나다** `(to finish)` is introduced in the context of the exam example.
