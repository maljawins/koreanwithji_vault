## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the Korean equivalents for the English words "also" and "again." While these concepts seem straightforward in English, Korean utilizes different words—도, 또한, 또, and 다시—depending on the grammatical function, the position in the sentence, and even the emotional nuance of the speaker. The instructor breaks down these four terms into two categories: those meaning "also" or "too," and those meaning "again."

The learning arc begins with the particle 도, which is attached to nouns, and moves to the more formal adverb 또한. The second half of the video distinguishes between the two ways to say "again": the adverb 또, which often carries a negative or repetitive nuance, and 다시, which is a neutral term for "one more time." The lesson concludes with a look at the emphatic combination 또 다시.

---

### Expressing "Also" and "Too" with 도 and 또한
The instructor introduces 도 and 또한 as the primary ways to express "also." The most common form is the particle 도, which is attached directly to the end of a noun. In this usage, it translates to "also" or "too," as in the English phrase "me too." A key grammatical rule for using 도 is that it replaces the topic markers (은/는) and the subject markers (이/가) that would otherwise follow the noun.

The instructor provides the example sentence:
> **저도 김치를 좋아합니다.** / I like kimchi too.

In this sentence, 도 is attached to 저 (I), replacing the markers 는 or 가. Another example demonstrates its use with other subjects:
> **그 사람도 가게에 갔어요.** / He went to the store also (or "He went to the store too").

While 도 is primarily used with standard nouns, it can also be attached to gerunds (verbs turned into nouns). The instructor demonstrates this with the word 요리하기 (cooking):
> **저는 요리하기도 합니다.** / I even cook (or "I cook too/also").

The instructor notes that the literal meaning of 도 is close to "even," which helps explain its function in emphasizing that an additional action or subject is included.

또한 is the second way to say "also," but it functions as an adverb rather than a particle. It can be translated as "furthermore" or "in addition." Unlike 도, which must follow a noun, 또한 can be placed at the beginning or in the middle of a sentence. Examples include:
> **또한 저는 미국 사람이에요.** / Furthermore, I am an American.
> **그것 또한 아름답다.** / That is also beautiful (or "In addition, that's beautiful").

---

### Expressing "Again" with 또 and 다시
To express "again," Korean uses 또 and 다시, but they are not always interchangeable due to their connotations. 또 is used as an adverb meaning "again." However, it often carries a negative connotation, suggesting that the speaker is complaining, disappointed, or embarrassed by the repetition of an event.

The instructor provides two examples of this negative nuance:
> **그는 또 약속에 늦었어요.** / He was late to his appointment again.
> **또 왔네요.** / You came again.

In the first example, 또 conveys disappointment that the person is late once more. In the second, the instructor warns that saying "또 왔네요" can sound like the speaker is unhappy to see the person again, or is speaking sarcastically.

When a speaker wants to say "again" in a neutral way, meaning "literally one more time" without any negative feeling, they should use 다시. This is the standard term for repetition. Examples include:
> **다시 한번 만날까요?** / Should we meet again once more?
> **그녀는 다시 그를 뽀뽀했다.** / She kissed him again.

In the first example, the word 한번 (one time/once) is used alongside 다시 to clarify the meaning of "one more time." The instructor also clarifies that 뽀뽀하다 refers to a light kiss.

---

### Emphasis with 또 다시
The instructor introduces a final combination: 또 다시. This phrase literally means "once more again." It is used to provide extra emphasis to the repetition, making the "again" feel much stronger. A common context for this is in a command or a strong warning:
> **또 다시 여기에 오지 마세요.** / Don't ever come here again.

Using 또 다시 in this sentence makes the prohibition feel more intense than using either 또 or 다시 alone.
