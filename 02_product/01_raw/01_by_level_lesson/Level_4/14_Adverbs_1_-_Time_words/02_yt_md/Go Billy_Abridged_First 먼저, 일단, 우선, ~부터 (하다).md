## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces four primary ways to express the concept of "first" in Korean: 먼저, 일단, 우선, and the grammar pattern ~부터 (하다). While all can be translated as "first" in English, each carries a distinct nuance—ranging from chronological order and priority to tentative actions and starting points. The lesson also clarifies the usage of 처음, which functions differently from the other four terms.

The instructor's approach is comparative, highlighting when one word is more appropriate than another based on the speaker's intent. For instance, while one word might emphasize doing something before someone else, another might emphasize that an action is the most important priority. The lesson concludes with a brief overview of how to express "the first time" using 처음이다.

---

### 먼저: Chronological Order
먼저 is an adverb that means "first" specifically in the sense of doing something before something else or before someone else. It is used to describe the sequence of events. The instructor demonstrates that 먼저 can be placed at the beginning of a sentence or directly before a verb. It is versatile and can be used in any speech level, from casual to formal.

A common social application of 먼저 is telling someone to go ahead of you. The instructor writes **먼저 가세요** on the board, explaining it as "You go first" or "After you." Conversely, a speaker can use it to announce their own departure by saying **제가 먼저 갈게요**, which translates to "I'm going to leave first" or "I'll be taking off now."

---

### 일단: Tentative First Steps
일단 carries the nuance of "first of all," "for now," or "for the time being." It is often used when an action is being done tentatively before moving on to the next step. Phonetically, the instructor notes a sound change: it is pronounced as 일딴. Unlike 먼저, which focuses on sequence, 일단 focuses on the immediate, temporary nature of the first action.

Because it functions as a way to frame a thought, 일단 is most commonly used at the beginning of a sentence. The instructor provides the example **일단 기다리고 있어요**, meaning "For the time being, I am waiting." Another example, **일단 같이 가요**, translates to "Let's just go together for now," suggesting a tentative plan.

---

### 우선: Priority and Importance
우선 means "first" in terms of priority or importance. It can be translated as "first of all" or "what is important first is..." While it is grammatically acceptable in any speech level, it carries a slightly more formal or "fancy" feeling compared to 먼저 or 일단. It signals that the action following it is the top priority among several possibilities.

The instructor compares **우선 창문을 닫으세요** (First of all, please close the window) with a version using 먼저. While 먼저 would simply mean "close the window before doing other things," 우선 emphasizes that closing the window is the most important task at hand. Another example shown is **우선 차를 사고 싶어요**, meaning "First [and most importantly], I want to buy a car."

---

### ~부터 (하다): Starting Points
The particle 부터, usually meaning "from," can be combined with a noun and a verb (often 하다) to mean "to do [noun] first" or "to start by doing [noun]." This form indicates that the noun is the starting point of the activity. It conveys a nuance of focusing on a specific task first.

The instructor illustrates this with the example **한글부터 배우세요**, which means "Start by learning Hangeul" or "Learn Hangeul first." This implies that among all the things one could do to learn Korean, Hangeul is the starting point. Another example is **숙제부터 하고 오세요**, which translates to "Go do your homework first" or "Start with your homework and then come back." This pattern is frequently used with 일단 for added emphasis, as in **일단 밥부터 먹자** (First of all, let's start by eating).

---

### 처음: The Beginning vs. The First Time
The instructor distinguishes 처음 from the previous adverbs. 처음 is a noun meaning "the beginning." It is frequently used in the form **처음에는**, which means "in the beginning" or "at first." It describes a state at the start of a duration, such as "At first, the movie was boring."

When combined with the verb 이다 (to be), the phrase **처음이다** expresses that something is a "first time" experience. The instructor writes **여기가 처음이에요?** on the board to mean "Is this your first time here?" Additionally, 처음 can function as an adverb or be used as **처음으로** to mean "for the first time," as in doing an activity for the very first time in one's life.
