## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor clarifies the nuanced difference between two Korean adverbs that both translate to "again" in English: **또** and **다시**. While they are often interchangeable for beginners, they carry distinct implications regarding the nature of the repetition. The lesson focuses on whether an action is an additional experience or a corrective redo.

---

### 또: Additional Experience
The word **또** indicates a new repetition of an action. It is primarily used when the speaker is referring to an additional experience that is added to previous ones. The instructor uses the common parting phrase **다음에 또 봐요** (See you again next time!) to illustrate this. In this context, **또** is used because the speakers are planning a new, additional meeting in the future, rather than trying to fix or redo a previous meeting.

---

### 다시: Retry or Redo
In contrast, **다시** indicates a retry or a redo of an action. It is used when an action needs to be performed again to fulfill a goal that was not completed or a standard that was not met previously. A clear example provided is **다시 말해주세요** (Please say it again). This is used when you did not catch or understand what was said the first time; you are asking for a "redo" of the speech so that the goal of communication can be fulfilled.

---

### Contextual Comparison: Watching a Movie
The instructor provides a side-by-side comparison using the act of watching a movie to highlight the subtle shift in meaning between the two words.

- **같은 영화를 또 봤어요** / I watched the same movie again.
In this sentence, using **또** implies that you watched the movie one more time simply for an additional experience—perhaps because you enjoyed it and wanted to experience it once more.

- **같은 영화를 다시 봤어요** / I watched the same movie again.
Using **다시** here implies a specific purpose for the repetition. It suggests you watched the movie again to check if you missed something the first time, or to fulfill an unmet goal from the initial viewing, such as fully understanding the plot or studying the dialogue.
