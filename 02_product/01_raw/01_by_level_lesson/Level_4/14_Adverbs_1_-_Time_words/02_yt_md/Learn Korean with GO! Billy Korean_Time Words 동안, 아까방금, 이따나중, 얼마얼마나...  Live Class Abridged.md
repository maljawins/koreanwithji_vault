## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on essential Korean time words and adverbs used to describe when or for how long an action occurs. The instructor clarifies common points of confusion between similar-sounding or similar-meaning words, such as the nuances between different ways to say "now," "later," and "a while ago." The session also covers how to express duration using nouns and verb stems, the distinction between noun and adverb forms of time expressions, and how to ask about unknown quantities or durations. By the end of the lesson, students will understand how to precisely frame actions in time, whether they happened just a moment ago, are happening from now on, or will occur in the distant future.

---

### Right Now vs. From Now: 지금 and 이제

The instructor begins by distinguishing between two words that both translate to "now" in English: **지금** and **이제**. **지금** refers specifically to "right now," focusing on the immediate present moment. In contrast, **이제** carries the nuance of "from now on" or "starting now." While it can mean right now, it is used when the focus is on a change in state or a future action beginning from the present point. For example, if you are about to head to the store, you would use **이제** to signal that "now (from this point), I am going."

---

### Expressing "Later": 나중에 vs. 이따(가)

Korean has different words for "later" depending on the timeframe. **나중에** is used for "later" in the sense of a different day or a more distant, indefinite future. If you tell a friend **나중에 봐요** (see you later) while passing them, they might assume you do not intend to meet them again today. For "later" within the same day—meaning "in a little bit" or "in a short while"—the correct word is **이따** or **이따가**. These two are interchangeable, as adding the suffix **가** does not change the meaning.

The instructor also notes a grammatical distinction between **나중** and **나중에**. **나중** is a noun meaning "later," while adding the particle **에** turns it into the adverb **나중에**. You use the adverbial form to describe when you will do an action, such as "I will do it later." The noun form **나중** is used in sentences like "It is later," though this is less common in everyday speech.

---

### A Moment Ago: 아까 vs. 방금

To describe things that happened in the past, the instructor compares **아까** and **방금**. **아까** means "a short while ago" or "a little bit before" (**조금 전에**). It refers to a point in the past that is relatively recent but clearly separated from the present. **방금** means "just now" or "a moment ago," referring to something that happened almost immediately before the present moment. For example, **아까 했어요** means "I did it a little while ago," whereas **방금 했어요** means "I just did it right this second."

---

### Duration and "While": 동안

The word **동안** is a noun that means "a period of time," "for," or "during." It is frequently used with the particle **에** as **동안에**, though both are common. It can be used in two primary ways:

1.  **After a time noun:** To say "for [amount of time]," you place **동안** after the time duration. For example, **한 시간 동안** means "for one hour." A full sentence would be **한 시간 동안 한국어를 공부했어요** (I studied Korean for an hour).
2.  **With verbs to mean "while":** To express "while doing [action]," you take an action verb stem and attach **~는 동안**. For example, the verb **보다** (to watch) becomes **보는 동안** (while watching). The instructor provides the example **TV를 보는 동안 운동했어요**, which means "I exercised while watching TV" or "During the period of time I watched TV, I exercised."

---

### Long Durations: 오래, 오랫동안, and 오랫만

The adverb **오래** means "for a long time." It is commonly paired with specific verbs:
- **오래 되다**: Literally "a long time has passed." This is the standard way to say an object is "old." Examples include **오래된 영화** (an old movie), **오래된 카메라** (an old camera), **오래된 책** (an old book), and **오래된 차** (an old car).
- **오래 걸리다**: To "take a long time."

**오랫동안** is a combination of **오래** and **동안**, literally meaning "for a long period of time." While it is largely interchangeable with **오래**, you cannot use it with **되다** to mean "old"; you must use **오래 되다**.

**오랫만** (a shortened form of **오래간만**) is a noun meaning "the first time in a long time." It is most famously used in the greeting **오랫만이에요** (It's been a long time / Long time no see). When used as an adverb by adding **에**, **오랫만에** describes doing something for the first time after a long hiatus. Examples include **오랫만에 가다** (going somewhere for the first time in a long time) or **오랫만에 친구를 만났어요** (I met my friend for the first time in a while).

---

### Before and After: 전 and 후

The instructor introduces **전** (before) and **후** (after). Like other time words, these have noun forms (**전**, **후**) and adverbial forms (**전에**, **후에**). Use the noun forms with the verb **이다** (to be) to say "It is before" or "It is after." Use the adverbial forms to say you did something before or after a certain point.

There is a nuance difference between **전에/후에** and **이전/이후**. **이전** and **이후** imply "from that time forward/backward," including the time leading right up to or starting right from the reference point. **전에** and **후에** are more general and can refer to times much further removed from the reference point.

---

### Asking "How Much": 얼마 vs. 얼마나

**얼마** is a noun meaning "how much," used for unknown quantities, amounts, or prices.
- **얼마예요?** / How much is it?
- **얼마 동안 공부했어요?** / For how long (how much time) did you study?

**얼마나** is the adverbial equivalent. It is used to modify verbs or adjectives (descriptive verbs).
- **얼마나 사랑해요?** / How much do you love [them]?
- **얼마나 커요?** / How big is it? (Literally: How much big is it?)

When **얼마나** is used before a descriptive verb in a statement rather than a question, it functions like "so," "very," or "how [adjective]!" to show emphasis. For example, **얼마나 맛있었는데!** means "It was so delicious!" or "You don't know how delicious it was!"
