---

### The Basic Structure: 을/ㄹ 뻔하다
The expression 뻔하다 is used to describe an event that nearly occurred but ultimately did not. It typically refers to unintended or negative outcomes that were avoided by a narrow margin. To conjugate this, you take an action verb stem and attach 을 if the stem ends in a consonant, or ㄹ if it ends in a vowel. The instructor notes that writing this with or without a space (e.g., 할 뻔하다 vs. 할뻔하다) is generally acceptable.

While the base form is 뻔하다, it is almost exclusively used in the past tense because it describes a situation that has already been avoided. Common endings include 했다, 했어요, or 했네요. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 할 뻔했어요 (I almost did it).

---

### Hypothetical Contexts and "That Was Close"
This grammar point frequently appears alongside hypothetical "if" structures. The instructor highlights the use of the past tense + 으면 or the more hypothetical-sounding 더라면. These structures set up the condition under which the near-miss would have become a reality.

A very common idiomatic use of this grammar involves the expression 큰일 나다, which means "for a big problem to occur" or "to be a big deal." When combined with the "almost" structure, it creates the phrase:
> **큰일 날 뻔했어요.** / That was close. (Literally: A big problem almost happened.)

Another example provided demonstrates how the structure works with the verb 잊어버리다 (to forget):
> **방금 얘기해주지 않으셨으면 잊어버릴 뻔했어요.** / If you hadn't told me just now, I would have forgotten.

---

### Emphasizing Relief with 하마터면
The core of the lesson focuses on the adverb 하마터면. While optional, it is used specifically with 뻔하다 to add emphasis and a strong sense of relief. The instructor explains that when a listener hears 하마터면 at the start of a sentence, their brain should immediately prepare for a "phew" moment—a realization that a dangerous or annoying situation was barely escaped.

Historically, the word evolved from 하마 (an old word meaning "by chance" or "possibly") and the past-tense hypothetical form 하도면. Over time, the ㅎ sound combined with the ㄷ to create the strong 터 sound we use today. The instructor shares a definition from the Naver Dictionary to help students conceptualize its meaning: "If I had made even one tiny mistake..."

Example sentences using 하마터면:
> **하마터면 버스를 놓칠 뻔했어요.** / I almost missed the bus (but I just barely caught it).
> **하마터면 깜빡할 뻔했어요.** / I was so close to forgetting.
> **하마터면 양말이 젖을 뻔했어요.** / My socks almost got wet.
> **하마터면 머리를 박을 뻔했어요.** / I almost hit my head.
> **하마터면 강아지 꼬리를 밟을 뻔했어요.** / I almost stepped on the dog's tail.
> **하마터면 손을 다칠 뻔했어요.** / I almost hurt my hand.

---

### Alternative Emphasis: 자칫하다
The instructor introduces 자칫하다 as another verb meaning "to be a little bit wrong" or "to go amiss." It is commonly used in the "if" forms 자칫하면 or 자칫했더라면. Like 하마터면, it pairs naturally with 뻔하다 to indicate that if things had been just slightly different, a disaster would have occurred.

> **자칫하면 큰일 날 뻔했어요.** / If we weren't careful, that would have been a big problem.

There is a key distinction between the two: 하마터면 is strictly used for past-tense near-misses that have already been resolved. 자칫하면, however, can be used more broadly to describe potential future mistakes or general conditions where things might go wrong if one is not careful. It does not always require the 뻔하다 structure to function.
