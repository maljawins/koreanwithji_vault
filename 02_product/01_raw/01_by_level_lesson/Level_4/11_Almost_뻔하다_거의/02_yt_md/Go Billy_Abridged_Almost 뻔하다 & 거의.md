## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on two distinct ways to express the concept of "almost" or "barely" in Korean. The instructor distinguishes between situations where something nearly happened by mistake or accident (often an undesirable outcome) and situations where something is nearly complete or occurs with very low frequency. The first half of the video covers the grammatical structure involving verb stems and the auxiliary verb 뻔하다, while the second half introduces the adverb 거의 and its common collocations.

---

### Expressing Near-Accidents with 뻔하다

The instructor introduces the first form, which is specifically used to mean "to almost do something" in the context of a near-miss or a mistake. This structure expresses that an event was very close to happening, but fortunately, it did not. Because it describes a state that was nearly reached in the past, the final verb is conjugated into the past tense.

To form this expression, you take a verb stem and attach the future presumptive suffix. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 을; if it ends in a vowel, you attach just the ㄹ patchim. This is followed by a space and then 뻔하다, which is conjugated to the past tense, such as 뻔했어요 or 뻔했습니다. While the instructor notes that a space before 뻔하다 is technically optional, he recommends using it for better readability.

The instructor provides several examples of things the speaker did not want to happen:
- **학교에 늦을 뻔했어요.** / I was almost late to school.
- **버스를 놓칠 뻔했어요.** / I almost missed the bus.
- **핸드폰을 떨어뜨릴 뻔했어요.** / I almost dropped my cell phone.
- **큰일 날 뻔했어요.** / That was close. (Literally: A big deal almost happened.)

A pronunciation note is included regarding the word 버스 (bus). Although written with a single ㅅ, it is commonly pronounced as if it had a double consonant (뻐쓰). The instructor explains that many foreign loanwords in Korean follow this pattern of being pronounced with more tension than their spelling suggests.

---

### General "Almost" and "Barely" with 거의

The second way to say "almost" is by using the adverb 거의. Unlike the previous form, 거의 is not restricted to accidental or negative situations. It can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs to indicate that something is "almost" a certain way or that someone "barely" did something.

When used with a negative verb, 거의 translates effectively to "barely." The instructor demonstrates this with the following examples:
- **거의 비슷해요.** / It's almost similar. / It's almost the same.
- **거의 안 먹었어요.** / I barely ate. (Literally: I almost didn't eat.)
- **여기는 눈이 거의 안 와요.** / It barely snows here. / It almost never snows here.

---

### Using 거의 with 다 for Completion

A very common and natural use of 거의 is in combination with the adverb 다, which means "all," "completely," or "finished." While the verb 끝내다 can be used to mean "to finish," the instructor emphasizes that using 다 with an action verb is often more natural for expressing that one is "done" with a task.

When 거의 and 다 are used together before a verb, the meaning becomes "almost done" or "almost finished." The instructor provides a practical example of packing for a trip:
- **짐을 거의 다 쌌어요.** / I'm almost done packing. (Literally: I packed almost all the luggage.)

This combination is a staple in daily Korean conversation to indicate that a process is nearing its conclusion.
