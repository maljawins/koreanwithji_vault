## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor explains that there are two primary ways to express the concept of "almost" in Korean. The choice between them depends entirely on the context of the situation: whether you are describing a state that is nearly complete or an event that nearly happened by accident or mistake. The lesson covers the adverb 거의 for general "almost" and the grammatical structure -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 for near-misses or accidental occurrences.

---

### Using 거의 for States and Completion

To say "almost" when something is not an accident—such as being almost finished, almost ready, or almost a certain type of person—you use the adverb 거의. This word functions as a standard adverb and is placed before the verb or noun it modifies.

The instructor provides an example of identity:
> **저는 거의 한국 사람입니다.** / I am almost a Korean.

He notes that while one might not be Korean by blood, if they love Korea, eat only Korean food, and speak only Korean, they could use this sentence. Another common usage is for comparison:
> **거의 같아요.** / It's almost the same.

When combined with the adverb 다 (all/completely), it creates the phrase 거의 다, meaning "almost all" or "almost completely." This is frequently used with tasks:
> **숙제를 거의 다 했어요.** / I almost finished all of my homework.

---

### Using -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 for Near-Misses and Accidents

If you want to express that something almost happened by mistake or accidentally—a "near-miss" situation—you must use a specific grammatical form rather than the adverb 거의. This structure is formed by taking the verb stem and attaching a future participle marker followed by the verb 뻔하다 (to almost [do]).

The conjugation rules are as follows: first, take the verb stem. If the stem ends in a vowel, attach ㄹ. If the stem ends in a consonant, attach 을. Finally, add 뻔하다 and conjugate it into the appropriate tense, usually the past tense (뻔했어요) since these situations typically describe something that was avoided in the past.

The instructor demonstrates this with several high-stakes examples:
> **죽을 뻔했어요.** / I almost died.

This structure is also used for missing appointments or transportation:
> **학교에 못 갈 뻔했어요.** / I almost couldn't go to school.
> **버스를 놓칠 뻔했어요.** / I almost missed the bus.

Finally, it is used for physical accidents:
> **오늘 차에 칠 뻔했어요.** / I almost got hit by a car today.
