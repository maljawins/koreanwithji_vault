## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces three distinct ways to express the concept of "no way" or "there is no reason/logic for something to be the case" in Korean. The primary focus is on the grammar construction ~리(가) 없다, which is the standard and most common version of this expression. The lesson covers the mechanical formation of the phrase using both present and past tense verb stems, the etymological roots of the word 리, and two variations: using 있다 in a rhetorical question format and a shortened spoken version that omits the final verb entirely.

---

### Basic Construction of ~리(가) 없다

The standard way to say "there's no way" involves taking a verb stem and attaching the future tense modifier followed by the noun 리. For verb stems ending in a vowel, you attach 릴, such as 하다 (to do) becoming 할. For stems ending in a consonant, you attach 을, such as 먹다 (to eat) becoming 먹을. The instructor notes that standard irregular rules apply: stems ending in 릴 like 살다 (to live) simply stay as 살, and ㅂ-irregular verbs like 춥다 (to be cold) transform into 추울.

Once the modifier is attached, you add the noun 리, followed optionally by the subject marker 가, and finally the verb 없다 (to not exist/not have). While the subject marker is optional, it is very common to include it. This form can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs to indicate that a situation is logically impossible or highly improbable.

---

### Past Tense Usage

The instructor demonstrates that this form is not limited to the present or future. To say "there is no way something happened" in the past, you use the past tense verb stem. This is formed by taking the past tense conjugation (e.g., 했어요 or 먹었어요), removing the final 어요, and adding 으 followed by 리(가) 없다. For example, "there's no way he ate" would use 먹었 + 을 to become 먹었을 리가 없어요. This allows the speaker to express disbelief about past events, such as a student being shocked that they got every answer right on a test.

---

### The Meaning of 리 (理)

To help students understand the "why" behind the grammar, the instructor explains the Hanja (Chinese character) origin of the word 리. He writes the character 理 on the board, explaining that it means "logic" or "reason." Therefore, the expression ~리(가) 없다 literally translates to "logic/reason does not exist" for a specific action or state. When a speaker says 할 리가 없어요, they are literally saying "there is no logic or reason why someone would do that," which functions as the natural equivalent to the English "no way."

---

### Variation 1: Using 있다 for Rhetorical Emphasis

A common variation involves replacing the negative 없다 with the positive 있다 (to exist/have). This is almost always used as a rhetorical question. Instead of stating "there is no reason," the speaker asks "Is there any reason/logic for that?" with the heavy implication that there is none. For example, if something is common sense (상식), the speaker might ask 이걸 몰랐을 리가 있나? (Is there any way they wouldn't know this?), which effectively means "There's no way they didn't know this."

---

### Variation 2: Shortened Spoken Form

In casual conversation, the final verb (없다 or 있다) is often omitted entirely. The instructor explains that you can end the sentence simply with 리가 or the slightly more polite 리가요. When using this shortened form, the subject marker 가 is no longer optional; you cannot simply end with 리. This version carries an implied 없다 and feels very natural in spoken Korean. A prime example provided is the set phrase 그럴리가요!, which means "There's no way it's like that!" or "That can't be!" based on the verb 그렇다 (to be so).

---

### Examples and Nuances

The instructor provides several sentences to illustrate these forms in context:
- **엄마가 이걸 모를 리가 없지.** / There's no way mom wouldn't know this. (Literally: There is no reason mom wouldn't know this, right?)
- **비도 오는데 아직도 기다리고 있을 리 없어요.** / It's raining, so there's no way they are still waiting.
- **사람이 그렇게 쉽게 변할 리 없어요.** / There's no way people change that easily.
- **내가 시험을 다 맞았을 리가 없는데?** / There's no way I got everything right on the test! (The ending ~는데 acts like an exclamation of disbelief).
- **바람이 분다고 소풍이 취소될 리가 없지.** / There's no way the picnic will be cancelled just because it's windy.
- **우리 집에 그렇게 큰 냄비가 있을 리가.** / There's no way we have a pot that big in our house. (Shortened form).
