## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces two primary grammatical structures used to express the English concept of "there's no way." The first and most common form, `리가 없다`, is used to express disbelief based on logic or reason. The second form, `길이 없다`, literally refers to a "road" or "path," implying that there is no physical or methodological possibility for an event to occur.

The lesson follows a progression from basic construction to nuanced usage, including past tense applications and the addition of emphatic adverbs. The instructor emphasizes that while both can be translated as "there's no way," they carry different underlying meanings: one focuses on the lack of logic (disbelief), while the other focuses on the lack of a method (impossibility).

---

### Expressing Disbelief with 리가 없다
The structure `리가 없다` is formed by taking a verb stem and attaching `~(으)ㄹ` followed by `리` and the negative verb `없다 (to not exist)`. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, attach `ㄹ`; if it ends in a consonant, attach `을`. For example, `하다 (to do)` becomes `할 리가 없다`, and `먹다 (to eat)` becomes `먹을 리가 없다`.

The word `리` originates from a Chinese character meaning "reason" or "logic." Therefore, the literal translation is "there is no logic" or "there is no reason." In natural English, this translates to "there's no way" or "it doesn't make sense." It is specifically used to show disbelief. If someone says something unbelievable, a common response is `그럴 리가 없어요 (There's no way that is so)`. The instructor notes that in casual speech, the `없다` is sometimes dropped, leaving just `그럴 리가 (No way)`.

To add even more emphasis to this disbelief, Koreans often use the adverb `설마`, which itself means "no way" or "surely not." Combining them results in `설마 그럴 리가 (Surely there's no way)`.

---

### Tense and Negation with 리가 없다
This form can be used in the past tense by attaching the past tense marker to the verb stem before adding `~(으)ㄹ`. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate these variations:

- **Past Tense Affirmative:** `숙제를 이미 다 끝냈을 리가 없는데` / There's no way you already finished all the homework. (Literally: There is no logic that you finished the homework already.)
- **Past Tense Negative:** `저한테 전화가 하나도 오지 않았을 리가 없어요` / There's no way I didn't get even one phone call. (Literally: There is no logic that a phone call did not come.)

The instructor also demonstrates a double negative for emphasis: `기억을 못할 리가 없어요` / There's no way you can't remember. This implies that it is logically impossible for the person to have forgotten.

---

### Impossibility with 길이 없다
The second form, `길이 없다`, uses the noun `길`, which means "street," "road," or "way." While it translates to "there's no way" in English, the nuance is "there is no method" or "there is no possibility." It is less common than `리가 없다`.

The most frequent verb paired with this structure is `알다 (to know)`. For example, `알 길이 없네요` means "I have no way of knowing." This doesn't mean it's illogical to know, but rather that there is no available method or information to reach that knowledge. Another example shown is `확인할 길이 없어요` / There is no way to check (no method exists to verify the information).

---

### Advanced Nuances and Variations
The instructor concludes with several advanced notes regarding the particle `가` in `리가 없다`. While `가` is the standard subject marker, it can be replaced with other particles for different nuances:
- `리도 없다`: Adds emphasis, suggesting there isn't "even" any logic.
- `리는 없다`: Used for contrast.

Furthermore, the form `리가 있다` can be used, but almost exclusively in rhetorical questions to imply the opposite. For example, `그럴 리가 있겠어?` / Could there be any logic in that? (Implying: Of course not).

Finally, a more formal or emphatic version of "there's no way" is `만무하다`. The character `만` means 10,000 and `무` means "to not exist," suggesting something is "10,000 times impossible." While not very common in daily conversation, it serves as a powerful alternative to `리가 없다`.
