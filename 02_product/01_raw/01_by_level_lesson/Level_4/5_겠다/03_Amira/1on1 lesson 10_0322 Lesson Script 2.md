Now, let’s learn how to say negative sentences.

In Korean there are 2 ways to make a negative sentence.

지 않다 is slightly more formal than the other one but they both mean pretty much the same thing.

지 않다 form is much easier to use because you can use it with any Korean verb whereas you can use 안 with Native or Pure Korean verbs or 3 syllable Sino-Korean verbs.

Slide 12

What do I mean by this?

You’re probably familiar with term native and sino from Korean numbers, right?

Sino-Korean are words that come from Chinese and they’re called 한자 in Korean.

Pure Korean word simply means it’s not Sino-Korean.

And this how to distinguish the two.

First of all, all verbs with 2 syllables like 가다 하다 오다 먹다 are pure Korean verbs

Secondly, all non-하다 verbs are pure Korean like 배우다, 고맙다, 가르치다.

Thirdly, this is important. All sino-Korean verbs have end in 하다 but that doesn’t mean all 하다 verbs are sino-Korean.

좋아하다, 사랑하다, 말하다, 사냥하다 are pure Korean. And that’s because what comes before 하다 can be both pure and sino-Korean.

And the best way to be sure is to use a dictionary.

And if you go to this dictionary website, made by the Korean government, you can search for any word, and if it has a Chinese character, it means it’s sino-Korean.

Or you can go to advanced search, click on verb, native word, and search for it.

Why so many?

From experience, I think future tense is universally the most difficult concept in every language, not just Korean because 미래라는 것 자체가 본질적으로 불확실성이라는 성격을 내포하고 있기 때문에 상황에 따라 쓰는 말도 달라지기 때문이다. And on top of that, Korean being a highly contextual language could make things more interesting.

And that’s why 상황에 따라서 쓰이는 ending들도 여러가지가 있고 and the same ending can mean something different depending on the context.

이것은 영어도 마찬가지이다. 영어의 예를 들자면,

English

"I'm coming to the party" doesn't mean you are currently on the move to the party location in a physical sense. It just means "I'll be there"

And "I'm going to come to the party" This doesn't mean you're currently going “in order” to come to the party. How are you supposed to go in order to come? It doesn't make any sense.

Its actual meaning is similar to the previous sentence, but it has a slightly more emphasis on the future timeline.

"I will come to the party". And this sentence shows a firm determination, almost like no one invited me but I’m going to the party.

"I should come to the party" And this one mean two things, right?

It could mean I "HAVE TO" come to the party but “should” in this context is not used like that.

And you’re saying “I’m pretty sure I’ll be at the party."

So, just like this, Future tense in Korean has several endings with each meaning slightly different meanings.

기타를 칠 줄 알아요? Do you know how to play the guitar – Again, a simple question.

So, you’d answer with 알아요 without the 겠

But 알겠어 is used for things you think you know, so naturally, it’s often associated with new information or concept because you’d have to think about whether you know or not.

And thinking about your answer before replying is generally more respectful right? Because it means you’re being thoughtful. And because of this, it’s also used to reply to commands or requests or to simply agree politely.

So, the first example is about new information.

[알아요]와 [알겠어요]의 차이를 알겠어요? Do you know the difference of 알아요 and 알겠어요?

And you would answer 네, 이제 알겠어요

But the second example is a simple command from A

이제부터 일찍 와 – Come early from now on, 일찍 means early. Or more naturally, don’t be late from now on.

And B would answer 알겠습니다

It’s just a respectful way to respond to A although he doesn’t really have to think about whether he knows not to be late anymore

Now, let’s look at 모르다 vs 모르겠어요, to not know.

Firstly, it can simply be the opposite of 알아요 where it’s used for things you’re sure you don’t know.

And 모르겠어요 is used for things you think you don’t know where it can be the opposite of 알겠어요. But because thinking before answering is a more respectful thing to do, it’s more commonly used than 몰라요 even for things you’re sure you don’t know.

And sometimes, a straight up 몰라요 can sound a bit too blunt and come off rude because it could like “Should I even know?” and [아, 몰라] is a very common phrase which is equivalent to “Screw it” or “F it” in English.

So, general rule of thumb is always stick with 모르겠어요 when in doubt.

Let’s look at this example.

A asks B 혹시 제 딸이 어디 있는지 아세요? – This sentence has a lot of grammar points we haven’t covered yet, but it literally means By any chance, do you know where my daughter is? Do you happen to know?

And B is sure he hasn’t seen any little girl, let alone A’s daughter.

But he goes, 아뇨, 모르겠어요 which is much more polite than a straight up 몰라요, which is equivalent to oh, no I’m sorry. I don’t think I’ve seen her

Next example.

Now, Sarah arranged a blind date for Kim and wants to know if she liked the guy. And asks

소개팅 어땠어? – How was the blind date? 마음에 들었어? – Did you like him?

마음에 들다 is an idiom which literally means to enter heart but it’s a more casual way to say to like something or someone.

And Kim didn’t like the guy but she can’t say that, can she?

So she goes, 잘 모르겠어요 to sound polite because Kim set her up with him.
