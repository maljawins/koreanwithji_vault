## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the concept of future tense in Korean, noting that while there are several specific forms, the regular present tense can often function as a future tense depending on the context. For example, the sentence **저는 해요** can mean "I do it" (general present), "I am doing it" (progressive), or "I will do it" (future). However, using dedicated future tense markers like **것이다** or **겠다** is essential for adding emphasis and clarifying that an action will specifically happen in the future rather than right now. The lesson focuses on the construction, conjugation, and nuanced differences between these two primary forms.

---

### The Primary Future Tense: (으)ㄹ 것이다

The most common way to express the future is by attaching **(으)ㄹ 것이다** to a verb stem. The instructor demonstrates the construction on screen: take the verb stem (the verb minus **다**) and attach **을** if it ends in a consonant, or just the letter **ㄹ** if it ends in a vowel. This is then followed by **것이다**. 

Several examples and exceptions are provided visually:
- **하다 (to do)** becomes **할 것이다** (vowel ending).
- **이다 (to be)** becomes **일 것이다** (vowel ending).
- **먹다 (to eat)** becomes **먹을 것이다** (consonant ending).
- **살다 (to live)** is an exception; because it already ends in **ㄹ**, you simply add **것이다** to get **살 것이다**.
- **춥다 (to be cold)** involves a **ㅂ** irregular change where **ㅂ** becomes **우**, resulting in **추울 것이다**.

The instructor explains the literal breakdown of this form: **것** means "thing" and **이다** is the verb "to be." In natural speech, **것** is almost always shortened to **거**. While **것** is more formal, **거** is perfectly polite for everyday conversation.

---

### Conjugating (으)ㄹ 것이다 for Speaking

To use this form in actual conversation, the final **이다** must be conjugated. The instructor lists several common endings:
- **입니다** style: **것입니다** or **겁니다**.
- **요** style: **거예요**. Note that while written as **예요**, it is often pronounced like **에요**.
- **반말** (casual): **것이야** or **거야**.

A critical pronunciation tip is highlighted: when using the shortened form **거야**, the **ㄹ** from the verb stem and the **거** combine to sound like a tense **꺼**. For example, **할 거야** is pronounced as if it were **할 꺼야**.

Example sentences shown:
> **오늘 뭐 할 거예요?** / What will you do today?
> **저는 공원에서 친구를 만날 거예요.** / I will meet my friend at the park.
> **내일 영화를 볼 거예요.** / I'll watch a movie tomorrow.

---

### The Formal Future Tense: 겠다

The second form taught is **겠다**. To form this, you simply attach **겠다** directly to the verb stem and then conjugate it like a regular verb. Common endings include **겠습니다** (formal), **겠습니까?** (formal question), **겠어요** (polite), and **겠어** (casual).

The instructor emphasizes that **겠다** is most naturally used as a future tense marker in highly formal settings. In non-formal contexts, it is less common as a pure future tense and instead takes on a different meaning.

Formal future examples:
> **제가 하겠습니다.** / I will do it.
> **내일 서울에 비가 오겠습니다.** / Tomorrow it will rain in Seoul.

---

### 겠다 as an Assumption ("Must be" / "Seems like")

When **겠다** is used in less formal speech (like the **요** form), its meaning usually shifts from "will" to making an assumption or expressing how something "looks" or "sounds." The instructor writes "Sounds...", "Looks...", "Seems...", and "Must be" on the board to categorize these assumptions.

Examples of assumptions:
- **서울은 겨울에 정말 춥겠어요.** / Seoul must be really cold in the winter. (Not "it will be cold," but an assumption based on knowledge).
- **맛있겠네요!** / That looks delicious! / That must be delicious!
- **정말 좋겠어요.** / That must be great. / That seems great.
