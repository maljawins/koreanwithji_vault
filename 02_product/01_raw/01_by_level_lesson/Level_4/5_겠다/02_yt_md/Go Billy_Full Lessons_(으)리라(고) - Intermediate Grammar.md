## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the intermediate-level grammar form (으)리라(고), which is often abbreviated as (으)리라. The instructor frames this form as a more poetic, emphatic, and formal version of the standard future tense. While it functions similarly to the modern future tense quoting form ~(을)거라고, it carries a distinct nuance of "trusting," "believing," or "assuming" that something will happen. The lesson covers the conjugation rules, the specific verbs it is commonly paired with, and how it differs from everyday future tense expressions.

---

### Conjugation and Basic Meaning

The conjugation of this form follows standard Korean verb stem rules. If a verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 의리라고; if it ends in a vowel, you attach 리라고. The instructor explains that this form is essentially a different version of the future tense used to show that the speaker thinks or believes something will be a certain way.

The core of the form, the (으)리 part, functions as a way of making an assumption or a guess. It is used to express the speaker's thoughts or to "quote" their own assumptions about a future state or action. In English, this can be translated as "I trust that," "I believe that," or "I think that."

---

### Usage with Quoting Verbs

Because (으)리라(고) expresses a belief or assumption, it is frequently used in conjunction with verbs that describe mental states or speech. Common pairings include:

- **믿다** (to believe)
- **생각하다** (to think)
- **마음 먹다** (to decide/make up one's mind)
- **말하다** (to say)

The instructor notes that using these verbs is optional. You can either include the verb (e.g., ~으리라 생각하다) or end the sentence directly with the (으)리라 form. When used at the end of a sentence without a following verb, it functions as a regular, albeit formal, future tense.

---

### Nuance: Poetic and Emphatic

The primary difference between (으)리라(고) and the standard future tense (~(을) 거예요 or ~(을) 것이다) is the "feeling" of the sentence. The instructor describes (으)리라(고) as sounding more poetic and emphatic. While the modern form simply states that something will happen, (으)리라(고) carries a weightier tone, similar to the English word "shall" (e.g., "This shall happen"). It is often found in literature, speeches, or when someone is making a firm internal resolution.

---

### Examples and Comparisons

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the form in context and demonstrates how to "translate" it into modern Korean for easier understanding.

In the first example, the speaker makes an assumption based on the weather:
> **비가 와서 공원에 사람들이 별로 없으리라 생각했어요.** / Because it rained, I thought there wouldn't be many people at the park.

To understand this more clearly, the instructor suggests replacing 없으리라 with the modern equivalent: **없을 거라고**. This transforms the sentence into a standard intermediate sentence: **비가 와서 공원에 사람들이 별로 없을 거라고 생각했어요.**

The second example shows the form used twice in one sentence, including a past-tense assumption:
> **철수가 모델로 성공하리라고는 아무도 예상하지 못했으리라.** / Nobody would have expected that Chul-soo would be successful as a model.

Here, **성공하리라고** represents the future-oriented assumption (that he would succeed), while **못했으리라** at the end of the sentence acts as a formal future-perfect assumption (nobody *would have* predicted).

The final example demonstrates a strong personal resolution:
> **올해는 꼭 합격하리라!** / I shall certainly pass (my classes/exam) this year!

In this context, the form is equivalent to saying **합격할 거예요** or **합격하겠습니다**, but with a much more determined and formal flair.
