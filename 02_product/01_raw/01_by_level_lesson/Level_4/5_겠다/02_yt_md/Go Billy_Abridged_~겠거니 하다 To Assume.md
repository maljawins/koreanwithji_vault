### The Logic of 거니 하다
To understand the full form, the instructor first explains its components. The ending `거니` is used when a speaker agrees with or confirms the preceding statement. The verb `하다` in this context does not mean "to do," but rather functions as a substitute for `생각하다 (to think)`. Therefore, the base construction `Verb Stem + 거니 하다` literally means "to think 'yes, [verb].'"

The instructor notes that `거니 하다` on its own is an older, very rare form that learners are unlikely to see. However, it serves as the foundation for the modern version. The speaker's agreement or confirmation is usually based on past experience or general knowledge, signaling a personal opinion that something is indeed the case.

---

### Constructing 겠거니 하다
The modern, common version of this grammar is created by inserting the suffix `겠다` before `거니`. While `겠다` is often taught as a future tense marker, its primary function here is to express a "guess" or "supposition" (e.g., `맛있겠다` meaning "That must be delicious"). By combining these elements, the form becomes `Verb Stem + 겠거니 하다`.

The literal meaning shifts to "I agree/confirm that it *would* or *must* be [verb]." More naturally, this translates to "I assume" or "I thought that..." The instructor highlights on the whiteboard that this form sounds a bit "fancy" or sophisticated, yet it remains "straightforward" because it simply conveys information without hidden nuances. It can also be used in the past tense as `~했겠거니 하다` to mean "assumed that [something] had happened."

---

### Example Sentences and Usage
The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate how the form functions in real sentences, often followed by the connector `하고` to show that the assumption led to a specific action or result.

> **오늘도 당연히 비가 오겠거니 하고 우산을 가지고 왔어요.** / I assumed it would rain today too, so I brought an umbrella.

In this case, the speaker made a guess (`비가 오겠다`) and confirmed that thought (`거니 하고`), which resulted in bringing the umbrella. Another example shows a failed assumption:

> **다시 말을 안 해도 기억하겠거니 했는데, 아니었어요.** / I thought you would remember even if I didn't say it again, but you didn't.

Further examples include assuming prices would rise (`비싸지겠거니 하고 그냥 샀는데...`) or assuming students understood a lesson (`이해했겠거니 하고 넘어갈게요`). A final humorous example involves a person returning empty-handed (`빈손으로 돌아오다`) after the speaker assumed they would at least buy pork shoulder (`목심이라도 사오겠거니 했는데`).

---

### Comparison: 겠거니 하다 vs 줄 알다
The instructor concludes by comparing `겠거니 하다` with the more common `~ㄴ/은/는 줄 알다`. Both are used in speech to express expectations, but they differ in emotional weight and outcome.

`줄 알다` implies a strong expectation. Depending on the result, it can mean "I knew it" (if correct) or "I thought..." (if wrong). Because it carries a heavy expectation, the speaker is often surprised by the outcome. In contrast, `겠거니 하다` is neutral. It simply states an assumption or a guess without implying that the speaker will be particularly surprised or invested in whether they are right or wrong. It is a more detached, matter-of-fact way to say "I assumed."
