## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces an older, somewhat archaic grammar form that remains in use today for specific rhetorical effects: `~(으)리라(고)`. This form is primarily used to express assumptions, guesses, or firm determinations about the future. While it functions similarly to modern endings like `~겠다` or `~(으)ㄹ 것이다`, it carries a "fancy" or formal nuance, often appearing in literature, songs, poetry, or highly stylized speech. The lesson covers the mechanical construction of the form, its relationship to historical Korean grammar, and its practical application in both quoting and non-quoting contexts.

---

### Construction and Historical Context

To form this expression, one takes a verb stem and attaches `~으리라고` if the stem ends in a consonant, or `~리라고` if it ends in a vowel. The instructor notes a specific exception for stems ending in the consonant `ㄹ`, such as `살다 (to live)`; in these cases, you simply attach `~리라고`. Visually, the instructor compares this to the future tense marker `~(으)ㄹ` found in modern forms like `할 거예요`. Historically, the future tense meaning originates from the archaic verb ending `~으리`, which is then combined with the standard quoting particle `~고`.

The instructor explains that `~(으)리라` is essentially the archaic equivalent of the modern `~겠다`. When used as a quoting form, it becomes `~(으)리라고`, which is equivalent to `~겠다고`. This allows the speaker to quote an assumption or a guess. The particle `고` is optional; omitting it to use `~(으)리라` within a sentence provides the same meaning but with slightly less emphasis on the quoting aspect. Using this form is described as "fancy," like "putting your pinky up while drinking," signaling a high level of sophistication or poetic intent rather than changing the fundamental meaning of the guess.

---

### Common Verbs Used with ~(으)리라고

Because this is a quoting form, it is almost always followed by verbs related to thinking, speaking, or internal determination. The instructor lists the most common verbs that pair with this ending:

- **생각하다** (to think): Thinking that something will likely happen.
- **믿다** (to believe/trust): Having faith that a future state will occur.
- **마음 먹다** / **결심하다** (to make up one's mind / to decide): Determining to do something.
- **말하다** (to say): Stating that something will be a certain way.
- **예상하다** (to predict/expect): Anticipating a future outcome.
- **다짐하다** (to pledge/promise): Making a firm commitment to a future action.

---

### Usage in Quoting Contexts

The instructor provides several example sentences to demonstrate how `~(으)리라고` functions as a sophisticated replacement for modern future-quoting forms.

> **혼자서도 잘 하리라 믿어요.** / I believe that you will do well even by yourself.

In this example, `하리라` replaces `하겠다고`. The sentence sounds more formal and encouraging. Another example focuses on determination:

> **다시는 전화하지 않으리라 마음먹었어요.** / I made up my mind not to call again.

Here, the speaker uses the negative form `~지 않으리라` to express a firm resolve. A similar sentiment is expressed regarding a specific object:

> **물 분필은 두 번 다시 쓰지 않으리라 결심했어요.** / I decided I won't use water chalk ever again.

The instructor also demonstrates how the form is used to express surprise or failed expectations, often using the topic marker `~는` for added emphasis:

> **다 먹을 수 있으리라고는 생각 못했어요.** / I never could have thought you would be able to eat all of that.
> **손님이 이렇게 많이 오리라고는 예상 못 했어요.** / I never would have expected that this many customers would come.

Finally, the form can be used in rhetorical questions to challenge someone's assumptions:

> **그렇게 하면 내가 모르리라 생각한 거야?** / Did you think I wouldn't know if you did that?

---

### Sentence-Final Usage: ~(으)리라

A secondary usage of this form occurs when it is used at the very end of a sentence without the quoting particle `고`. In this context, it functions as a fancy way of saying "will" or "would" when making a guess or assumption. The instructor emphasizes that this usage is quite rare in daily conversation and is mostly found in songs or poetry.

> **다섯 살 아이도 이 정도는 할 수 있으리라.** / Even a five-year-old child would be able to do this much.

This sentence-final `~리라` acts as a formal, almost prophetic declaration of a likely truth or capability.
