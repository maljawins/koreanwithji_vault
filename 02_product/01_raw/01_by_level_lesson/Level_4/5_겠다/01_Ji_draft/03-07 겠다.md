**겠다**

A few things to note about 겠다.

Now, 겠다 is treated like a verb of its own and it’s technically not a future tense.

The official name is “Presumptive” and it can only be used “like” a future tense the idea being you’re talking “about” the future in the present moment.

겠다 can be conjugated only in present tense. So it becomes 겠어(요) or 겠습니다.

겠다 has 4 main usages and the meaning depends on the subject of the sentence and context.

One, it can show willingness, as in “I WILL do something”, with a firm determination.

Two, to make a guess like “That must” or “I guess”

Three notifications, like on the news.

And four, questions: Either to confirm or to make a rhetorical question like Would you like to? Or Why would I?

**겠다 #01**

Let’s look at the first usage.

The keyword to remember here is “determination”. 그리고 내가 아닌 다른 사람의 “determination”을 우리는 알 수가 없기 때문에 the rule of three applies again, which is why when used with a 1st person subject, either “I” or “we”, it means “I or we WILL do something.”

And you have to use an action verb because you have to be willing to “do something” or “take action”

One thing to note is that strangely enough, the most common speech level used for this case is the formal speech.

If you wanted to use informal speech, you can always switch back to the promise form – ㄹ/을게요 we learned in the previous lesson. That’s not to say it’s incorrect to use 겠어(요), namely in informal speech. This sounds like you’re making a promise TO YOURSELF.

Let’s compare these two sentences

오늘부터 술을 끊을게요.

오늘부터 술을 끊겠어요.

**Both mean “From today, I’m going to quit alcohol.”**

첫번째 문장은 나는 사실 별로 술을 끊고 싶지 않지만 “그래도 네가 나를 걱정하니까 너를 위해 끊어줄게”와 같은 느낌이고 두번째 문장은 혼자 굳게 다짐을 하는 느낌이다.

However, since there can be no formal version of 할게요, 하겠습니다 is something you can say instead of 할게요.

**겠다 Example 01**

그 업무는 제가 처리하겠습니다.

예시 문장 보강 필요

**겠다 #02**

In the previous lesson, we learned that when the future tense “ㄹ/을 거예요” is used with a 3rd person subject, it means you’re making a guess.

Similarly, when you use 겠다 with a 3rd person subject, it also means you’re making a guess.

So, what is the difference? It shows different levels of certainty.

The keyword for ㄹ/을 거예요 is “Probably”.

예쁠 거예요 VS 예쁘겠어요

아래 내용 현재 레벨에 맞게 쉽게 풀어서 설명하기.

1. 예쁠 거예요 (객관적 전망)

* **의미:** (아마) 예쁠 것이다.
* **뉘앙스:** 화자가 가진 정보나 객관적인 근거(상황, 사진, 디자인 등)를 바탕으로, 미래에 그럴 것이라고 꽤 확실하게 예상할 때 사용합니다.
* **예시:** "이 옷을 입으면 **예쁠 거예요**." (옷을 보니 예쁠 확률이 매우 높음)

2. 예쁘겠어요 (주관적 감탄, 인상)

* **의미:** (보아하니) 예쁘겠구나.
* **뉘앙스:** 말하는 순간 느껴지는 주관적인 감탄이나 인상을 말합니다. 3인칭의 상태나 미래 행동을 추측할 때, 혹은 '보아하니 예쁘겠다'는 느낌을 전달할 때 씁니다.
* **예시:** "이 꽃을 받으면 정말 **예쁘겠어요**." (보자마자 예쁠 것이라고 감탄/예상함)

What about “YOU” then? The answer is: it depends on the type of the verb.

It doesn’t work with an action verb. “나”는 “너”에게 직접 말을 하고 있는데 “너”에게 너의 “action”에 대한 추측을 하는 것은 말이 되지 않는다. “너”가 뭘 할지 “내”가 궁금하면 “나”는 그냥 “너”에게 직접 물어보면 된다.

하지만 Descriptive verb는 사용이 가능하다. 너의 “상태”에 대한 나의 “추측된 의견”을 공유해 줄 수 있기 때문이다.

So, 피곤하시겠어요 could be talking about anyone else but me.

**겠다 Example 02**

Person A says 지난주에 강아지를 데려왔어요. I brought a puppy home last week.

데려오다 is a compound verb with “to bring” and “to come” combined. So, if you said 데려왔어요, it would mean you brought somebody back with you.

And to that, person B says, “정말 귀엽겠어요.” “it must be so cute.”

Another example.

Person A says 이 동네에 사람이 없어요, there’s nobody in this neighborhood.

And B responds “귀신 나오겠어요.” 귀신 means ghost and 나오다 means to show up.

B is saying this like “it looks like one COULD come out.”

**겠다 #03**

Now, 겠다 is also used to make formal notifications mostly by professional entities or organizations. So obviously, for this usage, it always ends in the formal speech - 겠습니다.

그리고 이 때 주어는 항상 3인칭인데 대부분의 경우 사람이 아니라 사물일 때가 많다.

**겠다 Example 03**

열차가 5분 후에 서울역에 도착하겠습니다 – The train will arrive to Seoul Station in 5 minutes

내일은 오후 세 시에 비가 오겠습니다 – Tomorrow, it will rain at 3 pm.

By the way, “it will rain” in Korean is “비가 오겠습니다” which literally translates to “The rain will come.” because in Korean the concept of “it” doesn’t exist.

**겠다 #04 Question - 01**

Now, let’s learn how to use 겠다 in questions. There are 2 usages with 겠다 questions.

First, to politely confirm or make sure. And it sounds extra polite and professional. So most of the time, you would be using an honorific verb.

[Show 정말 - Really / 괜찮다 – To be okay]

정말 괜찮으시겠어요? – “Are you really going to be okay?”

You can take it to the next level by conjugating into 습니까 form. Now, this sentence is super formal and professional.

상금을 받으시겠습니까? – Would you like to receive the prize money?

This is an actual line from Squid Game.

**겠다 #04 Question - 02**

And it can also be used make a rhetorical question and in this sense, it KIND OF works like “would” in English.

Example 1 보강

“그걸 내가 왜 하겠어?” – Why would I do that?

Another example.

A says 진호가 전화를 안 받아 – Jinho’s not answering his phone.

And B says 걔 새벽 3시에 잤어. He slept at 3 in the morning

By the way, 새벽 means morning before sunrise.

And says 전화를 받겠어? – Would he answer the call?, which implies “Of course, he’s not going to answer”

**겠다 Special case**

Now, there’s one more use with 겠다. This is like a special occasion that applies to these two verbs 알다 and 모르다 which mean to know and not know respectively.

There are things you know for sure but other things you have to think about whether you know or not right?

So, for things that you know you know or don’t know, you don’t add 겠.

But for things you think you know you would use 겠.

**알아요 vs 알겠어요**

Let me show you what I mean.

Let’s first look at 알다 to know.

이 가수를 알아요? Do you know this singer? – This is a simple question. You either do or don’t right?

기타를 칠 줄 알아요? Do you know how to play the guitar – Again, a simple question.

So, you’d answer with 알아요 without the 겠

But 알겠어 is used for things you think you know, so naturally, it’s often associated with new information or concept because you’d have to think about whether you know or not.

And thinking about your answer before replying is generally more respectful right? Because it means you’re being thoughtful. And because of this, it’s also used to reply to commands or requests or to simply agree politely.

Example

[알아요]와 [알겠어요]의 차이를 알겠어요? Do you know the difference of 알아요 and 알겠어요?

And you would answer 네, 알겠어요

Now, 알겠어(요) and 알겠습니다 are also used for answering a request, like “Okay” or “I got it”

이제부터 늦지 마 – Don’t be late from now on.

알겠습니다 – Okay / I got it / Yes.

It’s just a respectful way to respond although he doesn’t really have to think about whether he knows not to be late anymore

**몰라요 vs 모르겠어요**

Now, let’s look at 모르다 vs 모르겠어요, to not know.

In principle, 몰라요 is used for things you’re sure you don’t know. And 모르겠어요 is used for things you think you don’t know like this.

A asks 그 사람이 이 사실을 알아요? – Does she/he know about this?

Now if B answers 몰라요, it would indicate [she doesn’t know]

But if B answers 모르겠어요, it would indicate [I don’t know if she knows]

However, thinking or at least pretending to think before answering is a more respectful thing to do. Which is why 모르겠어요 is much more commonly used than 몰라요 even for things you’re sure you don’t know because sometimes, a straight up 몰라요 can sound a bit too blunt and come off rude.

For example, [아, 몰라] is a very common phrase which is equivalent to “Screw it” or “F it” in English.

So, as general rule of thumb, always stick with 모르겠어요 when in doubt.

Let’s look at this example.

A asks B 혹시 제 딸 보셨어요? – By any chance, have you seen my daughter?

And B goes 아뇨, 모르겠어요 – No, I don’t know (where she is)

Here, B is sure he hasn’t seen her, but he still uses 모르겠어요 instead of 몰라요.

Even in English, there are times when it’s better to say “I’m not sure” as opposed to a directly “I don’t know”. It’s pretty much the same idea.

Next example.

Now, Sarah arranged a blind date for Kim and wants to know if she liked the guy.

And asks

어땠어? – How was it? 마음에 들었어? – Did you like him?

마음에 들다 is an idiom which literally means to enter heart but it’s a more casual way to say to like something or someone.

And Kim didn’t like the guy so much but she doesn’t want to be so direct. So she goes, 잘 모르겠어요 to sound polite.
