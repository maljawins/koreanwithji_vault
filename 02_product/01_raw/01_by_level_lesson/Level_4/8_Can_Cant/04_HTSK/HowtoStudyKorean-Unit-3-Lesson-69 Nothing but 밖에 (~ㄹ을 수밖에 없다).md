Lesson 69: Nothing but: 밖에 (~ㄹ/을 수밖에 없다)

Introduction

In the previous lesson, you learned how to use the word “유일하다,” which has the meaning of “only” or “unique/sole.” In this lesson, you will learn another grammatical principle that can be translated to “only.” In this lesson, you will learn how to use the particle ~밖에. Let’s get started.

Nothing but/only: ~밖에

In [Lesson 26](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-26/), you learned the word “밖,” which has the meaning “outside.” It is often used as a place in a sentence to refer to the outdoors. For example:

Play[나는 9시에 밖에 나갈 거야](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s1.mp3) = I’m going outside at 9 o’clock
Play[혹시 밖에 비가 와요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s2.mp3) = Is it raining outside?
Play[북을 밖에서 치면 안 돼요?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s3.mp3) = Can you (please) play the drum outside?
Play[배추를 밖에 다 놓았어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s4.mp3) = I put all the (Korean) cabbage outside

It can also be used to describe that something is “outside” of a specific area – much like how “안” can refer to the inside of an area. For example:

Play[우리는 집 안에 있어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s5.mp3) = We are inside the house
Play[우리는 집 밖에 있어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s6.mp3) = We are outside the house
Play[답안지에 답을 쓰면 그 문제에 해당되는 박스 밖에 아무 글도 쓰지 마세요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s7.mp3) = When you write your answer on the answer sheet, don’t write any (writing) outside of the applicable box for that question

It is also possible to attach ~밖에 to nouns as a particle. This new construction may translate to something different, but the meaning when used as a particle like this is actually quite similar to the meaning when used as a noun.

By attaching the particle ~밖에 to a noun, the speaker indicates that *only* that noun – and nothing but that noun – is acted on. The common translation for “~밖에” is “nothing but.” For example:

친구밖에… = nothing but friend(s)
밥밖에… = nothing but rice
청소년들밖에… = nothing but young people (youth)
만 원밖에… = nothing but 10,000 won

This meaning is similar to the particle ~만, which simply means “only.” For example:

친구만 = only friend(s)
밥만 = only rice
청소년들만… = only young people (youth)
만 원만 = only 10,000 won

However, when ~밖에is used, the speaker is emphasizing that “nothing but” a particular noun is acted on.

When ~밖에 is attached to a noun to have this meaning, the final verb should be a negative word. It is common for these types of sentences to end in 없다 to indicate that one has “nothing but” something. For example:

Play[저는 친구밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s8.mp3)
= I have nothing but friends

Play[우리가 먹을 것은 밥밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s9.mp3)
= We have nothing but rice to eat

Play[유행을 따라가는 사람들이 청소년들밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s10.mp3)
= The only people who follow trends are young people

Play[그거를 사고 싶지만 만 원밖에 없어서 못 사요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s11.mp3)
= I want to buy that, but I only have 10,000 won, so I can’t buy it

You can see the similarity with the meaning of “outside” if you translate those sentences slightly differently. For example:

Play[저는 친구밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s8.mp3)
= Outside of friends, I have nothing

Play[우리가 먹을 것은 밥밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s9.mp3)
= Outside of rice, we have nothing to eat

Play[유행을 따라가는 사람들이 청소년들밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s10.mp3)
= Outside of young people, nobody follows trends

그거를 사고 싶지만 만원밖에 없어서 못 사요
= I want to buy that, but outside of 10,000 won I have nothing, so I can’t buy it

~밖에 is also commonly attached to counters or to the word “하나.” For example:

Play[저는 친구가 세 명밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s12.mp3) = I have nothing but three friends
Play[저는 신용카드가 하나밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s13.mp3) = I have nothing but one credit card
Play[저는 핸드폰이 한 개밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s14.mp3) = I only have nothing but one cell-phone

The examples above all show sentences ending in 없다, but it is possible to end a sentence with other verbs. Even though the (noun)밖에 construction translates to “nothing but (noun),” the sentence should end with a negative conjugation. This often makes it look like there is a double negative in the sentence for English speakers. For example:

Play[코치가 우리 팀에게 충고를 하나밖에 안 했었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s15.mp3)
= The coach gave us nothing but one piece of advice

*Notice how this would be awkward in English if it translated to “The coach didn’t give us nothing but one piece of advice.”*

Below are many more examples:

Play[남자 친구가 너한테 돈밖에 안 줬다고?](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s16.mp3)
= (You say that…) Your boyfriend gave you nothing but money?

*Also, notice that this sentence is an indirect quote. You learned in*[*Lesson 52*](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-3-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-3-lessons-51-58/lesson-52/)*that it is possible to omit the word*하다*or*말하다*after these types of sentences. It is common to finish a sentence like this when somebody says something, and you can’t believe (or hear) what they say. Before the sentence above was uttered, another person would probably have said*“나의 남자친구가 나한테 돈밖에 안 줬어”

Play[아빠가 배추를 하나밖에 주문하지 않았어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s17.mp3) = Dad ordered nothing but one (head of) cabbage

*Also, you might be thinking that this is probably a sentence that would never be uttered. However, Korean people make kimchi every year (out of*배추*) and store it in their houses all winter. This means that they have to make a ton of kimchi and order a ton of*배추*. Ordering 100 heads of it isn’t uncommon.*

*In all of the examples so far, I tried to translate ~*밖에 *to “nothing but.” I usually find those sentences unnatural in English. I find it much natural to simply use “only.” As always, pay more attention to the Korean sentence than the English translation.*

Play[제가 아는 공연 종류는 연극밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s18.mp3)
= The only type of performance I know about is plays *((Of) the types of performances I know about, I know about nothing but plays)*

Play[선생님! 저에게 답안지밖에 안 주셨어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s19.mp3)
= Teacher! You only gave me an answer sheet *(Teacher, you gave me nothing but an answer sheet)*

Play[그 선생님의 수업을 한번밖에 참관하지 않았어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s20.mp3)
= I only attended that teacher’s class once *(Outside of one time, I didn’t attend that teacher’s class)*

Play[저는 평생 동안 오렌지를 세 번밖에 안 먹어 봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s21.mp3)
= I have only eaten oranges three times in my life
*(I have eaten oranges nothing but three times in my life)*

Play[그 사람이 지금까지 영화를 하나밖에 연출을 안 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s22.mp3)
= That person has only directed one movie until now *(Outside of one movie, that person hasn’t directed a movie until now)*

~밖에 is also commonly used to emphasize that an action will take a short amount of time. For example:

Play[30 분밖에 안 걸려요!](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s23.mp3) = It only takes 30 minutes (It takes nothing but 30 minutes)
Play[한 시간밖에 안 걸려요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s24.mp3) = It only takes 1 hour (It takes nothing but 1 hour)

In order to create more complicated sentences that describe “It only takes 30 minutes *to get from Seoul to Incheon,*” you need to learn how to use ~는 데, which will be introduced in [Lesson 76](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/upper-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-4-lessons-76-83/lesson-76/) and [Lesson 77](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/upper-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-4-lessons-76-83/lesson-77/).

Before you move on, let’s look at another practical usage of ~밖에.

There is nothing I can do but…: ~ㄹ/을 수밖에 없다

In [Lesson 45](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-42-50/lesson-45/) you learned about the endings ~ㄹ/을 수 있다 and ~ㄹ/을 수 없다. In that lesson, I explained that “수” is a noun. This means that particles, including ~밖에, can be attached to 수. By attaching ~밖에 to 수 in “~할 수 없다” you describe that the best/only option is to do the specified action. For example:

Play[우리가 밥을 먹을 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s25.mp3) = We have nothing to do but eat

This commonly translates to “to have no choice but to…” For example

Play[우리가 밥을 먹을 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s25.mp3) = We have no choice but to eat

Below are many more examples:

Play[우리가 연극을 다른 데에서 할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s26.mp3)
= We have no choice but to do the play in a different place

Play[죄송하지만 저는 당신을 해고할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s27.mp3)
= I’m sorry, but I can’t do anything but fire you

Play[쓰레기가 많아서 방을 싹 청소할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s28.mp3)
= I have no choice but to completely clean the room because it is so dirty

Play[일이 너무 심심해서 다른 분야로 옮길 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s29.mp3)
= I have no choice but to move/switch fields because my work is so boring

Play[시험을 네 번 연속으로 못 봐서 포기할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Lesson69s30.mp3)
= I have no choice but to give up because I didn’t write the exam well four times in a row

Play[내일도 비가 올 거라서 연극을 또 취소할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s31.mp3)
= It will rain again tomorrow, so we have no choice but to cancel the play

Play[친구가 늦게 올 거라고 해서 우리는 기다릴 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s32.mp3)
= Our friend said he will come late, so we have nothing to do but wait for him

Play[내일 해변에 갈 거라서 겨드랑이 털을 면도할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s33.mp3)
= I am going to the beach tomorrow, so I have no choice but to shave my armpits

Play[오늘도 수업을 못 해서 내일부터 3일 연속으로 할 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s34.mp3)
= We couldn’t do class again today, so we have no choice but to do class for three days in a row starting from tomorrow

Play[오랫동안 운동해서 에너지를 다 소모해서 밥을 많이 먹을 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s35.mp3)
= I exercised for a long time and used up all of my energy, so I have no choice but to eat a lot of food

Play[그 커플이 매일 싸워서 사람들이 그 커플의 이혼사유를 알 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s36.mp3)
= That couple fights every day, so there is no way for people to not know the reason why they got a divorce

Play[소모품이 사무실에 아직 오지 않아서 올 때까지 기다릴 수밖에 없어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Lesson69n.mp3)
= The supplies still haven’t arrived/come to the office yet, so we have no choice but to wait until they come

Play[그 문제를 예방할 수 없어서 또 문제가 생기면 그때 해결할 수밖에 없을 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson69s38.mp3)
= We can’t prevent that problem, so if it comes up again, we have no choice but to deal with it/fix it then
