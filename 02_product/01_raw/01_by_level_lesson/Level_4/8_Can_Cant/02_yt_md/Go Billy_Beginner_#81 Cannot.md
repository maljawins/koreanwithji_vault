## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces alternative ways to express inability in Korean, moving beyond the previously taught forms of 수 있다 (can) and 수 없다 (cannot). The primary focus is on the word 못 and the verb ending 지 못하다. While the 수 없다 form often implies a lack of ability or the absence of a way for something to happen, 못 and 지 못하다 are more general expressions of "cannot" that are frequently used in daily conversation.

The lesson follows a structured progression: first, the short-form negation using 못; second, the long-form negation using 지 못하다; and finally, a comparison between these forms and the 수 없다 construction. The instructor also provides essential vocabulary notes regarding verbs for playing instruments and the distinction between bringing people versus objects.

---

### The Short Form: 못 + Action Verb
The most common way to say "cannot" is by placing the word 못 before an action verb, separated by a space. This functions similarly to the negative marker 안. For example, the sentence "I can go too" changes from 저도 갈 수 있어요 to 저도 못 가요. Similarly, "I can't believe it" becomes 저는 못 믿어요.

The instructor notes that 못 is used with the same types of action verbs as 안. It is generally not used with descriptive verbs (adjectives). A critical pronunciation rule is highlighted for the combination of 못 and 하다. Due to sound change rules, the final ㅅ in 못 is pronounced as a ㄷ sound, which then combines with the ㅎ in 하다 to create a aspirated ㅌ sound, resulting in the pronunciation 못 했어요 (mo-thaess-eo-yo).

---

### Specific Vocabulary: Playing Instruments
When using 못 with instruments, the instructor clarifies that Korean uses different verbs for "to play" depending on the physical action involved. The verb 치다 (to hit) is used for instruments like the guitar, piano, and drums. The verb 켜다 (to bow) is used for string instruments played with a bow, such as the violin. Finally, 불다 (to blow) is used for all wind instruments. An example sentence provided is 저는 기타를 못 쳐요 (I can't play the guitar).

---

### The Long Form: Verb Stem + 지 못하다
Similar to how 지 않다 is the long-form version of 안, the ending 지 못하다 is the long-form version of 못. To create this form, take the verb stem and attach 지 못하다, then conjugate the final 하다. In the present tense, this becomes 지 못해요.

Examples shown include:
- **저도 가지 못해요.** / I can't go too.
- **저는 이해하지 못해요.** / I can't understand.

The instructor explains that if a verb can use the short form 못, it can almost always use the long form 지 못하다 as well. For instance, 저는 매달 못 가요 and 저는 매달 가지 못해요 both mean "I can't go every month" and are interchangeable.

---

### Comparing 못, 지 못하다, and 수 없다
The instructor explains the subtle nuance between these three forms. The 수 in 수 없다 literally means "ability" or "way." Therefore, using 수 없다 can specifically imply that one lacks the physical or mental ability to do something, or that there is simply no possible way for the action to occur. In contrast, 못 and 지 못하다 do not carry this literal "ability/way" meaning; they are just general negations of "can." However, the instructor emphasizes that this difference is very small and learners can feel free to use any of the three forms to mean "cannot."

---

### Bringing People vs. Objects
During the conversation practice, a distinction is made between taking/bringing objects and people. For objects, one uses 가지다 (to have/carry) combined with 가다 (to go) or 오다 (to come), resulting in 가지고 가다 (to take) or 가지고 오다 (to bring). These are often shortened to 가져가다 and 가져오다.

For people or animals, the verb 데리다 is used instead of 가지다. This results in 데리고 가다 (to take a person) and 데리고 오다 (to bring a person). These can also be shortened to 데려가다 and 데려오다.
