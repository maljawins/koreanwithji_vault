### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammatical structures used to express ability and inability in Korean, equivalent to the English words "can" and "can't." ---

### Expressing Ability: Verb Stem + 을/ㄹ 수 있다

To say that you "can" do something, you must follow a three-step conjugation process. First, identify the verb stem. Second, attach a connecting suffix based on the final sound of that stem: attach **을** if the stem ends in a consonant, or attach **ㄹ** as a bottom consonant if the stem ends in a vowel. The instructor notes that while this looks like the object marker, it functions differently here. Finally, add **수 있다** and conjugate the verb **있다** into the desired politeness level.

The instructor demonstrates this with two verbs. For **먹다 (to eat)**, the stem is **먹**; since it ends in a consonant, you add **을**, resulting in **먹을 수 있다**, which conjugates to **먹을 수 있어요 (I can eat)**. For **배우다 (to learn)**, the stem is **배우**; since it ends in a vowel, you attach **ㄹ** to the bottom, resulting in **배울 수 있다**, which conjugates to **배울 수 있어요 (I can learn)**.

Further examples provided on screen include:
> **저는 요리할 수 있어요.** / I can cook.
> **빌리는 한국에 갈 수 있어요.** / Billy can go to Korea.
> **저는 한국어를 잘 할 수 있어요.** / I can speak Korean well.

---

### Expressing Inability: Verb Stem + 을/ㄹ 수 없다

To say that you "can't" do something, the process is nearly identical to the affirmative form, but you replace the verb **있다 (to exist)** with its opposite, **없다 (to not exist)**. The steps are: get the verb stem, attach **을** (for consonants) or **ㄹ** (for vowels), and finally add **수 없다**.

Using the previous verbs as examples, **먹다** becomes **먹을 수 없어요 (I can't eat)**, and **배우다** becomes **배울 수 없어요 (I can't learn)**. The instructor mentions that **없다** will be covered more deeply in future lessons regarding general negative sentences, but for now, it is the standard way to express "can't."

Example sentences for inability include:
> **빌리는 노래를 잘 할 수 없어요.** / Billy can't sing well.
> **저는 도서관에 갈 수 없어요.** / I can't go to the library.
> **저는 숙제를 빨리 할 수 없어요.** / I can't do the homework quickly.

### Linguistic Notes and Irregulars

The instructor explains the literal meaning behind these forms. The word **수** literally means "ability" or "way/method." Therefore, saying **수 있다** literally means "the ability exists," and **수 없다** means "the ability does not exist."

A note is made regarding the use of particles. While not required, particles like **도 (also)** or **만 (only)** can be attached to **수** for emphasis. For example, **저는 피자를 만들 수도 있어요** means "I can *also* make pizza," implying the speaker has other abilities as well.

Finally, the instructor addresses verb stems ending in **ㄹ**. If a verb stem already ends with the consonant **ㄹ**, such as **만들다 (to make)**, you do not add another **을** or **ㄹ**. You simply attach **수 있다** or **수 없다** directly to the stem.
- **만들다** → **만들 수 있어요** (I can make it)
- **만들다** → **만들 수 없어요** (I can't make it)
