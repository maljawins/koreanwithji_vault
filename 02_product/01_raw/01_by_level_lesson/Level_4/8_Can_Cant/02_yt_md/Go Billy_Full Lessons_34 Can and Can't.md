### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammatical structures used to express capability and inability in Korean. The lesson focuses on the most common way to say "can" and "can't" by using the noun 수 (way/method/possibility) combined with the verbs 있다 (to exist) and 없다 (to not exist). This form specifically addresses whether one is capable or not capable of performing a certain action.

The video follows a structured progression: first, the conjugation rules for the primary "can/can't" form are explained; second, several example sentences are provided to show the form in context; and finally, a shorter, alternative way to express "can't" using 못하다 is introduced. ### Expressing Capability with ~(으)ㄹ 수 있다/없다
To say "can" or "can't" in Korean, you must attach a specific ending to the verb stem. The instructor explains the conjugation rules based on the final sound of the verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you add 을 수; if the verb stem ends in a vowel, you simply attach the bottom consonant ㄹ followed by 수. 

After this modifier is attached, you add the verb 있다 (to exist/have) to express that you "can" do the action, or 없다 (to not exist/not have) to express that you "can't." This literally translates to "the possibility/way exists" or "the possibility/way does not exist."

The instructor provides basic examples to illustrate this transformation:
- **할 수 있어요.** / I can do it. (From 하다)
- **할 수 없어요.** / I can't do it. (From 하다)
- **먹을 수 있어요.** / I can eat it. (From 먹다)
- **먹을 수 없어요.** / I can't eat it. (From 먹다)

### Example Sentences in Context
The lesson provides three full sentences to demonstrate how these forms function in natural speech. The first example is a question about capability regarding a future plan:
> **내일 식당에 갈 수 있어요?** / Can you go to the restaurant tomorrow?

The second example shows the "can't" form used within a conditional clause:
> **이해할 수 없으면 물어보세요.** / Ask me if you can't understand.

The third example demonstrates the form used in reported speech:
> **철수는 할 수 있다고 말했어요.** / Chul-soo said that he can do it.

---

### Alternative Way to Say "Can't": ~지 못하다 and 못하다
The instructor introduces a shorter and simpler way to express inability. One method is to take the verb stem and add 지 못하다. However, a more common and even simpler method exists for verbs ending in 하다. For these verbs, you simply replace 하다 with 못하다.

Examples of this shorter form include:
- **못해요.** / I can't do it.
- **저는 영어를 못해요.** / I can't speak English.

The instructor also provides an example using the ~지 못하다 ending with a non-하다 verb:
> **저는 우유를 마시지 못합니다.** / I can't drink milk.

---

### Practical Application: The Phone Call
The video concludes with a roleplay of a phone call to a restaurant. The speaker uses the "can" form to ask for a favor:
> **여보세요? 네, 한 10분 전에 주문을 했는데 혹시 취소할 수 있을까요?** / Hello? Yes, I placed an order about 10 minutes ago, but would it be possible to cancel it?

This demonstrates that the ~(으)ㄹ 수 있다 form is not just for physical capability, but also for asking about the possibility or permissibility of an action in a polite context.
