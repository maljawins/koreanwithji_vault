### Expressing Past Beliefs with 줄 알았다
The instructor analyzes the lyric `난 내가 스무 살 때 천재인 줄 알았어` to explain how to express a past thought or belief, often one that turned out to be incorrect. The structure `Noun + 인 줄 알았다` translates to "I thought it was [Noun]." This expression frequently implies that the speaker no longer holds that belief. For example, `난 네가 내 친구인 줄 알았어` means "I thought you were my friend," implying a change in the relationship or a realization.

A nuance of this expression is that it can occasionally mean "I knew it was [Noun]" depending on the tone. If the speaker says `난 네가 선생님인 줄 알았어` with a specific intonation, it could mean they already knew the person was a teacher. However, the "misunderstanding" meaning is much more common. The instructor also notes the use of double subjects in these sentences: the main subject `난 (나는)` uses the topic marker, while the nested subject `내가` uses the identifier particle.

---

### Quoting Thoughts and Beliefs with -다고 믿다/생각하다
To express a belief in a full statement, the instructor introduces the pattern `[Sentence ending in -다] + 고 + 믿다/생각하다`. In the lyric `어릴 때 모든 것이 다 간단하다고 믿었어`, the base sentence is `모든 것이 다 간단하다 (Everything is simple)`. By adding `고 믿었어`, it becomes "I believed that everything was simple."

The instructor provides further examples to show the versatility of this quoting structure:
- `나는 한국어가 재미있다고 생각해.` / I think that Korean is interesting.
- `제프는 네가 예쁘다고 생각해.` / Jeff thinks that you are pretty.

The instructor also points out the abbreviation `모든 게` for `모든 것이`, noting that both are acceptable in speech and lyrics.

---

### Describing Similarities: 처럼 vs 같은
The lesson distinguishes between two ways to say "like [Noun]": `처럼` and `같은`. While both indicate similarity, they function as different parts of speech. `처럼` is an adverbial particle that modifies verbs or entire sentences, whereas `같은` (from `같다`) is an adjective that must modify a noun.

The instructor uses these examples to clarify:
- `꽃이 너처럼 아름답다.` / The flower is beautiful like you. (Modifies the adjective "beautiful")
- `너 같은 꽃을 봤어.` / I saw a flower like you. (Modifies the noun "flower")

In the lyric `딸기향 해열제 같은 해결책`, `같은` is used because it modifies the noun `해결책 (solution)`.

---

### Conditionals and Existence: -면 and 필요하다
The instructor explains the conditional structure `-면`, which means "if" or "when." In the line `휴대폰을 열면`, the verb `열다 (to open)` combines with `면` to mean "If/When I open the phone." This is followed by `이상한 광고 메시지가 있어`, meaning "there is a weird ad message."

Regarding the verb `필요하다 (to need)`, the instructor emphasizes that the object being needed takes the subject marker `이/가`, not the object marker `을/를`. This is a common point of confusion for learners. Therefore, "I need a solution" is correctly phrased as `해결책이 필요해`.

---

### Vocabulary Spotlight: 뻔하다 and 모습
The word `뻔하다` is highlighted as a common term in K-pop lyrics (citing BTS and Blackpink). it describes something that is "obvious," "predictable," or "cliché," usually with a negative connotation. `뻔한 말` refers to cliché speech or predictable words.

The noun `모습` is described as a versatile word that is difficult to translate directly into English. It can refer to one's physical appearance, personality, attitude, or the general "way" someone is. In the lyrics, `용감한 내 모습` refers to the speaker's "brave self" or "brave appearance."

---

### Expressing Desire with -고 싶어
The final teaching point covers the standard "I want to" construction: `Verb Stem + 고 싶어`. The instructor uses the lyric `용감한 내 모습을 찾고 싶어 (I want to find my brave self)` to demonstrate. This pattern is applied to several daily activities:
- `한국어를 공부하고 싶어.` / I want to study Korean.
- `아침을 먹고 싶어.` / I want to eat breakfast.
- `영어를 배우고 싶어.` / I want to learn English.
