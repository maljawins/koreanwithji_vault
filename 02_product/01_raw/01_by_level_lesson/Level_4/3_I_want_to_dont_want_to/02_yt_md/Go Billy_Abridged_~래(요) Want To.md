## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form ~(으)ㄹ래(요), which is primarily used to express a speaker's desire or intention to do something. While it is often translated as "want to," it carries a stronger nuance of "will" or "intend to" compared to the standard ~고 싶다 form. The lesson covers the conjugation rules for action verbs, including common exceptions, and emphasizes the social nuances of the form. Specifically, the instructor highlights that this form is restricted to first-person statements and second-person questions, and it possesses an informal, conversational tone that makes it unsuitable for formal writing.

---

### Conjugation Rules

To create this form, you start with an action verb stem by removing the 다 ending. The attachment depends on the final letter of the stem. If the verb stem ends in a vowel or the consonant ㄹ, you simply attach ㄹ래요. For example, the stem of 하다 (to do) is 하, which ends in a vowel, so it becomes 할래요. If the stem ends in a consonant other than ㄹ, you attach 을래요. For example, the stem of 먹다 (to eat) is 먹, so it becomes 먹을래요.

The instructor notes that standard verb exceptions still apply. For instance, with the verb 눕다 (to lie down), the ㅂ irregular rule dictates that the ㅂ is removed and replaced with ㅜ. This results in the stem 누우, which ends in a vowel, leading to the final conjugated form 누울래요.

A critical distinction is made between this form and similar-looking quoting forms. The instructor warns learners not to confuse ~(으)ㄹ래(요) with forms like ~으래요 (attached to verb stems) or ~이래요 (attached to nouns), which are used for indirect speech. The "want to" form discussed in this lesson always requires that extra ㄹ at the bottom of the stem or within the 을 syllable.

---

### Usage and Social Nuance

This form is strictly used in two types of sentences: statements and questions. It has a distinctly casual and informal feel, even when the polite ending 요 is attached. Because of this conversational quality, it should never be used in formal writing, such as essays. 

When asking a question to someone who requires a higher level of politeness, the instructor recommends combining the form with the honorific infix ~(으)시. For example, instead of asking a superior 할래요?, it is better to ask 하실래요? If the situation requires even more formality or politeness, the instructor suggests avoiding this form entirely and using ~(으)시겠어요? instead. Examples provided include changing 가다 (to go) to 가시겠어요? and using the honorific version of 먹다, which is 드시다, to form 드시겠어요?

---

### ~(으)ㄹ래(요) vs. ~고 싶다

The instructor explains the nuance difference between these two "want to" forms. The ~고 싶다 form expresses a pure desire or wish; the speaker wants to do something but may not have the means or a concrete plan to actually do it. In contrast, ~(으)ㄹ래(요) expresses both desire and intention. If you say 갈래요, you are not just saying you wish to go, but that you actually intend to go. It functions as a combination of "I want to" and "I am going to."

---

### The Third Person Restriction

A major rule for ~(으)ㄹ래(요) is that it cannot be used for third-person subjects (he, she, they). In Korean culture, one generally does not assume or state the internal intentions or desires of another person as a fact. Therefore, the form is limited to:
- **First person (Statements):** "I want/intend to..."
- **Second person (Questions):** "Do you want/intend to...?"

To talk about what a third party wants to do, a different grammar form (such as ~고 싶어 하다) must be used.

---

### Example Sentences

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate the form in context, emphasizing the "intention" nuance:

> **오늘은 그냥 걸어갈래요.** / Today I just want to walk (and I'm going to walk).
> **나는 파스타 안 먹을래요.** / I don't want to eat pasta (and I'm not going to eat it).
> **김치 더 드실래요?** / Would you like to have some more kimchi? (Friendly but polite).
> **다른 셔츠 입을래요.** / I'm going to wear a different shirt.
> **이따가 할래요.** / I want to do it a little later (I intend to do it later).
> **오늘은 쉴래요.** / Today I just want to rest.
> **하나 가지래요?** / Do you want to have one? / Do you want to take one?
> **같이 볼래요?** / Do you want to watch it together?
> **같이 점심 먹으러 갈래요?** / Do you want to go eat lunch together?
