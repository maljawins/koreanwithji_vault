## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson introduces the grammar necessary to express desire in Korean. The instructor focuses on two primary ways to say "I want": first, expressing the desire to perform an action (wanting to do something), and second, expressing the desire to possess an object (wanting to have something). The core of the lesson revolves around the grammar form 고 싶다, which is the first true verb-attaching grammar pattern introduced in this course.

The learning arc begins with the concept of the verb stem and how to correctly identify it from an unconjugated verb. From there, the instructor demonstrates how to combine this stem with 고 싶다 and subsequently conjugate the final verb into a polite sentence. The lesson then transitions into the specific construction used for wanting objects, clarifies the difference between "wanting to do" and "wanting to have," and concludes with important usage notes regarding speech levels, contractions, and person-specific restrictions.

---

### Expressing "Want to Do": The 고 싶다 Form

To express that you want to perform an action, you must use the grammar pattern: **Verb Stem + 고 싶다**. The instructor emphasizes that this is a two-step process. First, you must identify the verb stem. In Korean, all dictionary-form verbs end in 다. To find the verb stem, you simply remove the 다. For example, the verb 가다 (to go) has the stem 가; 먹다 (to eat) has the stem 먹; and 보다 (to see/watch) has the stem 보.

A critical rule highlighted on screen is that you must always start with the original, unconjugated verb to find the stem. The instructor warns against trying to extract a stem from a verb that has already been conjugated. For instance, starting with 좋아해요 (like) and assuming the stem is 좋아해 is incorrect and will lead to grammatical errors. You must start with the dictionary form 좋아하다 to get the correct stem.

Once you have the stem, you attach 고 and then the verb 십다. However, 십다 itself is a verb and must be conjugated to be used in a real sentence. A common polite conjugation for 십다 is 싶어요. Therefore, the final forms for the previous examples become:
- **가고 싶어요** / I want to go.
- **먹고 싶어요** / I want to eat.
- **보고 싶어요** / I want to see/watch.

The instructor provides a visual conversation practice to illustrate this:
> **저는 티비를 보고 싶어요.** / I want to watch TV.
> **저는 나가고 싶어요.** / I want to go out.

In the first sentence, 티비 (TV) is marked with the object marker 를 because it is the object of the action being watched.

---

### Expressing "Want an Object": The 가지고 싶다 Form

When you want a physical object rather than wanting to perform an action, Korean uses a slightly different construction. Instead of a direct "want" verb for objects, you use the verb 가지다, which means "to have" or "to carry with you." By combining this with the "want to" form, you get **가지고 싶다**, which literally translates to "want to have."

The sentence structure for this is: **Noun + Object Marker (을/를) + 가지고 싶다**. The instructor reminds viewers that the object marker is 을 after a consonant and 를 after a vowel. This form is distinct from the action-based "want to do" form and should be kept separate in usage.

A conversation practice between characters Jimin and Mangi demonstrates the difference:
> **저는 영화를 보고 싶어요.** / I want to see a movie. (Action)
> **네, 저는 팝콘을 먹고 싶어요.** / Yes, I want to eat popcorn. (Action)
> **저는 팝콘을 가지고 싶어요.** / I want to have popcorn. (Possession)
> **저는 핫도그를 가지고 싶어요.** / I want to have a hot dog. (Possession)

---

### Important Usage Notes and Nuances

The instructor concludes with several vital points regarding the usage of these forms. First, he addresses the verb 원하다. While 원하다 literally means "to want," it is not commonly used in daily speech to express desire. It is a very direct and strong way of speaking, often found in song lyrics but rarely in conversation. For example, while 티비를 원해요 is grammatically possible, 티비를 가지고 싶어요 is much more natural.

Second, the instructor explains a common contraction. The verb 가지다 is frequently shortened to 갖다 in spoken Korean. Consequently, the form 가지고 싶다 often becomes **갖고 싶다**. Both 티비를 가지고 싶어요 and 티비를 갖고 싶어요 are correct and carry the same meaning.

Finally, a major grammatical restriction is introduced: the 고 싶다 form (and its variants) can only be used for the first person (I want) or the second person (Do you want? / You want). It cannot be used to describe the desires of a third person (he, she, or they). Expressing what someone else wants requires a different grammar form that will be covered in a later, more advanced lesson.
