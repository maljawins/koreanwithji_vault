## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the two primary ways to express desire in Korean: wanting to perform an action and wanting a specific object. The lesson distinguishes between the verb-based structure used for actions and the noun-based structure used for objects. Through a series of practical examples ranging from casual conversation to political statements, the instructor demonstrates how to conjugate these forms and apply them to various parts of speech.

---

### Wanting to Do an Action: ~고 싶다

To express that you want to do something, you must use the grammatical structure ~고 싶다. This is attached to the verb stem of the action you desire to perform. The instructor explains that finding the verb stem is a simple process of taking the dictionary form of the verb and removing the final syllable 다. For example, the verb 먹다 (to eat) becomes the stem 먹, 하다 (to do) becomes 하, and 가다 (to go) becomes 가. Once you have the stem, you simply attach 고 싶다 to it.

The instructor provides several examples of this in practice. To say you want to hang out or play, you use the verb 놀다 (to play); removing 다 gives the stem 놀, resulting in the sentence:
> **놀고 싶어요.** / I want to play (or hang out).

The instructor notes that 놀다 is used by adults to mean "hanging out," not just by children. Other examples include expressing a desire for food, such as **비빔밥을 먹고 싶어요** (I want to eat bibimbap), or expressing that you missed someone using the past tense of 보다 (to see):
> **보고 싶었어요.** / I wanted to see you. (Commonly used to mean "I missed you.")

This structure can also be used with the verb 되다 (to become) to talk about aspirations, such as **선생님이 되고 싶어요** (I want to become a teacher) or asking a question like **어떤 사람이 되고 싶습니까?** (What kind of person do you want to become?).

---

### Wanting an Object: 원하다

When the object of desire is a noun rather than an action, the verb 원하다 is used. This follows the standard Korean sentence structure where the noun receives an object marker (을 or 를) followed by 원하다. The instructor provides a visual chart on screen summarizing these two rules:
- **Want to (Action):** Verb Stem + 고 싶다
- **Want (Noun):** Noun(을/를) + 원하다

Examples of 원하다 range from simple personal desires to more formal contexts. For instance, **여자는 사랑을 원해요** (The woman wants love) or a more formal societal statement:
> **국민은 새로운 정치를 원합니다.** / The citizens want new politics.

The instructor also demonstrates its use in direct questions and statements, such as **그래서 당신이 원하는 것이 뭐예요?** (So, what is it that you want?), **나는 너를 원해** (I want you), and asking about gifts:
> **생일 선물로 뭐를 원하세요?** / What do you want for a birthday present?
