**Intro**

Welcome. Let’s get straight into it and learn how to say **“I want to do something”**

**The rule is very simple.**

**👉** Action Verb Stem + 고 싶다

**You can take any action verb, then you attach 고 싶다,** then you conjugate 싶다 however you want like 싶어, 싶어요, or 싶습니다 and so on.

Now, this very important. You can only use this form with the first person and the second person.

Which means you can only say “I want to” or ask someone else like “Do you want to?” because Korean doesn’t allow you to assume what somebody else wants.

And also, when the subject is a 2nd person, namely “YOU”, your sentence should ALWAYS be in question, not a statement.

In English though, we say “You want to” all the time as a statement. This is especially common in instruction videos, like in this video [Insert video - what you wanna do is, you wanna add some~~]

But Korean doesn’t do that. If you really think about it, how are THEY supposed to know what YOU want? And how are YOU supposed to know what THEY want?

You can only GUESS what they want but even so, who are YOU to say?

This is why you're talking about what someone else wants, you need a different form, which is 고 싶어 하다. Basically, this 하다 after 싶어 indicates that they’re behaving in a way they want to do something. We’ll learn that one in detail when we talk about “Compound verbs”.

🧾 Examples

Let me show you how this form is used in real sentences:

피자(를/가) 먹고 싶어요.

This means: "I want to eat pizza." What’s the subject marker doing after 피자? I’ll get to that.

호주에 여행가고 싶어요.

"I want to travel to Australia."

Notice there’s no “저는” or “나는” because like I said, you can only know what YOU want so it’s obvious that you're talking about yourself.

When it’s a question, it’s about what YOU want, the listener. So, we drop the subject again then use the honorifics if we want to sound polite.

“왜 한국어를 배우고 싶으세요?” - "Why do you want to learn Korean?"

Or in casual speech, “뭐 하고 싶어?” "What do you want to do?"

This is why even though the subject is omitted in all of these example sentences, the context tells us who the subject is.

Let’s move on.

**Why do we say “피자*가* 먹고 싶어요”?**
Let’s talk about why we sometimes use **"가"** instead of **"를"** in sentences with **~고 싶다**.

Now, since 피자 is the object of the verb 먹다, you’d expect **피자** to be marked with **를**, right?

But, both of these sentences are actually **grammatically correct**:

1. **피자가 먹고 싶어요**
2. **피자를 먹고 싶어요**

But the nuance is slightly different. Let me explain why that is.

**✅ Sentence 1: 피자가 먹고 싶어요**

In this sentence, **피자** is marked with **가**, which means it’s the **subject** of the verb **싶다**.

Here’s the key point: **싶다 is a descriptive verb**.
It works more like **"to be desirable"** or **"to be wanted."**

So, what’s the subject of 싶다? It’s **pizza!** Think of it like this – “Pizza is what I want to eat.

When Koreans say **피자가 먹고 싶어요**, they’re not just listing pizza as an object—they’re putting **emphasis on the pizza itself**.

So, using 가 is actually **more natural** in this case.

**✅ Sentence 2: 피자를 먹고 싶어요**

Now let’s look at the other version.

Here, **피자** is marked with **를**, so it’s clearly the **object** of the verb **먹다**—to eat.

In this case, the verb **싶다** works more like an **auxiliary verb** attached to **먹다**.
So the structure focuses more on the **action** of eating.

The nuance is a little more neutral:

“I want to eat pizza.” It’s less about emphasizing pizza itself, and more about wanting to do the action of eating it. This version is still fine and natural, but it sounds just slightly more **mechanical or a little too direct**.

**🧠 So, which one should you use?**

Honestly, both are fine.
But if you want to sound **more natural and native-like**, use the **subject marker**. This is just how we Koreans speak to put the spotlight on that specific thing we want.

**Don’t want to**

There are 2 ways to say “I don’t want to do something”.

**✅ 1. Negating ~고 싶다**

One option is to just **negate** the “고 싶다” form, just like we negate any regular verb in Korean. We learned this in the last lesson of level 1.

You can say:

**안 하고 싶어요** — I don’t want to do it
**하고 싶지 않아요** — I don’t want to do it

These are both **completely correct**, and Koreans do use them.
But they can sound a bit too weak, as in, the degree of not wanting to do it is just “meh”.

**Simply put, It’s just “absence of desire**.”

You're saying: “I don’t *WANT* to do it, but I don’t mind doing it.”

If you really don’t feel like doing it, you should use the next form.

**✅ 2. ~기(가) 싫다**

👉 **Action verb stem + 기(가) 싫다**

The subject marker is optional.

And this form directly expresses the idea that you **dislike** the idea of doing something, using the descriptive verb 싫다, meaning “to be undesirable” or “to be disliked.”

**🧠 What is ~기?**

What 기 does is, it turns a verb into a noun like “to go” becomes “going”. This is called nominalization and we’re going to learn about this in level 4.

So, you're literally saying is “The act of [doing something] is unpleasant.”

For example, **가기 싫어요** is like: “Going is unpleasant.” → “I don’t want to go.”

You can think of **~기(가) 싫다** as "Doing [this] is unpleasant to me."

**💬 Examples:**

* **가기 싫어요** — I don’t want to go
* **먹기 싫어요** — I don’t want to eat
* **일하기 싫어요** — I don’t want to work
* **운동하기 싫어요** — I don’t want to exercise

To sum it up, native speakers almost always default to ~기 싫다 form.

But if you wanted to say this professionally you could say “가고 싶지 않습니다” because it feels less direct and more considerate.

**I want something**

Now, let’s talk about how to say **“I want something”** in Korean.

It looks simple, doesn’t it? It looks like we could just use the basic SOV sentence like “I” “Something” “Want”. Well in that case, the only word you would need to know is “to want”, which, according to google translate, is 원하다.

So, you end up with “김치를 원해요”.

Well, this is not incorrect but it’s definitely not how Koreans speak. So, again, never trust a translator and let me explain.

**김치를 원해요** sounds way too poetic. It’s like saying **“I desire kimchi”**. In other words, it’s way too dramatic.

What we say instead is “김치(가/를) 먹고 싶어요.” – I want to EAT Kimchi.

It’s always about what you want to do with what you want. Kimchi is food. So obviously, you’d want to EAT it.

Then what if you just want an object? Like “I want a new phone”? Now, what would you want to do with your new phone? You’d want to HAVE it.

THIS is where 가지다 comes in. Remember in level 1 – lesson 15 where we learned how to say “I have something”? We learned that **“나는 핸드폰을 가져요”. Is wrong**

**As a reminder,** the most natural way to say I have a cell phone in Korean **is “나는 핸드폰이 있어요”,** which literally translates to: **“As for me, a cell phone exists.”**

So, “I want to HAVE a new cell phone” in Korean would be “저는 새 핸드폰을 가지고 싶어요.”

Also, 가지다 can be shortened to 갖다. So you could alternatively say “저는 새 핸드폰을 갖고 싶어요.” Both sentences mean exactly the same thing.

Bottomline is “Always think of what you’d want to DO with it.”

If you want to say “I want Kimchi.”, say “I want to EAT Kimchi.”

If you want a new phone, say “I want to HAVE a new phone”

And this is why I purposefully decided to cover “I want to do something” before “I want something”

**가지다 VS 먹다**

There’s another difference between English and Korean I’d like to touch on.

In English, you can HAVE breakfast, you can HAVE pizza where “have” essentially means “eat”.

But if you say “저는 피자를 갖고 싶어요.”, this sounds super awkward, like you want to OWN the pizza. So, nobody ever says something like this.

So, all you need is a little bit of common sense and choose the right word to use, which in this case would be “먹다”

Let’s move on.

**So, When is 원하다 used?**

Earlier in the lesson, I mentioned that 원하다 sounds poetic. And that’s why **you can see it often being used** in songs, especially in love songs like this one

“너를 원해 - I want you by 드렁큰타이거”

But this is not the only case.

**We use 원하다** in embedded sentences like “Something you want” where “you want” is modifying the noun something. We’re going to learn all about them in level 4.

But just to show you, in Korean this would be **네가 원하는 것** = **“the thing that you want.”**

A common phrase “What do you want?” would be “원하는 게 뭐야?” which literally translates to “What is it **that you want?**”

Here, **원하는** is used like an adjective to describe “것 - **the thing.”**
