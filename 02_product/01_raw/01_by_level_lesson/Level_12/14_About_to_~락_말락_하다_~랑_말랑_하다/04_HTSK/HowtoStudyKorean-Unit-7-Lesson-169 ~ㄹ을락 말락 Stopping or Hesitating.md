Lesson 169: ~ㄹ/을락 말락: Stopping or Hesitating

Introduction

In this lesson, you will continue to learn about applications of 말다. Specifically, you will see how it can be used with ~ㄹ/을락 말락. Let’s get started.

~ㄹ/을락 말락: Stopping or Hesitating

In the past two lessons ([Lesson 167](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-7/lessons-167-175/lesson-167/) and [168](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-7/lessons-167-175/lesson-168/)), you have seen how you can use 말다 as a way to refer to an action being done or not done. When you attach ~ㄹ/을락 말락 to an action, you can indicate that somebody is about to do something, but then hesitates and stops. As a result, the action wasn’t done, but it was on the verge of happening. For example:

Play[갑자기 기절할락 말락 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s1.mp3)
= All of a sudden I was about to faint (but didn’t)

Play[두피가 가려워서 긁을락 말락 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s2.mp3)
= My scalp was itchy, so I was about to scratch it but stopped

Play[제 동생의 장난감을 망가뜨릴락 말락 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s3.mp3)
= I was about to destroy by younger sibling’s toy but I stopped

Play[입에 넣자마자 솜사탕이 녹을락 말락 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s4.mp3)
= As soon as I put it in my mouth, the cotton candy almost melted

In the sentences above, the speaker simply ends the sentences with 하다. This implies that the action attached to ~ㄹ/을락 말락 didn’t happen, although it was on the verge of happening. Often sentences with ~ㄹ/을락 말락 end with an indication that the speaker is worried or thinking about doing the action or not. For example:

Play[통풍이 안 돼서 다른 창문을 설치할까 말까 고민 중이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s5.mp3)
= I’m considering whether to install another window because the ventilation is bad

Play[아이가 장난감을 집을락 말락 하면서 고민했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s6.mp3)
= The child hesitated, about to pick up the toy but then thought about it

It’s also possible that the speaker can end the sentence with more context as to the result of the action almost happening. In the sentences below, you’ll see ~ㄹ/을락 말락 attached to something that was about to happen, followed by some sort of context about that action. For example:

Play[제가 말을 할락 말락 하다가 결국 안 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s7.mp3)
= I was about to say something but ended up not saying it

Play[강아지가 벼룩을 잡을락 말락 하다가 결국 놓쳤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s8.mp3)
= The puppy was about to catch the flea but ended up missing it

Play[친구의 말을 듣고 당혹스러울락 말락 했지만 참았어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s9.mp3)
= I was about to feel embarrassed by my friend’s words but held it in

Play[병문안을 갈락 말락 하다가 결국 시간이 없어서 못 갔어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s10.mp3)
= I was about to visit but ended up not going because I didn’t have time

Play[반지가 손가락에 쏙 들어갈락 말락 해서 조금 더 밀어 넣었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s11.mp3)
= The ring was about to snugly fit on my finger, so I pushed it in a little more

Play[군대에 있는 오빠를 면회할락 말락 했는데 시간이 안 맞더라고요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s12.mp3)
= I was going back and forth on whether to visit my brother in the military, but the timing didn’t work out

Play[사탕을 먹을락 말락 하다가 결국 건강에 얼마나 안 좋은 지를 생각했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s13.mp3)
= I was about to eat the candy but ended up thinking about how bad it would be for my health

Play[빵이 오븐에서 다 구워져서 촉촉해질 것 같아서 꺼낼락 말락 하다가 조금 더 구웠어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s14.mp3)
= The bread was about to be taken out of the oven, and it seemed like it would be moist, but I decided to bake it a little longer

Play[그녀는 고급스러운 드레스를 입고 무대에 올라갈락 말락 했지만 긴장해서 한 걸음 물러섰어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s15.mp3)
= She was about to step onto the stage in a luxurious dress but stepped back due to nervousness

Depending on the context, it is also possible for ~ㄹ/을락 말락 to have the connotation that something looks like it might be the case. In this way, from the perspective of the speaker, they are describing a scenario that looks like it might happen or is on the verge of happening. For example:

Play[이 레몬차는 상큼할락 말락 해서 맛이 애매해요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s16.mp3)
= This lemon tea is almost refreshing but not quite, so the taste is ambiguous

Play[치통이 생길락 말락 해서 단 것을 피하고 있어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s17.mp3)
= I’m avoiding sweets because I feel like I’m about to get a toothache

Play[건전지가 다 닳을락 말락 해서 새 것으로 교체했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s18.mp3)
= The battery was about to die, so I replaced it with a new one

Play[그 일이 제 적성에 맞을락 말락 해서 계속 고민 중이에요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s19.mp3)
= That job seems like it suits my aptitude but not quite, so I’m still thinking about it

Play[동생이 내 물건을 멋대로 가져갈락 말락 해서 제가 쳐다봤어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson169s20.mp3)
= My younger sibling seemed like she was about to take my stuff as they pleased, so I stared at them
