## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the versatile Korean particle and adverbial form ~별(로), which is used to categorize or organize items into groups. The lesson begins by distinguishing this grammatical usage from the common adverb 별로, which means "not really" or "not especially" when paired with negative verbs. While they share a common linguistic root, their functions in modern Korean are distinct.

The core of the lesson focuses on how to attach ~별 or ~별로 to nouns to express the concept of "by," "for each," or "according to." The instructor explains the Hanja origin of 별 (separate/divide) to help students visualize the concept of dividing a whole into individual categories. Through various examples—ranging from organizing photos by date to recommending songs by genre—the video demonstrates how this form applies an action or a description to each specific subgroup within a larger set.

---

### The Adverb 별로 (Not Really)
Before diving into the categorization marker, the instructor clarifies the most common use of 별로 as a standalone adverb. In this context, it is always used with negative verbs or constructions. It translates to "not really" or "not especially." For example, 별로 안 좋아해요 means "I don't really like it," and 별로 재미없어요 means "It's not really fun." The instructor also notes that it can be used with the verb 이다 (to be) in the form 별로예요 to mean "It's not that great" or "It's just so-so."

---

### ~별 vs. ~별로: Categorization and Organization
The main grammar point involves attaching 별 to a noun. This 별 comes from the Hanja character meaning "separate" or "divide." When used as an adverb to describe how an action is performed, the particle 로 is added, resulting in ~별로. This is used before a verb to show that the action is being done "by [noun]" or "categorized by [noun]."

The instructor provides a clear rule for choosing between the two: use ~별로 when it functions as an adverb modifying a verb, and use ~별 when it is part of a compound noun or describing another noun. For instance, if you are organizing something by date, you use 날짜별로 (by date) because it describes the act of organizing. However, if you are talking about "normal blood pressure figures by age," you use 나이별 (by age) because it is directly modifying the noun "figures."

---

### Common Translations and Equivalents
The instructor explains that ~별(로) can be translated in several ways depending on the context: "separated by," "by," "depending on," "according to," or "for each." To help students grasp the nuance, he suggests that it can often be swapped mentally with the grammar form ~에 따라서 (depending on/according to). The key concept is that the speaker is looking at items one at a time or group by group rather than as a single mass.

### Practical Examples and Usage
The video illustrates the grammar through several sentence examples written on the whiteboard:

> **사진을 날짜별로 정리했어요.** / I organized the photos by date.

In this example, the instructor emphasizes that the photos were not just organized generally, but specifically sorted into individual groups for each date.

> **나라별로 영어 발음이 조금씩 달라요.** / English pronunciation is a little different for each country.

Here, the description "a little different" applies to each country individually.

> **그럼 제가 장르별로 노래를 몇 곡 추천해 드릴게요.** / Then I will recommend a few songs for you by genre.

This implies the speaker will pick a few songs from classical, a few from hip-hop, a few from pop, etc., rather than just a random selection.

> **저는 요리할 때 재료별로 다른 칼을 써요.** / When I cook, I use a different knife depending on the ingredient.

This shows a specific action (using a knife) that changes according to the category (the ingredient).

> **장난감을 색깔별로 모아놓으니 보기 좋네요.** / Since I gathered the toys together by color, it looks nice.

Finally, the instructor shows a noun-modifying example:

> **나이별 정상혈압 수치를 아세요?** / Do you know the normal blood pressure figures by age?

Because "by age" is describing the "blood pressure figures" (a noun) rather than the act of "knowing," the 로 is omitted.
