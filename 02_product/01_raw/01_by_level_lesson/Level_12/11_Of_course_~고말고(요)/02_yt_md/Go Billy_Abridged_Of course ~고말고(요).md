### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form ~고말고(요), which is used to express the meaning of "of course" or "for sure" in Korean. The lesson covers the basic construction of the form, its specific conversational nuances, and its limitations in usage. Throughout the video, the instructor emphasizes that this is a reactive form—meaning it is primarily used when responding to someone else to show strong agreement or confirmation. The lesson also introduces a similar, though less common, alternative form ~다마다(요) and provides several practical examples to illustrate how these expressions function in natural dialogue.

---

### Basic Construction and Usage

To create this form, you take a verb stem and simply attach 고 말고. If you wish to be polite, you add 요 to the end, resulting in ~고 말고요. For example, the verb stem of 좋아하다 (to like) is 좋아하; by attaching the ending, you get 좋아하고 말고요 (Of course I like it). The instructor notes that there are no spaces between the stem and the ending. This form functions as a sentence ending and is predominantly used in informal spoken conversation with friends or regular acquaintances.

---

### Conversational Nuance and Constraints

A critical rule for ~고말고(요) is that it is only used when replying to someone else. It carries the nuance of "Of course, I agree with you" or "For sure." You cannot use it to state "of course" as a standalone fact about yourself without a preceding prompt. For instance, if you wanted to say "I am a Korean teacher, and of course I speak Korean," you would not use this form because you are not responding to a question or statement. It is a reactive expression of agreement.

The instructor also points out that this form is generally not used with negative sentences. Specifically, sentences using the negative prefix 안 before a verb sound awkward with this ending. However, an exception exists for the verb 없다 (to not exist/to not have). Because 없다 often feels like a unique verb rather than a standard negation, it is perfectly acceptable to say 없고 말고요 (Of course there isn't/Of course I don't).

---

### Comparison with Other "Of Course" Expressions

While ~고말고(요) is a useful ending, it does not replace standard words for "of course" like 물론 (of course) or 당연하지 (of course/naturally). An easy way to determine if ~고말고(요) is appropriate is to see if the sentence could naturally start with 그럼 or 그럼요 (Sure/Of course). Since 그럼요 and ~고말고(요) serve a similar purpose of signaling agreement, they are frequently used together in the same response to add emphasis.

---

### Alternative Form: ~다마다(요)

The instructor introduces ~다마다(요) as an alternative that carries the same meaning of "of course" or "for sure." To construct this, you take a verb stem and attach 다마다 (or 다마다요 for politeness). For example, with the verb 알다 (to know), the stem is 알, resulting in 알다마다 (Of course I know). This form can also be used with past tense stems, such as 했다마다 (Of course I did it). The instructor notes that ~다마다(요) is slightly less common in daily speech than ~고말고(요), but otherwise functions identically.

### Practical Examples

The instructor provides several dialogues to demonstrate the forms in context. In a scenario about homework, one person asks 숙제 다 했지? (You did all the homework, right?), and the response is 응, 다 했고 말고 (Yeah, of course I did it all). In another example regarding food, someone asks 파김치도 먹어봤어요? (Have you tried green onion kimchi too?), and the reply is 네, 먹어봤고 말고요 (Yes, of course I've tried it).

Further examples include:
> **주소 제대로 썼어?** / Did you write the address correctly?
> **그럼, 제대로 썼고 말고.** / Of course, I wrote it correctly.

> **조금 무거운데 괜찮아요?** / It's a little heavy, are you okay?
> **네, 괜찮고 말고요. 저 힘세요.** / Yes, of course I'm okay. I'm strong.

> **제 시간에 끝낼 수 있는 사람이 없겠죠?** / There won't be anyone who can finish on time, right?
> **그럼요, 없고 말고요.** / Of course, there isn't.

Finally, using the alternative form:
> **철수도 알아요?** / Do you know Chul-soo too?
> **물론이지. 알다마다.** / Of course. Of course I know him.

> **파리도 가봤어?** / Have you been to Paris too?
> **가봤다마다요. 에펠탑도 직접 봤는걸요!** / Of course I've been there. I even saw the Eiffel Tower in person!
