## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the advanced grammatical ending ~건대, which is used by a speaker to introduce their own opinions, thoughts, or wishes. While it is considered an advanced form, the instructor emphasizes that the conjugation itself is straightforward, making it accessible to learners of various levels. The primary function of this ending is to frame a statement as a personal perspective or a "confession" of the speaker's internal state.

The instructor explains that ~건대 carries a slightly formal or "fancy" nuance, sometimes sounding like an older style of speech. It is almost exclusively used with verbs related to perception, thought, and desire. Throughout the video, the instructor provides clear examples of how this form transforms common verbs and explains the specific phonological rules that govern its shortened forms, particularly when dealing with verbs ending in 하다.

---

### Distinguishing ~건대 from 건대 (Shortened 것 + 이다)
Before diving into the main lesson, the instructor clarifies a common point of confusion. There is a similar-sounding 건대 which is actually a combination of `것 (thing)` + `이다 (to be)` + the `~ㄴ데` connector. This results in `거인데`, which then shortens to `건대`. This version is simply a way to say "it is a thing that..." and is not the specific grammatical ending being taught in this lesson.

---

### Conjugation and Usage
The conjugation for the grammatical ending ~건대 is very simple: take the stem of an action verb and add `~건대`. There are no complex steps or tense changes involved; it is always attached to the plain verb stem. For example, `하다 (to do)` becomes `하건대`.

This form is used specifically for the speaker to express their own opinions, thoughts, or wishes. It translates roughly to "I'll tell you what I [verb]..." or "What I [verb] is...". It is used with a specific set of verbs related to internal processes, including:
- **생각하다** (to think)
- **듣다** (to hear)
- **보다** (to see/look) — including its use as an auxiliary verb `~어/아 보다` (to try doing)
- **느끼다** (to feel)
- **짐작하다** (to guess/estimate)
- **원하다** (to want)
- **바라다** (to hope/wish)

The instructor notes that using this form gives the impression that the speaker is making a formal statement or a confession. An example sentence provided is:
> **개인적으로 바라건대 길에 쓰레기통이 많이 생겼으면 좋겠어요.** / Personally, what I hope for is that many trash cans would appear on the street.

---

### Shortened Forms: ~건대 vs. ~컨대
When ~건대 is used with verbs ending in `하다`, it often shortens. There are two ways this shortening happens: `~건대` or `~컨대`. This is determined by the sound of the syllable immediately preceding `하`.

The instructor explains the linguistic reason behind this: the `ㅎ` in `하` is essentially "extra air." When the `하` is removed, that air sometimes remains and affects the following `ㄱ` sound, turning it into the stronger `ㅋ` sound.

The rule for choosing between the two is as follows:
1. If the syllable before `하` ends in the consonants `ㄱ`, `ㅂ`, or `ㅅ`, the ending becomes `~건대`. This is because these sounds "eat" the extra air from the `ㅎ`, leaving none to affect the `ㄱ`.
   - **생각하다** → **생각건대** (What I think is...)
   - **부탁하다** → **부탁건대** (What I request is...)
2. In most other cases, the extra air from the `ㅎ` combines with the `ㄱ` to create `~컨대`.
   - **짐작하다** → **짐작컨대** (What I guess is...)
   - **원하다** → **원컨대** (What I want is...)
   - **단언하다** → **단언컨대** (I assure you / What I assure is...)

### Practical Examples
The instructor provides several sentences to demonstrate the form in context:
> **부탁건대 여기에 쓰레기를 버리지 말아주세요.** / What I ask of you is, please do not throw away trash here.
> **옷차림으로 짐작컨대 이 사람은 고려시대 사람이네요.** / Judging by their clothing, what I assume is that this person is from the Goryeo dynasty.
> **원컨대 제게도 가르침을 주세요.** / What I want is for you to please give me your teachings as well.
> **요컨대 철수는 잘못한 게 없다는 거예요.** / In short (summarizing), Chul-soo did nothing wrong. (Note: `요하다` is a shortened form of `요약하다` meaning to summarize).
> **단언컨대 이 경기는 이길 수 없어요.** / I assure you (What I assure is), they cannot win this match.
