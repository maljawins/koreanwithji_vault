## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form **다시피**, which is primarily used to mean "as you [verb]." This form is most frequently encountered in common conversational anchors like "as you know" or "as you see," where the speaker is referencing shared knowledge or a visible situation. The lesson covers the basic construction of this form using action verb stems in both present and past tenses.

Beyond its primary meaning, the lesson explores a secondary usage: **다시피 하다**. When combined with the verb **하다 (to do)**, the meaning shifts significantly to "almost" or "nearly." The instructor emphasizes a critical nuance here: this version of "almost" refers to actions done intentionally or habitually, distinguishing it from other "almost" forms that imply accidental occurrences.

---

### Basic Usage: "As you..."
To create this form, you take an action verb stem and attach **다시피**. This can be done with the present tense stem or the past tense stem depending on the context of the action being referenced. The instructor provides several common paradigms:

- **알다 (to know)** becomes **알다시피** (as you know)
- **보다 (to see)** becomes **보시다시피** (as you see/can see — using the honorific **시**)
- **듣다 (to hear)** becomes **들었다시피** (as you heard — using the past tense)
- **말하다 (to speak/tell)** becomes **말했다시피** (as I told you — using the past tense)

Example sentences shown on screen illustrate these points:
> **알다시피 이건 내가 산 컴퓨터야.** / As you know, this is the computer I bought.
> **보시다시피 고양이가 저보다 더 커졌어요.** / As you can see, the cat got bigger than me.
> **들었다시피 우리 만나고 있어요.** / As you've heard, we are dating.
> **저번에 말했다시피 BTS는 정말로 인기가 많다고.** / As I told you last time, BTS is really popular.

---

### Extended Usage: 다시피 하다 ("Almost/Nearly")
When **다시피** is followed by **하다**, it expresses that an action was "almost" or "nearly" performed. The instructor clarifies that this is used for intentional or exaggerated actions rather than accidents. He contrasts this with **~을 뻔하다**, which is used when something almost happened by mistake (like almost falling). **다시피 하다** is often paired with the adverb **거의 (almost/nearly)** for added emphasis.

The instructor provides examples of this "intentional almost" in practice:
> **너무 배가 고파서 2인분을 혼자서 다 먹다시피 했어요.** / I was so hungry that I almost ate two servings completely by myself.
> **한동안은 운동을 매일 하다시피 했어요.** / For a while I almost exercised every day.
> **요즘은 비가 너무 많이 와서 거의 집에만 있다시피 하고 있어요.** / Lately it's raining so much that I'm almost only staying at home.

In the final example, the instructor notes that **있다 (to exist/be)** is used here to mean "to stay" in a specific location.
