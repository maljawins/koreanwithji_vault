## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor explores various ways to express preference and contrast in Korean, focusing on the grammar form `~느니` and related adverbs like `차라리` and `오히려`. The lesson begins with the adverb `차라리`, which sets the stage for choosing between options. It then transitions into the `~느니` grammar form, explaining how it is used to indicate that a second option is preferable to a first, often implying that the first option is undesirable. The lesson further distinguishes between `차라리` (used for choosing the lesser of two evils) and `오히려` (used for positive preferences or contrary outcomes). Finally, the instructor introduces the advanced form `~느니만 못하다` to express that one action is significantly worse than another.

---

### The Adverb 차라리 (Rather/Preferably)

The adverb `차라리` translates to "rather" or "preferably" in English. While it is often omitted in translation, its literal meaning is to highlight a preferred choice among possibilities. The instructor demonstrates this by comparing two sentences: `더운 것보다 추운 게 나아요` (It is preferable to be cold more than hot) and `더운 것보다 차라리 추운 게 나아요` (I'd rather be cold than hot). Adding `차라리` provides emphasis, signaling that between the two choices, the speaker is identifying the better one.

Another example illustrates using `차라리` to express a humorous or hyperbolic preference. If someone is "wasting" their good looks by making weird faces, a speaker might say:
> **잘생긴 얼굴을 그렇게 쓸 거면 차라리 저한테 주세요.** / If you're going to use a handsome face like that, you should rather just give it to me.

---

### The Grammar Form ~느니

The grammar form `~느니` is attached to action verb stems. It indicates that the speaker prefers the second part of the sentence over the first. This preference often arises because the first option is viewed as particularly bad, making the second option the "lesser of two evils." The structure can be thought of as "Rather than A, B." Adding `차라리` to the second part of the sentence is optional but adds further emphasis to the choice.

The instructor provides several examples to show the versatility of this form:
- **팝콘을 먹느니 뻥튀기를 먹겠어.** / I'd rather eat `뻥튀기` (puffed rice snack) than eat popcorn.
- **이런 침대에서 잠을 자느니 바닥에서 자는 게 더 편해.** / Rather than sleep in this kind of bed, it would be more comfortable to sleep on the floor.
- **혼자서 백 년을 사느니 사랑하는 사람과 하루를 살겠다.** / Rather than live a hundred years alone, I would live one day with the person I love.
- **주말에 마라톤을 하느니 차라리 등산을 할래요.** / I'd rather go hiking than run a marathon on the weekend.
- **이렇게 스트레스를 받고 사느니 차라리 아무 생각도 하지 않는 게 낫겠어요.** / I'd rather just not think about anything than live getting this sort of stress.

The instructor notes that `~느니` implies the first option is not a good choice. If a speaker wants to compare two options without this negative implication, they should use `~는 것보다` instead. Additionally, `~느니` has the same meaning as the form `~느니보다` and is similar to the form `~ㄹ 바에`.

---

### 차라리 vs. 오히려 vs. 도리어

While both `차라리` and `오히려` can mean "rather," they are used in different contexts. `차라리` is typically used when both options are negative or undesirable, and the speaker is choosing the "lesser evil." In contrast, `오히려` is used when the second option is actually something good or preferable.

`오히려` also has a second meaning: "contrary to expectations" or "on the contrary." The instructor provides an example of this usage:
> **일을 시작하면 바빠질 줄 알았는데 오히려 시간이 더 많아졌어요.** / I thought I'd get busy once I started work, but actually (on the contrary), I had more time.

The adverb `도리어` is a less common synonym for `오히려` in the sense of "on the contrary." It is often heard in its shortened forms `되려` or `대려`. An example of its use is:
> **인삼을 잘못 먹으면 도리어 건강을 해칠 수 있어요.** / If you eat ginseng incorrectly, you might actually (contrary to what you think) harm your health.

---

### Advanced Form: ~느니만 못하다

The advanced grammar form `~느니만 못하다` is used to express that option A is significantly worse than option B, or that A "doesn't hold a candle" to B. It literally means that A does not reach the level of B. This is a stronger way of saying B is better by emphasizing how poor A is.

The instructor uses an exercise-related example to clarify:
> **운동을 바른 자세로 하지 않으면 안 하는 이만 못해요.** / If you don't exercise with proper posture, it's worse than just not doing it (it's better to not exercise at all).
