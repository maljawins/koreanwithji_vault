### Far From and Let Alone: 커녕 and 고사하고
The form 커녕 is used after nouns and verbs to indicate that something has a very low chance of happening. It translates to English as "far from," "let alone," "never mind," or "forget that." When using the structure "A 커녕 B," it implies that A is unlikely, and B—which is even more basic or easier than A—is also unlikely. To use it with nouns, you attach 커녕 directly after the noun along with the topic marker 은 or 는. For example, 집 (house) becomes 집은 커녕 and 학교 (school) becomes 학교는 커녕. With action verbs, the form 기는 커녕 is attached to the verb stem, such as 하기는 커녕 for the verb 하다 (to do).

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this usage:
- **여행 갈 돈은 커녕 외식할 돈도 없어요.** / I don't even have money to eat out, let alone go on a trip.
- **선물은 커녕 연락도 받지 못했어요.** / Far from a gift, I couldn't even get a call.
- **주말에 쉬기는 커녕 바빠서 잠도 못 잤어요.** / Far from resting on the weekend, I couldn't even sleep because I was busy.
- **식사는 커녕 물을 마시지도 못할 정도로 목이 아파요.** / Never mind a meal; my throat hurts so much that I can't even drink water.

A similar but more formal and less common form is 고사하고. It functions identically to 커녕 and is used as 은/는 고사하고 with nouns or 기는 고사하고 with action verbs. An example shown is: **새 차는 고사하고 중고차를 사기도 힘들어요** (Never mind buying a new car, it's difficult to buy a used car).

### Preferential Rather: 느니
The form 느니 is used to express "rather than A, B" specifically when the first action (A) is not preferable. It is frequently paired with the adverb 차라리 (preferably) for emphasis. To conjugate, attach 느니 directly to an action verb stem in the present tense. If the stem ends in the consonant ㄹ, that consonant is removed before adding the suffix (e.g., 놀다 becomes 노느니). While the particle 보다 can be added for extra emphasis (느니보다), it is less common in standard speech.

Example sentences include:
- **거짓말을 하느니 아무 말도 안 하는 게 나아요.** / Rather than lying, it's better to not say anything.
- **이렇게 맛없는 걸 먹느니 굶을래요.** / I want to starve rather than eat something that tastes this bad.

---

### Better Descriptions: 기보다
The form 기보다 (or more commonly 기보다는/기보단) is used to say that one description is better than another. It translates as "rather than saying..." or "it's more that..." The structure "A 기보다 B" suggests that B is a more accurate description than A. To use this, conjugate the verb into the plain form in any tense and attach 기보다. For the verb 이다 (to be), it becomes 이라기보다 after a consonant and 라기보다 after a vowel.

Examples of this descriptive comparison:
- **시간이 없다기보다 그냥 하기 싫어요.** / It's more that I just don't want to do it, rather than not having time.
- **운동을 좋아한다기보다 필요해서 해요.** / Rather than say I like exercising, it's more that I do it because I need it.
- **철수는 친구라기보다 가족에 가까워요.** / Chul-soo is closer to family than a friend.

---

### Situational Rather: 바에
The word 바 refers to a "case" or "situation." When used as 할 바에 (future adjective form + 바), it implies "in a situation where I am doing A anyway, I would rather do B." It is often used with the particle 야 or the topic marker 는 for emphasis (바에야 / 바에는).

Example sentences:
- **하나만 먹을 바에 안 먹는 게 나아요.** / Rather than eating only one, it's better to not eat it.
- **친구 없이 혼자 갈 바에 안 갈래요.** / Rather than going alone without any friends, I don't want to go.

---

### On the Other Hand: 반면에
반면 means "the opposite side." It can be used at the start of a sentence as 반면에 to mean "on the other hand," or as a connector between clauses. To connect sentences, conjugate the verb into an adjective form (present or past) and add 반면에. This is used to provide a direct contrast between two facts.

Examples:
- **기차는 속도가 빠른 반면에 가격이 비싸요.** / While trains are fast, on the other hand, their price is expensive.
- **도시는 살기 편한 반면에 생활비가 많이 들어요.** / While the city is convenient to live in, on the other hand, the cost of living is high.
