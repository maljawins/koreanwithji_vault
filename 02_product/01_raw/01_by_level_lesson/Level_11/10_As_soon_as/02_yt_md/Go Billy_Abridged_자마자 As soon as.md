### Overview

The instructor introduces three distinct ways to express the concept of "as soon as" or "immediately after" in Korean, categorized by difficulty level. The lesson begins with the most common beginner-level form, moves to a more restrictive intermediate form, and concludes with an advanced emphatic expression. The primary focus is on how to attach these endings to action verb stems to link two sequential events where the second happens right after the first.

---

### Beginner Level: Action Verb Stem + 자마자

The most versatile and common way to say "as soon as" is by using the form 자마자. To conjugate this, you take any action verb (verbs that describe doing something like eating, going, or seeing), remove the final 다 to find the verb stem, and simply attach 자마자. This form is used to show that Action B happens immediately after Action A.

The instructor emphasizes that this form is very flexible regarding the tense of the final clause. You can use it for the present tense, past tense, future tense, or even for commands and suggestions. For example, if you want to say "As soon as I go inside, it got quiet," you use the past tense ending at the very end of the sentence, while the 자마자 part remains attached to the simple verb stem.

Example sentences provided:
> **제가 들어가자마자 조용해졌어요.** / As soon as I went inside, it got quiet.
> **자리에 앉자마자 영화가 시작됐어요.** / The movie started as soon as I sat down in my seat.
> **두 사람은 만나자마자 사랑에 빠졌대요.** / They say those two fell in love as soon as they met.
> **여자친구는 점심을 끝내자마자 디저트를 사러 갔어요.** / My girlfriend went to buy dessert as soon as she finished lunch.
> **집에 도착하자마자 손부터 씻으세요.** / As soon as you get home, wash your hands first.
> **빵 냄새를 맡자마자 배가 고파졌어요.** / As soon as I smelled the bread, I felt hungry.

---

### Intermediate Level: Action Verb Stem + 자

A less common and more formal version of this expression is simply attaching 자 to the verb stem. While it also translates to "as soon as," it carries a specific nuance: it implies that the first action was the cause or the requirement for the second action to happen. It suggests a "because of A, B happened" relationship.

Because of this causal link, this form has strict grammatical restrictions. It cannot be used with commands or suggestions. Furthermore, because it describes a result that has already occurred, the sentence must end in the past tense. You are essentially describing how one event led immediately to another in the past.

Example sentence provided:
> **모두 자리에 앉자 불이 꺼졌어요.** / As soon as everyone sat down, the lights turned off.

---

### Advanced Level: Action Verb Stem + 기가 무섭게 / 바쁘게

For a more advanced and emphatic way to say "as soon as," one can use the nominalized form of the verb followed by the adverbs 무섭게 (scarily) or 바쁘게 (busily). Specifically, you take the verb stem, add 기가, and then add the adverb. This emphasizes that the second action happened so incredibly fast after the first that it is almost "scary" or "hectic."

Like the intermediate 자 form, this advanced version is not used for commands or suggestions. It is purely descriptive, used to highlight the extreme speed of the sequence of events. The instructor notes that while this is a great form to know for advanced proficiency, it is much less common in daily conversation than 자마자.

Example sentence provided:
> **눈을 치우기가 무섭게 또 눈이 쌓이고 있어요.** / As soon as I cleared the snow, it's piling up again (scarily fast).
