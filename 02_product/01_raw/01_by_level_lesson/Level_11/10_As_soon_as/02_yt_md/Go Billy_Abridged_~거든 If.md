## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form ~거든, which exists in two distinct functional categories: a beginner-level sentence ending used for explaining information, and an advanced-level sentence connector used to express a hypothetical "if." The lesson focuses primarily on the latter, distinguishing it from the common conditional marker ~(으)면.

The learning arc begins with a brief review of the sentence-ending ~거든요 to establish a baseline, then moves into the mechanics of the connective ~거든. The instructor emphasizes that while ~거든 can often be translated as "if," it carries a specific nuance of "once something has actually happened" or "after confirming a condition is met." The lesson concludes with practical usage rules, tense restrictions, and a look at a rare, more emphatic variation.

---

### Review: The Sentence Ending ~거든요
The instructor first reviews the beginner-level form used at the end of a sentence to explain something to the listener. To conjugate it, one simply attaches ~거든 to a verb stem, adding 요 for politeness. For example, the verb stem 하 from 하다 (to do) becomes 하거든요. This form is used when the speaker assumes the listener does not already know the information being shared. It functions like saying "FYI," "just so you know," or "it's because..." in English.

A critical nuance involves intonation. When spoken with a downward intonation, it sounds like a neutral explanation or a helpful piece of information. However, if the speaker uses a rising intonation on the final syllable (요), it can sound sarcastic or like the speaker is mocking the listener. The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence:
> **저도 한국어를 배우고 있거든요.** / FYI, I am also learning Korean.

---

### The Connective ~거든: Hypothetical "If"
The advanced version of ~거든 is used in the middle of a sentence as a connector. Unlike the sentence-ending version, it does not take 요. While it can be replaced by the standard conditional ~(으)면 (if/when) in almost every case, it is not a perfect synonym. The instructor explains that ~거든 adds emphasis to the condition. It implies a sense of "if/once something actually happens" or "once you have confirmed that the condition is met."

Visually, the instructor contrasts the two on his whiteboard:
- **~(으)면** — General "if" or "when."
- **~거든** — Hypothetical conditions or requirements; "once it actually happens."

For example, while both **출발하면 문자 주세요** and **출발하거든 문자 주세요** mean "Text me when you leave," the version using ~거든 emphasizes the moment the departure is confirmed.

---

### Usage Rules and Tense Restrictions
The instructor notes that ~거든 is more common in written Korean than in everyday speech. When it is used in speech, it follows specific structural patterns. Most commonly, a sentence using ~거든 will end with a command (imperative) or a suggestion (propositive). The logic is: "Once [Condition A] is confirmed to have happened, then do [Action B]."

There are several cases where ~거든 cannot replace ~(으)면:
- **Assumptions or Guesses:** You cannot use ~거든 when making a wish or a guess about a state. For example, "It would be good if it rained" must use ~(으)면.
- **Regular Present Tense:** You cannot use ~거든 to describe a general habit or a present-tense fact in the second clause. The instructor explains that the "once confirmed" nuance of ~거든 clashes with a result that is already happening in the present.
- **Past Tense:** You cannot use ~거든 to say "If X happened, then Y happened." The tenses do not align logically.
- **Future Tense:** It can be used with specific future forms like ~겠다, ~것이다, or ~려고 하다, though this is less common than using commands or suggestions.

---

### Example Sentences
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the "once confirmed" nuance:

> **출발하거든 문자 주세요.** / Send me a text once you actually leave.
> **집에 먼저 도착하거든 창문 좀 닫아주세요.** / If you happen to arrive home first, please close the window.
> **누가 저를 봤냐고 묻거든 못 봤다고 해 주세요.** / If someone actually asks if you've seen me, please say you didn't.
> **좋은 선생님을 찾거든 저한테도 알려주세요.** / If you find a good teacher (once you've confirmed they are good), let me know too.
> **수업이 끝나거든 깨워주세요.** / Once class is over (and you've confirmed it's finished), please wake me up.

---

### Emphatic Variation: ~거들랑
The lesson concludes with a brief mention of the form ~거들랑. This is a spoken variation of ~거든 that is even more emphatic. It is never used in writing and is strictly paired with commands or suggestions. The instructor notes that learners will likely never encounter it, but it is useful to recognize as a stronger version of the same "if/once" meaning.
