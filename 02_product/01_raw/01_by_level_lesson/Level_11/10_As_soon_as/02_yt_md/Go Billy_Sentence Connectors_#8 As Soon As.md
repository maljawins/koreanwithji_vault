### Overview

The instructor introduces various ways to express the concept of "as soon as" in Korean. Building on previous lessons about temporal connectors like "before" and "after," this lesson covers four distinct grammatical forms: the standard and most common ~자마자, the more restrictive and causal ~자, the formal and future-oriented ~는 대로, and the emphatic or exaggerated ~기가 무섭게. The lesson also explores secondary meanings of 대로 when used with verbs and nouns to mean "just as" or "in accordance with."

---

### The Standard Form: ~자마자

The most versatile and common way to say "as soon as" is by using the connector ~자마자. This form indicates that one action (B) happens immediately after another action (A) is completed. It is compatible with all tenses—past, present, and future—based on the final verb of the sentence. To construct this form, simply attach ~자마자 to the present tense action verb stem. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하자마자.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate its flexibility across tenses and situations:
> **집에 오자마자 손을 씻었어요.** / As soon as I came home, I washed my hands.
> **아빠는 침대에 눕자마자 잠이 들어요.** / As soon as Dad lies down on the bed, he falls asleep.
> **정류장에 도착하자마자 버스가 왔어요.** / The bus came as soon as I arrived at the bus stop.
> **오늘은 전화가 오자마자 받을 거예요.** / As soon as I get a phone call today, I'll answer it.
> **창문을 열자마자 모기가 들어왔어요.** / As soon as I opened the window, a mosquito came in.

---

### The Causal and Restrictive Form: ~자

The shortened form ~자 is less common than ~자마자 and carries several specific restrictions. While it also means "as soon as," it implies a causal link where the first action directly results in the second. Unlike ~자마자, this form can only be used in sentences ending in the past tense. Consequently, it cannot be used for commands or suggestions. Furthermore, the subjects of the two actions must be different. To use it, attach ~자 to the present tense action verb stem, such as 하자.

Visual examples on screen illustrate these causal relationships:
> **불을 켜자 전구가 깨졌어요.** / As soon as I turned on the light, the bulb broke.
> **사이렌을 켜자 차들이 멈췄어요.** / As soon as I turned on the siren, the cars stopped.
> **문이 열리자 사람들이 몰려나왔어요.** / As soon as the door opened, people poured out.
> **에어컨을 끄자 더위가 느껴졌어요.** / As soon as I turned off the air conditioner, I could feel the heat.

---

### The Formal and Future Form: ~는 대로

The form ~는 대로 is used for "as soon as" in more formal contexts, particularly when conveying information or making requests. It is typically used in sentences that end with a future tense form. To conjugate, change the action verb into its present tense adjective form (adding ~는) and add 대로. If a verb stem ends in ㄹ, the ㄹ is dropped before adding ~는 대로; for instance, 놀다 (to play) becomes 노는 대로.

Examples of formal information exchange:
> **전화 오는 대로 알려주세요.** / Please let me know as soon as I get a call.
> **차를 사는 대로 여행을 갈 거예요.** / As soon as I buy a car, I'll go on a trip.
> **도착하는 대로 일을 시작할게요.** / I'll start work as soon as I arrive.
> **운동이 끝나는 대로 연락해 주세요.** / Please contact me as soon as your exercise ends.
> **대학생이 되는 대로 알바를 시작하겠습니다.** / As soon as I become a university student, I'll start a part-time job.

---

### Secondary Meanings of 대로

Beyond "as soon as," ~는 대로 can mean "in the same way as" or "just as" when describing an experience. In this usage, it can follow both action and descriptive verbs in the present or past tense.
- **친구들이 한 대로 따라 했어요.** / I followed along just as my friend did.
- **주시는 대로 먹을게요.** / I'll eat what you give me.
- **약속한 대로 내일 소풍을 갈 거예요.** / As promised, tomorrow we'll go on a picnic.

Additionally, 대로 can be attached directly to certain nouns to mean "just like," "in accordance with," or "as."
- **엄마 말대로 해.** / Do as mom says.
- **순서대로 먹어보세요.** / Try eating them in order.
- **계획대로 잘 될 거야.** / It'll work out well as planned.
- **그대로 멈춰라.** / Stop there as you are.
- **마음대로 되지를 않아요.** / It doesn't work out in the way I want.

---

### The Emphatic Form: ~기가 무섭게

The most dramatic way to say "as soon as" is ~기가 무섭게. The word 무섭게 literally means "scarily," implying that the second action happened so fast it was almost frightening. This form is used for exaggeration and is less common in daily speech. Like ~자, it cannot be used for commands or suggestions. It is formed by attaching ~기가 무섭게 to an action verb stem. This form is interchangeable with ~기가 바쁘게.

Examples of rapid, "scary" transitions:
> **올해는 시작되기가 무섭게 끝나버렸어요.** / This year ended as soon as it started.
> **벚꽃이 피기가 무섭게 떨어지네요.** / The cherry blossoms are falling down as soon as they blossom.
> **우리 집 김치는 만들기가 무섭게 사라져요.** / Our family's kimchi disappears as soon as we make it.
> **요리를 끝내기 무섭게 다음 주문이 들어왔어요.** / The next order came in as soon as we finished cooking.
