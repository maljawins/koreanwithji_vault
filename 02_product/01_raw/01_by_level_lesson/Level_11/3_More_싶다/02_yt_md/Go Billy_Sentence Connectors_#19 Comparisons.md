### Basic Comparisons with 보다
The most fundamental way to make a comparison in Korean is by using the particle **보다**, which corresponds to "than" in English. When attached to a noun, it indicates that the noun is the standard of comparison. This particle is frequently paired with the adverbs **더** (more) and **덜** (less). If **보다** is used alone without these adverbs, its default meaning is "more than," making **더** optional in many cases. For example, saying `저는 철수보다 잘생겼어요` means "I am more handsome than Chul-soo." Conversely, `저는 철수보다 덜 잘생겼어요` means "I am less handsome than Chul-soo."

To use **보다** with verbs, the verb must first be turned into a noun form using **~는 것**. For instance, `하다` (to do) becomes `하는 것`. This allows for sentences like `지금 하는 것보다 내일 하는 게 더 좋아요`, which translates to "It is better to do it tomorrow than to do it now." While less common, action verbs can also use the **~기** nominalization form, such as `하기`, to achieve a similar effect.

---

### Expressing "Compared To" with 비하다
While **보다** is used for simple "more/less" comparisons, the verb **비하다** (to compare) is used in grammar forms to emphasize the difference between two things. The most common form is **~에 비해서** (compared to), where the **서** is optional. This can be attached to nouns or nominalized verbs. For example, `올해는 작년에 비해서 덜 바빠요` means "Compared to last year, this year is less busy." 

A related but less common form is **~에 비하면**, which literally means "if one compares to." This is used similarly to **~에 비해서** but carries a slightly different nuance of hypothetical comparison. An example provided is `저에 비하면 제 동생은 천사예요`, meaning "Compared to me, my younger sibling is an angel."

---

### Similarity and Difference: 처럼, 같이, and 달리
To say something is "like" or "similar to" something else, the particle **처럼** is used. It can be attached to nouns or nominalized verbs. For instance, `연예인처럼 메이크업을 했어요` means "I did my makeup like a celebrity." The word **같이** can also be used in the same way, though it is slightly less common. However, **같이** is very frequently used with "and" particles like **~와/과**, **~이랑**, or **~하고** to mean "the same as" or "like."

The opposite of these forms is **달리**, which means "unlike" or "different from." It is often used with **~와/과** to create formal comparisons, such as `고양이는 개와 달리 혼자 있는 걸 좋아해요` (Cats, unlike dogs, like being alone). To add emphasis to "like," the adverb **마치** (just like) can be placed before the comparison, as in `영이가 마치 로봇처럼 말했어요` (Young-i talked just like a robot).

---

### Expressing "Practically the Same": 마찬가지 and 다름없다
The noun **마찬가지** means "the same" or "similar." It can be used with the verb **이다** to say something "is the same," or with **~와/과** to say it is "the same as" something else. When used as an adverb, it becomes **마찬가지로**. For example, `한국과 마찬가지로 일본이 더워요` means "Japan is hot, the same as Korea."

A more advanced usage involves the form **~(으)나 마찬가지**, which translates to "practically" or "basically." This expresses that even if things aren't identical, the result or situation is the same. For example, `공부를 하나 안 하나 마찬가지일 거예요` means "No matter if I study or not, it'll be the same." When used with nouns, it becomes **~(이)나 마찬가지**, such as `책이 많아서 도서관이나 마찬가지네요` (It's practically a library because there are a lot of books). The expression **~다름없다** (to be no different) functions almost identically to **마찬가지**.

---

### Advanced Metaphors: 듯(이) and 다시피
The form **~듯(이)** is used with action verbs to mean "as if" or "like." It is often used in metaphors and idioms. For example, `미친 듯이 공부를 했어요` means "I studied like crazy" (literally, "like one went crazy"). When attached directly to a verb stem, it creates strong idiomatic expressions like `밥 먹듯이` (like eating a meal, meaning "frequently") or `물 쓰듯이` (like using water, meaning "extravagantly").

The form **~다피** means "as" or "in the way of" and is typically used with verbs of perception like "see," "know," or "hear." Common phrases include `알다시피` (as you know) and `보시다시피` (as you see). When combined with **하다**, the form **~다시피 하다** means "to almost do" or "to practically do," indicating that someone is performing an action so intensely it's as if they are doing something else. For example, `도서관에서 살다시피 해요` means "I practically live at the library."

---

### Extent and Degree: 만큼 and 정도(로)
The particle **만큼** expresses "as much as" or "to the extent that." With nouns, it indicates an equal amount: `철수만큼 잘생겼어요` (I'm as handsome as Chul-soo). With verbs, it uses an adjective form to show degree: `참을 수 없을 만큼 보고 싶어요` (I want to see you so much that I can't bear it).

Similarly, **정도** (degree) is used to mean "so much that" or "to the level that." It is often used as **~정도(로)** to describe the intensity of an action. For example, `밥 먹을 시간도 없을 정도로 바빠요` means "I'm so busy that I have no time to even eat." While **만큼** and **정도** are often interchangeable, **정도** specifically emphasizes the level or degree of the situation.
