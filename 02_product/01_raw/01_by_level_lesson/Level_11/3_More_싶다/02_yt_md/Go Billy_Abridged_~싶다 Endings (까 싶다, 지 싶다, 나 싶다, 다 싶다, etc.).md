## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor explores various intermediate-level grammar structures that utilize the verb 싶다. While most beginners learn 싶다 in the context of 고 싶다 (to want to do something), it carries a secondary, broader meaning related to thinking, wondering, or feeling a certain way about a situation. This lesson focuses on how 싶다 combines with different sentence endings like 까, 나, 지, and the plain form 다 to express nuances ranging from curiosity and doubt to worry and wishing.

The core concept presented is that these forms are essentially two separate grammar points stuck together. By understanding the function of the first part (the ending attached to the verb stem) and combining it with the "thinking/wondering" aspect of 싶다, learners can intuitively grasp the meaning of the entire phrase. The instructor emphasizes that 싶다 in these contexts often implies a sense of uncertainty or a subjective feeling rather than a concrete fact.

---

### The Foundation: 까 생각하다 and 까 하다
Before diving into 싶다, the instructor establishes a baseline using the familiar 까 ending combined with 생각하다 (to think). The structure Verb Stem + (을/ㄹ)까 생각하다 (or simply 까 하다) literally means "to think 'shall I [verb]?'" When used with action verbs, it expresses that the speaker is thinking about or considering doing something. For example, 친구를 만날까 생각하고 있어 means "I am thinking about meeting a friend." If used with descriptive verbs or in other contexts, it shifts toward "wondering if" something is the case. This serves as the logical bridge to the 싶다 forms.

---

### Wondering and Curiosity: 까 싶다
The form (을/ㄹ)까 싶다 is used primarily with descriptive verbs to express "wondering if" or "thinking that maybe" something is true. While 싶다 can mean "to want," in this structure, it functions as "to think" with a nuance of curiosity or worry. The instructor provides the example 친구가 집에 있을까 싶어 to show the speaker is wondering if their friend is at home. 

A key stylistic note is that this form is frequently used with the negative structure 지 않다 to create a "wouldn't it be...?" or "couldn't it be...?" meaning. For instance, 어머니가 이 선물은 좋아하시지 않을까 싶었어요 translates to "I was wondering, wouldn't Mom like this gift?" This doesn't necessarily make the sentence negative; rather, it's a common way to express a tentative thought or hope. Additionally, this form often appears in the middle of a sentence as 싶어서 (because I thought/wondered), explaining the reason for a subsequent action.

---

### Softening Curiosity: 나 싶다
The structure Verb Stem + 나 싶다 is very similar to 까 싶다 but carries a softer tone and shows extra curiosity. The 나 ending naturally softens the sentence and is used when the speaker is genuinely curious about a possibility. An example provided is 다 먹을 수 있나 싶었어, meaning "I wondered, 'Can he eat all of this?'" Like the previous form, it is often paired with 지 않다 to emphasize uncertainty, as seen in 이미 너무 늦지 않았나 싶어요 (I'm wondering if it's not already too late).

---

### Seeking Confirmation and Uncertainty: 지 싶다
The 지 싶다 form has two distinct uses based on the type of 지 ending used. First, it can be used to request confirmation or express a thought while hoping the listener agrees. For example, 이게 더 낫지 싶네요 means "This is better, isn't it? (I think so, what do you think?)" 

Second, it can use the "uncertainty 지" (whether/if). This is used to express wondering about a specific unknown detail, such as 왜 그렇게 말을 했는지 싶었어요 (I wondered why he said that). It can also express wondering about a capability or future possibility, such as 할머니가 이걸 해주실 수 있을지 싶어요 (I'm wondering if Grandma will be able to do this for me).

---

### Announcing a Thought: Plain Form + 다 싶다
When 싶다 is attached to the plain form (usually a descriptive verb + 다), it is used to "announce" a subjective realization or feeling. It translates to "I thought..." or "It felt like..." in response to a specific observation. The instructor uses the example 음식이 좀 이상하다 싶었는데 (I thought the food was a bit strange...) to show how the speaker reached a conclusion based on their impression. This form is often followed by 는데 to provide background for what happened next, such as getting a stomachache.

---

### Rare and Specific Forms: 가 싶다 and (으)면 싶다
The instructor briefly covers two less common forms. The 가 싶다 structure is essentially a variation of 나 싶다 used with descriptive verbs in the present tense, such as 철수가 정말 한국 사람인가 싶어요 (I'm wondering if Chul-soo is really Korean). 

Finally, the (으)면 싶다 form combines the "if" ending with "want" to express a wish or hope, particularly for things that might not happen. This is almost exclusively used with past tense verb stems. For example, 찰수가 숙제를 대신 해줬으면 싶었어 means "I wished Chul-soo would do the homework for me." It expresses a subjective desire or a "would be nice if" sentiment.
