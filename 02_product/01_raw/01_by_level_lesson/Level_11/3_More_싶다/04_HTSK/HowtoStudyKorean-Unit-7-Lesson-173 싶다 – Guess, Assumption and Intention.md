Lesson 173: 싶다 – Guess, Assumption and Intention

Introduction

In this lesson, you will continue to learn about 싶다. In this lesson, you will learn two more uses that follow a similar form after indications of doubt or question. Let’s get started.

Usage 1: A Guess or Assumption

In addition to the two uses of 싶다 that you learned in [Lesson 171](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-7/lessons-167-175/lesson-171/) and [Lesson 172](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-7/lessons-167-175/lesson-172/), there are two more that take a similar form – both which require the use of a grammatical principle that indicates speculation. In the first usage I will describe, 싶다 is used to indicate the speaker’s guess or assumption. One common way that you can do this is by using ~ㄹ/을까, which was introduced in [Lesson 63](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-3-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-3-lessons-59-66/lesson-63/). For example:

Play[하늘이 너무 검어서 폭풍이 올까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s1.mp3)
= The sky is so dark that I wonder if a storm is coming

Here, you can see that the speaker is not actually sure if a storm is coming or not. 싶다 in this sense is usually translated to “I wonder if…” or something to that effect. Below are many more examples:

Play[하늘이 이렇게 검으니 곧 폭우가 쏟아질까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s2.mp3)
= Since the sky is this black, I wonder if a heavy rainstorm is coming soon

Play[이 튀김이 너무 바삭한 것을 보니 오래 튀긴 걸까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s3.mp3)
= Since this fried food is so crispy, I wonder if it was fried for too long

Play[지폐의 촉감이 이상해서 혹시 위조된 돈이 아닐까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s4.mp3)
= The texture of the bill feels strange, so I wonder if it’s counterfeit

Play[광대뼈가 아픈 걸 보니 어젯밤에 장난치다가 다친 걸까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s5.mp3)
= Since my cheekbone hurts, I wonder if I got hurt while playing around last night

Play[승인 요청이 아직 안 된 것을 보니 뭔가 문제가 생긴 걸까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s6.mp3)
= Since the approval request hasn’t gone through yet, I wonder if there’s a problem

Play[비행기가 경유하는 동안 공항에서 밤낮없이 기다려야 할까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s7.mp3)
= Since the plane has a layover, I wonder if I’ll have to wait at the airport day and night

Of course, you can use 싶다 in another location in the sentence other than the final word. For example, ~아/어서 is often added to 싶다 to indicate that something as a result of the speculation. For example:

Play[전람회에서 본 그림이 진짜일까 싶어서 자세히 살펴보았어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s8.mp3)
= I examined the painting at the exhibition closely, wondering if it was real

~ㄹ/을까 is often used before 싶다 as you have seen in the sentences so far. However, there are other grammatical principles that can indicate speculation. In [Lesson 21](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit1/unit-1-lessons-17-25-2/lesson-21-asking-questions-in-korean-why-when-where-and-who/) you learned about ~나, ~니 and ~ㄴ/은가 as ways to ask questions. These can also be placed before 싶다 to create a similar meaning as described above. For example:

~나 싶다
Play[이렇게 늦게까지 회사에 남아 있어야 할 의무가 있나 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s9.mp3)
= I wonder if I really have an obligation to stay this late at work

Play[전람회에 사람이 너무 없어서 제대로 열린 게 맞나 싶었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s10.mp3)
= There were so few people at the exhibition that I wondered if it was really being held properly

Play[얼굴을 만져보니 광대뼈가 더 튀어나온 것 같아서 살이 빠졌나 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s11.mp3)
= Touching my face, my cheekbones seem more prominent, so I wonder if I’ve lost weight

—–

~니 싶다
Play[저렴한 가격을 보니 위조 상품이니 싶어 의심스러워요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s12.mp3)
= Looking at the cheap price, I suspect it might be counterfeit

Play[길에서 주운 전단지가 광고인가 싶었는데 자세히 보니 경고문이었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s13.mp3)
= I thought the flyer I picked up on the street was an advertisement, but upon closer look, it was a warning notice

—–

~ㄴ/은가 싶다
Play[제출한 서류가 승인된 건가 싶어서 이메일을 확인했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s14.mp3)
= I checked my email, wondering if my submitted documents had been approved

Play[친구가 갑자기 조용하길래 저한테 장난칠 계획인가 싶었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s15.mp3)
= My friend suddenly got quiet, so I wondered if they were planning to prank me

Play[이 소설의 주인공이 정말 용맹스러운가 싶어서 읽어 보기로 했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s16.mp3)
= I wondered if the protagonist in this novel was truly brave, so I decided to read it

Play[처음 본 사람들 앞에서 수줍어하는 것을 보니 낯가리는 성격인가 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s17.mp3)
= Seeing how shy they are in front of new people, I wonder if they have a reserved personality

Furthermore, in [Lesson 26](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-2-lower-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-2-lessons-26-33/lesson-26/), you saw how ~ㄴ/은/는/ㄹ/을 지 can be used to indicate speculation in a sentence.

~ㄴ/은/는/ㄹ/을 지 싶다
Play[처음 만든 튀김이 바삭할지 싶어 긴장돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s18.mp3)
= I’m nervous, wondering if the fried food I made for the first time will be crispy

Play[밤낮없이 일하는 게 건강에 괜찮은지 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s19.mp3)
= I wonder if working day and night is okay for my health

Play[이 전단지가 합법적으로 배포된 것인지 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s20.mp3)
= I wonder if this flyer was distributed legally

Play[너무 빨리 승인이 나서 절차가 제대로 진행된 건지 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s21.mp3)
= The approval came so quickly that I wonder if the process was done properly

Usage 2: Intention

There is one final usage for 싶다. This usage, just like the previous one, requires the use of a grammatical principle that indicates speculation. Also as above, it is common to use ~ㄹ/을까. However, in this usage, the speaker is indicating their intention to do an action. For example:

Play[의무를 다 했으니 이제 퇴근할까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s22.mp3)
= Since I’ve fulfilled my duty, I think I’ll go home now

The use of 싶다 and the speculative grammatical principle gives a bit of nuance to these sentences. The intention isn’t very strong. The speaker is thinking about it, but hasn’t quite made up their mind yet. Below are many more examples:

Play[위조 서류에 대한 신고를 경찰서에 할까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s23.mp3)
= I’m thinking about reporting the counterfeit documents to the police

Play[수줍은 성격이지만 용기를 내서 발표해 볼까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s24.mp3)
= Even though I’m shy, I think I’ll muster up the courage to give the presentation

Play[주말에 전람회에 갈까 싶었는데 너무 바빠서 포기했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s25.mp3)
= I was thinking about going to the exhibition this weekend, but I was too busy, so I gave up

Play[광대뼈가 너무 도드라져 보여서 화장으로 가릴까 싶어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s26.mp3)
= Since my cheekbones look too prominent, I think I’ll cover them with makeup

Play[검은 정장을 입을까 싶었지만 너무 더울 것 같아서 그냥 밝은 색을 골랐어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Lesson173s27.mp3)
= I thought about wearing a black suit, but I figured it’d be too hot, so I chose a lighter color
