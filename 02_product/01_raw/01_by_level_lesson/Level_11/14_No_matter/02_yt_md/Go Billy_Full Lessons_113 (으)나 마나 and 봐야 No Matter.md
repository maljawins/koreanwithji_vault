### The Grammar Form (으)나 마나
This form is used to indicate that the result of an action is irrelevant or that the action itself is a waste of time. It carries the sentiment of "it's no use doing..." or "don't bother doing..." because the outcome is already known or won't change.

To conjugate this form, you attach **(으)나 마나** to an action verb stem. If the stem ends in a vowel, you use **나 마나**, and if it ends in a consonant, you use **으나 마나**. For example, **하다 (to do)** becomes **하나 마나**, and **읽다 (to read)** becomes **읽으나 마나**.

A critical restriction for this form is that it only attaches to positive action verb stems. It cannot be used with negative verb stems. Therefore, a construction like **안 하나 마나** is incorrect and does not exist in standard usage.

The instructor highlights a very common way to use this form by following it with the copula **이다 (to be)**, often as **예요**. This specifically emphasizes the pointlessness of the action. For instance, in a situation where it is raining so hard that an umbrella is useless, one might say:
> **이런 날씨에는 우산을 쓰나 마나예요.** / It's no use using an umbrella in this weather.

Other examples include:
> **어차피 제가 그 파티에 가나 마나 아무도 모를 거예요.** / Either way, even if I go to the party, nobody will know.
> **그 책은 읽으나 마나 도움이 안 될 거예요.** / Even if you read that book, it won't be helpful.
> **그렇게 하면 하나 마나예요.** / It doesn't make a difference if you do that.

### The Grammar Form 봤자
The form **봤자** is similar to **(으)나 마나** but has distinct characteristics. It originates from the **보다** grammar form (meaning "to try doing something"), so it literally translates to "even if you try doing..." It is used to express that no matter how much effort is put into an action, it is a waste of time or the result is already determined.

Unlike **(으)나 마나**, which is limited to action verbs, **봤자** can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs. To conjugate it, you use the **아/어/여** infinitive form of the verb and attach **봤자**. For example, **하다** becomes **해봤자**, **읽다** becomes **읽어봤자**, and the descriptive verb **맛있다 (to be delicious)** becomes **맛있어봤자**.

This form is frequently paired with the future tense ending **(을/ㄹ) 것이다** to assume a negative or futile outcome. For example:
> **지금 가 봤자 늦었을 거예요.** / Even if you go now, you'll be late.

The instructor notes that **늦다 (to be late)** is often used in the past tense (**늦었다**) to indicate being late for a specific event or deadline. Other examples provided include:
> **제 방은 정리를 해 봤자 금방 더러워질 거예요.** / Even if I organize my room, it'll get dirty right away.
> **비싼 옷을 입어 봤자 티도 안 나요.** / It doesn't make a difference if I wear expensive clothes, because nobody can tell.

The phrase **티가 나다** is explained as meaning something is apparent, obvious, or easy to notice. Finally, an example using a descriptive verb is shown:
> **감자가 맛있어 봤자 얼마나 맛있겠어요.** / Even if the potatoes taste good, I mean, how good could they be?
