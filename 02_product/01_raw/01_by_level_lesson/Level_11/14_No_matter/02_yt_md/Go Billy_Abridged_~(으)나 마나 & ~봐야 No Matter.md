### Overview
The instructor introduces two grammatical structures used to express the concept of "no matter" or "even if": **~(으)나 마나** and **~봤자**. Both forms convey a sense of futility, implying that regardless of whether an action is performed or a certain state exists, the result remains unchanged or the effort is useless. ### The Structure and Origin of ~(으)나 마나
The first form, **~(으)나 마나**, can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs. To conjugate, you attach **~나 마나** to a verb stem ending in a vowel and **~으나 마나** to a stem ending in a consonant. The instructor notes that because this form contains the letter ㄴ, it triggers standard irregular rules: for verbs ending in ㅂ like **덮다** (to be hot), the ㅂ is removed and replaced with ㅜ, resulting in **더우나 마나**. For verbs ending in ㄹ like **살다** (to live), the ㄹ is dropped, resulting in **사나 마나**.

The instructor explains the origin of this form to help students understand its literal meaning. It is a combination of two parts: **~(으)나**, which is an older or more formal way of saying "whether" (similar to **~든지**), and **마나**, which is the **~(으)나** form applied to the verb **말다** (to stop/not do). Therefore, the structure literally means "whether you do it or stop doing it," which naturally leads to the idiomatic meaning of "no matter if you do it or not, it's no use."

---

### Using ~(으)나 마나 in Context
The instructor provides several example sentences to show how this form implies that an action is a waste of time or that the outcome is already obvious. One common idiomatic use involves the verb **보다** (to see/look). Saying **보나 마나** implies that something is so obvious you don't even need to look to know the truth.

> **안경을 쓰나 마나 너무 멀어서 안 보여요.** / No matter if I wear glasses or not, it's too far away so I can't see it.
> **철수는 보나 마나 또 게임하고 있을 거야.** / It's obvious (whether you look or not) that Chul-soo will be playing games again.
> **물건이 좋으나 마나 파는 곳이 없으면 살 수가 없어요.** / No matter if the item is good or not, if there is no place that sells it, you can't buy it.
> **바람이 안 불어서 창문을 여나 마나 더워요.** / Because the wind isn't blowing, it's hot no matter if you open the window or not.

### The Alternative Form: ~봤자
The second form, **~봤자**, carries the same meaning and nuance as **~(으)나 마나**, implying that an action or state is useless or won't change the situation. The conjugation for **~봤자** follows the same rules as the standard **~어/아/여** (요) form. You take the verb stem, conjugate it as if adding **~요**, and then attach **봤자**. For example, **하다** becomes **해 봤자**, and **덥다** becomes **더워 봤자**.

While the two forms are largely interchangeable, the instructor notes a tiny distinction: **~(으)나 마나** explicitly contains the "whether you do it or not" logic, whereas **~봤자** focuses more on "even if you do it [to the fullest extent], the result is still [negative/useless]." This form is very frequently paired with the adverb **아무리** (no matter how) to add emphasis.

---

### Practical Examples of ~봤자
The instructor illustrates **~봤자** through scenarios involving price comparisons, physical limits like being full, and unavoidable delays due to traffic.

> **아무리 비싸 봤자 저것만큼 비싸진 않을 거예요.** / No matter how expensive it is, it won't be as expensive as that thing.
> **맛있어 봤자 배가 불러서 더 못 먹어요.** / No matter if it tastes good, I'm full so I can't eat any more.
> **영화가 길어 봤자 3시간 보다는 짧겠죠?** / No matter if the movie is long, it'll be shorter than three hours, right?
> **길이 막혀서 택시를 타 봤자 늦을 거예요.** / Because the road is blocked, even if you take a taxi, you're going to be late.
