## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor focuses on the word 나름, which has two distinct uses in Korean. While many learners are familiar with its grammatical function meaning "it depends on," this lesson highlights its second, more common usage as an adverb meaning "in its own way" or "all things considered." The instructor explains the structural differences between these two uses, provides common conversational examples, and offers a specific cultural nuance for how to "feel" the word rather than just translating it literally.

---

### The First Meaning: Depending On

The instructor briefly reviews the first usage of 나름 as a grammar point. To express that something depends on a certain factor, you take a noun and attach 나름, or take a verb stem, attach the nominalizing suffix ~기, and then add 나름. The sentence is typically finished with the copula 이다 (to be). For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하기 나름이다. Visually, the instructor writes these structures on the screen: **N + 나름** and **~기 나름 + 이다**. He notes that while other expressions exist for "depending on," this specific form implies that the noun or action mentioned is the main deciding factor. It carries the nuance of "it all depends on" rather than a simple dependency.

---

### The Second Meaning: In Its Own Way (Adverbial Use)

The core of the lesson is the second usage where 나름 functions as an adverb. In this context, it translates to "in its own way" or "all things considered." The instructor notes that you will see this used in two forms: **나름** by itself or **나름대로**. While both are used to mean "in its own way," 나름 is more common in everyday casual conversation, whereas 나름대로 is slightly more emphatic. 

A specific usage rule is mentioned regarding self-reference: when you are talking about yourself—for instance, saying you did something well in your own way—it is much more common to use 나름대로. The instructor suggests using 나름대로 for yourself and 나름 for other people or general situations as a helpful guideline, though both are heard in practice.

---

### Nuance and Translation Tips

The instructor emphasizes that "all things considered" is a natural way to think about the meaning in everyday situations. Because of this nuance, the expression is typically used with positive or neutral-to-positive meanings. He demonstrates this with three common examples written on the screen:

> **나름 잘 지내고 있어요.** / All things considered, I'm doing well.
> **나름 괜찮았어요.** / All things considered, it was okay.
> **나름 재밌었어요.** / All things considered, it was fun.

The instructor provides a "pro tip" for advanced fluency: do not always try to translate 나름 into English words. Often, it functions as a nuance that colors the sentence. To illustrate the "feeling" of the word, he uses a physical gesture—a slight shrug and a tilt of the head—to show that it conveys a sense of "it was pretty good, considering the circumstances." For example, **나름 재밌었어요** can simply be translated as "It was pretty fun." It adds a layer of subjective qualification to the statement.
