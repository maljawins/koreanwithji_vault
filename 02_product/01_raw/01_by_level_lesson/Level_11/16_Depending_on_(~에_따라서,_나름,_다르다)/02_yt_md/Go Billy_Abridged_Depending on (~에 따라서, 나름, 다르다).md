---

### Basic Expressions of Difference: 마다 다르다

The most basic way to say "it depends" is to literally say that each instance of a noun is different. This is achieved by taking a noun, attaching the particle `마다 (each/every)`, and following it with the verb `다르다 (to be different)`. For example, `사람마다 달라요` literally translates to "Each person is different," which is a common way to express "It depends on the person."

The instructor notes that this form is somewhat limited in English-to-Korean translation. It only works well when the English sentence "Each [noun] is different" makes logical sense. It cannot be used for complex situations like "It depends on how I'm feeling." Common examples include:
- **사람마다 달라요** / It depends on the person (Each person is different).
- **날마다 달라요** / It depends on the day (Each day is different).

---

### Versatile "Depending On": ~에 따라서 다르다

A more useful and versatile form for "it depends on [noun]" involves the particle `~에` followed by `따라서`, which is a conjugation of the verb `따르다 (to follow)`. The full structure is `[Noun]에 따라서 다르다`. This literally means "Following [noun], it is different" or "According to [noun], it is different."

The instructor highlights that `따라서` can also be used on its own in formal writing to mean "therefore" or "consequently," as it literally means "it follows that." However, in the context of "depending on," it is paired with `다르다`. This form can be used with a wide variety of nouns:
- **규칙에 따라서** / According to the rules.
- **시간에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on the time.
- **장소에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on the place.
- **색깔에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on the color.
- **가격에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on the price.
- **상황에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on the situation.

The verb `다르다` can be conjugated into various forms depending on the desired nuance, such as `다르죠`, `다르지`, `다르겠지`, or `다르네`.

---

### Depending on Actions: ~는지에 따라서 다르다

To express that something depends on a verb (e.g., "It depends on who did it" or "It depends on how you feel"), the instructor introduces the `~는지` form. This is attached to an action verb stem, followed by `~에 따라서 다르다`. This structure allows for different tenses.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate this construction:
- **누가 하는지에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on who does it.
- **누가 했는지에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on who did it (past tense).
- **어떤 색을 좋아하는지에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on which color you like.
- **어떻게 만들었는지에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on how you made it.
- **어떤 사람인지에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on what kind of person they are (using the descriptive verb `이다`).

---

### Advanced "Deciding Factor": 나름이다

The instructor introduces `나름` as an advanced way to express "depending on." Unlike `따라서 다르다`, which simply notes a difference, `나름` implies that the noun or action is the "deciding factor" for a decision or outcome.

When used with a noun (`[Noun] 나름이다`), it can sometimes sound unnatural if used too broadly. It is most effective when emphasizing that the specific nature of the noun matters. For example, `농담 나름이에요` means "It depends on the joke," implying that whether a joke is acceptable or not is decided by the joke itself.

A more common and natural use of this form is with action verbs using the `~기` nominalizer: `[Verb Stem]기 나름이다`. This often carries the nuance of "it depends on *how* one [verbs]," even without the word "how" (`어떻게`) being present.
- **그건 받아들이기 나름이에요** / It all depends on how you take/accept it.
- **한국어가 빨리 느는 것은 공부하기 나름이에요** / Improving your Korean quickly depends on how you study.

---

### Formal Alternative: ~느냐에 따라서 다르다

Another advanced variation for verbs is using the ending `~느냐` instead of `~는지`. The structure `[Verb Stem]느냐에 따라서 다르다` has the exact same meaning as the `~는지` version but sounds slightly more formal or rhetorical.

A specific conjugation rule is noted: if an action verb stem ends in `ㄹ` (like `살다`), the `ㄹ` is dropped before attaching `느냐`.
- **공부를 얼마나 하느냐에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on how much you study (Same as `하는지`).
- **어디에 있느냐에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on where it is (Same as `있는지`).
- **어디에 사느냐에 따라서 달라요** / It depends on where you live (From `살다`).

---

### The "Hanging" Metaphor: ~에 달려 있다

The final advanced form taught is `~에 달려 있다`. The verb `달다` means "to hang," and `달려 있다` means "to be hanging." Metaphorically, this means "it all hangs on" or "it all depends on" a certain factor.

This is very common and functions similarly to `나름이다` in that it points to a deciding factor. It can be used with nouns or with the `~기` verb form.
- **그건 받아들이기 달려 있어요** / It all depends on (is hanging on) how you accept it.

The instructor notes that `달려 있다` is generally more common in daily usage than `나름` when expressing that an outcome is pending on a specific action or condition.
