### Assuming and Pretending with 셈치다

The form 셈치다 is a contraction of 셈을 치다, though the full version is rarely used in speech. It primarily means to assume, think, or figure that something is a certain way, regardless of whether it is actually true. While the standard word for "to assume" in a formal sense is 가정하다, 셈치다 is used when you want to "pretend" or "just say" that a situation is true for the sake of moving forward. The word 셈 comes from the verb 세다 (to count), meaning a calculation or counting, while 치다 in this context means to accept something as true.

To use this form, you must change an action verb into an adjective form. For the present tense, you attach ~는 to the verb stem, such as 하는 셈치다. For the past tense, you attach ~은 or ~ㄴ, such as 한 셈치다 or 먹은 셈치다. This form is not used with the future tense. The instructor provides several practical examples:

> **이번엔 네가 이긴 셈치자.** / Let's just say you won this time. (Even if you actually lost).
> **너무 배가 불러서 이건 못 먹겠다. 먹은 셈 칠게.** / I'm so full I can't eat this. I'll just pretend I ate it.
> **너무 바쁘니까 이건 다 한 셈치고 다음 일을 하자.** / Since we're so busy, let's just say we finished this and do the next job.

A specific social use case involves treating a friend to a meal. If a friend tries to pay, you can say 받은 셈 칠게 `(I'll just pretend I received the money)` to tell them to put their wallet away.

---

### Comparisons with 치고(는)

The word 치다 can be used independently in the form 치고 or 치고는 (often shortened to 치곤). This attaches directly to a noun and translates to "for being a [noun]" or "considering it's a [noun]." It is used to indicate that something is out of the ordinary or unexpected given the category of the noun.

> **미국 사람 치고는 한국어를 잘 가르치시네요.** / For an American, you teach Korean well.

---

### The Quoting Form: ~고 치다

A more common and versatile way to express "supposing" or "assuming" is to use the quoting form followed by 치다. This works with any verb (action or descriptive) and even nouns using 이다. You take the plain form of the verb, add 고, and then conjugate 치다. For example, 하다 becomes 한다고 치다 (present) or 했다고 치다 (past). This carries the same meaning as 셈치다—to suppose, figure, or let's just say.

> **그냥 그렇다고 쳐.** / Just assume it's that way. / Just say it is.
> **만난 적 없다고 치고 다시 시작하자.** / Let's assume we've never met and start over.

---

### Taking a Risk: 속는 셈치고

The instructor highlights a very common idiomatic expression: 속는 셈치고. Literally, this means "assuming that I am being tricked/deceived" (from 속다, to be fooled). In practice, it translates to "taking a risk," "giving the benefit of the doubt," or "taking a leap of faith." It is used when you aren't sure if something will work or if someone is telling the truth, but you decide to do it anyway because you have nothing to lose.

> **속는 셈치고 써봤는데 효과가 너무 좋네요.** / I took a risk and tried using it, and the effect is really good.
> **속는 셈치고 한 번 먹어봐.** / Just humor me and try it once.
> **속는 셈치고 투자했는데 대박 성공했어요.** / I took a gamble and invested, and it was a huge success.
> **속는 셈치고 한 번만 더 믿어주세요.** / Give me the benefit of the doubt and trust me one more time.

---

### Calculations and Intentions with 셈이다

The final form, 셈이다, has two distinct meanings based on the tense of the preceding adjective-verb.

When preceded by a present or past tense adjective form, it means "I would say that," "it's practically," or "it's almost as though." It is similar to the expression ~와/과 마찬가지다 (to be the same as). It indicates a conclusion based on a "calculation" of the facts.

> **그런 셈이지.** / You could say that. / It's practically that.
> **할인을 받고 나면 한 병에 500원인 셈이에요.** / After the discount, it's practically 500 won per bottle.
> **결국 저 혼자 다 한 셈이지만, 괜찮아요.** / In the end, I practically did it all by myself, but it's okay.
> **눈 가리고 아웅 하는 셈이에요.** / It's like you're just hiding your eyes and saying "meow" (an idiom for burying your head in the sand/ignoring the obvious).

When preceded by the future tense adjective form (~을/ㄹ), the meaning changes completely to "to plan to" or "to intend to."

> **정말 거길 또 갈 셈이야?** / Do you really intend to go there again?
