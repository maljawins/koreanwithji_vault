## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammatical form ~(으)되, a specific type of "but" or "however" connector in Korean. While many learners are familiar with ~지만, ~(으)되 carries a unique pragmatic function: it is used when the speaker accepts or approves of the first statement but immediately follows it with a specific limit, condition, or requirement. This form is primarily found in formal, literary, or "fancy" contexts, making it distinct from everyday conversational connectors.

The lesson covers the conjugation rules for various verb stems, including past and future tenses, and highlights special forms for the copulas 이다 and 아니다. Beyond the primary "limit/requirement" usage, the instructor also explains a secondary, archaic usage of ~(으)되 used to introduce quotes in religious or historical texts.

---

### Meaning and Nuance: The "Requirement" But
The core meaning of ~(으)되 is "but," similar to ~지만, but it is restricted to situations where the second clause provides a limit or requirement for the first clause. The instructor explains that you are essentially saying, "Yes, I approve/accept this action, BUT only under these conditions." 

This is contrasted with a sentence like "I like kimchi but I don't like pizza," where no limit or requirement is being set; in that case, ~(으)되 would be incorrect. The instructor emphasizes that "requirement" is the key nuance. Because of its specific function and formal tone, it is often found in instructions, laws, or formal advice.

---

### Conjugation Rules
The instructor demonstrates that ~(으)되 does not follow the typical consonant/vowel pattern that many learners expect (like ~은/는). Instead, the rules are based on the final sound of the verb stem:

- **Verb stems ending in a vowel:** Attach ~되 directly. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 하되.
- **Verb stems ending in a consonant:** Attach ~으되. For example, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹되.
- **Special cases for 있다 and 없다:** These follow the consonant rule, becoming 있으되 and 없으되.
- **Past Tense:** Attach ~었/았/했으되 to the past tense stem. For example, 했으되 (did, but...).
- **Future Tense:** Attach ~겠으되 to the ~겠 stem. For example, 하겠으되 (will do, but...).
- **Copulas (이다/아니다):** These have a special irregular form. Instead of 이되, they become 이로되 and 아니로되.

---

### Usage in Context: Formal and Fancy Speech
The instructor notes that ~(으)되 is rarely used in casual conversation. It is considered "fancy sounding" or formal. It appears frequently in written Korean, formal speeches, or "old-style" speaking. 

The instructor provides several example sentences to illustrate the "limit/requirement" function:
- **한국 드라마를 보되 영어 자막은 보지 마세요.** / Watch Korean dramas, but (the requirement is) don't look at the English subtitles.
- **여행을 함께 갔으되 시간은 따로 보냈어요.** / We went on a trip together, but (the limit was) we spent our time separately.
- **다이어트를 위해서 저녁을 먹되 조금만 먹고 있어요.** / I am eating dinner for my diet, but (the limit is) I'm only eating a little bit.
- **철수는 능력이 있으되 운이 없어서 승진을 못해요.** / Chul-soo has skills, but (the limiting factor is) he has no luck, so he can't get promoted.

---

### Archaic Quoting Usage
A secondary, much rarer usage of ~(으)되 involves quoting. This is almost exclusively found in very old-style speech, such as religious scriptures or historical documents. In this context, it is used with verbs of "saying" or "speaking" (like 이르다) to introduce a quote. 

The instructor provides a biblical example:
> **예수께서 이르시되 도움이 필요한 자를 지나치지 말지요다.** / Jesus said, "Thou shalt not pass by those in need."

In this specific usage, the form doesn't necessarily imply a "but" or a "limit" in the modern sense; it serves as a formal, archaic introductory connector for a significant statement or commandment.
