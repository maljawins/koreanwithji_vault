### Function and Usage Rules

The primary function of -(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 is to add a new piece of information to a previous statement. It functions similarly to "not only... but also..." in English. For example, if someone is good at the Korean language, you can add that they are also knowledgeable about Korean history by saying:
> **한국어를 잘할 뿐만 아니라 한국 역사도 잘 아네요.** / Not only is [this person] good at Korean, but they also know Korean history well.

A crucial rule for using this expression is that the two clauses must share the same "polarity" or emotional direction. This means you must connect a positive trait with another positive trait, or a negative trait with another negative trait. You cannot mix them. The instructor provides a visual demonstration on a chalkboard: connecting "singing well" (positive) with "dancing well" (positive) is correct, but connecting "singing well" (positive) with "not dancing well" (negative) is grammatically and logically incorrect for this specific pattern.
- Correct: **노래를 잘 부를 뿐만 아니라 춤도 잘 추네요.** (Positive + Positive)
- Incorrect: **노래를 잘 부를 뿐만 아니라 춤은 잘 못 추네요.** (Positive + Negative)

---

### Conjugation Rules

The form of the expression changes depending on whether it is attached to a verb, an adjective, or a noun, and whether the stem ends in a consonant (받침).

For verbs and adjectives, if the stem has a final consonant (받침), you add -을 뿐만 아니라. For example, the verb 읽다 (to read) becomes 읽을 뿐만 아니라. If the stem does not have a final consonant, you add -ㄹ 뿐만 아니라. For example, 배우다 (to learn) becomes 배울 뿐만 아니라. The instructor notes that for stems already ending in ㄹ, such as 만들다 (to make) or 길다 (to be long), you simply add 뿐만 아니라, resulting in 만들 뿐만 아니라 and 길 뿐만 아니라. Irregular verbs like 걷다 (to walk) change to 걸을 뿐만 아니라, and 덥다 (to be hot) changes to 더울 뿐만 아니라.

For nouns, the conjugation is simpler because it does not matter whether there is a final consonant or not; you always use 뿐만 아니라. This is demonstrated with examples like 축구 (soccer) becoming 축구뿐만 아니라 and 어른 (adult) becoming 어른뿐만 아니라.

### Practice and Application

The instructor provides several dialogue-based examples to show how this grammar is used in natural conversation. When discussing a cake, one might say it is not only pretty but also delicious:
> **예쁠 뿐만 아니라 진짜 맛있어요.** / It's not only pretty but really delicious.

In a shopping context, the instructor advises that when buying a phone, one should look at more than just the price:
> **살 때 물건의 가격뿐만 아니라 성능도 잘 살펴봐야 해요.** / When buying [something], you should look closely at not only the price but also the performance.

The pattern can also be used in the past tense or to describe negative situations. For instance, if someone failed to both call and text, they would say:
> **전화를 안 했을 뿐만 아니라 문자도 안 보냈어요.** / Not only did I not call, I didn't send a text either.

Finally, the instructor uses a reading passage about a movie to show the noun form in action, noting that the film is suitable for both adults and children:
> **어른뿐만 아니라 아이들도 볼 수 있는 영화예요.** / It is a movie that not only adults but also children can watch.
