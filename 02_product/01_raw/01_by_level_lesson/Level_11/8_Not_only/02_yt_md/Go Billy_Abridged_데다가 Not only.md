## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces 데다가 as an intermediate-level grammar form used to express the concept of "not only A, but also B" or "in addition to." The lesson breaks down the literal etymology of the form to help students understand its underlying logic: the word 데 refers to a "place," "case," or "thing," while 다가 implies "on top of" or "in addition to." By combining these, the speaker describes one situation or action and then layers another significant piece of information on top of it. The instructor emphasizes that this form is not just for listing random facts; it is primarily used to provide a multi-layered explanation or reason for a specific outcome.

---

### Conjugation and Literal Meaning

To form this expression, a verb or adjective must first be conjugated into its attributive (adjective) form, followed by a space and then 데다가. The final 가 is optional, so it is common to see it shortened to just 데다. For example, the action verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하는 데다가, and the descriptive verb 춥다 (to be cold) becomes 추운데다가. 

The instructor explains that 데 literally means a "work," "case," or "thing." When you say 하는데, you are literally referring to "the task of doing something." The addition of 다가 adds the figurative meaning of "on top of." Therefore, the whole construction literally translates to "on top of the task/case of [Verb]." This applies to both actions and states of being.

---

### Usage Rules and Tone Consistency

A critical rule for using 데다가 is that the two pieces of information being joined (A and B) must share the same "tone." This means both parts should be positive or both parts should be negative. The instructor provides a humorous counter-example: one would not say "My foot hurts, and in addition to that, I won the lottery." Because a hurting foot is negative and winning the lottery is positive, they do not fit the pragmatic requirements of this grammar form.

Furthermore, the second piece of information (B) should make a "big difference." It is used to add an extra piece of information that actually matters to the context of the conversation. Simply listing two very similar, minor things (like a jacket being ripped in two slightly different spots) would feel unnecessary and unnatural with this form.

---

### 데다가 as a Reason or Explanation

While 데다가 translates to "in addition to," its most common function in a sentence is to provide a reason for something else. It often appears in "because" sentences (using forms like ~아서/어서). Instead of giving just one reason for an outcome, the speaker uses 데다가 to layer two reasons together to explain why a certain result occurred.

The instructor demonstrates this with the example:
> **이 식당은 맛있는 데다가 가까워서 자주 가요.** / I go to this restaurant often because, on top of being delicious, it is also close.

In this sentence, the outcome is "going often." The two reasons layered together are "it's delicious" (A) and "it's close" (B). The use of 데다가 highlights that both factors contribute to the final action.

---

### Further Examples of 데다가

The instructor provides several sentences to illustrate how the form layers reasons in various contexts:

> **홍삼은 맛있는 데다가 몸에도 좋아서 매일 먹고 있어요.** / I am eating red ginseng every day because not only is it delicious, but it is also good for my body.

> **저는 두꺼운 옷을 입은 데다가 장갑도 껴서 춥지 않아요.** / I am not cold because, on top of having worn thick clothes, I also put on gloves. (Note the past tense 입은 used here).

> **저는 영양제를 매일 먹는 데다가 운동도 하고 있어서 건강해요.** / I am healthy because, on top of taking supplements every day, I am also exercising.

> **오늘까지 시험 기간인데다가 과제도 많아서 잠을 못 잤어요.** / I couldn't sleep because, on top of it being exam season until today, I also had a lot of assignments.

---

### The Particle 에다가

The instructor transitions to a related but distinct form: the particle 에다가. This is the combination of the location particle 에 and 다가. Crucially, this is not the standard 에 used for simple direction (going to a house) or existence (being at a house). Instead, 에다가 is used to emphasize the specific location where an action "goes into" or "onto."

For example, instead of a simple 우체통에 넣어주세요 (Please put it in the mailbox), saying 우체통에다가 넣어주세요 adds emphasis to the destination of the action. Similarly, when asking someone to sign a specific spot on a paper, one might say 여기에다가 쓰세요 (Write it onto here) to emphasize the exact location.

When used with the words 여기 (here), 거기 (there), 저기 (over there), and 어디 (where), the 에 becomes optional. This results in common shortened forms like 여기다가 or even 여기다.

---

### 에다가 with Nouns for "In Addition"

Just like the verb form 데다가, the particle 에다가 can be attached directly to a noun to mean "in addition to [Noun]" or "on top of [Noun]." This is used when two events or things are piling up.

The instructor provides a complex example involving overlapping events:
> **출산에다가 여동생 결혼식까지 겹쳐서 정신이 없어요.** / On top of giving birth, even my younger sister's wedding overlapped, so I'm out of it (distracted/busy).

In this case, the speaker uses 에다가 to layer the "sister's wedding" on top of "giving birth" to explain why they are so overwhelmed (정신이 없다).
