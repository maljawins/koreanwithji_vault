## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the intermediate-level grammar form 뿐만 아니라, which translates to "not only... but also..." in English. To help students understand the nuance, the instructor breaks the form down into its three constituent parts: the particle 뿐 (only/just), the particle 만 (only), and the verb 아니다 (to not be). By combining these, the literal meaning becomes "it is not only just [A], but actually [B]." The lesson covers how to attach this form to nouns, action verbs, and descriptive verbs, including past tense variations and the specific usage with the copula 이다 (to be).

---

### Etymology and Literal Meaning

The form is composed of three distinct pieces of grammar. First is 뿐, a particle meaning "only" or "just," which singles out a subject and implies nothing else exists besides it. It carries a similar feeling to the adverb 그냥 (just) when downplaying something, such as saying it was "just" five dollars. Second is 만, another particle meaning "only." Combining them into 뿐만 adds extra emphasis, essentially saying "only just." Finally, 아니라 comes from the verb 아니다 (to not be). While similar to the connective 아니고요, 아니라 is used to show stronger contrast, often when correcting someone or explaining a situation with the nuance of "actually no." Therefore, the structure "A 뿐만 아니라 B" literally means "It is not only just A, but actually B."

---

### Usage with Nouns

When using this form with a noun, 뿐만 아니라 is attached directly to the noun without any spaces. This expresses "not only [noun] but also..." The second part of the sentence often includes the particle 도 (also/even) or words like 다 (all) to complete the "but also" thought. The instructor provides several examples of this construction:

> **저 뿐만 아니라 다른 사람들도 다 이해를 못했어요.** / Not only me, but other people also couldn't understand at all.

In this example, the instructor notes that 저 (me) is the noun, followed by the form, and the second clause uses 도 and 다 for emphasis. Another example demonstrates daily life usage:

> **아침 뿐만 아니라 점심도 못 먹었어요.** / I couldn't eat not only breakfast but even lunch.

The instructor explains that 아침 (morning) is used here as shorthand for 아침 식사 (breakfast). A third example shows how to describe a place:

> **여긴 라면뿐만 아니라 모든 게 다 비싸요.** / Here, not only the ramen but everything is expensive.

The instructor points out that 여긴 is a contraction of 여기는, and 모든 게 is a contraction of 모든 것이. Finally, an example involving memorization:

> **반 친구들 이름뿐만 아니라 전화번호까지 다 외웠어요.** / I memorized not only my classmates' names but even their phone numbers.

Here, the particle 까지 (even/until) is used in the second clause to show the extent of what was memorized.

---

### Usage with Verbs (Action and Descriptive)

To attach 뿐만 아니라 to a verb, the verb must first be changed into the future tense adjective form (also known as the prospective modifier). If the verb stem ends in a consonant, attach 을; if it ends in a vowel or the letter ㄹ, attach ㄹ. Unlike the noun form, there is a space between the modified verb and 뿐만 아니라.

> **이 요리는 다이어트에 좋을 뿐만 아니라 눈에도 좋아요.** / Not only is this dish good for a diet, but it's also good for your eyes.

In this case, the descriptive verb 좋다 (to be good) becomes 좋을. The instructor also demonstrates usage with action verbs:

> **저는 여기서 일을 할 뿐만 아니라 잠도 자요.** / Not only do I work here, but I also sleep here.

The verb 일하다 (to work) becomes 일을 할. The instructor notes that this form can also be used in the past tense. To do this, take the past tense verb stem (the part remaining after removing 어요/아요 from the polite past tense) and attach 을 뿐만 아니라.

> **저는 아침에 청소를 했을 뿐만 아니라 설거지도 했어요.** / Not only did I clean in the morning, but I also did the dishes.

The instructor provides a final complex example involving a lack of sleep:

> **수면 부족은 사람을 피곤하게 만들 뿐만 아니라 면역력을 떨어뜨려요.** / Not only does a lack of sleep make a person tired, but it also lowers the immune system.

The instructor highlights 면역력 (immune system) as a particularly difficult word to pronounce for non-native speakers.

---

### The "Not Only Is" Form: Noun + 일 뿐만 아니라

A specific variation involves using the copula 이다 (to be) with a noun. Because 이다 is a verb, it follows the verb conjugation rule: the stem 이 takes the modifier ㄹ, resulting in 일 뿐만 아니라. This is used to say "not only is it [noun]."

> **여기는 제 서재일 뿐만 아니라 방송 스튜디오예요.** / Not only is this my study, but it's also a broadcast studio.

The instructor explains that adding the 일 from 이다 provides the "is it" part of the translation. Another example shows how to describe a method:

> **이게 가장 효과적인 방법일 뿐만 아니라 유일한 방법이에요.** / Not only is this the most effective method, but it's also the only method.

The instructor notes that 효과적이다 (to be effective) and 유일하다 (to be the only) are used here to create a strong contrast.
