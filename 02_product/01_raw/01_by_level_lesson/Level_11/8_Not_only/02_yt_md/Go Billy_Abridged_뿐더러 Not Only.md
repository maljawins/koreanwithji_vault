### Overview
The instructor introduces the advanced grammar form 뿐더러, which is used to express "not only [A] but also [B]" or "in addition to [A], [B]." By understanding these individual pieces, learners can see how the combined form literally suggests "on top of only [this], there is also [that]."

The lesson covers the conjugation rules for verbs, adjectives, and nouns in both present and past tenses. ### Component 1: The Particle 뿐
Before diving into the full form, the instructor reviews 뿐. On its own, 뿐 is a particle attached to a verb stem that means "only," "just," or "it is only that." It establishes the base layer of the "not only" construction by limiting the first action or state before the second part of the grammar adds more information on top of it.

---

### Component 2: The Particle 더러
The second part of the form is the particle 더러. This is a rarely used particle in modern Korean that is equivalent to 에게, 한테, or 보고, all meaning "to" a person. However, 더러 has specific restrictions: it is only used with people, and it is almost exclusively used when quoting what someone said to that person. For example, 영희더러 사오라고 했어요 means "I told 영희 to go buy it." In this context, 더러 marks the person who is being addressed in a quote.

---

### The Origin and Meaning of 뿐더러
While the exact linguistic origin of 뿐더러 is unknown, the instructor proposes a logical breakdown. Since 뿐 means "only" and 더러 functions like "to" (similar to the particle 에), the form can be thought of as "in addition to only [A]." Just as the particle 에 can mean "in addition to" in certain contexts, 뿐더러 signifies that on top of the first state or action, another one exists. This results in the meanings "not only," "in addition to," or "on top of."

---

### Conjugation Rules
The form is created by attaching 을 뿐더러 or ㄹ 뿐더러 to a verb or adjective stem. This follows the same pattern as creating a future tense adjective.

- **Present Tense:** If the stem ends in a consonant, use 을 뿐더러 (e.g., 먹다 becomes 먹을 뿐더러). If the stem ends in a vowel or ㄹ, use ㄹ 뿐더러 (e.g., 하다 becomes 할 뿐더러).
- **Past Tense:** Attach the suffix to the past tense stem (e.g., 했다 becomes 했을 뿐더러, 먹었다 becomes 먹었을 뿐더러).
- **Nouns:** Use the copula 이다. In the present tense, this becomes 일 뿐더러. In the past tense, it becomes 이었을 뿐더러 or 였을 뿐더러 depending on the final consonant of the noun.

Unlike some other particles, there is no space between the verb stem and 뿐더러; it is written as one continuous block.

---

### Comparison with Similar Forms
The instructor notes that 뿐더러 is largely interchangeable with several other "not only" forms, though it sounds more formal or "fancy" and is more common in writing.

- **뿐만 아니라:** Much more common than 뿐더러. Notably, 뿐만 아니라 requires a space before it, whereas 뿐더러 does not.
- **~ㄴ/은/는 데다가:** Means "on top of" or "in addition to."
- **~고:** The simple "and" connector.
- **~거니와:** Another advanced form meaning "as well as" or "not only."

---

### Sentence Patterns and Natural Translation
In many sentences using 뿐더러, the second clause often includes a "because" form (usually ~아서/어서). This is because the speaker is often providing multiple reasons to explain why something is the case. While a literal translation might be "Not only is [A] true, but also because [B] is true, [C] happens," it is often more natural in English to translate it as "[C] is true not only because of [A], but also because of [B]."

Example sentences provided:
> **꽃차는 예쁠 뿐더러 맛도 있어요.** / Not only is the flower tea pretty, but it's also tasty.
> **이 신발은 흔하지 않을 뿐더러 편해서 인기가 많아요.** / These shoes are popular not only because they are rare, but also because they are comfortable.
> **이 집은 학교에서 가까울 뿐더러 버스정류장도 바로 앞에 있어서 인기가 많아요.** / This house is popular not only because it's close to school, but also because there's a bus stop right in front.
> **고구마는 섬유질이 많을 뿐더러 달고 맛있어서 간식으로 좋아요.** / Sweet potatoes are good as a snack not only because they are high in fiber, but also because they are sweet and tasty.
