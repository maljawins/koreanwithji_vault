## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form 거니와, which is categorized as an advanced and somewhat archaic connector. While it is rarely used in modern spoken Korean, it frequently appears in older literature, formal speeches, or historical contexts. The primary function of this form is to acknowledge the truth of a preceding statement while simultaneously adding related information, effectively functioning as a more formal version of the connector ~고 (and).

The lesson expands into several variations of this form, including (으)려니와 and 겠거니와, which incorporate nuances of intention, assumption, or future probability. The instructor emphasizes that despite its advanced label, the underlying logic remains a simple "A is true, and also B." The session concludes by distinguishing 거니와 from other similar-looking archaic forms like 거니, 거니하다, and the repetitive 거니... 거니 pattern.

---

### Basic Usage: 거니와

The most straightforward version of this form is created by attaching 거니와 directly to a verb stem. The instructor demonstrates this on the whiteboard: **Verb Stem + 거니와**. This form is used to express that the first part of the sentence is true and then provide additional information. It is functionally equivalent to the common connector ~고 (and).

Because it simply means "and" while acknowledging the first clause, it can often be swapped with ~고 without changing the core meaning, though the tone will shift from casual/standard to formal/archaic. When used with nouns, the instructor explains that you must first attach the copula 이다 (to be). This results in 두 가지 options: **Noun + 이고니와** (after a consonant) or **Noun + 고니와** (after a vowel).

---

### Intentions and Assumptions: (으)려니와

A variation of this form is (으)려니와, which is formed by adding (으)려 to the stem before 거니와. This version carries two specific nuances: it can indicate the speaker's intention ("I intend to do A, and also B") or an assumption/guess about a situation ("I assume A is true, and also B").

The conjugation rules follow standard patterns:
- **~으려니와** is used after a verb stem ending in a consonant.
- **~려니와** is used after a verb stem ending in a vowel.

When applied to nouns, the instructor notes that you use **Noun + 이려니와** (consonant) or **Noun + 려니와** (vowel). This allows the speaker to say "Not only is it [Noun A], but it is also [B]" with an added layer of assumption or future-leaning context.

---

### Future Probability: 겠거니와

The instructor introduces 겠거니와 as a combination of the future/conjectural marker ~겠 and the connector 거니와. This form is used when the speaker is making a strong guess or stating something that will likely happen. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 하겠거니와, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹겠거니와.

This form is essentially the same as using (으)려니와 for assumptions. The instructor humorously notes that the whiteboard starts to look like a complex web of connections, but the takeaway is that these forms all serve to link two ideas with the meaning of "not only A, but also B," while adding a flavor of probability or intention.

### Example Sentences and Analysis

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate how these forms function in context:

> **성공하려면 능력이 좋아야 하겠거니와 운도 좋아야 해.** / Not only would you need to be skilled in order to be successful, but you also have to be lucky.

In this sentence, 하겠거니와 acknowledges that skill is a requirement while adding that luck is also necessary.

> **영이는 매운 음식을 잘 먹거니와 뜨거운 음식도 잘 먹는다.** / Not only does Young-i eat spicy food well, she also eats hot food well too.

Here, the instructor shows that 먹거니와 can be replaced with 먹고. The use of 잘 (well/no problem) emphasizes her ability.

> **인왕산은 거리도 가깝거니와 높지 않아서 가볍게 등산하기 좋아요.** / Inwang Mountain is good for a casual hike because not only is it close, but it's also not tall.

This example uses 가깝거니와 to link two positive attributes of the mountain (proximity and low height).

> **손님을 부르려면 청소를 해야겠거니와 음식 준비도 해야 돼.** / If I'm going to invite guests over, not only would I have to do cleaning, but I also would have to prepare food.

This uses 해야겠거니와 to express the necessity of two different tasks.

### Related Archaic Forms

To prevent confusion, the instructor briefly explains other forms that look similar to 거니와:

- **~거니**: An archaic form used for confirming something about the past with emphasis. It is essentially the root of 거니와 without the additive particle 와.
- **~거니하다**: Used for making a guess or assumption about something in the past. The instructor explains that the 하다 here means "to think," so it translates to "I think/assume it was so."
- **~거니... ~거니**: This pattern involves repeating the form with two related or opposite verbs to show a repeating action. The instructor provides the examples **밀거니 당기거니** (pushing and pulling repeatedly) and **오거니 가거니** (coming and going back and forth).
