## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the noun 한, which originates from a Chinese character meaning "limit" or "boundary." In a literal sense, it refers to the furthest point one can go or the "end" of something. While it appears in common expressions like 한이 없다 (to be endless/limitless), this lesson focuses on its grammatical function as a conditional marker. When combined with action verbs, it creates a structure that translates to "as long as" or "unless" in English.

The core logic of this grammar point is that the first clause (A) is the absolute requirement or condition for the second clause (B) to occur. If the condition in A is not met, B will not happen. The instructor emphasizes that this form is essentially an "if" statement that defines the boundaries of a possibility.

---

### Basic Conjugation and Usage

To use this form, you take an action verb stem and attach the present tense adjective marker 는 followed by the noun 한. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하는 한. The instructor notes a standard irregular rule: verb stems ending in ㄹ drop the ㄹ before attaching the marker.

The resulting phrase ~는 한 functions as a modifier for the "boundary" or "limit," effectively saying "within the limits of doing [verb]." This is most naturally translated as "as long as" or "under the condition that."

> **주변의 도움이 없는 한 성공하기 힘들어요.** / Unless you have help from others (As long as we don't have help), it is difficult to succeed.

In this example, the instructor points out that while Korean uses the negative verb 없다 (to not have), English often translates this as "unless you have" to maintain natural flow.

---

### Examples of Conditional Boundaries

The instructor provides several sentences to illustrate how this form sets a strict requirement for a future outcome or a current state.

> **10시 버스를 놓치지 않는 한 제 시간에 도착할 거예요.** / As long as I do not miss the 10 o'clock bus, I will arrive on time.

Here, the condition for arriving on time is specifically not missing the bus. If that condition is violated, the result in the second clause is no longer guaranteed.

> **법이 바뀌지 않는 한 피해를 받는 사람이 계속 늘어날 거예요.** / As long as the law doesn't change, the number of people getting hurt will continue to increase.

The instructor demonstrates that this can also apply to broader social contexts, where the lack of change in a "limit" (the law) dictates a continuous outcome.

> **시간이 허락하는 한 끊임없이 도전하기로 해요.** / As long as time permits, let's keep trying constantly.

> **살아있는 한 우린 뭐든지 할 수 있어요.** / As long as we are alive, we can do anything.

In the final example, the instructor explains that the "limit" is life itself; within the boundaries of being alive, all possibilities remain open.

---

### Exceptions: 가능하다 and 관하다

While the ~는 한 form is typically reserved for action verbs, there are two major exceptions involving descriptive verbs that are frequently used in this pattern.

The first is 가능하다 (to be possible), which becomes 가능한 한. This is often translated as "as [adjective] as possible." The instructor notes that many Koreans mistakenly drop the 한 and just say 가능한, but the grammatically correct form requires the noun. However, for everyday speech, Koreans often prefer using the word 최대한 (to the maximum/as much as possible) instead.

> **결혼식에 가능한 한 많은 분들이 오셨으면 좋겠어요.** / I hope as many people as possible come to the wedding ceremony.

> **가능한 한 많은 것을 끝내려고 노력했어요.** / I tried to finish as many things as possible.

The second exception is 관하다 (to be about/refer to), which becomes 관한 한. This is used with the particle 에 to mean "when it comes to [noun]" or "as long as it's about [noun]."

> **나무에 관한 한 그분은 세계가 인정한 전문가임이 분명해요.** / When it comes to trees, it is clear that person is a world-renowned expert.

> **스웨덴은 복지에 관한 한 세계에서 따라갈 나라가 없어요.** / When it comes to welfare, there is no country in the world that can come close to Sweden.

---

### Comparison: 한 vs. 이상

The instructor compares 한 with the form ~ㄴ/은/는 이상, which was covered in a previous lesson. While both can be translated as "as long as" or "since," they carry different nuances.

The 한 form is an "if" form. It establishes A as the strict requirement for B. If A isn't true, B isn't true. In contrast, 이상 is a "because" form. It implies that because A is already a fact, B is a natural or expected consequence.

The instructor uses a comparison to show the difference:
- **날아갈 수 없는 한...** (If you can't fly...): Focuses on the condition of flying as a requirement for the result.
- **날아갈 수 없는 이상...** (Since you can't fly...): Focuses on the fact that you can't fly, making the result expected.

While the English translation might be the same, 이상 is more flexible, as it can be used with descriptive verbs and in the past tense, whereas 한 is generally restricted to the present tense of action verbs.
