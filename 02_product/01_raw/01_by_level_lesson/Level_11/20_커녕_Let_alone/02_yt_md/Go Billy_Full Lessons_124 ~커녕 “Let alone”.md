### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form ~커녕, which is used to express the English equivalent of "let alone," "forget," or "never mind." This is considered a slightly advanced grammar form, so the instructor recommends that learners already have a firm grasp of Korean basics—such as verb conjugation, adverb usage, and general conversation—before tackling this lesson.

The lesson follows a logical progression: starting with the basic construction for nouns, moving to the construction for action verbs, and finally demonstrating the form through various example sentences. Visually, the instructor uses a chalkboard-style background to display formulas and a "difficulty meter" to illustrate the conceptual relationship between the two parts of a ~커녕 sentence.

---

### Basic Construction with Nouns
To use this form with a noun, you must first attach the topic marker (은/는) to the noun, followed by 커녕. The choice between 은 and 는 depends on whether the noun ends in a consonant or a vowel.

- **Noun ending in a consonant:** Noun + 은 + 커녕. For example, the noun `집 (house)` becomes `집은 커녕`.
- **Noun ending in a vowel:** Noun + 는 + 커녕. For example, the noun `학교 (school)` becomes `학교는 커녕`.

The instructor emphasizes that while the topic marker is technically part of the construction, the entire unit functions together to set up the "let alone" comparison.

---

### Usage and Logic
The instructor explains the underlying logic of ~커녕 using a comparison between two points, A and B. In a sentence structure where "A-은/는커녕 B," the speaker is expressing that A is something that is already unlikely to happen. By using this form, the speaker emphasizes that B—which is something even more basic, simple, or easy than A—has an even lower chance of occurring.

Essentially, the grammar form is used to highlight that because even the "basic" thing (B) won't happen, the "greater" thing (A) is definitely out of the question. A visual chart on screen illustrates this: A is the noun attached to 커녕, and B represents the rest of the sentence. If B is impossible, then A is certainly impossible. While not strictly required, this form is most frequently used in negative sentences to provide this kind of emphasis.

---

### Construction with Action Verbs
While ~커녕 is primarily used with nouns, it can also be applied to action verbs by first transforming the verb into a noun. This is done by taking the verb stem and attaching the nominalizer ~기.

The formula shown on screen is: **Action Verb Stem + 기는커녕**.

This allows the speaker to apply the "let alone" logic to actions. For example, one might say "let alone helping" or "let alone eating" by using this nominalized form before 커녕.

---

### Examples and Context
The instructor provides several example sentences to show how the form functions in natural speech. He notes that the English translation is flexible—whether you use "let alone," "forget," or "never mind"—as long as the relationship between the two items remains clear.

> **소갈비는커녕, 빵 사 먹을 돈도 없어요.** / Forget beef ribs, I don't even have the money to buy bread.

In this example, beef ribs (A) are the "greater" item, and bread (B) is the "basic" item. Since the speaker can't even afford bread, they certainly can't afford beef ribs.

> **도움이 되기는커녕, 방해만 된 거 같아 죄송해요.** / I'm sorry because I think I was only a disturbance, let alone being helpful.

Here, the instructor uses the verb form `되다 (to become/be)`. Being helpful is the unlikely positive outcome (A), while being a disturbance is the actual negative reality (B).

> **사람은커녕, 개미 한 마리도 안 보인다.** / I can't even see one ant, let alone a person.

This emphasizes total emptiness; if even a tiny ant (B) isn't visible, a person (A) definitely isn't there.

> **이 성적이면, 취업은커녕, 졸업도 힘들 것 같네요.** / With these grades, I think even graduating will be difficult, let alone finding a job.

In this context, graduating (B) is the more immediate/basic goal compared to finding a job (A).

> **한자는커녕, 한글도 잘 못 써요.** / Forget 한자, I can't even write 한글.

Since `한글 (Korean alphabet)` is more basic than `한자 (Chinese characters)`, the inability to write the former makes the latter impossible.
