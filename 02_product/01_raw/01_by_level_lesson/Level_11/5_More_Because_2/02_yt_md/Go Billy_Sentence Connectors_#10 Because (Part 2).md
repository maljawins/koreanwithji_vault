## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson serves as an advanced continuation of the previous study on causal connectors in Korean. While basic forms like 아/어서 and (으)니까 are sufficient for general conversation, the instructor introduces a wide array of nuanced connectors that express specific conditions such as negative results, unintended consequences, extreme states, or quoting-based reasons. The learning arc moves from connectors used with action verbs to those used with nouns, and finally to formal or literary forms rarely heard in daily speech but essential for understanding news and official documents.

---

### Expressing Negative Results with 느라고(요)

The form 느라고 (often shortened to 느라) is used to express that a specific action resulted in a negative outcome. The instructor emphasizes several strict rules for this connector. First, it can only be used with action verbs that require conscious effort; it cannot be used with descriptive verbs or natural phenomena like the weather. Second, the subject of both clauses must be the same. Third, the resulting second clause is almost always negative and typically ends in the past tense. Because the result is inherently negative, this form cannot be used for commands or suggestions, as that would be logically inconsistent.

To conjugate, attach 느라고 directly to an action verb stem. If the stem ends in ㄹ, that consonant is dropped before adding the connector. The instructor provides several examples:

> **지금 공부하느라고 바빠요.** / I'm busy because I'm studying now.
> **물을 마시느라고 말을 못했어요.** / I couldn't talk because I was drinking water.
> **새 차를 사느라고 돈을 빌렸어요.** / I borrowed money because I was buying a new car.
> **눈물을 참느라고 힘들었어요.** / It was difficult because I was holding back tears.

---

### Unintended Consequences with 는 바람에

Similar to 느라고, the connector 는 바람에 indicates a negative result, but it carries the specific nuance that the result was unintended, accidental, or surprising. Unlike 느라고, it can be used with any action verb, even those not requiring effort. The sentence almost always ends in the past tense and, like other negative-result connectors, it is incompatible with commands or suggestions.

Conjugation involves attaching 는 바람에 to the action verb stem, with the ㄹ-drop rule applying to stems like 놀다 (to play), which becomes 노는 바람에.

> **버스가 늦게 도착하는 바람에 늦었어요.** / I was late because the bus arrived late.
> **눈이 오는 바람에 사고가 났어요.** / There was an accident because it snowed.
> **창문 닫는 걸 깜빡하는 바람에 침대가 비에 젖었어요.** / The bed got wet by the rain because I forgot to close the window.

---

### Listing One of Many Reasons: 고 해서 and 고 하니까

When a speaker wants to provide one reason while implying there are other factors involved, they use 고 해서 or 고 하니까. These function similarly to the basic 서 and 니까 forms respectively. Specifically, 고 해서 cannot be used for commands or suggestions, whereas 고 하니까 is perfectly acceptable for them. These forms are often paired with the particle 도 (also) or the form 기도 하다 for added emphasis.

The instructor notes that 기도해서 and 기도하니까 are common variations that add the nuance of "also" to the verb itself.

> **이제 여름이고 해서 수영복을 샀어요.** / Because it's summer (among other reasons), I bought a swimming suit.
> **오늘은 좀 피곤하기도 해서 일찍 집에 갔어요.** / Today I'm also a bit tired, so I went home early.
> **날씨도 덥고 하니까 냉면 먹을래요?** / The weather's also hot, so do you want to eat cold noodles?

---

### Quoting Reasons: 다고 해서 and 다고 하니까

These forms combine quoting structures with causal connectors. 다고 해서 (often shortened to 다고) and 다고 하니까 (shortened to 다니까) mean "because I heard" or "because someone said." In casual speech, the shortened versions are frequently used to express "just because," often followed by a negative statement like "it doesn't mean..."

> **웃어도 된다고 해서 웃었어요.** / They said it was okay to laugh, so I laughed.
> **비가 온다고 소풍이 취소되진 않아요.** / Just because it's raining doesn't mean the picnic is cancelled.
> **한 끼 굶는다고 안 죽어.** / Just because you skipped a meal doesn't mean you'll die.

---

### Blame and Stress: 탓에 and 통해

The word 탓 literally means "fault." When used as a connector (탓에 for verbs, 탓이다 to end a sentence), it explicitly blames the cause for a negative result. Similarly, 통해 (used after an action verb as 는 통해) indicates that the cause was something stressful, chaotic, or difficult. 통해 is often used with nouns like 전쟁 (war) or 난리 (chaos/panic).

> **늦게 나온 탓에 버스를 놓쳤어요.** / I missed the bus because I came out late (my fault).
> **옆에서 크게 떠드는 통해 하나도 못 들었어요.** / I couldn't hear at all because they were talking loudly next to me (stressful).
> **전쟁통에 잃어버렸어요.** / I lost it due to the war.

---

### Extreme States and Formal Causes: 나머지, 으로, and 인해서

The form 은/ㄴ 나머지 is used when a cause is so extreme that it triggers a result, usually negative. It is more common in writing. For nouns, the particle 으로 is the standard way to show cause (due to/because of). In formal or official contexts, this is expanded to 으로 인해서 (acting as an adverb) or 으로 인한 (acting as an adjective). A very formal, old-style alternative is 말미암아.

> **배고픈 나머지 차가운 피자를 먹었어요.** / I was so hungry that I ate cold pizza.
> **탈수로 쓰러졌어요.** / I collapsed from dehydration.
> **제 실수로 인해서 여러 사람이 고생했어요.** / Many people suffered because of my mistake.
> **홍수로 인한 피해가 커요.** / The damage due to the flood is big.

---

### Formal Writing and Invalid Reasons: 음으로 and 기로선이

The instructor introduces 음으로 as a form strictly reserved for formal instructions or official writing. Finally, he covers 기로선이 (shortened to 기로서 or 기로), which is used to acknowledge a reason but argue that it is not a valid justification for the resulting action. It translates to "even if it's because of..." or "regardless of..."

> **침대에서 떨어질 수 있음으로 주의하시기 바랍니다.** / You could fall from the bed, so please be careful.
> **아이가 거짓말을 했기로선이 그렇게까지 혼을 내요?** / Even if the child lied, are you going to scold them that much?
> **아무리 맛있기로선이 두 시간을 기다리고 싶진 않아요.** / No matter how delicious it is, I don't want to wait for two hours.
