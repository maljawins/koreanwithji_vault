## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the intermediate Korean grammar pattern -고 해서, which is used to provide reasons for an action or state. The instructor explains that this pattern is particularly useful when the speaker wants to suggest that the reason being mentioned is just one of several possible reasons, or when they want to list multiple reasons together. The lesson follows a structured path: defining the two primary usage cases, demonstrating the simple conjugation rules for verbs and adjectives, and providing extensive practice through transformation drills and situational dialogues.

---

### Two Primary Functions of -고 해서

The instructor identifies two main ways to use this expression. The first function is to indicate that the preceding content is just one of many reasons for the following result. It implies that while other factors might exist, the speaker is highlighting this specific one. An example shown on screen is:

> **날씨가 아직 춥고 해서 걱정이에요.** / I'm worried because the weather is still cold (among other things).

Another example illustrates a life milestone as a reason:

> **20살이 되고 해서 운전면허증을 땄어요.** / I got my driver's license because I turned 20 (and perhaps for other reasons too).

The second function is used when listing two or more explicit reasons. In this case, the speaker attaches -고 to the first reason and -고 해서 to the final reason in the list. This creates a sense of "this, and that, and so..." The instructor provides a sentence about a student resting after finishing their work:

> **단어도 다 외우고 숙제도 다 하고 해서 쉬고 있어요.** / I'm resting because I memorized all the words and did all my homework.

---

### Conjugation Rules and Practice

The conjugation for -고 해서 is straightforward because it does not change based on whether the verb or adjective stem ends in a vowel or a consonant (받침). The instructor presents a table on a chalkboard graphic showing that both 동사 (verbs) and 형용사 (adjectives) simply attach -고 해서 to the stem. However, it is noted that this pattern is not used with 명사 (nouns) directly in this form.

The lesson includes a transformation drill to reinforce this rule. The instructor lists several words and their conjugated forms:
- **오다 (to come)** becomes **오고 해서**
- **읽다 (to read)** becomes **읽고 해서**
- **걷다 (to walk)** becomes **걷고 해서**
- **배우다 (to learn)** becomes **배우고 해서**
- **만들다 (to make)** becomes **만들고 해서**
- **공부하다 (to study)** becomes **공부하고 해서**
- **크다 (to be big)** becomes **크고 해서**
- **작다 (to be small)** becomes **작고 해서**
- **길다 (to be long)** becomes **길고 해서**
- **덥다 (to be hot)** becomes **덥고 해서**
- **시원하다 (to be cool)** becomes **시원하고 해서**

### Situational Dialogues and Application

To help students apply the grammar in context, the instructor presents five dialogue scenarios. Each scenario uses -고 해서 to explain a choice or a feeling.

In the first example, a person explains why they started studying a foreign language:
> **새해도 되고 해서 외국어 공부를 시작했어요.** / I started studying a foreign language since it's the new year (and for other reasons).

The second example demonstrates listing multiple reasons for being at a cafe:
> **배도 고프고 목도 마르고 해서 학교 근처 카페에 왔어요.** / I came to a cafe near the school because I'm hungry and thirsty.

The third example explains a trip to Jeju Island:
> **네, 방학이고 해서 제주도 여행을 왔어요.** / Yes, I came on a trip to Jeju Island because it's vacation time.

The fourth example expresses hesitation about taking the subway:
> **한국어를 잘 못하고 해서 혼자 지하철 타기가 무서워요.** / I'm scared to take the subway alone because I can't speak Korean well (among other reasons).

The fifth example explains a shopping trip:
> **마트에서 물건을 싸게 팔고 해서 장보러 가요.** / I'm going grocery shopping because the mart is selling things cheaply.

The lesson concludes with a reading practice involving a longer dialogue about weekend plans. One speaker declines an invitation to Jeju Island, citing that they have work and want to rest (**일도 있고 쉬고 싶고 해서**), and further notes that Jeju is too far (**멀고 해서**). The instructor encourages students to write their own sentences in the comments using the prompt: "What did you do this weekend?" and provides a sample answer:

> **날씨가 따뜻하고 해서 산책하고 왔어요.** / I went for a walk because the weather was warm.
