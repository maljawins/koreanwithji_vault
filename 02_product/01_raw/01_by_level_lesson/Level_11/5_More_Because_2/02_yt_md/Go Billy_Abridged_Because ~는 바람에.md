## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form ~는 바람에, which is a specific way to express "because" or "as a result of" in Korean. While Korean has many ways to express causality, such as ~서 or ~니까, this particular form carries a very distinct nuance. It is used to describe a situation where an action or event occurs unexpectedly, like a sudden "wind" blowing in, which then leads to an unintended and typically negative consequence. The lesson covers the construction of the form, its strict grammatical constraints, and provides numerous examples to illustrate the "unfortunate surprise" aspect of its usage.

---

### Form and Literal Meaning

To create this form, one takes an action verb stem and attaches ~는 바람에. For example, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하, and with the addition of the suffix, it becomes 하는 바람에. The instructor explains the etymology of the word 바람, which literally means "wind" in Korean. Therefore, the form can be thought of as "in the wind of something happening." The visual metaphor is that an action occurred and caused a "wind" to blow, and because of that wind, a subsequent result followed.

---

### Usage Constraints and Nuance

The instructor emphasizes that ~는 바람에 is almost exclusively used with negative or bad causes and results. It is not suitable for positive situations, such as winning the lottery, unless that event is somehow viewed as a negative burden in a specific context. Furthermore, the result must always be something unintended or surprising to the speaker.

There are several critical grammatical rules for using this form. First, it is only used for things that have actually happened in the past; it cannot be used for future intentions, suggestions, or commands. Consequently, the final clause of the sentence must be in the past tense. Second, the form is technically only supposed to be used with action verbs. While some speakers might occasionally use it with descriptive verbs (adjectives) by attaching ~은/ㄴ 바람에, the instructor notes that this is grammatically incorrect and sounds very awkward to native speakers.

---

### Comparison with ~서

The instructor compares ~는 바람에 to the common causality marker ~서. Both forms share the restriction that they cannot be used for suggestions or commands. For instance, one cannot say "Because it is hot, go to the pool" using either form. However, ~는 바람에 is even more restrictive because of its requirement for an unintended, negative, and past-tense result. It is best translated in one's head as "as a result of [unexpected action], [unfortunate thing] happened."

---

### Sentence Examples

The instructor provides a variety of sentences to demonstrate the form in action, highlighting the "cause + 는 바람에 + past tense result" structure.

> **갑자기 차가 나오는 바람에 사고가 났어요.** / Because a car suddenly came out, there was an accident.

In this example, the "wind" is the car suddenly pulling out (negative/unexpected cause), and the result is an accident (negative/past tense).

> **어제 일찍 자는 바람에 이메일 확인을 못했어요.** / I couldn't check my email because I went to bed early yesterday.

Even though sleeping early isn't inherently "bad," in this context it caused the unintended negative result of missing emails.

> **택시를 타는 바람에 돈을 다 썼어요.** / I used all my money as a result of taking a taxi.
> **차가운 물만 나오는 바람에 샤워를 못했어요.** / I couldn't take a shower because only cold water came out.
> **물을 주는 것을 잊어버리는 바람에 꽃이 모두 시들었어요.** / The flowers all withered because I forgot to water them.
> **컴퓨터가 고장 나는 바람에 숙제를 못했어요.** / I couldn't do my homework because my computer broke.
> **전차를 잘못 타는 바람에 늦었어요.** / I was late because I took the wrong subway.
> **인형 솜이 다 빠지는 바람에 새 인형을 살 수밖에 없었다.** / I had no choice but to buy a new doll because all the stuffing came out.
> **개들이 한꺼번에 짖는 바람에 깜짝 놀랐어요.** / I was really startled because the dogs all barked at once.

The final example demonstrates that the cause doesn't always have to be a "bad" action, but the result must be unintended:

> **새로 나온 핸드폰을 사는 바람에 용돈이 하나도 안 남았어요.** / I don't have any pocket money left because I bought the newly released cell phone.

Buying a new phone is generally positive, but the speaker uses ~는 바람에 to highlight the surprising and somewhat negative consequence of having no money left over.
