## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form ~(은/ㄴ) 나머지, an advanced-level "because" structure that focuses on the aftermath or leftover result of an extreme action or state. While Korean has many ways to express causality, this specific form carries a unique nuance: it implies that the preceding situation was so intense or excessive that it naturally spilled over into a subsequent result, which is typically negative. The lesson covers the mechanical conjugation for both action and descriptive verbs, the logical constraints of the form, and its primary usage in written contexts.

---

### Conjugation and Mechanics

The structure is formed by taking a verb stem and attaching the adjective-making suffix ~은 or ~ㄴ followed by the noun 나머지. For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the instructor explains that you conjugate them to the present tense adjective form. For example, the verb 덥다 (to be hot) becomes 더운 나머지. Action verbs, however, are conjugated into their past tense adjective form. The verb 하다 (to do) becomes 한 나머지, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹은 나머지. Visually, the instructor demonstrates these transformations on a whiteboard, emphasizing that the result always looks like a past or present adjective modifying the word 나머지.

---

### Meaning and Nuance: The "Leftover" Logic

The word 나머지 literally means "the rest," "the remainder," or "the part that is left over," derived from the verb 남다 (to remain). The instructor explains the logic of the grammar: if you have "A 나머지 B," it means that after the extreme state of A occurs, the "leftover part" or the unavoidable aftermath is B. Because of this "aftermath" logic, the form implies that A is an extreme or excessive situation. It is not used for slight feelings; saying "because I was angry" with this form implies you were so incredibly angry that something happened as a result.

---

### Usage Constraints and Context

There are several key rules for using this form correctly. First, it cannot be followed by commands or suggestions. Since the second part of the sentence is a logical "leftover" result, it makes no sense to follow it with an imperative like "go get a sandwich" or a proposal like "let's eat pizza." Second, the subject of both parts of the sentence is typically the same person; the result B happens to the person experiencing state A. Third, while not strictly mandatory, the result is almost always negative or undesirable—referred to by the instructor as "the carnage" of the first action. Finally, this form is primarily found in written Korean rather than casual spoken conversation.

---

### Examples and Applications

The instructor provides several sentences to illustrate the nuance of excess leading to a negative result. In the first example, `Ice cream을 여러 개 먹은 나머지 배탈이 났어요` (Because I ate several ice creams, I got a stomachache), the use of 나머지 highlights that eating *several* bars was the extreme cause of the illness. Another example, `영화를 보다가 깜짝 놀란 나머지 팝콘을 쏟았어요` (Because I was so startled while watching the movie, I spilled my popcorn), shows an involuntary reaction to a sudden shock. 

Further examples include:
> **너무 피곤한 나머지 영화를 보다가 코를 골았어요.** / I was so tired that I snored while watching the movie.
> **사진이 전부 예쁜 나머지 하나만 고를 수가 없네요.** / Because the photos are all so pretty, I can't pick just one.
> **하루 종일 굶은 나머지 피자 한 판을 혼자 다 먹었어요.** / Because I was starving all day, I ate a whole pizza by myself.
> **너무 기쁜 나머지 모르는 사람을 끌어안았어요.** / I was so happy that I hugged a stranger.

---

### Comparison with ~는 바람에

The instructor briefly compares ~(은/ㄴ) 나머지 with ~는 바람에, another "because" form used for negative results. Key differences include: ~는 바람에 is only used with action verbs, whereas 나머지 works with both action and descriptive verbs. Additionally, ~는 바람에 always requires the final verb of the sentence to be in the past tense, and it is commonly used in both speaking and writing, unlike the more literary 나머지.

---

### Etymology Bonus

The lesson concludes with a deep dive into the word 나머지. It comes from 남다 (to remain). Historically, it was `남-` plus the suffix `-아지`. This suffix, also seen in words like 강아지 (puppy/small dog) or 송아지 (calf/small cow), indicates something small or a diminutive version. Thus, 나머지 originally referred to a "small bit that is left over." Over time, the spelling and pronunciation shifted from 남아지 to the modern 나머지.
