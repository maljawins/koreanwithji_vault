## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces two grammatical forms that share a common core element: 테. These forms are used when discussing the future or making suppositions about future events. The first form, 테니까, functions as a future-tense version of "because," while the second form, 텐데, functions as a future-tense version of "but" or "though." The lesson covers the conjugation rules for both, their placement within sentences, and how to adjust them for different levels of politeness.

---

### The Future Causative: 테니까

The form 테니까 is used to express a reason or cause (because) specifically when referring to the future. It is a combination of the future-oriented 테 and the causative marker 니까. To conjugate a verb into this form, the instructor explains that you take the verb stem and add either ㄹ (if the stem ends in a vowel) or 을 (if the stem ends in a consonant). After this, you attach 테니까.

When used in the middle of a sentence to connect two clauses, the form remains 테니까. However, it can also be used to end a sentence. In a sentence-final position, it can be used as-is or changed to 테니까요 to speak politely. The instructor provides an example of a weather-related warning:

> **오늘 비 올 테니까 우산을 가지고 가세요.** / It will rain today, so bring an umbrella.

In this example, the speaker is providing a reason (the future event of raining) for the suggestion to bring an umbrella. Another example demonstrates the sentence-final usage:

> **지금 나가도 너무 늦었을 테니까요.** / Because even if you leave now, you're already too late.

---

### The Future Contrastive: 텐데

The second form, 텐데, is used to express contrast (but or though) in the future tense. Similar to the previous form, conjugation involves taking the verb stem and adding ㄹ (for vowel endings) or 을 (for consonant endings) before attaching 텐데. 

The instructor notes a specific usage constraint for this lesson: this form is primarily presented as a way to end a sentence rather than appearing in the middle. To make the sentence-final form polite, you can use 텐데요. This form often implies a sense of worry, expectation, or a "but" that leaves the rest of the thought unsaid.

Example of a simple contradiction:
> **아닐 텐데.** / But it won't be.

Example of a statement regarding future availability:
> **내가 그 시간에는 집에 없을 텐데요.** / But I won't be home at that time.
