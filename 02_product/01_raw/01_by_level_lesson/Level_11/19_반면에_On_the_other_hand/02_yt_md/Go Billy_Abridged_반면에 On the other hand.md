## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces an advanced grammar form used to express contrast between two situations or facts. The lesson is framed around a scenario where the instructor is stuck at a subway station while traveling to see cherry blossoms, using the wait time to explain the mechanics and usage of the connector 반면에. This form is the equivalent of the English phrase "on the other hand" or "while [A], on the other hand [B]."

The lesson covers the etymology of the word, the specific conjugation rules for both action and descriptive verbs in present and past tenses, and provides several practical examples. The instructor emphasizes that while the form can technically start a sentence, it is almost exclusively used as a mid-sentence connector to link two contrasting ideas.

---

### Etymology and Basic Structure
The word 반면에 is derived from Hanja (Chinese characters). The instructor breaks it down on the whiteboard: 반 (opposite), 면 (face or side), and 에 (on). Literally, it translates to "on the opposite side" or "on the opposite face." In natural English, this is translated as "on the other hand."

While it is possible to start a new sentence with 반면에 (On the other hand, ...), the instructor notes that 99% of the time it is used in the middle of a sentence to connect two clauses. The general structure is: [Clause A] + [Verb/Adjective conjugated] + 반면에 + [Clause B]. The 에 at the end is optional, so you may see it simply as 반면, though the instructor notes that the version with 에 is more common.

---

### Present Tense Conjugation Rules
The conjugation depends on whether the stem belongs to an action verb or a descriptive verb.

For action verbs, you take the verb stem and attach 는 followed by 반면에. The instructor provides the example of 살다 (to live), which becomes 사는 반면에.

For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the conjugation follows the standard noun-modifying rules. You attach 은 if the stem ends in a consonant, or 니은 (ㄴ) if it ends in a vowel. For example, 안전하다 (to be safe) becomes 안전한 반면에. The instructor highlights irregular cases, such as 춥다 (to be cold) becoming 춘 반면에.

A simplified way to think about this is to treat the first clause as an adjective modifying the noun-like word 반면.

---

### Present Tense Examples
The instructor demonstrates the contrastive nature of the form through several sentences:

> **아빠는 청소를 잘 하는 반면에 요리를 못해요.** / While my father cleans well, on the other hand, he can't cook.

In this example, the action verb 청소하다 (to clean) is conjugated to 청소를 잘 하는 to contrast a skill with a lack of skill.

> **산을 돌아서 가는 건 안전한 반면에 시간이 오래 걸려요.** / While it's safer to go around the mountain, on the other hand, it takes a long time.

Here, the descriptive verb 안전하다 (to be safe) is conjugated to 안전한 to contrast safety with the disadvantage of time.

> **이 강아지는 크기가 작은 반면에 짖는 소리가 커요.** / While this dog's size is small, on the other hand, it has a loud bark.

This contrasts the physical size (작다) with the volume of the bark (크다).

> **이 TV는 기능이 다양한 반면에 가격이 비싸지 않아서 인기가 많아요.** / This television has various features, but on the other hand, it's not expensive, so it's popular.

This shows how 반면에 can be used to contrast a positive attribute (diverse features) with another positive attribute (low price) that might normally be expected to be a negative (high price).

---

### Past Tense Conjugation Rules
The contrast can also be applied to past events. The conjugation rules change to reflect the past tense.

For action verbs in the past tense, you attach 은 (after a consonant) or 니은 (ㄴ) (after a vowel) to the stem. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 한 반면에, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹은 반면에.

For descriptive verbs in the past tense, you use the past tense stem (ending in 었/았/였) and attach 던. For example, 하다 becomes 했던 반면에, and 맛있다 (to be delicious) becomes 맛있었던 반면에.

---

### Past Tense Examples
The instructor provides two examples to illustrate past tense usage:

> **지난주에는 계속 비가 온 반면에 이번 주는 덥고 건조하네요.** / While it kept raining last week, on the other hand, this week is hot and dry.

The action of raining (비가 오다) is put into the past tense (온) to contrast last week's weather with this week's.

> **그 음식점은 맛있었던 반면에 가격이 많이 비쌌어요.** / While that restaurant was delicious, on the other hand, the price was very expensive.

The descriptive verb 맛있다 (to be delicious) is conjugated to 맛있었던 to contrast the quality of the food with the high cost in the past.
