## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the intermediate-level grammar form 반면에, which is the Korean equivalent of the English expression "on the other hand." This form is primarily used to show a strong contrast between two statements or situations. The lesson covers the literal meaning of the word 반면, the specific conjugation rules for both present and past tenses, and how the form can be used either as a connector within a sentence or as a standalone transition to start a new sentence.

---

### Meaning and Usage of 반면에

The word 반면 literally translates to "opposing sides." When used as a grammar marker, it functions similarly to saying "while A is true, on the other hand, B" or "on one hand A, and on the other hand B." It effectively highlights the difference or contradiction between the first clause and the second clause. While the 에 at the end of 반면에 is technically optional, the instructor notes that it is most often included in natural speech and writing.

---

### Present Tense Conjugation

To use this form in the present tense, a verb must first be conjugated into its adjective form before attaching 반면에. The specific attachment depends on whether the verb is an action verb or a descriptive verb. For action verbs, the stem is followed by 는 반면에. For descriptive verbs, the stem is followed by 은 or ㄴ 반면에 depending on whether the stem ends in a consonant or a vowel.

The instructor provides several present-tense examples to illustrate this contrast:
- **친구가 요리를 잘 하는 반면에 설거지를 못해요.** / My friend cooks well, but on the other hand, he can't do the dishes.
- **강아지가 너무 예쁜 반면에 털이 많이 빠져요.** / The puppy is so pretty, but on the other hand, she sheds a lot.
- **제레미는 게임을 많이 좋아하는 반면에 잘하는 게임이 없어요.** / Jeremy likes games a lot, but on the other hand, there's no game he's good at.
- **철수는 친구가 많은 반면에 철수의 동생은 친구가 한 명만 있어요.** / Chul-soo has a lot of friends, but on the other hand, Chul-soo's younger sibling has only one friend.
- **철수는 목소리가 큰 반면에 말이 적어요.** / Chul-soo has a loud voice, but on the other hand, he doesn't talk much.

A vocabulary note is included for the puppy example: the expression **털(이) 빠지다** literally means "fur comes off," which is the standard way to say an animal "sheds" in Korean.

---

### Past Tense Conjugation

The grammar form can also be used to contrast events that happened in the past. For action verbs in the past tense, the stem is followed by 은 or ㄴ 반면에. For descriptive verbs in the past tense, the stem is followed by 았던 or 었던 반면에.

Examples of past tense usage include:
- **철수와 영희가 같이 운동을 한 반면에 철수만 체중이 늘었어요.** / Chul-soo and Young-hee exercised together, but on the other hand, only Chul-soo gained weight.
- **그 음식점은 맛있었던 반면에 가격이 많이 비쌌어요.** / That restaurant was delicious, but on the other hand, the price was very expensive.

---

### Using 반면에 as a Sentence Starter

Just like the English phrase "On the other hand," 반면에 can be used at the beginning of a sentence to contrast it with the previous sentence. This requires sufficient context from the preceding statement to make sense.

Example of standalone usage:
- **서해 바다에는 갯벌이 많아요. 반면에 동해 바다에는 절벽이 많아요.** / The west coast beach has a lot of mudflats. On the other hand, the east coast beach has a lot of cliffs.
