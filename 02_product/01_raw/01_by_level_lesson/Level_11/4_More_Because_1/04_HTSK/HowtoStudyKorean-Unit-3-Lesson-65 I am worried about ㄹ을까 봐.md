Lesson 65: I am worried about: ㄹ/을까 봐

Introduction

In the previous two lessons, you learned about a variety of situations where you can use “~ㄹ/을까(요).” In this lesson, you will learn another way that you can use this grammatical form. In this lesson, you will learn about adding ~ㄹ/을까 봐 to sentence to indicate that the speaker is worried about something. Let’s get started.

I am worried about/that…: ~ㄹ/을까 봐

Before we get into anything, let’s talk about the word “걱정하다.” This is actually a weird verb because there doesn’t seem to be any meaningful difference between ‘걱정하다’ and ‘걱정되다.’ Because ‘걱정하다’ ends in ‘하다,’ you would think that it would have the active meaning of “to worry” (as in, I am worrying my brother). Conversely, because ‘걱정되다’ ends in ‘되다,’ you would think that it would have the meaning of “to be worried” (as in, I am worried about my brother). The weird thing about this word, is that it appears that both 하다 and 되다 forms have the *passive*meaning of “to *be* worried” and not “to (*make*) worry.”

Though it appears you can use ‘걱정되다’ and ‘걱정하다’ to mean essentially the same thing, personally, whenever I talk about myself being worried, or somebody else being worried, I always say “걱정되다.”

The most common time you will see 걱정하다 being used is when telling somebody *not*to be worried.

For example:

Play[걱정하지 마!](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s1.mp3) = Don’t worry!

Using 되다 in this case is awkward:

걱정되지 마!

Anyways, now that we know that, let’s talk about the grammatical form ~ㄹ/을까 봐. When putting this at the end of a sentence, the speaker is indicating that he or she is worried about the clause before ~ㄹ/을까 봐 occurring.

걱정되다 is usually placed after ~ㄹ/을까 봐 in this usage. For example:

Play[시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s2.mp3) = I’m worried that I won’t do well on the exam
Play[비가 올까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s3.mp3) = I’m worried that it will rain
Play[그녀가 나를 좋아하지 않을까 봐 걱정돼](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s4.mp3) = I’m worried that she won’t like me
Play[논문을 여름까지 끝낼 수 없을까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s5.mp3) = I’m worried that I won’t finish my thesis by summer

If you want to say that you are worried about something in the past, you can conjugate the verb/adjective before  “~ㄹ/을까 봐.” For example:

Play[시험을 잘 못 봤을까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s6.mp3) = I am worried that I didn’t do well on the exam
Play[비가 왔을까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s7.mp3) = I am worried that it rained

You can also conjugate 걱정되다 into the past tense to indicate that you *were* worried:

Play[시험을 잘 못 봤을까 봐 걱정되었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s9.mp3) = I was worried that I didn’t do well on the exam
Play[비가 왔을까 봐 걱정되었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s10.mp3) = I was worried that it rained
Play[그녀가 나를 좋아하지 않을까 봐 걱정되었어](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s11.mp3) = I was worried that she didn’t like me

Often you will see 걱정되다 omitted in these types of sentences, and the sentence just ends with ~ㄹ/을까 봐. Usually when 걱정되다 is omitted, the meaning of it being there is implied in the sentence. For example:

Play[시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s13.mp3) = I’m worried that I won’t do well on the exam
Play[비가 올까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson65ns1.mp3) = I’m worried that it will rain
Play[그녀가 나를 좋아하지 않을까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s14.mp3) = I’m worried that she won’t like me
Play[논문을 여름까지 끝낼 수 없을까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s15.mp3) = I’m worried that I won’t finish my thesis by summer

In the [previous lesson](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/unit-3-intermediate-korean-grammar/unit-3-lessons-59-66/lesson-64/), you saw how ~ㄹ/을까 봐 can be added to the end of a sentence to indicate intention. You can see how one might be confused between these meanings because the grammar within these sentences is identical. For example:

Play[그 영화를 볼까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s12.mp3) = I intend to watch that movie
Play[시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s13.mp3) = I’m worried that I won’t do well on the exam

Just like many other things in Korean, the context can clarify the specific meaning. For example, what would happen if you heard the sentences above and confused their meanings

Play[그 영화를 볼까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s12.mp3) = I am worried that I will watch that movie
Play[시험을 잘 못 볼까 봐](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s13.mp3) = I am thinking about doing bad on the exam

As you can see, the sentences above (with their meanings changed) are illogical when used like that. Of course, you could find a context where they might be appropriate, but I’d be hard pressed to find a situation where you would want to say “I am thinking about doing bad on the exam.” By paying attention to the context and the information within the sentence, it is usually clear what meaning is being implied.

Below are many more examples:

Play[예산이 없을까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s16.mp3) = I’m worried that there isn’t enough budget
Play[다과가 부족할까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Lesson65ns2.mp3) = I’m worried that there won’t be enough refreshments
Play[작업을 완료할 수 없을까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s18.mp3) = I’m worried that we won’t be able to finish the job
Play[그 소리가 학생들을 방해할까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s19.mp3) = I’m worried that the sound will distract the students
Play[경찰관이 저에게 총을 쏠까 봐 걱정됐어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s20.mp3) = I was worried that the police officer would shoot me
Play[집주인이 부서진 거울을 찾아낼까 봐 걱정돼요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s21.mp3) = I’m worried that the landlord will find the shattered mirror

In each of the examples above, the word 걱정되다 can be removed with no real difference in meaning. These types of constructions are more likely to be used in the middle of a conversation. For example, if somebody asks you “Why are you worried?” you can eliminate 걱정되다 from the sentence because it can be assumed. However, when walking into a room and starting a conversation, you will be more likely to hear “걱정되다” used in these sentences.

예산이 없을까 봐
다과가 부족할까 봐
작업을 완료할 수 없을까 봐
그 소리가 학생들을 방해할까 봐
경찰관이 저에게 총을 쏠까 봐
집주인이 부서진 거울을 찾아낼까 봐

~ㄹ/을까 봐 can also be used as a connector between two clauses. We will talk about how to do this in the next section.

I am worried that/about, so…: ~ㄹ/을까 봐

In the previous section, you learned about adding ~ㄹ/을까 봐 to the end of sentences. You learned that these sentences can end with the word 걱정되다 or simply end with ~ㄹ/을까 봐. By placing another clause after ~ㄹ/을까 봐, you can create the meaning of “I am worried about/that, so….” For example:

Play[비가 올까 봐 우산을 가져왔어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s22.mp3)
= I was worried that it would rain, so I brought an umbrella

Play[비가 올까 봐 우산을 가져올 거예요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s23.mp3)
= I am worried that it will rain, so I will bring an umbrella

Notice that the tense of the sentence is determined by the final verb and not the verb that ~ㄹ/을까 봐 is added to. Below are many more examples:

Play[저의 여자 친구가 슬플까 봐 그녀를 위해 꽃을 샀어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s24.mp3)
= I am worried that my girlfriend will be sad so I bought her flowers

Play[그것이 너무 비쌀까 봐 돈을 많이 가져왔어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s25.mp3)
= I’m worried that it will be too expensive, so I brought more money

Play[집주인이 물이 흘러나올까 봐 지붕을 수리했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s26.mp3)
= The landlord was worried that water would flow out, so he fixed up (repaired) the roof

Play[손이 더러울까 봐 소독했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s27.mp3)
= I was worried that my hands were dirty, so I disinfected them

Play[그 사람이 내가 무례하다고 생각할까 봐 그에게 사과했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s28.mp3)
= I was worried that that person would think I am rude, so I apologized to him

Play[파도가 너무 높을까 봐 바다에 안 들어갔어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s29.mp3)
= I was worried that the waves would be too big, so I didn’t go into the water

Play[누가 저를 찾아낼까 봐 저는 방 한구석에 몸을 숨겼어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s30.mp3)
= I was worried that somebody would find me, so I hid in the/a corner of the room

Play[애기를 만지기 전에 손이 더러울까 봐 손을 소독약으로 씻었어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s31.mp3)
= Before touching the baby, I was worried that my hands were dirty, so I washed them with disinfectant

Play[나이가 많을 때 돈이 없을까 봐 오래전부터 돈을 모으기 시작했어요](https://www.howtostudykorean.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Lesson65s32.mp3)
