### The Formal Causal Marker 기에
The first form introduced is 기에. To conjugate this form, you simply take a verb stem and attach 기에. The instructor explains that while it shares the same meaning as 기 때문에 (because/since), it is a less direct version. While 때문에 explicitly points to a "reason" or "cause," 기에 provides the background cause in a more subtle way.

In terms of usage, 기에 is primarily a written form. It is considered quite formal and professional rather than slang. You are likely to encounter it in news broadcasts, formal speeches, or poetry. It is rarely used in casual daily conversation, where it would sound overly stiff or awkward. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate its formal tone:

- **한 번 사면 계속 사야 하기에 사지 않았어요.** / Because if you buy it once you have to keep buying it, I didn't buy it.
- **저만 먹을 수 없기에 넉넉하게 사왔어요.** / Because I can't be the only one who eats it, I bought plenty.
- **한 번 한 말은 취소할 수 없기에 조심해야 해요.** / Because you can't take back something you've said once, you have to be careful.
- **배움에는 끝이 없기에 꾸준히 배워야 합니다.** / Because there is no end to learning, we must learn continually.

---

### The Spoken Causal Marker 길래
The second form is 길래, which is conjugated the same way as 기에 (verb stem + 길래). Officially, 길래 is the casual, spoken equivalent of 기에. It is used significantly more often than 기에 in actual conversation. One unique feature of 길래 is that it can be used to end a sentence abruptly without adding any further polite endings like 요 or 입니다, though adding 요 is also perfectly acceptable to maintain a polite but casual tone.

The instructor highlights a specific natural usage pattern for 길래: it is most commonly used when there are two different subjects in the sentence. For example, "Because [Person A] did something, I [Person B] did this." While not a strict requirement, this "subject shift" makes the sentence sound much more natural to native speakers. Additionally, the clause following 길래 must describe an action that the speaker took, rather than a state or a future plan.

Examples of 길래 in context:
- **아무도 안 하길래 제가 했어요.** / Nobody was doing it, so (because of that) I did it.
- **많이 먹길래 맛있는 줄 알았지.** / Because you were eating so much, I thought it tasted good.
- **약을 먹어도 아프길래 병원에 다녀왔어요.** / Because it still hurt even after taking medicine, I went to the hospital.
- **아무 말도 안 하길래 이해한 줄 알았어요.** / Because you weren't saying anything, I thought you understood.
- **여자친구가 갑자기 보고 싶다길래 사진을 찍어서 보내줬어요.** / Because my girlfriend suddenly said she missed me, I took a photo and sent it to her.

---

### Grammatical Constraints for 기에 and 길래
The instructor outlines three critical "No" rules that apply to both 기에 and 길래. These forms cannot be followed by suggestions, commands, or the future tense. This means you cannot use them with endings like ~까요? (Shall we...?), ~세요 (Please do...), or ~ㄹ 거예요 (I will...). 

If a speaker wants to express a cause followed by a suggestion or a command, they must revert to a more basic form like 니까. The instructor explains that 기에 and 길래 are strictly used to describe things that are currently happening or have already happened. They provide the reason for a factual state or a completed action, rather than serving as a basis for future intent or instructions to others.
