### Overview

This lesson is the first part of a comprehensive guide to expressing "because" in Korean. The instructor explains that while many learners are familiar with basic forms, there are numerous ways to express cause and effect, each with its own nuance, level of formality, and grammatical restrictions. The lesson covers foundational connectors like 서 and 은니까, formal structures like 때문 and 기에, and more colloquial or specific forms like 길래, 가지고, and 만큼.

The instructor's approach is to categorize these connectors by their primary function: showing cause and effect, relating two actions, or describing the method of an action. Throughout the video, visual charts and comparison tables are used to highlight the differences between similar-sounding forms, such as the distinction between the objective reason provided by 때문 versus the subjective opinion expressed through 은니까.

### The Versatile 서 Form

The 서 form is one of the most common ways to connect sentences in Korean. It has three primary uses: showing cause and effect (because/so), showing related actions (and then), and showing the method of an action (by). To conjugate, you simply take the verb stem and attach 서. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 해서. The instructor notes specific conjugations for 이다 (to be), which becomes 이라서 after a consonant and 라서 after a vowel, and 아니다 (to not be), which becomes 아니라서.

When used for cause and effect, the structure is "A 서 B," meaning A causes B. An example provided is:
> **비가 와서 빨래를 안 했어요.** / Because it rained, I didn't do the laundry.

A critical rule for the 서 form is that it cannot be used with commands or suggestions. You cannot say "Because A, do B" using 서. Furthermore, the verb before 서 should not be conjugated into the past tense; the tense of the entire sentence is determined by the final verb. While some speakers might use the past tense (e.g., 했어서), it is considered grammatically incorrect or awkward. To express a past cause correctly, one should use the 거라서 structure, such as 한 거라서.

---

### Relating Actions and Describing Method with 서

Beyond cause and effect, 서 connects two actions that are closely related in time or purpose. This differs from the connector 고, which simply lists actions in order without implying a relationship.
> **친구를 만나서 점심을 먹었어요.** / I met a friend and (then) ate lunch (with them).

If you used 고 in the above sentence, it would imply you met a friend and then ate lunch separately. The instructor also shows how 서 describes the method of an action, often translated as "by."
> **집에 걸어서 갔어요.** / I went home by walking (I walked home).

Visually, the instructor demonstrates that while 서 is often optional in written or formal contexts for cause/effect, it is almost always removed when describing a method in natural speech.

---

### The Subjective 은니까 Form

The 은니까 form is used to express a reason that is the speaker's opinion. It is attached to verb stems ending in a consonant (은니까) or a vowel (니까). Unlike 서, this form can be used with past tense stems (했으니까) and is the standard choice for commands and suggestions.
> **시간이 없으니까 빨리 말할게요.** / Because I don't have time, I'll tell you quickly.

Because it reflects an opinion, it sounds awkward when used with basic emotions like "I'm sorry" or "I'm happy." Saying 늦으니까 미안해요 sounds like "I'm sorry because it's my opinion that I'm late." In these cases, 서 is the correct choice: 늦어서 미안해요. The instructor also mentions that adding 까 is optional but common in speech for emphasis; the shorter 은니 form sounds more formal or literary.

---

### Objective Reasons with 때문 and 덕분

때문 is used for objective reasons and often carries a nuance of "blame" when used with nouns. It is used as [Noun] 때문에 or [Verb]기 때문에.
> **모기 때문에 잠을 못 자요.** / I can't sleep because of mosquitoes.

The 기 때문에 form is quite formal and common in writing or news broadcasts. To avoid the negative nuance of "blame," speakers use 덕분 (thanks to) for positive causes.
> **선생님 덕분에 합격했어요.** / Thanks to the teacher, I passed.

---

### Advanced and Colloquial Connectors: 기에, 길래, and 가지고

The instructor introduces several advanced forms. 기에 is a literary, formal version of 기 때문에, often seen in writing. Its spoken counterpart is 길래, which is used when the speaker does an action based on an external observation or reason.
> **비가 오길래 우산을 썼어요.** / I used an umbrella because (I saw) it was raining.

가지고 (or the shorter 갖고) is a very common colloquial alternative to 서. It is informal and adds a bit more emphasis to the reason.
> **잠을 못 자가지고 머리가 아파요.** / My head hurts because I couldn't sleep.

---

### Fancy and Emphatic Forms: 만큼 and 은니만큼

Finally, the lesson covers 만큼, which usually means "as much as" but can mean "because" in a formal, "fancy" context. It is used after an adjective-conjugated verb.
> **오랫동안 기다린 만큼 재미있게 봤어요.** / Because I waited for a long time, I enjoyed watching it.

The form 은니만큼 is even more emphatic and formal, combining the subjective nuance of 은니까 with the weight of 만큼. It is used to express that because a certain condition is true, a specific result or opinion follows.
> **아직 젊으니만큼 기회가 많을 거예요.** / Because you're still young, you'll have many opportunities.
