## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form 느라고, which is a specific type of "because" or "due to" connector in Korean. Unlike general causal markers like ~서 or ~(으)니까, 느라고 has several strict constraints regarding the types of verbs it can attach to, the nature of the result it produces, and the tense of the verb stem itself. The lesson focuses on how to conjugate this form, the specific "negative" nuance it carries, and its shorter, more casual variant 느라.

---

### Conjugation and Sentence Placement

To use this form, you take an action verb stem and simply add 느라고. It can function as a sentence connector in the middle of a sentence, or it can be used at the very end of a sentence to provide a reason for something previously mentioned. When used at the end of a sentence, the polite ending 요 can be added (느라고요) or omitted for casual speech (느라고).

The instructor emphasizes a critical conjugation rule: you must only use the base action verb stem. You cannot use past tense stems or future tense stems. For example, with the verb 일하다 (to work), you would say 일하느라고. You cannot say 일했느라고 or 일하겠느라고. Even if the entire situation happened in the past, the past tense is marked only at the very end of the sentence, not on the 느라고 form itself.

---

### The "Negative Reason" Constraint

The most important characteristic of 느라고 is that it is used to explain a negative result or a situation that is "too bad." The action occurring before 느라고 is the reason why a negative outcome happened. For instance, you can say you are busy because you are working:

> **지금 일하느라고 바빠요.** / I am busy because I am working now.

The instructor notes that while the result must be negative, the speaker does not necessarily have to be emotional or complaining about it; it is simply a statement of a negative causal relationship. Because of this constraint, you cannot use 느라고 for positive results. You cannot say "I won the lottery because I bought many tickets" using this form, as winning the lottery is a positive outcome. However, you could say:

> **컴퓨터를 사느라고 돈을 다 썼어요.** / I spent all my money because I bought a computer.

In this case, having no money is the negative result caused by the action of buying a computer.

---

### Action Verbs and Personal Effort

느라고 can only be used with action verbs, and specifically those that involve a person (or agent) actively doing something that requires effort. It cannot be used with nouns; for nouns, one should use 때문에. Furthermore, it cannot be used with "natural" action verbs that don't require conscious effort, such as the weather. The instructor provides the example of rain: you cannot use 비가 오느라고 because the rain does not exert effort to fall.

Common examples of correct usage involving effort include:
- **음악을 듣느라고 네 말을 못 들었어.** / I couldn't hear what you said because I was listening to music.
- **어제 친구를 도와주느라고 파티에 못 갔어요.** / I couldn't go to the party because I was helping a friend yesterday.
- **시험 공부하느라고 오늘 철수를 못 만났어요.** / I couldn't meet Cheol-su today because I was studying for a test.

---

### The Question "뭐 하느라고?"

A very common idiomatic use of this grammar is the question 뭐 하느라고?, which translates to "What were you doing (that caused you to be late/miss something)?" In English, we often just ask "Why are you late?", but in Korean, asking what specific action caused the negative result of being late is very natural.

---

### The Variant: 느라

The instructor concludes by introducing 느라, which is a shortened version of 느라고. It carries the exact same meaning and follows the same rules. However, 느라 is slightly more casual and can feel a bit more emphatic or "stronger" than the full form. It is frequently used among friends. Note that unlike 느라고, you cannot add 요 to the end of 느라.
