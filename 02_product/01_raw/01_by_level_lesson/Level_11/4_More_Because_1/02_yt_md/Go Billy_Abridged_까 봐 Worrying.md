## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the grammar structure ~(으)ㄹ까 봐, which is primarily used to express doing an action because of a worry or concern. The instructor explains that while the form itself is a combination of the "would/could" question form and the verb "to see," its pragmatic function in Korean is to provide a reason for an action based on a potential negative outcome. The learning arc moves from basic conjugation and literal meaning to nuanced usage in sentences, common collocations, and a secondary usage meaning "thinking of doing something."

---

### Conjugation and Literal Meaning

The instructor demonstrates that ~(으)ㄹ까 봐 is formed exactly like the standard ~(으)ㄹ까요 question form. For verb stems ending in a consonant, you add ~을까 봐, and for stems ending in a vowel, you add ~ㄹ까 봐. This applies to both action verbs and descriptive verbs. For example, 갈까요 (shall we go) becomes 갈까 봐, and 먹을까요 (shall we eat) becomes 먹을까 봐. It can also be used in the past tense by adding the past tense marker before the form, such as 했을까 봐 (worried that someone did something).

Literally, the form combines the ~(으)ㄹ까 ending, which signifies "would" or "could," with the verb 보다 (to see). On screen, the instructor writes that it translates to "I see that something could/would [verb]." In English, this is often most naturally translated using the word "might." For instance, 눈이 올까 봐 literally means "I see that it might snow," which leads into the functional meaning of being worried that it might snow.

---

### Expressing Worry as a Reason

The core usage of this form is to explain the reason why a speaker did or is doing something. The instructor provides a structural formula: **A ~(으)ㄹ까 봐 B**. This means the speaker does or did action B because they were worried or afraid that situation A would happen or be a certain way. The instructor emphasizes that although the word for "worry" (걱정) is not explicitly in the grammar form, the worry is inherent in the structure.

This "worry" does not have to be a profound fear; it can be a light concern or a simple precaution. For example, one might use it to say they brought a jacket because they were worried it might be cold, or they didn't eat a certain food because they were worried their stomach might hurt. The instructor notes that while you can follow the form with verbs like 걱정하다 (to worry) or 긴장하다 (to be nervous), you can also follow it with any action verb that represents the response to that worry.

---

### Usage Rules and Variations

There are specific constraints on how this form can be used. The instructor highlights that you cannot use the future tense (like ~을 거예요) immediately after the ~(으)ㄹ까 봐 form. If you want to express a future thought or reason, you should revert to the ~것 같아서 (because it seems like) form. 

Additionally, the instructor points out two common variations. First, you can add the ~서 ending to make it ~(으)ㄹ까 봐서. This does not change the meaning and is entirely optional. Second, you can end a sentence directly with ~(으)ㄹ까 봐(요) to answer a "why" question or to trail off, implying the worry as the reason for a previously mentioned state.

---

### Secondary Meaning: "Thinking of Doing"

Toward the end of the lesson, the instructor introduces a second, distinct usage of ~(으)ㄹ까 봐. When used at the end of a sentence without a following action, it can mean "I'm thinking of maybe doing [verb]." This is derived from the "Shall I?" nuance of the ~(으)ㄹ까 form. 

The instructor explains that in this context, ~(으)ㄹ까 봐 is interchangeable with ~(으)ㄹ까 하다 or ~(으)ㄹ까 생각하다. For example, 내일 갈까 봐 means "I'm thinking about maybe going tomorrow." This usage expresses a tentative plan or a thought the speaker is currently entertaining.

---
