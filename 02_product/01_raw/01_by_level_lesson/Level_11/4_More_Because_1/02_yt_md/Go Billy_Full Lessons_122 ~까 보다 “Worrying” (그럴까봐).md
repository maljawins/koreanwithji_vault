## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the grammar form ~까 봐, which is used to express that an action was taken specifically because the speaker was worried about a potential negative outcome. The lesson begins with a short skit where Billy gives his bear, Keykat, a plant and warns her not to overwater it. Keykat immediately overwaters it, setting a context of concern and potential consequences. The instructor explains that this form functions as a specialized version of "because," limited strictly to reasons involving anxiety or worry.

---

### Construction and Conjugation

The grammar is built by taking a verb stem and attaching either 을 or ㄹ depending on whether the stem ends in a consonant or a vowel. This is followed by the syllable 까 and finally the word 봐. The instructor notes that 봐 originates from the verb 보다 (to see/look), which means learners might encounter variations of this form conjugated differently, such as ~까 봐요 or ~까 보네요, depending on the sentence's politeness level or specific nuance.

This form is versatile regarding tense. It can be used with a present tense stem, such as 할까 봐 (worrying that I might do), or a past tense stem, such as 했을까 봐 (worrying that I might have done). The choice depends entirely on the timeframe of the situation the speaker is worried about.

---

### Usage and Sentence Structure

The primary function of ~까 봐 is to explain the reason for an action when that reason is a worry. It is typically used in the middle of a sentence: the first part describes the source of the worry using ~까 봐, and the second part describes the resulting action or state. Because the form inherently implies worry, it is very frequently followed by the verb 걱정하다 (to worry) or other verbs related to taking precautions.

Alternatively, the form can be used at the end of a sentence to stand alone. In this case, it explains the reason for a previously mentioned action or state by implying "It's because I was worried that..." When used as a sentence ending in polite conversation, the speaker must attach the polite ending 요 to the end, resulting in ~까 봐요.

---

### Examples and Nuance

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate how the form translates into natural English while maintaining its "worry" nuance.

> **음식이 맛이 없을까 봐 걱정했어요.** / I was worried the food wouldn't taste good.

In the above example, the instructor points out that while the literal translation might feel repetitive in English (I worried because I was worried...), it sounds perfectly natural and common in Korean.

> **시험에 떨어질까 봐 열심히 공부했어요.** / I studied hard because I was worried I'd fail the test.

> **마크가 매운 음식을 못 먹을까 봐 햄버거를 사줬어요.** / I bought Mark a hamburger because I was worried he wouldn't be able to eat spicy food.

> **새로 산 핸드폰에 기스가 날까 봐 쓰고 싶지도 않아요.** / I don't even want to use my new cell phone because I'm worried it'll get scratched.

The lesson concludes with a humorous follow-up to the opening skit. Six months later, the plant has grown into a massive tree. Billy is impressed by Keykat's "green thumb" until he realizes the tree is actually a "Realistic Tree" made of plastic, costing $99.99. Keykat's lack of worry was justified because the plant was never alive.
