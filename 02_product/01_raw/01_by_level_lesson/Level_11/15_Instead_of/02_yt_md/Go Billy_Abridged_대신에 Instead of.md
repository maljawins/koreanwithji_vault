## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the word 대신, which is derived from Hanja. The character 대 (代) signifies replacing or exchanging something, while 신 (身) literally refers to a person or a body, though in this context, it broadly refers to the act of swapping one thing for another. The lesson covers how to use this word with nouns, action verbs, and descriptive verbs to express "instead of," "in exchange for," or "despite." The core concept is that 대신 marks the item or action that is being removed or replaced, while the subsequent part of the sentence introduces the replacement.

---

### Using 대신 with Nouns

When using 대신 with a noun, the structure is "Noun + 대신(에)." The instructor highlights a common mistake made by beginners: the word order is the reverse of English. In English, we say "B instead of A," but in Korean, the item being replaced (A) comes before 대신. Therefore, "A 대신 B" means "B instead of A." The particle 에 is frequently attached to 대신 (대신에), but it is completely optional and often omitted in natural speech.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this noun-based usage:
- **드라마 대신 영화를 봤어요.** / I saw a movie instead of a drama. (The drama is replaced by the movie.)
- **소고기 대신 돼지고기를 샀어요.** / I bought pork instead of beef. (Beef is replaced by pork.)
- **동물원 대신 박물관은 어때?** / How about the museum instead of the zoo?

A specific nuance is noted with the sentence **저 대신 전화를 받아주세요** (Please answer the phone instead of me). While "instead of me" is the literal translation, in English, we often say "for me" in this context. However, in Korean, this specifically means "taking my place" or "replacing me" to perform the action.

---

### Using 대신 with Action Verbs

To use this grammar with action verbs, the verb must be changed into an adjective form to describe the noun 대신. The instructor demonstrates the conjugation: take the action verb stem and attach ~는. This creates the form "Action Verb Stem + ~는 대신(에)."

Example sentences include:
- **한국 드라마를 보는 대신에 한국어 문법을 공부했어요.** / I studied Korean grammar instead of watching Korean dramas.
- **밤에는 노래를 부르는 대신에 책을 읽으세요.** / Read a book instead of singing a song at night.
- **케이크를 만드는 대신에 쿠키를 만드는 건 어때요?** / How about making cookies instead of making a cake?

The instructor also mentions an alternative method using nominalization (~는 것). Instead of ~는 대신, one can use ~는 것 대신. For example, **직접 가는 대신에** (instead of going in person) can also be expressed as **직접 가는 것 대신에**. Both are grammatically correct and used interchangeably.

---

### Meaning: "In Exchange For" or "In Return For"

While 대신 usually translates to "instead of" (where one action is canceled and replaced by another), it can also mean "in exchange for" or "in return for." In these cases, both actions actually happen. This usage is common when discussing favors or trades. The instructor explains that if the first part of the sentence involves a favor (often using the ~주다 form), it likely means "in exchange for."

- **제가 한국어를 가르쳐주는 대신에 스페인어를 가르쳐주세요.** / In exchange for me teaching you Korean, please teach me Spanish.
- **저녁을 만들어주세요. 대신에 설거지는 제가 할게요.** / Please make dinner, and in return, I'll do the dishes.

In the second example, 대신에 is used at the start of a new sentence to mean "In exchange [for that]..." or "In return..."

---

### Using 대신 with Descriptive Verbs

When used with descriptive verbs (adjectives), the meaning shifts slightly. The descriptive verb is conjugated into its adjective form by adding ~은 or ~ㄴ to the stem. While the literal meaning remains "in exchange for," the most natural English translation is often "despite" or "but."

The instructor uses the example **맛이 없다** (to taste bad/have no flavor):
- **이건 맛이 없는 대신에 몸에 좋아요.** / Despite this tasting bad, it's good for your body. (Literally: In exchange for this tasting bad, it is good for the body.)

This structure acknowledges a negative trait but balances it with a positive one, effectively saying "It has [Negative Quality], but it makes up for it with [Positive Quality]."
