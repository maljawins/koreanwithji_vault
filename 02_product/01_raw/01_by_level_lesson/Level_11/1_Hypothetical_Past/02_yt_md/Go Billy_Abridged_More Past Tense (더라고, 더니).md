### Overview

The instructor provides a deep dive into two advanced past tense grammar forms: ~더라고(요) and ~더니. These forms go beyond simple past tense by incorporating the speaker's personal experience, observation, and realization. The lesson focuses on how these markers signal that the speaker is recalling something they personally witnessed, heard, or felt, often adding a nuance of surprise or unexpected discovery. The instructor breaks down the conjugation rules for both descriptive and action verbs, explains the subtle differences in meaning when using different tenses, and provides practical examples to illustrate cause-and-effect relationships and contrasts.

### Recalling Personal Experience with ~더라고(요)

The form ~더라고(요) is used to express something the speaker personally witnessed, heard, or experienced in the past. It is created by taking a verb stem and attaching ~더라고(요). The instructor emphasizes that this is not the same as the quoting form ~다고. The core meaning can be thought of as "I recall that..." or "I realized that...". It emphasizes a moment of realization or surprise about a fact that was previously unknown to the speaker.

When using descriptive verbs (adjectives) with ~더라고(요), the speaker should generally use the present tense verb stem. For example, `맛있더라고요 (I recall it tasted good)` uses the present stem of `맛있다`. Even though the English translation is often in the past tense, the Korean form implies the past through the ~더~ element. Using the present stem can also imply that the state still holds true, such as saying `날씨가 춥더라고요` to mean "I recall the weather is/was cold" based on personal experience.

For action verbs, the speaker can use either the present or past tense stem before ~더라고(요), depending on the specific nuance. Using the present stem, like `나가더라고요`, focuses on the process of the action (I recall him leaving/in the act of leaving). Using the past tense stem, like `나갔더라고요`, focuses on the completed state (I recall he had left/was already gone).

A critical rule for ~더라고(요) is that it is generally not used for the speaker's own intentional actions. Saying `제가 갔더라고요` is awkward because you don't usually "recall" your own deliberate movements with surprise. However, it can be used for the speaker's own feelings or spontaneous reactions, such as `슬프더라고요 (I recall feeling sad)`.

---

### Connecting Past Observations with ~더니

The form ~더니 functions as a sentence connector that links a past observation to a subsequent result or contrast. The instructor explains that it is a combination of ~더~ (past experience) and ~니까~ (because/since). It is used when the speaker personally observed the first part of the sentence and is now commenting on what followed.

The first usage of ~더니 is to contrast two situations. It shows that something happened in the past (which the speaker witnessed), but now something different or unexpected is happening. For example, `철수가 원래 바쁘더니 이번 주는 시간이 많이 있어요` translates to "Chul-soo is normally busy (I've seen this), but this week he has a lot of time." This usage highlights a change that surprises the speaker.

The second usage of ~더니 emphasizes a cause-and-effect relationship or a "larger result." It indicates that because of the first action (which the speaker experienced or did), a specific result occurred. When the speaker is the subject of the first clause in this cause-and-effect context, they should use the past tense stem before ~더니. For example, `제가 2시간 동안 운동을 했더니 다리에 힘이 없어요` means "I exercised for two hours (past action), and as a result, my legs have no strength."

The instructor also notes that ~더니 is frequently paired with the ending ~군요, which expresses realization or surprise. For instance, `빌리가 한국어를 그렇게 좋아하더니 한국어 선생님이 됐군요` means "Billy liked Korean so much (I saw), and [look!] he became a Korean teacher." Finally, a brief mention is made of ~다더니, which is a combination of the quoting form and ~더니, used when recalling something someone else said.
