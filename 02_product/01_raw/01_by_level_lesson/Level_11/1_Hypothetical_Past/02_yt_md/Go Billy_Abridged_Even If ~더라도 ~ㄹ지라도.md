## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces two intermediate-level grammar forms used to express the concept of "even if" in Korean: **~더라도** and **~(을/ㄹ)지라도**. While both can be translated similarly to the basic **~아/어/여도** form, they carry specific nuances related to the likelihood of an event occurring and the strength of the speaker's statement. The lesson focuses on how to construct these forms, the specific hypothetical contexts they require, and how they differ from the standard "even if/although" markers.

---

### The Hypothetical "Even If": ~더라도
The first form, **~더라도**, is used specifically for hypothetical situations. The instructor emphasizes that this form is reserved for situations that the speaker believes are unlikely to happen. It carries the nuance of "I don't think this will happen, but even if it does..." To construct this form, you simply attach **~더라도** directly to a verb stem. For example, the verb **하다 (to do)** becomes **하더라도**, and **먹다 (to eat)** becomes **먹더라도**.

The instructor compares **~더라도** to the basic **~도** form (as in **해도**). While **해도** is attached to a conjugated verb, **~더라도** is attached to the stem. Beyond the grammatical construction, **~더라도** feels stronger and more emphatic than the simple **~도**. In most cases, **~더라도** can be replaced by **~도** without losing the basic meaning of the sentence, provided the specific "low probability" nuance is not critical to the speaker's intent.

Example sentences provided on screen:
> **실제 얼굴이 사진과 다르더라도 놀라지 않을게요.** / I won't be surprised even if your real face is different from the photo.
> **점심을 굶더라도 이 돈을 쓸 수 없어요.** / I can't use this money even if it means skipping lunch.
> **늦더라도 전화 주세요.** / Please give me a call even if you are late.
> **다이어트를 하더라도 식사는 꼭 하세요.** / Even if you go on a diet, make sure to eat.

---

### The Strong Acknowledgment: ~(을/ㄹ)지라도
The second form, **~(을/ㄹ)지라도**, is another way to say "even if," but it is much less common in daily conversation than **~도** or **~더라도**. It is constructed by taking a verb stem and adding **~을지라도** if it ends in a consonant, or **~ㄹ지라도** if it ends in a vowel. If a verb stem already ends in **ㄹ**, you simply add **~지라도**.

This form is often used when the second part of the sentence (Clause B) is the opposite of what might be expected from the first part (Clause A). Unlike **~더라도**, this form does not necessarily imply a low chance of the event happening; instead, it functions as a very strong, formal "even if." The instructor notes that this form is frequently paired with adverbs like **비록 (although/even though)** or **아무리 (however much)** to add further emphasis.

Example sentences provided on screen:
> **능력이 좋은 사람일지라도 이걸 혼자 다 할 수는 없어요.** / Even if you are a capable person, you cannot do this all alone.
> **잠을 3시간 잘지라도 숙제는 다 할 거예요.** / Even if I only sleep for 3 hours, I will do all my homework.
> **조금 부족할지라도 꾸준히 노력해서 성공하겠습니다.** / Even if it is a bit lacking, I will succeed by putting in constant effort.
> **눈에 보이지 않을지라도 많은 사람들이 일하고 있어요.** / Even if you can't see it, many people are working.

---

### Comparison and Limitations
The instructor concludes by contrasting these two forms with the standard **~아/어/여도** form. A key takeaway is that while you can almost always swap **~더라도** or **~(을/ㄹ)지라도** for the basic **~도** form, the reverse is not true. Specifically, **~더라도** and **~(을/ㄹ)지라도** cannot be used for things that have already happened. If you want to say "Even though I was tired (in the past), I did my homework," you must use the standard **~도** form attached to a past-tense conjugation. These intermediate forms are strictly for hypothetical or future-facing "even if" scenarios, not for established facts or past events.
