## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of turning action verbs into adjectives to modify nouns. In English, this is often achieved using relative pronouns like "who" or "that" (e.g., "a person who goes" or "a building that is in Korea"). In Korean, this is done by conjugating the verb into a modifier form that sits directly in front of the noun it describes.

The lesson covers the three main tenses for these modifiers: present, past, and future. The instructor emphasizes that if a student already knows how to conjugate descriptive verbs (adjectives) like 예쁘다 (to be pretty) into descriptive forms like 예쁜 (pretty), they are already familiar with the underlying concept. The primary difference lies in the specific endings used for action verbs versus descriptive verbs.

---

### Present Tense Modifier: ~는
To describe a noun with an action currently taking place or a general fact, the ending 는 is attached to the action verb stem. The instructor demonstrates the process by taking the verb 가다 (to go), removing the 다 to get the stem 가, and attaching 는 to create 가는. This can then be placed before a noun like 사람 (person) to mean "a person who goes."

A specific rule applies to verb stems ending in the consonant ㄹ, such as 살다 (to live) or 알다 (to know). In these cases, the ㄹ must be removed before attaching 는. For example, 알다 becomes 아는 (who knows). The instructor provides a practical example: "Is there someone you know here?" is translated as 여기에 아는 사람이 있어요?

The instructor also notes that the progressive form 고 있다 can be turned into a modifier by using the form 있는. For example, 공부하고 있는 원숭이 means "a monkey who is (currently) studying." While 있다 is technically a descriptive verb, it follows this 는 pattern in the present tense.

---

### Past Tense Modifier: ~(으)ㄴ
To describe a noun with an action that has already happened, the instructor explains the use of the ~(으)ㄴ ending. If the action verb stem ends in a consonant, the ending 은 is attached. If the stem ends in a vowel, a 니은 (ㄴ) is attached directly to the bottom of the stem.

The instructor illustrates this with the verb 쓰다 (to write). Since the stem 쓰 ends in a vowel, it becomes 쓴. When combined with 책 (book) and 사람 (person), the phrase 책을 쓴 사람 means "the person who wrote the book." Another example involves the ㄹ irregular verb 만들다 (to make). Following the rule to drop the ㄹ, the stem becomes 만드, and adding the ㄴ results in 만든. This is used in the sentence "The pasta that I made did not taste good": 제가 만든 파스타가 맛이 없었어요.

---

### Future Tense Modifier: ~(으)ㄹ
To describe a noun with an action that will happen in the future, the ending ~(으)ㄹ is used. Similar to the past tense, the choice depends on whether the stem ends in a consonant or a vowel. Stems ending in a consonant take 을, while stems ending in a vowel take 리을 (ㄹ).

For example, the verb 가다 (to go) becomes 갈. The instructor uses this to build the question "Are there many people who will go to the party tomorrow?": 내일 파티에 갈 사람이 많아요? Another example uses the verb 주다 (to give), which becomes 줄. This is applied in the sentence "I'm going to buy a gift that I will give to a friend today": 오늘 친구한테 줄 선물을 살 거예요.
