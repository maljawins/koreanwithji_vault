## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common area of confusion for Korean learners: the fundamental difference between how adjectives function in English versus Korean. While English adjectives are relatively static words that precede a noun, Korean adjectives are actually "descriptive verbs." This means they undergo conjugation and change their form based on their position and function within a sentence.

The lesson is structured to move from the basic concept of descriptive verbs to the practical rules of conjugation. It emphasizes the importance of learning the dictionary form of adjectives and explains how these words carry tense, unlike their English counterparts. Finally, the instructor introduces a small category of non-conjugating adjectives known as determiners to provide a complete picture of how description works in the Korean language.

---

### Adjectives as Descriptive Verbs
The most significant difference between the two languages is that Korean adjectives are verbs. In English, the word "small" remains "small" whether you say "a small cat" or "the cat is small." In Korean, the word changes. To say "a small cat," you use **작은 고양이**, but to say "the cat is small," you use **고양이가 작아요**. The instructor demonstrates that the root word **작다 (to be small)** transforms depending on whether it is describing a noun directly or acting as the predicate at the end of a sentence.

Because of this verbal nature, the instructor's primary advice is to always learn the dictionary form (the base form) of an adjective, which always ends in **다**. Learners should not memorize **작은 (small)** or **작아요 (is small)** as the primary word, but rather **작다 (to be small)**. Learning the dictionary form allows the student to apply conjugation rules to any situation, whereas learning a specific conjugated form limits the word's utility. For example, **작다** can become **작아요**, **작네요**, **작지**, or **작은데** depending on the desired nuance and sentence structure.

---

### Common Mistakes in Predicate Usage
A frequent error among beginners is trying to use the modifying form of an adjective with the copula **이다 (to be)**. The instructor highlights the incorrect sentence **똑똑한이에요** as a common mistake for "is smart." This happens because learners try to translate the English structure "is [adjective]" literally. In Korean, the "is" is already built into the descriptive verb. Therefore, you do not add **이다**; you simply conjugate the descriptive verb **똑똑하다 (to be smart)** into its polite present form, **똑똑해요**. The correct way to say "the cat is smart" is **고양이가 똑똑해요**.

---

### Tense in Korean Adjectives
Unlike English adjectives, which require a separate helping verb to show time (e.g., "was small," "will be smart"), Korean descriptive verbs have tense built directly into them. **똑똑하다** means "to be smart" in the present. To change the time, you conjugate the verb itself into the past or future. The instructor explains that when an adjective is used as a modifier before a noun, such as **똑똑한 고양이 (a smart cat)**, it is technically in the present tense—literally "a cat that is smart." While the lesson focuses on the present tense, the instructor notes that past and future modifying forms also exist to describe "a cat that was smart" or "a cat that will be smart."

---

### Non-Conjugating Adjectives: Determiners
While most Korean adjectives are descriptive verbs that conjugate, there is a small group of words that function more like English adjectives. These are called determiners. These words do not change their form and are always placed directly before the noun they describe. They cannot be used at the end of a sentence as a predicate. The instructor lists several common examples on screen:

- **무슨** (what)
- **어떤** (what kind of)
- **새** (new)
- **옛** (old)
- **모든** (every)
- **이 / 그 / 저** (this / that / that over there)

These words are used simply by placing them before the noun, such as **새 고양이 (a new cat)** or **모든 고양이 (every cat)**. Because they do not end in **다** in their base form, they are not verbs and do not undergo the complex transformations discussed earlier in the lesson.
