## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces a critical grammatical concept: transforming action verbs into adjectives to modify nouns. While previous lessons covered how to conjugate descriptive verbs (adjectives) to describe nouns—such as turning `좋다 (to be good)` into `좋은 사람 (a good person)`—this lesson focuses on how to take an action like "eating" or "speaking" and use it to describe a person or thing. This allows for more complex sentences like "the cat that eats pizza" or "the book that I wrote."

The lesson is structured by tense, covering the present, past, and future forms of these verbal modifiers. The instructor emphasizes that this transformation is essential for creating relative clauses in Korean, where the modifying action always precedes the noun it describes.

---

### Present Tense Modifiers: Verb Stem + 는

To describe a noun with an action happening in the present, the instructor demonstrates the rule: take the action verb stem and attach `는`. This rule applies regardless of whether the stem ends in a vowel or a consonant. For example, `먹다 (to eat)` becomes `먹는`, `하다 (to do)` becomes `하는`, and `가다 (to go)` becomes `가는`.

The instructor provides several example sentences to illustrate this in context:
> **피자를 먹는 고양이를 봤어요.** / I saw a cat that eats pizza.
> **한국어를 잘 하는 사람을 만나보고 싶어요.** / I want to meet someone who speaks Korean well.
> **여기서 제가 아는 사람이 없어요.** / There is nobody who I know here.

A specific spelling rule is highlighted for verbs ending in `ㄹ`: the `ㄹ` is dropped before attaching `는`. This is shown with the verb `알다 (to know)`, which becomes `아는`. This `ㄹ` drop rule applies across all three tenses covered in the lesson.

---

### Past Tense Modifiers: Verb Stem + 은/ㄴ

When the action describing the noun happened in the past, the conjugation changes based on whether the verb stem ends in a consonant or a vowel. If the stem ends in a consonant, attach `은`; if it ends in a vowel, attach `ㄴ`. The instructor provides the following transformations: `먹다` becomes `먹은`, `하다` becomes `한`, and `쓰다 (to write)` becomes `쓴`.

The instructor uses these examples to show the past tense modifier in action:
> **제가 쓴 책이 아주 좋아요.** / The book that I wrote is very good.
> **집에 일찍 간 사람이 없었어요.** / There was nobody who went home early.
> **제 친구가 준 과자가 맛있었어요.** / The snacks my friend gave me were delicious.
> **2시간 동안 공부한 학생이 누구였어요?** / Who was the student who studied for two hours?

---

### Future Tense Modifiers: Verb Stem + 을/ㄹ

To describe a noun with an action that will happen in the future, the rule is to attach `을` to stems ending in a consonant and `ㄹ` to stems ending in a vowel. Examples shown on screen include `먹다` becoming `먹을`, `하다` becoming `할`, and `믿다 (to believe)` becoming `믿을`.

The instructor demonstrates the future tense modifier with these sentences:
> **오늘 공부할 사람이 없어요.** / There is nobody who will study today.
> **같이 갈 사람을 찾고 있어요.** / I'm looking for someone who will go together (with me).
> **이건 제가 살 컴퓨터예요.** / This is the computer that I will buy.
> **부모님에게 받을 선물이 있어요.** / There is a present that I will get from my parents.
