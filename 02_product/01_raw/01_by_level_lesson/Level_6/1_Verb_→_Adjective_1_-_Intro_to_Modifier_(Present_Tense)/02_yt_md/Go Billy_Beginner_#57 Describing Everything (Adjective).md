## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on transforming descriptive verbs into noun modifiers, allowing them to function like adjectives in English. While previous lessons taught how to use descriptive verbs at the end of a sentence to say "The bag is small," this lesson teaches how to say "a small bag" by placing the conjugated verb directly before the noun.

The instructor breaks down the conjugation process into a simple two-step rule based on whether the verb stem ends in a vowel or a consonant. The lesson also covers essential irregular patterns for stems ending in ㅂ, ㄹ, and ㅎ, as well as the unique conjugation for the verbs 있다 (to exist/have) and 없다 (to not exist/not have).

---

### Basic Conjugation Rules
To use a descriptive verb before a noun, you must first identify the verb stem by removing 다. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach the consonant 니은 (ㄴ) to the bottom of the stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 은 to the end of the stem.

The instructor provides two primary examples to illustrate this. For the verb 좋다 (to be good), the stem 좋 ends in a consonant, so it becomes 좋은. This allows for sentences like 현우 씨는 좋은 사람이에요 (Hyunwoo is a good person). For the verb 바쁘다 (to be busy), the stem 바쁘 ends in a vowel, so it becomes 바쁜. This results in sentences like 현우 씨는 바쁜 사람이에요 (Hyunwoo is a busy person).

---

### Exceptions for ㅂ and ㄹ Stems
Specific rules apply to descriptive verbs with stems ending in ㅂ or ㄹ. For stems ending in ㅂ, you must remove the ㅂ and add 우 followed by the 니은 (ㄴ) modifier. For example, 맵다 (to be spicy) becomes 매운. This is demonstrated in the sentence 저는 매운 음식을 먹을 수 없어요 (I can't eat spicy food).

For stems ending in ㄹ, the ㄹ is removed entirely and replaced with a 니은 (ㄴ). The verb 길다 (to be long) follows this pattern, becoming 긴. An example sentence provided is 어제 아주 긴 영화를 봤어요 (Yesterday I saw a very long movie). Additionally, many stems ending in ㅎ will also remove the ㅎ and add 니은 (ㄴ), a pattern frequently seen with color words.

---

### The 있다 and 없다 Exception
The verbs 있다 (to exist/to have) and 없다 (to not exist/to not have) do not follow the standard descriptive verb rules. Instead of using 은 or ㄴ, they always use 는 when modifying a noun. Therefore, 있다 becomes 있는 and 없다 becomes 없는.

This applies to any compound verb ending in these words, such as 재미있다 (to be interesting/fun) and 맛없다 (to be tasteless/bad tasting). The instructor demonstrates this with the sentences 저는 재미있는 영화를 봤어요 (I saw an entertaining movie) and 맛이 없는 배를 먹었어요 (I ate a bad tasting pear).

---

### Practice and Application
The lesson concludes with a practice session where learners conjugate various descriptive verbs to describe specific nouns. The examples include:
- 유명하다 (to be famous) + 사람 (person) → 유명한 사람을 봤어요 (I saw a famous person).
- 지루하다 (to be boring) + 영화 (movie) → 어제 지루한 영화를 봤어요 (Yesterday I saw a boring movie).
- 작다 (to be small) + 가방 (bag) → 작은 가방을 샀어요 (I bought a small bag).
- 맛있다 (to be delicious) + 음식 (food) → 맛있는 음식을 먹었어요? (Did you eat delicious food?).
- 비싸다 (to be expensive) + 치약/칫솔 (toothpaste/toothbrush) → 저는 항상 비싼 치약하고 칫솔을 써요 (I always use expensive toothpaste and a toothbrush).
- 덥다 (to be hot) + 날씨 (weather) → 저는 더운 날씨를 싫어해요 (I dislike hot weather).
