## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Korean noun 모습, which translates to appearance, image, figure, or form. While the literal definition refers to the physical look of a person or object, the instructor explains that it often translates more naturally into English as "the way someone looks" or "how someone looks." The lesson progresses from using the word as a simple noun to using it in combination with action verbs to describe a person's manner or state while performing an action.

The learning arc begins with basic noun usage, moves into the grammatical construction required to modify 모습 with verbs, and concludes with complex sentence examples. By the end of the lesson, students should understand how to describe specific "images" of people, such as the way someone looks when they are smiling, dancing, or working hard.

### Basic Meaning and Usage of 모습
The word 모습 refers to the appearance, figure, form, or image of someone or something. It is used to describe the outward look or the "way" something appears at a specific time. The instructor provides a simple sentence to demonstrate this: 십년 뒤 내 모습을 상상해봤어요. In this sentence, 십년 means ten years and 뒤 means after. Together with 내 (my) and 모습, the phrase refers to "my appearance after ten years." The verb 상상하다 (to imagine) is used in the form 상상해보다 (to try to imagine), resulting in the full meaning: "I tried to imagine what I would look like in ten years."

Another example shows 모습 used to describe a captured image or a scene: 철수의 모습이 찍혔어요. Here, 찍히다 is the passive form of 찍다 (to take a photo/film), meaning "to be photographed" or "to be filmed." This sentence implies that an image of 철수 was captured, perhaps while he was doing something specific, like "the way 철수 looked while throwing away trash."

---

### Modifying 모습 with Action Verbs
The most common way to use 모습 is by pairing it with an action verb to describe the manner in which someone does something. To do this, the instructor explains that you must change an action verb stem into a present tense adjective (modifier). For most verbs, this is done by taking the verb stem and attaching 는. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 하는, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹는. There are exceptions for stems ending in ㄹ, such as 살다 (to live), where the ㄹ is removed before adding 는, resulting in 사는.

When combined with 모습, this construction translates to "the way," "the manner," or "how" someone looks while performing that action. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this:
- **웃는 모습** / The way (someone) smiles; a smiling appearance
- **춤추는 모습** / The way (someone) dances; a dancing appearance
- **열심히 하는 모습** / The way (someone) works hard; a diligent appearance

---

### Practical Sentence Examples
The instructor demonstrates how these modified forms function within full sentences to convey nuanced meanings about a person's behavior or state.

> **영희의 웃는 모습이 예뻐요.** / The way 영희 smiles is pretty. (Literally: 영희's smiling appearance is pretty.)

In this case, the speaker isn't just saying 영희 is pretty in general, but specifically that her "smiling image" or the way she looks when she smiles is beautiful.

> **철수가 춤추는 모습을 봤어요.** / I saw 철수 dancing. (Literally: I saw the image of 철수 dancing.)

This sentence emphasizes that the speaker witnessed the specific scene or manner of 철수's dancing.

> **열심히 하는 모습을 보여주세요.** / Show me you working hard. (Literally: Show me the appearance of you doing [something] diligently.)

This is often used as an encouragement or a challenge to show one's best effort or diligence.

The final, most complex example combines several grammatical points to describe a sentimental scene:
> **제가 준 선물을 받고 엄마가 아이처럼 좋아하는 모습을 보니 뿌듯했어요.** / I felt good seeing my mom enjoy the gift I gave her like a child.

The instructor breaks this down: 제가 준 선물 is "the gift I gave," 받고 means "received and," and 아이처럼 좋아하는 모습 refers to "the way (she) liked it like a child." The speaker uses 보니 (seeing that/since I saw) and concludes with 뿌듯하다 (to feel proud/pleased/satisfied). The sentence captures the specific "image" of the mother's joy, which caused the speaker to feel happy.
