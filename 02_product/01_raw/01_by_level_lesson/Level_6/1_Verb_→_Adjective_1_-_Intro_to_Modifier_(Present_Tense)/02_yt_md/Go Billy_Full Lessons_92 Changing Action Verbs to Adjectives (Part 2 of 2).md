## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson is the second part of a series focusing on how to transform Korean action verbs into adjectives (noun modifiers). While the previous episode introduced the basic conjugation mechanics for present, past, and future tenses, this episode explores a specific and highly common application: combining these modified verbs with the noun 것, which means "thing."

By attaching the modifier forms to 것, learners can create expressions that describe "a thing that one does," "a thing that one did," or "a thing to do." This structure is essential for defining objects by their function or for creating complex sentences where an entire action acts as the subject or object of a sentence. The instructor provides a comprehensive breakdown of this pattern across all three primary tenses with practical examples.

---

### Present Tense Modifier with 것
To describe something that one currently does or a general fact about an object's purpose, you use the present tense modifier. The formula shown on screen is: **Action Verb Stem + 는 것**. This translates to "something that one does" or "something that you do." In spoken Korean, the combination of 것이 or 것은 often contracts for easier pronunciation. For example, the instructor notes that 게 is a common contraction of 것이.

The instructor provides several examples of this in practice:
- **피자는 먹는 거예요.** / Pizza is something that you eat.
- **책은 읽는 거예요.** / A book is something that you read.
- **그 사람이 하는 게 뭐예요?** / What is it that he does?
- **숙제는 그냥 하는 거죠.** / Homework is just something you do.

A specific pronunciation tip is highlighted for the verb 읽다 (to read). When conjugated as 읽는, the regular sound change rules of Korean apply, and it is pronounced as 잉는.

---

### Past Tense Modifier with 것
To describe something that has already happened or to identify an object based on a past action, the past tense modifier is used. The construction is: **Action Verb Stem + 은/ㄴ 것**. You use 은 after a stem ending in a consonant and ㄴ after a stem ending in a vowel. This translates to "something that one did."

The instructor uses this form to show how it can function as a natural way to express "I did it" or "I didn't do it" by framing the action as a "thing."
- **제가 한 것이 아니에요.** / I didn't do it. (Literally: It is not a thing that I did.)
- **제가 한 거예요.** / I did it. (Literally: It is a thing that I did.)
- **누가 만든 거예요?** / Who made it? (Literally: It is a thing that who made?)
- **제가 먹은 것은 뭐였어요?** / What was it that I ate?

---

### Future Tense Modifier with 것
The future tense modifier is used to describe potentiality or necessity—things that are yet to happen. The formula is: **Action Verb Stem + 을/ㄹ 것**. You use 을 after a consonant and ㄹ after a vowel. This translates to "something to [verb]." This is particularly useful for expressing that there is "nothing to do" or "something to eat."

Examples provided include:
- **할 게 없어요.** / There is nothing to do. (Literally: There is no thing to do.)
- **먹을 것을 찾고 있어요.** / I am looking for something to eat.
- **볼 게 없어요.** / There is nothing to see.
- **읽을 것이 없어요.** / There is nothing to read.

The instructor concludes by encouraging students to practice these forms, as they are foundational for moving beyond simple subject-verb sentences into more descriptive and natural Korean.
