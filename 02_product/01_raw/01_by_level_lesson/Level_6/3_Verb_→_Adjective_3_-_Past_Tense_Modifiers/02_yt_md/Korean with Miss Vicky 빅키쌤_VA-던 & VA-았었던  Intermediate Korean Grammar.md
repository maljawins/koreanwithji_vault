## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson provides an in-depth exploration of the past tense modifiers 던 and 았/었던, comparing them with the standard verb past tense modifier ㄴ/은. The instructor emphasizes that while native speakers often use these forms interchangeably, they carry distinct nuances regarding the completion, regularity, and recollection of past actions or states. The learning arc begins with the three primary meanings of verb + 던, moves into the specific uses of adjective + 던, and concludes with the completion-focused 았/었던. The instructor uses a text-based approach with numerous example sentences and context-based tips to help students distinguish between these overlapping grammar points.

---

### The Three Meanings of Verb + 던

The modifier 던 is used with verbs to describe a past action with three specific nuances. First, it describes an action that started in the past but was left unfinished or undone. For example, 아빠가 먹던 피자 can mean "the pizza that Dad was eating (but hasn't finished yet)." Second, it refers to an action that someone used to do continuously, regularly, or repeatedly in the past but no longer does. Third, it describes an action that has been happening regularly up to the present or the last time remembered, often implying uncertainty about whether the action will continue.

In contrast, the standard past tense modifier ㄴ/은 (e.g., 아빠가 먹은 피자) simply indicates a one-time action that happened in the past without specific information about completion or regularity. The instructor notes that 던 is inherently "recollective," meaning it carries a feeling of looking back on a memory.

---

### Identifying the Meaning of 던 Through Context and Adverbs

Since 던 has three possible meanings, students must rely on context, time adverbs, and the nature of the verb to determine the intended sense. The instructor provides several examples: in the sentence "The TV broke, so I can't watch the drama I was enjoying anymore," 던 implies a regular action that was happening up to the point of the TV breaking. In "Will you finish the coffee I was drinking?", the context of "finishing" clearly points to an unfinished action.

Time adverbs provide the most obvious hints. Adding 어제 (yesterday) to a phrase like "the book mom was reading" usually points to an unfinished action because a specific day is designated rather than a long-term habit. Conversely, adverbs like 주말마다 (every weekend) or 평소에 (usually) signal regularity. The nature of the verb also matters; verbs like 살다 (to live), 다니다 (to frequent), and 좋아하다 (to like) are inherently continuous, so they rarely take the "unfinished" meaning of 던 and instead focus on past habits or states.

---

### Adjective + 던 and the Recollective Nuance

When used with adjectives, 던 narrows down to two meanings: a trait something used to have or a state that has been continuous up to a certain point. Adjectives describe traits, which are naturally continuous, so the "unfinished" meaning does not apply. For example, 뚱뚱하던 아빠 (Dad who used to be fat) describes a past state that has changed.

The instructor highlights that 던 is the primary way to create a past tense modifier for adjectives, as the ㄴ/은 suffix functions as a present tense modifier for adjectives (e.g., 친절한 means "kind" now, while 친절하던 means "who was kind"). Furthermore, using 던 with verbs can add a poetic or endearing "recollective nuance" even for one-time actions. This is frequently seen in song lyrics, such as "you who shyly handed me a flower," where 던 makes the memory feel more vivid and cherished than the plain past tense modifier.

---

### V/A-았/었던: Completed Past States and Actions

The form 았/었던 incorporates the past tense conjugation, emphasizing the completion of an event or state. It is used in two main ways. First, it describes a past state of affairs that is completely finished and no longer true, such as 어렸을 때 아주 작았던 지수 (Jisoo, who was very small when she was young). In these cases of past states, 았/었던 and 던 are often interchangeable.

Second, it describes a past event or action that was fully completed in the past. For example, 어제 읽었던 책 refers to a book the speaker finished reading, whereas 어제 읽던 책 would imply a book they started but didn't finish. While 았/었던 can sometimes be replaced by the general past tense modifier ㄴ/은, 았/었던 is more specific about the result being finished. For instance, 유학 간 곳 (the place he went to study) focuses on the action of going, while 유학 갔던 곳 (the place he had gone to study) implies he has completed his studies and returned.

---

### Summary Comparison: 던 vs. 았/었던 vs. ㄴ/은

The instructor concludes with a visual diagram (Venn circles) to summarize the overlaps.
- **Verb + 던:** Focuses on unfinished actions, past habits, or recollecting memories with an endearing nuance.
- **V/A + 았/었던:** Focuses on completed past states (interchangeable with 던) and completed past actions (interchangeable with ㄴ/은).
- **Verb + ㄴ/은:** The general past tense modifier focusing on the fact that an action occurred, regardless of completion or regularity.

Crucially, adjectives and the copula 이다 (to be) can only use 던 or 았/었던 for the past tense, as ㄴ/은 is reserved for their present tense modifier form.
