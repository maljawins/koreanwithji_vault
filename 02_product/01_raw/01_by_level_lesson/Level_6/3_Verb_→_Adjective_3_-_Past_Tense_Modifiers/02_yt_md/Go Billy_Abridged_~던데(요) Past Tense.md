### The Meaning and Nuance of ~던데(요)
The form ~던데(요) is a combination of two distinct grammatical elements. The first part, 던, is used when a speaker is recalling a past experience they personally witnessed or experienced. It carries the nuance of "I remember" or "I recall." The second part is the 데 form, which has three primary functions: adding soft contrast, introducing background information, or inviting a response from the listener.

The instructor visually illustrates the nuance of the 데 form by comparing it to holding one's palm up while speaking in English. This gesture signifies that the speaker is bringing attention to a piece of information or "holding out" an opinion for the listener to consider. When combined as ~던데(요), the speaker is essentially saying, "I remember this past event, and I'm bringing it up now to provide context or see what you think about it." This form can be used both in the middle of a sentence as a connector or at the end of a sentence (with the polite particle 요) to finish a thought.

---

### Conjugation Rules for Action and Descriptive Verbs
For action verbs, the speaker can attach ~던데 directly to the verb stem or use the past tense stem. While the 던 marker already implies the past tense, using the past tense stem (e.g., 했던데 instead of 하던데) emphasizes that the action was fully completed or finished at that specific point in the past. In most casual contexts, the past tense stem is not strictly necessary unless this specific emphasis on completion is desired.

Descriptive verbs follow a stricter rule: they are only used with the basic verb stem followed by ~던데. The past tense stem is not used with descriptive verbs in this construction. For example, to say something was cold, one uses 춥던데 (I remember it was cold) rather than a past-tense version of the stem. The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate these rules in practice:

> **다 먹어도 배고프던데요.** / I remember I was still hungry even after I ate everything.
> **생각했던 것보다 안 춥던데요.** / I recall it wasn't as cold as I thought.
> **거기 오늘 문 안 열던데.** / I remember they weren't open today (and I'm sharing this as background).

---

### Using ~던데(요) with Quoting Forms
An advanced way to use this grammar is by attaching it to a quoting plain form. This is a shortened version of saying "I heard that..." or "Someone said that..." followed by the retrospective marker. For regular verbs, this looks like ~ㄴ/는다고 하던데 shortening to ~ㄴ/는다는 던데 or simply ~ㄴ/는다던데.

When quoting with the verb 이다 (to be), the form becomes ~(이)라던데. This is derived from ~(이)라고 하던데. Similarly, the negative 아니다 (to not be) becomes 아니라던데. These forms allow the speaker to recall a past instance where they heard information from someone else, rather than witnessing the event directly. Examples include:

> **그것보다 이게 더 인기가 많다던데요.** / I heard that this one is more popular than that.
> **일찍 가야 살 수 있다던데 언제 갈 거예요?** / I heard you can only buy it if you go early, so when are you going?
> **이게 제일 큰 사이즈라던데 그래도 작아요.** / They said this is the biggest size, but it's still small.
