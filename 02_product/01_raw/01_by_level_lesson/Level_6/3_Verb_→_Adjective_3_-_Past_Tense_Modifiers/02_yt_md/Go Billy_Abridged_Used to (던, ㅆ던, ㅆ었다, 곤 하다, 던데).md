## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of "past tense modifiers" in Korean, focusing primarily on the syllable 던. He explains that in Korean grammar, the presence of ㄷ (specifically in forms like 던 or 탈) almost invariably signals the past tense. The lesson covers how to transform descriptive and action verbs into past-tense adjectives, how to express "used to" or "had done" using various structures, and how to use specific sentence endings to invite a response while referencing past observations.

The learning arc moves from the most basic adjectival use of 던 with descriptive verbs to more complex nuances involving action verbs, the "double past tense" (었었), the habitual marker 곤 하다, and finally the conversational ending 던데. Throughout the video, the instructor uses a whiteboard to contrast present and past states, often using a timeline diagram to illustrate whether an action is completed or potentially ongoing.

---

### Descriptive Verbs as Past Tense Adjectives
To describe a noun with a quality it *used to* have (a "was [adjective]" noun), Korean uses the past tense stem of a descriptive verb plus 던. For example, to change "a small house" (작은 집) into "a house that was small" (작았던 집), you take the verb 작다 (to be small), conjugate it to the past tense 작았다, drop the 다, and attach 던. This creates a past tense adjective.

The instructor demonstrates this with several examples. If you want to say "the coffee that was here," you use 여기 있었던 커피. He notes that using these past tense adjectives is only natural when the context specifically requires emphasizing that the state existed in the past; otherwise, it can sound forced. This form is also essential when using the "I think" structure (것 같다) for past states. While English says "I thought the house was small," Korean keeps the final verb in the present (같아요) and puts the past tense on the adjective: 집이 좀 작았던 것 같아요. This also applies to the copula 이다; for instance, "I think it was Cheolsoo" becomes 철수였던 것 같아요.

---

### Action Verbs with 던: Continuity and Frequency
When attached to an action verb stem, 던 functions differently than it does with descriptive verbs. It implies that an action was happening continually or frequently over a period of time in the past. It cannot be used for one-time past actions, such as buying a computer once. Because it implies frequency or ongoing status, it often translates to "was [verb]-ing" or "used to [verb]."

The instructor provides the example 저는 철수가 먹던 피자를 버렸어요, which means "I threw away the pizza that Cheolsoo was eating (or had been eating)." Similarly, "the thing I wanted" (continually wanting it in the past) is expressed as 제가 원하던 것.

---

### Contrasting 던 and 았던/었던
There is a subtle but critical distinction between using the plain stem + 던 and the past stem + 던 with action verbs. The instructor illustrates this using a timeline. The plain 던 form (e.g., 살던 집) implies the action happened in the past but leaves open the possibility that it is still happening now. However, the past-stem version (e.g., 살았던 집) explicitly signals that the action is finished and no longer occurs. Therefore, "the shirt I used to wear (but don't anymore)" is 입었던 셔츠, and "the house I used to live in (but moved out of)" is 살았던 집.

---

### The Double Past Tense (었었)
To express "used to" or "had done" as a sentence ending rather than an adjective, Koreans frequently use the "double past tense." This is formed by taking the regular past tense and adding another 었/았 before the final ending (e.g., 했었어요, 갔었어). While it doesn't literally contain a "habitual" marker, Koreans use this to emphasize that something is "really in the past" or to translate the English "used to." For example, instead of just saying "I lived in Korea" (한국에서 살았어요), saying 제가 한국에서 살았었어요 more naturally conveys "I used to live in Korea."

---

### Habitual Action with 곤 하다
The structure [Action Verb Stem] + 곤 하다 is used to describe actions that happen frequently. While it can be used in the present tense to mean "I frequently do X," it is most commonly seen in the past tense (곤 했다) to mean "I used to frequently do X." For example, 그 식당에서 일하곤 했어요 means "I used to work at that restaurant (frequently)."

The instructor notes that 곤 comes from a shortening of 고 (and) and 는 (topic marker). This form is often paired with adverbs like 가끔 (sometimes) or 자주 (often). However, it cannot be used with verbs that describe a state or a one-time occurrence; you wouldn't say "I used to frequently like her" (좋아하곤 했어요) because "liking" isn't a repetitive action in that sense. In most casual cases, the double past tense (했었어요) remains the more common way to say "used to" unless you specifically want to emphasize frequency.

---

### The Conversational Ending 던데(요)
The ending 던데 (or 다던데 for quotes) combines the past-tense 던 with the connector 데. It is used to make a statement based on something the speaker observed or heard in the past, while simultaneously "expecting a reply" from the listener. It functions similarly to "up-speak" in English, where a statement is made with a rising intonation to invite feedback or contrast.

- **던데:** Used for direct observations. 철수가 매운 음식을 안 좋아하던데요 means "I saw/noticed Cheolsoo didn't like spicy food (what do you think/what should we do?)"
- **다던데:** Used for reporting what someone else said (quoting). 철수가 매운 음식을 안 좋아한다던데요 means "I heard Cheolsoo doesn't like spicy food (so what now?)"

The instructor emphasizes that even though the ending looks like present tense (좋아한다), the 던 syllable within the suffix ensures the entire meaning is rooted in the past.
