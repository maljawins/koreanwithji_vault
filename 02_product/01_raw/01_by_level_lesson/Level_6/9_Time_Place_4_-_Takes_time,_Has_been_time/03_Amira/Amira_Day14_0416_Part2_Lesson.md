## Teaching Points

### 1. Contrastive Connector: ~는데 / ~은데 / ~ㄴ데
Ji explains that while ~지만 shows clear contrast ("however"), ~는데 is softer and more versatile, often providing background information or a mild "yet/and" connection.
*   **Action Verbs:** Always add ~는데 regardless of 받침.
    *   가다 → 가는데 (go, but/and)
    *   믿다 → 믿는데 (trust, but/and)
*   **Descriptive Verbs:**
    *   With 받침: Add ~은데. (작다 → 작은데 / small, but)
    *   Without 받침: Add ~ㄴ데. (예쁘다 → 예쁜데 / pretty, but)
*   **Irregulars:**
    *   ㄹ irregular: Remove ㄹ and add 는데. (살다 → 사는데 / live, but)
    *   ㅂ irregular: Replace ㅂ with 우 and add ㄴ데. (쉽다 → 쉬운데 / easy, but)
*   **Exceptions:** 있다 and 없다 always take ~는데 (있는데, 없는데) despite being descriptive.

### 2. Sentence Ending: ~데(요)
Ji teaches that ending a sentence with ~데(요) leaves the thought unfinished, implying contrast, surprise, or hesitation.
*   **Indirect Disagreement:** 비가 오는데요 (It's raining, though... implying we shouldn't go).
*   **Surprise:** 맛있는데요! (It's good, though! / I didn't expect it to be good).
*   **Hesitation:** 토요일에는 바쁜데... (I'm busy on Saturday, but... implying a soft "no").
*   **Curiosity:** 누구데? (Who is it, though? / Asking for more info).

### 3. Temporal and Conditional Markers: ~ㄹ/을 때 vs. ~면/으면
*   **~ㄹ/을 때 (When):** Refers to a specific, definite moment in time ("at the time when").
    *   집에 갈 때 전화해 (Call me when you go home).
*   **~면/으면 (If/When):** Refers to hypothetical or uncertain situations.
    *   도착하면 연락주세요 (Contact me if/when you arrive).
    *   **Hope/Desire:** Combined with 좋겠다 (it would be good).
        *   잘 지내면 좋겠어 (I hope you do well).
        *   생겼으면 좋겠어요 (I hope [it] appears/happens).

### 4. The Verb 되다 (To become / To be okay)
Ji introduces 되다 as a vital verb with four main categories:
1.  **To become:** 건축가가 되고 싶어요 (I want to become an architect).
2.  **To be done (Passive):** 집중이 잘 돼요 (Focus is done well / I can focus).
3.  **To work/function:** 컴퓨터가 잘 안 돼요 (The computer doesn't work well).
4.  **To be okay/acceptable:** 안 돼 (It's not okay/No).

### 5. Connectors + 되다 (Expressing Should/Must/May)
Korean uses 되다 with specific connectors to replace English helping verbs:
*   **~면 되다:** "It's okay if..." → You can just / You just have to.
*   **~면 안 되다:** "It's not okay if..." → You shouldn't.
*   **~도 되다:** "It's okay even if..." → You may / You can.
*   **~지 않아도 되다:** "It's okay even if you don't..." → You don't have to.
*   **~야 되다/하다:** "It's okay only if..." → You must / You have to.

## Lesson Flow

**Ji** begins by picking up from the previous discussion on the connector ~는데. He explains that it is more ambiguous than ~지만 and can mean "but," "and," or "yet" depending on the context. He walks through the conjugation steps on screen, highlighting how action verbs and descriptive verbs differ, and specifically notes the exceptions for 있다 and 없다.

**Amira** listens as Ji provides examples like 이 식당은 작은데 음식이 정말 맛있어요 (This restaurant is small, but the food is really good). Ji explains that in this case, it shows contrast, whereas in 친구를 만났는데 같이 밥을 먹었어요 (I met a friend and we ate together), it simply connects two actions.

**Ji** then introduces the sentence ending ~데(요). He explains that it functions like "though" in English, leaving something unsaid. He provides four scenarios: polite disagreement, surprise, hesitation, and curiosity. He contrasts this with ~지만, noting that ~지만 is a sentence connector only, while ~는데 can be both a connector and an ending.

**Ji** moves to temporal markers, starting with ~ㄹ/을 때. He explains it means "at the time when" and is used for specific moments. He then introduces ~면/으면, explaining its dual role as "if" and "when" for hypothetical situations. He shows how to use it with 좋겠다 to express hope, such as 빨리 여자친구가 생겼으면 좋겠어요 (I hope I get a girlfriend soon).

**Ji** introduces the verb 되다, explaining its importance in Korean grammar. He details its four meanings (become, be done, work, be okay). He then shows how 되다 combines with the previously learned connectors (~면, ~도, ~야) to create meanings like "should," "must," and "may." For example, 샤워를 해야 개운해요 (It's refreshing only if you take a shower).

**Amira** comments that while the material makes sense, it is "a lot" to remember. Ji reassures her that the assignments will help reinforce the concepts.
