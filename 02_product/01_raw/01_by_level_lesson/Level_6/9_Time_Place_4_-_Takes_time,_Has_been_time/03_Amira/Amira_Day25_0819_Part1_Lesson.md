## Teaching Points

- **Direct Quotations (Plain Form):** Ji clarifies that while direct quotations are often taught as formal, they are not uncommon in speech. He corrects Amira's use of "물었어다," explaining that one cannot combine a past tense marker with the plain form ending "~다" in that manner. For written-style pronouns like "그" (he), the plain form "물었다" is the most natural choice.
- **Vocabulary Nuance (나아지다 vs 실력):** Ji explains that while "나아지면" means "when I get better," using it as a standalone subject-less phrase often implies physical recovery from illness. To describe improving a skill (like dancing), one should specify "실력이 나아지면" (when my skills improve).
- **Negative Question Logic:** In Korean, answering a negative question (e.g., "Do you not have a pet?") requires agreeing with the negative premise. To confirm "No, I don't have one," the student must say "네" (Yes, [that is correct, I don't]). Saying "아니요" would imply the student *does* have a pet.
- **Natural Pronunciation & Contractions:**
    - **같아요:** Often pronounced as "같애요" in the Seoul/Gyeonggi dialect.
    - **경험:** The "허" sound in the second syllable is often softened or omitted in fast speech, sounding more like "경엄."
    - **Object Marker Contraction:** "한국어를" is frequently contracted to "한국얼" in natural conversation.
- **~(으)며 (Formal Connector):** A connector used primarily in written texts or poetic lyrics (e.g., song lyrics by the band 부활). 
    - With **Descriptive Verbs**, it means "and" (listing qualities).
    - With **Action Verbs**, it means "while" (simultaneous action), functioning identically to "~면서" but with a much higher level of formality.
