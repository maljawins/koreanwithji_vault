## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form used to express the amount of time that has passed since a specific action occurred. This is equivalent to the English expression "since doing" or "it has been [time] since." The lesson covers the basic conjugation rules for action verbs, how to incorporate time durations, and common variations used to express "a long time," "more than," or "not even" a certain amount of time.

The core of this grammar revolves around the noun 지, which represents the span of time from a past event until the present moment. By combining this with the verb 되다 (to become), speakers can quantify how long a state or the aftermath of an action has persisted. The instructor also provides an intermediate-level nuance check regarding whether the action is still ongoing or has concluded.

---

### Basic Conjugation: 은/ㄴ지
To form the "since" part of the sentence, you must attach a specific ending to an action verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, attach **은지**. For example, the verb 먹다 (to eat) has the stem 먹, so it becomes **먹은지**. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, attach the consonant **ㄴ** (니은) directly to the bottom of the stem followed by **지**. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes **한지**.

There is a special rule for verb stems ending in the consonant **ㄹ**. In these cases, the **ㄹ** is removed before attaching the ending. The instructor demonstrates this with the verb 살다 (to live): the **ㄹ** is dropped, and **ㄴ지** is attached to the remaining stem, resulting in **산지**.

---

### Sentence Structure: Time + 되다
Once the verb is conjugated into the **은/ㄴ지** form, the rest of the sentence specifies the duration. The full structure is: **Action Verb Stem + (은/ㄴ) 지 + Time + (이/가) 되다**. The subject marker **이** or **가** after the time amount is optional. The verb **되다** is typically conjugated into the past tense **됐어요** to indicate that the time has already passed or "become" that amount.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this:
> **우리가 만난 지 9년이 됐어요.** / It's been 9 years since we met.
> **한국어를 공부하기 시작한 지 3년이 됐어요.** / It's been 3 years since I began studying Korean.
> **한국에 온 지 10개월이 된 것 같아요.** / I think it's been 10 months since I came to Korea.

---

### Expressing "A Long Time" and Asking Questions
If you do not want to specify an exact number of years or months, you can use the adverb **오래** (a long time). This is frequently used in questions to ask if someone has been doing something for a while.
> **여기에서 산 지 오래됐어요?** / Has it been a long time since you lived here?

To ask "how long" it has been since an event, use the word **얼마나** (how much/how long) in place of the specific time duration.
> **결혼한 지 얼마나 됐어요?** / How long has it been since you got married?

---

### Nuance: Ongoing vs. Completed Actions
In an "Intermediate Content" callout, the instructor clarifies a common point of confusion. While this form can technically be used for actions that happened once in the past and ended, it is most commonly used to describe actions that are still happening or whose effects are still in place.

For example, saying **여기에서 일한 지 3년이 됐어요** usually implies that you started working there three years ago and still work there now. While it could theoretically mean you worked there for a period three years ago and then stopped, Koreans would rarely use this specific grammar for that context. Similarly, **공부하기 시작한 지 3년이 됐어요** implies you are still a student of the language.

---

### Variations: "Over," "Not Even," and "Not Long"
The instructor introduces three useful modifiers to add detail to time expressions. First, to say "over" or "more than" a certain time, add the adverb **넘게** after the time amount.
> **수영을 한 지 두 달 넘게 됐어요.** / It's been over two months since I swam.

Second, to say it has "not even" been a certain amount of time, use the particle **도** (even) with the negative verb **안 되다**.
> **여기서 산 지 1년도 안 됐어요.** / I haven't been living here for even one year.

Finally, to say it "hasn't been long" since something happened, use **얼마** (some amount/not much) with **안 되다**.
> **이 자동차를 산 지 얼마 안 됐어요.** / It hasn't been that long since I bought this car.
