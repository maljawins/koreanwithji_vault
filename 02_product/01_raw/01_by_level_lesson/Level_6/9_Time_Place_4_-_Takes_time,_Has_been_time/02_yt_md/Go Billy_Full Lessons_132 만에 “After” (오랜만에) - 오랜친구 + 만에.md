## Teaching Points

### Overview
In this intermediate-level lesson, Billy Go introduces the grammar form 만에, which is commonly translated as "after." The instructor frames the lesson by explaining that 만에 specifically describes an amount of time that passes after a certain event occurs. It is often used to express that something happens for the first time in a while, or to denote the duration it took for an outcome to be reached since a starting point.

The learning arc begins with the basic attachment of 만에 to time nouns, moves into its frequent pairing with the "since" form (~ㄴ/은 지), and concludes with the sentence-ending variation 만이다. Throughout the video, the instructor emphasizes the nuance of "time passing since an event," distinguishing it from other "after" forms like 후에 which simply denote chronological sequence.

---

### Basic Usage and Nuance of 만에
The form 만에 attaches directly to a time duration noun with a space. While it translates to "after" or "in," its technical meaning is "an amount of time that passes after something happens." The instructor provides basic examples: `1년 만에 (after one year)`, `한 시간 만에 (after one hour)`, and the question form `얼마 만에 (after how much time / after how long)`.

A critical distinction is made between 만에 and 후에. While `1년 후에` simply means "one year later" or "after one year" in a general sense, `1년 만에` implies that the year has passed since a specific event occurred. For example, saying you moved to Korea `1년 만에` implies it was one year since you started a specific process (like learning the language), whereas `1년 후에` lacks this specific "since something happened" nuance.

---

### Combining 만에 with the "Since" Form (~ㄴ/은 지)
Because 만에 implies time passing since an event, it is very frequently paired with the grammar form ~ㄴ/은 지. The instructor demonstrates this through a two-step transformation. First, a standard "since" sentence is established:
> **한국어를 공부하기 시작한 지 3년이 됐어요.** / It's been three years since I began studying Korean.

Then, 만에 is integrated to show an action occurring after that duration:
> **한국어를 공부하기 시작한 지 1년 만에 한국으로 이사 갔어요.** / I moved to Korea one year after I started learning Korean.

The instructor notes that while 만에 can be used on its own without the 지 form explicitly present, the "since" meaning is always implied by the context of the sentence.

---

### Sentence Examples and Contextual Translations
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate how 만에 translates naturally into English depending on the context. In some cases, "in" is a more natural translation than "after."

- **Accidents and Timeframes:**
  > **새 차를 산 지 한 달 만에 사고가 났어요.** / One month after buying a new car, I had an accident.
- **Efficiency and Capability:**
  > **자전거를 타면 30분 만에 200칼로리를 태울 수 있어요.** / If you ride a bicycle, you can burn 200 calories in 30 minutes.
- **Business and Success:**
  > **오픈 이틀 만에 방문자가 3천 명이 넘었어요.** / Two days after opening, there were over 3,000 visitors.
  > **방법을 바꾸니, 두 달 만에 성공했어요.** / Since I changed the method, I succeeded in two months.
- **Higher Level Usage:**
  The instructor shows how 만에 can describe a verb from a previous sentence, such as in a dialogue about sales:
  > **하루 매출이 500만 원이 넘어요. 개업 일주일 만에요.** / One day sales are over 5 million won. [It happened] one week after opening.

---

### The Sentence-Ending Form: 만이다
While 만에 functions like an adverb to describe a following verb, the form can be used as a predicate by attaching the copula 이다, resulting in 만이다. This is used to describe the time after something happens as the main point of the sentence, often translated as "it has been [duration]."

The most common example of this is the greeting for seeing someone after a long time:
> **오랜만이에요.** / Long time no see. (Literally: It is after a long time.)

Other examples of 만이다 include:
> **거의 두 달 만이었어요.** / It had been almost two months.
> **공부를 시작한 지 100일 만이에요.** / It's been 100 days since I started studying.
> **철수랑 통화한 건 몇 년 만이었어요.** / It had been several years since I talked with Chulsoo.

The instructor concludes by noting that even without the 지 form, 만이다 carries the "since" meaning because it describes the state of time having passed after a specific event (like graduation or a previous conversation).
