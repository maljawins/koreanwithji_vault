## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form used to express how much time has passed since a specific action occurred. In English, this is equivalent to saying "It has been [amount of time] since I [did something]." For example, one might say it has been a year since they last watched a baseball game. The core of this lesson focuses on the construction and usage of the `~(은/ㄴ) 지 되다` structure, which literally describes the time from when an action happened until the present moment.

The learning arc begins with the basic conjugation rules for action verbs, moves into common variations using different verbs like `넘다 (to exceed)` or `지나다 (to pass)`, and provides several practical example sentences. The lesson concludes with an advanced section addressing the nuance of whether an action is still ongoing or has completely stopped, and how to clarify that distinction using negative forms.

---

### Basic Construction and Conjugation
To build this form, you start with an action verb stem and change it into a past tense adjective form. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach `ㄴ`. For example, `하다 (to do)` becomes `한`. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach `은`. For example, `먹다 (to eat)` becomes `먹은`. There is a specific rule for stems ending in `ㄹ`: you must remove the `ㄹ` and then attach `ㄴ`. Thus, `살다 (to live)` becomes `산` and `팔다 (to sell)` becomes `판`.

After the modified verb, you add the word `지`. The instructor emphasizes that there must be a space before `지`. This is a critical orthographic detail because `~ㄴ지` without a space is a completely different grammar form meaning "whether" or "if." Following `지`, you state the amount of time (e.g., one year, two hours, three months). You may optionally attach the subject marker `이/가` to the time word. Finally, you add the verb `되다 (to become)`, which is almost always conjugated into the past tense as `됐어요`.

---

### Alternative Verbs for Time Passage
While `되다` is the most common verb used with this structure, the instructor introduces three other verbs that can replace it to add specific nuance. `넘다` means "to exceed" or "to be more than," used when it has been over a certain amount of time. `지나다` means "to pass," functioning similarly to "three years have passed since..." in English. Lastly, `흐르다` literally means "to flow" (like a river) but is used metaphorically to say that time has "gone by" or "flowed by." Regardless of which verb is chosen, they are typically conjugated in the past tense for this specific grammar pattern.

---

### Practical Examples and Questioning
The instructor provides several sentences to demonstrate the form in context:
> **한국어를 공부하기 시작한 지 17년이 됐어요.** / It's been 17 years since I started studying Korean.
> **우리가 만난 지 1년이 됐어요.** / It's been 1 year since we met.
> **결혼한 지 10년 넘었어요.** / It's been over 10 years since we got married.

When asking a question about duration, the word `얼마나 (how long/how much)` is used in place of a specific time amount. A common question for learners is:
> **한국에 온 지 얼마나 됐어요?** / How long has it been since you came to Korea? (How long have you been in Korea?)

The instructor also notes the word `오래되다`, which is written as one word without a space. It means "to be a long time."
> **새 옷을 산 지 오래됐어요.** / It's been a long time since I bought new clothes.

For negative sentences, you can use `안 됐어요` to say it hasn't been a certain amount of time. Adding the particle `도` after the time word adds the meaning of "not even."
> **한국에 온 지 2년도 안 됐어요.** / It hasn't even been 2 years since I came to Korea.

---

### Advanced Nuance: Ongoing vs. Completed Actions
A common point of confusion is whether the action that started in the past is still happening. In many cases, it is assumed to be ongoing based on the verb and context. For instance, "It's been 2 years since I got married" implies you are still married. However, "It's been 2 years since I ate pizza" implies you haven't eaten it again since then.

If you want to be explicitly clear that an action has *not* happened for a certain duration, the instructor suggests two methods. First, you can use the word `동안 (for/during)` with a simple negative past tense verb:
> **1년 동안 안 만났어요.** / I haven't met (them) for a year.

Alternatively, you can use a negative verb stem within the `지` form itself by adding `안` before the verb:
> **안 만난 지 1년이 됐어요.** / It has been a year since we (last) met. (Literally: The time since not meeting has been a year.)
