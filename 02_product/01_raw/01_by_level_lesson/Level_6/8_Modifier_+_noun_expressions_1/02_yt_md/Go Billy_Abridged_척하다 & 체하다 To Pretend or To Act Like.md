## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on the grammatical structures used to express the concept of "pretending" or "acting as if" something is true when it is not. The primary form taught is `~ㄴ/은/는 척하다`, which allows speakers to combine verbs and adjectives with a specialized noun to describe deceptive behavior or social acting. 

The instructor breaks down the conjugation rules for both action verbs and descriptive verbs across different tenses. He also introduces a synonymous but more formal alternative, `체하다`, and provides various practical examples to illustrate how these forms function in natural conversation. The lesson concludes with a brief look at the colloquial expression `~기 싫다` (not wanting to do something) as it often appears in context with pretending.

---

### Basic Form and Conjugation
The core structure involves taking a verb stem and turning it into an adjective form before adding the word `척` followed by the verb `하다 (to do)`. The specific adjective marker used depends on the type of verb and the tense. For action verbs in the present tense, the marker is `~는`, resulting in `~는 척하다`. For action verbs in the past tense, the marker is `~ㄴ` or `~은`, resulting in `~ㄴ/은 척하다`. Descriptive verbs (adjectives) typically use the present tense marker `~ㄴ` or `~은`.

The instructor notes a small detail regarding the object marker `을`. While it is optional, its presence changes the spacing of the written form. If you include the marker, it is written with spaces as `척을 하다`. However, if you omit the marker, it is commonly written as a single word, `척하다`, though adding a space (`척 하다`) is also considered acceptable.

---

### Usage with Different Verb Types
When using an action verb like `먹다 (to eat)`, the tense significantly changes the meaning. `먹는 척하다` means "to pretend to be eating" (present action), whereas `먹은 척하다` means "to pretend that one has already eaten" (completed action). The instructor demonstrates this by acting out being full to pretend he has already finished a meal.

For descriptive verbs, the present tense is standard. For example, taking the verb `귀엽다 (to be cute)`, one would say `귀여운 척하다` to mean "to act cute" or "to pretend to be cute." This implies the person is behaving in a certain way that does not necessarily reflect their true state or nature. A special case is the verb `알다 (to know)`; when used with this grammar, it is almost exclusively conjugated in the present tense as `아는 척하다` (pretending to know).

---

### Comparing 척하다 and 체하다
The instructor introduces `체하다` as an alternative to `척하다`. Both carry the exact same meaning of "to pretend." However, `체하다` is described as being less common in daily speech and having a slightly more "bookish" or formal feel. In most casual or standard situations, `척하다` is the preferred choice, but `체하다` remains a valid and non-awkward option.

---

### Practical Examples and Context
Several sentences are provided to show the grammar in action. To say "I am pretending to study," one uses the present progressive:
> **저는 공부하는 척하고 있어요.** / I am pretending to study.

To describe pretending to have knowledge about a topic, the instructor uses basketball:
> **농구에 대해 잘 아는 척했어요.** / I pretended to know a lot about basketball.

The form is also useful for describing social masks, such as hiding pain:
> **아팠지만 괜찮은 척했어요.** / I was hurting, but I pretended to be okay.

The instructor highlights a nuance in tense using `마시다 (to drink)`. `마시는 척했어요` implies acting out the motion of drinking (perhaps with an empty cup), while `마신 척했어요` implies trying to convince someone that you already drank something in the past.

---

### Related Expression: ~기 싫다
The lesson briefly covers `~기 싫다`, a colloquial way to say "I don't want to do [verb]." This is formed by taking an action verb stem and adding `~기 싫다`.
- `가기 싫어요` (I don't want to go)
- `하기 싫어요` (I don't want to do it)

This is combined with the main lesson point in the following example:
> **하기 싫어서 아픈 척했어요.** / I pretended to be sick because I didn't want to do it.

Finally, the instructor shows how to use the prohibitive form `~지 마요` with this grammar:
> **귀여운 척하지 마요.** / Don't act cute. (Don't pretend to be cute.)
