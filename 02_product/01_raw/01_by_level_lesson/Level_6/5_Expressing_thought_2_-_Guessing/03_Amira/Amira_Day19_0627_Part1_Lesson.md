## Teaching Points

*   **Sentence Endings and Realization:** Ji explains that `~네요` is used for things just realized in the moment and logically does not fit with the future tense. Instead, `~군요` or `~구나` should be used when processing information told by someone else about a future event. For example, `살 거네요` is incorrect, but `살 거군요` (I see you will live...) works.
*   **Double Nominalization:** Ji corrects the phrase `운동하는 것 잘 하는구나` to `운동 잘 하는구나`. Since `운동 (exercise)` is already a noun, adding `하는 것` creates an unnecessary double nominalization.
*   **Descriptive vs. Action Verb Negation:** For descriptive verbs, the negation `~지 않다` follows the same rule as the affirmative: it does not take `~는`. Therefore, `크지 않는군요` is incorrect; it should be `크지 않군요`.
*   **Advanced Verb Transformations:** Ji breaks down the complex verb `정해져 있어요 (it is set/fixed)` into three steps:
    1.  `정하다 (to set)` + `~아/어지다 (to become)` = `정해지다 (to be set/passive)`.
    2.  `정해지다` + `~아/어 있다 (state of completed action)` = `정해져 있다 (to be in the state of having been set)`.
    3.  Final conjugation to polite present tense: `정해져 있어요`.
*   **Cultural Vocabulary - 눈치 (Nunchi):** Ji provides an extensive explanation of `눈치`, defined as the ability to pick up on subtle, unspoken social cues. He contrasts it with `분위기 (atmosphere/vibe)`. Common expressions include:
    *   `눈치가 있다/없다`: To have/not have social sense.
    *   `눈치를 보다`: To try to read the room/test the waters.
    *   `눈치가 빠르다/느리다`: To be quick/slow at picking up cues.
    *   `눈치를 주다`: To drop hints/signal something indirectly.
*   **Slang - 칼퇴 (Kal-toe):** A combination of `칼 (knife)` and `퇴근 (leaving work)`, meaning to leave work exactly on time (e.g., at 6:00 PM sharp). The opposite is `야근 (overtime)`.
