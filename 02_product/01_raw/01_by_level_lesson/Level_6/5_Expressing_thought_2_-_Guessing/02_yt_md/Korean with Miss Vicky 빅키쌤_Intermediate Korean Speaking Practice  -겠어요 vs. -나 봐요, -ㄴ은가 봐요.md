### Speculating Results with -겠다
The first grammar point, `-겠다`, is used when the speaker observes a situation and guesses the likely result or the immediate state of the person involved. It translates roughly to "must be," "would probably," or "I bet." To speculate about the present or future, one attaches `-겠다` directly to the verb or adjective stem. For the past tense ("must have been"), the past stem is used: `-았/었/했겠다`.

The instructor provides three scenarios to illustrate this "result-oriented" guessing. First, if a coworker says they only slept three hours, the observer guesses the result: `피곤하겠어요 (You must be tired)`. Second, if someone has lived in Korea for ten years, the observer guesses the result of that long stay: `한국어를 잘하시겠네요 (You probably speak Korean fluently)`. Third, looking at a massive party spread, one might guess the past effort required: `시간이 많이 걸렸겠어요 (That must have taken a lot of time)`. In all these cases, the speaker is looking at a known fact and projecting a logical consequence.

---

### Speculating Causes with -나 보다 and -(ㄴ/은)가 보다
The second grammar point is used when the speaker notices a situation and tries to guess the hidden cause or reason behind it. The form changes depending on the part of speech. For verbs, the pattern is `Verb stem + -나 보다`. For adjectives, it is `Adjective stem + -(ㄴ/은)가 보다`. For the verb `이다 (to be)`, it becomes `Noun + -인가 보다`.

The instructor highlights several important exceptions and nuances. While adjectives usually take `-(ㄴ/은)가 보다`, adjectives ending in `ㅂ` (like `어렵다`) are frequently used with `-나 보다` by native speakers (e.g., `어렵나 봐요`). This is now officially accepted despite the traditional rule. Additionally, adjectives ending in `있다` or `없다` (like `재미있다` or `맛없다`) always follow the verb pattern and use `-나 보다`.

Examples of "cause-oriented" guessing include observing someone's bad facial expression and guessing the cause: `피곤한가 봐요 (She must be tired)`. If someone isn't answering the phone, the guessed cause is `자나 봐요 (She must be sleeping)`. If a stranger is lost and looking at a map, the guessed cause is `외국인인가 봐요 (He must be a foreigner)`. Finally, if a movie has great reviews, the guessed cause for those reviews is `재미있나 봐요 (It must be good/fun)`.

### Comparison and Dialogue Analysis
The instructor returns to the dialogue to show these rules in action. Hyunji says to Tina, `한국어를 거의 마스터하셨겠네요 (You must have practically mastered Korean)`. This is a guess about the *result* of Tina living in Korea for 10 years. Later, Hyunji says, `토픽 시험이 되게 어렵나 봐요 (The TOPIK exam must be really hard)`. This is a guess about the *cause* of why Tina (who speaks so well) hasn't passed level 6 yet.

The instructor emphasizes that switching these would sound unnatural. Using `-겠다` for the TOPIK exam would imply the exam is *about to be* hard as a result of something, rather than explaining the current reality of its difficulty. This distinction between "guessing the result" vs. "guessing the cause" is the primary key for intermediate learners to master these speculation markers.
