## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor provides a comprehensive breakdown of the versatile Korean syllable **걸**. While it is most frequently encountered as a simple contraction of the noun **것** (thing) combined with the object marker **을**, it also serves as a critical component in advanced grammar patterns used for making assumptions and expressing regret. The lesson is structured to move from the most common noun-based usages to the more nuanced sentence-ending forms.

The learning arc begins with the basic mechanics of how **것을** becomes **걸** and how it functions as a "thing" that is being acted upon in a sentence. From there, the instructor explains how to use this structure with various adjective-conjugated verbs to describe things in the past, present, and future. The second half of the lesson shifts focus to the sentence endings **~을/ㄹ 걸(요)**, used for making assumptions that might contradict a listener's expectations, and **~을/ㄹ 걸 그랬다**, used for expressing personal regret about past actions.

---

### The Basic Noun Form: 걸 as "Thing + Object Marker"
The instructor establishes that 99% of the time, **걸** is simply a contraction of **것을**. In Korean, **것** means "thing," and **을** is the object marker. When **것** follows a vowel, it is often shortened to **거**, and when the object marker **을** is added, it further contracts to **걸**. This is a fundamental building block for describing objects or concepts that are receiving an action.

For example, to say "Don't eat mine," one starts with **내 거** (my thing/mine). Adding the object marker results in **내 것을**, which naturally contracts to **내 걸**.
> **내 걸 먹지 마.** / Don't eat mine.

---

### Describing Things: Past, Present, and Future
By combining **걸** with verbs conjugated into adjective forms, speakers can describe specific "things" based on actions. The instructor demonstrates three temporal variations:

**1. Something one did (Past Tense)**
To describe a thing that was already acted upon, use the past tense adjective form (verb stem + **은/ㄴ**) followed by **걸**.
> **빌리가 제가 만든 걸 훔쳐갔어요.** / Billy stole the thing that I made.

**2. Something one does (Present Tense)**
To describe a general thing or an ongoing action, use the present tense adjective form (verb stem + **는**) followed by **걸**.
> **내가 하는 걸 따라하지 마세요.** / Please don't copy/follow what I am doing.

**3. Something to do (Future Tense)**
To describe something that will be done or "something to [verb]," use the future tense adjective form (verb stem + **을/ㄹ**) followed by **걸**.
> **같이 볼 영화를 선택했어요?** / Did you choose a movie to watch together?

---

### Making Assumptions: ~을/ㄹ 걸(요)
The instructor introduces a specific sentence ending used for making assumptions: **[Verb Stem] + 을/ㄹ 걸(요)**. This form is strictly informal and is used to state a guess or assumption, often when the speaker believes the reality is contrary to what the listener might think. It carries the nuance of "probably" or "I assume."

A key feature of this grammar is its intonation. Even though it is not written with a question mark, the pitch must rise at the end, specifically on the final syllable (**걸** or **요**). The instructor notes that you generally do not use this about yourself unless you are making a genuine assumption about your own capabilities that isn't a settled fact.

Examples of assumptions:
> **별로 안 매울 걸.** / It's probably not that spicy (contrary to what you might think).
> **이미 늦었을 걸요.** / You're probably late already.
> **빌리가 한국어 선생님일 걸요.** / Billy is probably a Korean teacher.

---

### Expressing Regret: ~을/ㄹ 걸 그랬다
When the same future tense conjugation is followed by **그랬다**, the meaning changes entirely to expressing regret or saying "I should have [verb]." In this pattern, the word **그랬다** is optional in casual speech, but the intonation remains flat or falling, unlike the rising intonation of the assumption form.

To express "I should have":
> **우산을 가져올 걸.** / I should have brought an umbrella.
> **어제 그 컴퓨터를 살 걸 그랬어.** / I should have bought that computer yesterday.

To express "I should NOT have," the pattern uses the negative auxiliary **지 말다**:
> **말하지 말 걸.** / I shouldn't have said that.
> **그냥 오지 말 걸.** / I just shouldn't have come.

---

### Directing Regret to Others: ~지 그랬다
While **~을/ㄹ 걸 그랬다** is primarily for the first person ("I should have"), the instructor explains that **~지 그랬다** is used to tell someone else what they should have done ("You should have"). In this case, **그랬다** is mandatory and cannot be omitted.

> **우산을 가져오지 그랬어요.** / You should have brought an umbrella.
> **더 열심히 공부하지 그랬어.** / You should have studied harder.

The instructor clarifies a point of potential confusion: while you might occasionally hear **걸 그랬다** used for "you," it must include the full **그랬다** ending and usually requires a subject like a name to be clear. Conversely, **지 그랬다** can never be used to refer to oneself.
