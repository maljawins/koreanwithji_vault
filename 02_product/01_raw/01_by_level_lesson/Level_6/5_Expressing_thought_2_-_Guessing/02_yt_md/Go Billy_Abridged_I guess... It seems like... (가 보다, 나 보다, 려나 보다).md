## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar forms **나 보다** and **가 보다**, which are used to express that something "seems like" or "appears to be" a certain way based on some evidence. While they carry the same fundamental meaning of "I guess that..." or "It seems like...", the choice between them depends entirely on the type of verb they follow and the tense being used. The instructor notes that **나 보다** is the more versatile of the two, used in roughly 90% of situations, including all past and future tense guesses.

---

### Present Tense Usage: Action vs. Descriptive Verbs
In the present tense, the choice of ending is determined by whether the preceding word is an action verb or a descriptive verb (adjective). For action verbs, the rule is simple: attach **나 보다** directly to the verb stem. For example, the instructor takes the verb **좋아하다 (to like)**, identifies the stem as **좋아하**, and attaches the marker to create **좋아하나 봐요 (I guess they like it / It seems they like it)**.

Descriptive verbs require a more complex construction using **가 보다**. The instructor demonstrates that you must first attach **은** or **ㄴ** to the descriptive verb stem before adding **가 보다**. If the stem ends in a consonant, use **은**; if it ends in a vowel, use **ㄴ**. Using the verb **좋다 (to be good)**, the instructor shows the stem **좋** plus **은** results in **좋은가 봐요 (I guess it's good)**. For the verb **멀다 (to be far)**, the **ㄹ** is dropped from the stem, resulting in **먼가 봐요 (I guess it's far)**.

---

### Irregular Conjugations and Exceptions
The instructor highlights several irregular patterns when using these forms with descriptive verbs. For stems ending in **ㅂ**, such as **덥다 (to be hot)**, the **ㅂ** is removed and replaced with **우**, leading to **더운가 봐요 (I guess it's hot)**. Similarly, stems ending in **ㅎ** like **그렇다 (to be so)** drop the **ㅎ** to become **그런가 봐요 (I guess so / It seems that way)**.

A critical exception is noted for the descriptive verbs **있다 (to exist/to have)** and **없다 (to not exist/to not have)**. Despite being descriptive verbs, they conjugate like action verbs in this context, meaning they take **나 보다** instead of **가 보다**. This results in forms like **재밌나 봐요 (I guess it's fun)**. Furthermore, the instructor explains that for many descriptive verbs ending in a consonant, **나 보다** is often used colloquially instead of **은가 보다**. For instance, both **매운가 봐요** and **맵나 봐요 (I guess it's spicy)** are acceptable, though **가 보다** remains mandatory for descriptive stems ending in a vowel, such as **크다 (to be big)** becoming **큰가 봐요**.

---

### Past and Future Tense Guessing
When guessing about the past, the distinction between action and descriptive verbs disappears. Regardless of the verb type, if it is in the past tense, you must use **나 보다**. The instructor provides examples like **안 왔나 봐요 (I guess they didn't come)** and **더웠나 봐요 (I guess it was hot)**. Even descriptive verbs that used **가 보다** in the present tense, like **좋다**, switch to **나 보다** in the past: **좋았나 봐요 (I guess it was good)**.

For future tense guesses, the instructor introduces the form **(으)려나 보다**. This is a combination of the "intending to" marker **(으)려고 하다** and the guessing form. It is used for both action and descriptive verbs to mean "It seems like [someone] is going to..." or "I guess it will...". Examples shown include **숙제를 못 하려나 봐 (I guess I won't be able to do the homework)** and **내일도 더우려나 봐요 (It seems it will be hot tomorrow too)**.

---

### Sentence Examples and Nuance
The instructor provides a variety of sentences to illustrate these rules in context:
- **안 왔나 봐요.** / I guess they didn't come.
- **그 사람을 모르나 봐요.** / I guess he/she doesn't know that person.
- **피곤한가 보네요.** / You seem really tired.
- **정말 배가 고픈가 봐요.** / I guess you're really hungry.
- **옆집에서 파티를 하나 봐요.** / It seems like they're having a party next door.

The instructor also mentions that the final **보다** can be conjugated with different sentence endings to add nuance, such as **보죠** (adding a "doesn't it?" or "right?" feel) or **보네요** (expressing a realization or observation). For example, **오늘 날씨가 더운가 보죠** translates to "Today's weather really seems hot, doesn't it?"
