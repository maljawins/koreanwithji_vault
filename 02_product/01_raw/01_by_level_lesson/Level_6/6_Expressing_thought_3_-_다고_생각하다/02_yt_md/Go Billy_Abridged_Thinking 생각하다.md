## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar structure used to express personal thoughts and opinions in Korean using the verb 생각하다 (to think). This lesson is divided into two primary parts: first, a comprehensive review of the "plain form" conjugation, which is a prerequisite for this structure; and second, the application of the plain form with the connector 고 and the verb 생각하다.

The core of the lesson focuses on the nuance of 생각하다 compared to other "thinking" expressions like 것 같다. While 것 같다 is softer and often used for external observations or feelings, 생각하다 is used for internal logic, firm opinions, and deliberate consideration. The instructor demonstrates how this form can be used across various tenses and sentence types, including commands and progressive forms, which are not possible with descriptive-based expressions like 것 같다.

---

### Prerequisites: The Plain Form
To use the "I think that..." structure, one must first master the plain form. The conjugation rules for the plain form in the present tense depend on whether the verb is an action verb or a descriptive verb. For action verbs, if the stem ends in a consonant, attach 는다; if it ends in a vowel, attach ㄴ followed by 다. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 한다, and 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹는다. An exception occurs with stems ending in ㄹ, such as 살다 (to live); the ㄹ is removed before attaching ㄴ다, resulting in 산다. Descriptive verbs are simpler: the plain form is identical to the dictionary form. Thus, 춥다 (to be cold) remains 춥다, and 멀다 (to be far) remains 멀다.

Special attention is required for the copula 이다 (to be) and 아니다 (to not be). While their plain forms are simply 이다 and 아니다, they undergo a unique change when followed by the connector 고. In this specific context, the 다 changes to 라, resulting in 이라고 (or 라고 after a vowel) and 아니라고.

---

### Plain Form in Past and Future Tenses
The past tense plain form is created by taking the past tense conjugated stem and attaching 다. The instructor demonstrates this by taking the past tense of 하다, which is 했(어요), removing the polite ending, and adding 다 to get 했다. Similarly, 가다 becomes 갔다 and 먹다 becomes 먹었다.

For the future tense, there are two main options. The first is attaching 겠다 to the verb stem, such as 하겠다. The second, more common option uses the 을/ㄹ 것이다 structure. In the plain form, this is expressed as 을/ㄹ 것이다 or the shortened 을/ㄹ 거다. However, because this structure ends in 이다, it follows the special rule mentioned previously when used with 고: it transforms into 을/ㄹ 거라고.

---

### Expressing Thoughts with 고 생각하다
The full structure for expressing a thought is [Plain Form] + 고 생각하다. Literally, 생각 means "a thought," so the phrase translates to "to do a thought" or "to think/consider." Unlike 것 같다, which functions as a descriptive expression ("it seems like"), 생각하다 is a regular action verb. This allows it to be used in ways 것 같다 cannot. For instance, you can use it in the progressive tense (생각하고 있어요 / I am thinking) or the future intention tense (생각할게요 / I will think about it). You can even use it in commands, telling someone to think a certain way or not to think a certain way.

---

### Nuance: 생각하다 vs. 것 같다
The instructor highlights a key distinction in usage. 것 같다 sounds more conversational, soft, and is typically used for things you sense or feel based on external factors. It is equivalent to "I guess" or "It seems." In contrast, 생각하다 is used for internal factors—your actual logic, opinions, and deliberate thoughts. A helpful rule of thumb is to check if the sentence sounds natural starting with "What I think is..." in English; if it does, 생각하다 is likely the more appropriate choice in Korean.

---

### Example Sentences and Applications
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the grammar in practice:

- **한국어가 재밌다고 생각해요.** / I think Korean is fun. (Present tense descriptive verb)
- **좋다고 생각하지 않아요.** / I do not think it is good. (Negative thought)
- **철수가 한국 사람이라고 생각해요.** / I think Chul-su is a Korean person. (Copula 이다 changing to 이라고)
- **철수가 한국에서 산다고 생각해요.** / I think Chul-su lives in Korea. (Present tense action verb)
- **영화가 짧다고 생각했어요.** / I thought the movie was short. (Past tense thought)
- **이미 했다고 생각해요.** / I think that I already did it. (Thought about a past action)
- **괜찮을 거라고 생각해요.** / I think it will be okay. (Future tense thought using 을 거라고)
