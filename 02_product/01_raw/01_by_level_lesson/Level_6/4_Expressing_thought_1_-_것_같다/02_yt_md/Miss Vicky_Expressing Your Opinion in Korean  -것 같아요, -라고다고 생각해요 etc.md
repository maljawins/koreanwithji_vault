### Expressing Opinions with 고 생각하다
The expression `고 생각하다` is derived from the verb `생각하다` (to think). When used in the pattern `[Sentence Content] + 고 생각해요`, it translates to "I think that [Sentence Content]." The instructor explains that the form of the word immediately preceding `고` changes depending on whether it is an adjective, the copula `이다` (to be), or a regular action verb.

For adjectives in the present tense, the rule is straightforward: use the base form (dictionary form) of the adjective. The instructor demonstrates this with several examples: `똑똑하다` (to be smart) becomes `똑똑하다고 생각해요`, and `예쁘다` (to be pretty) becomes `예쁘다고 생각해요`. This rule also applies to adjectives like `재미있다` (to be fun/interesting) and `어렵다` (to be difficult), resulting in `재미있다고 생각해요` and `어렵다고 생각해요`.

When the sentence involves the copula `이다` (to be), the structure changes to `라고` or `이라고`. If the preceding noun ends in a vowel (no 받침), use `라고`, such as `천재라고 생각해요` (I think [he] is a genius). If the noun ends in a consonant (has 받침), use `이라고` for ease of pronunciation, such as `미인이라고 생각해요` (I think [she] is a beauty).

For regular action verbs, the instructor explains a specific conjugation rule for the present tense. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, add a `ㄴ` patchim to the stem followed by `다`. For example, `좋아하다` (to like) becomes `좋아한다고 생각해요`, and `잘하다` (to be good at) becomes `잘한다고 생각해요`. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, add `는` followed by `다`. For instance, `읽다` (to read) becomes `읽는다고 생각해요`, and `먹다` (to eat) becomes `먹는다고 생각해요`. The instructor notes that `ㄹ` irregular verbs like `만들다` (to make) and `알다` (to know) require dropping the `ㄹ` and adding `ㄴ`, resulting in `만든다고 생각해요` and `안다고 생각해요`.

### Softening Opinions with 것 같다
The instructor introduces `것 같다` as a more common and culturally nuanced way to express thoughts. While its literal meaning is "it seems like" or "it looks like," it is frequently used to mean "I think" in a way that sounds more polite and less assertive. The structure involves turning the preceding verb or adjective into a modifying form that describes the noun `것` (thing/fact), followed by the adjective `같다` (to be like/same).

For adjectives, the modifying form is created by adding `ㄴ` to stems ending in a vowel or `은` to stems ending in a consonant. Thus, `똑똑하다` becomes `똑똑한 것 같아요` and `예쁘다` becomes `예쁜 것 같아요`. The instructor highlights several irregulars: adjectives ending in `있다` (like `재미있다`) use the `는` form, resulting in `재미있는 것 같아요`. Adjectives with a `ㅂ` patchim like `어렵다` (difficult) or `맵다` (spicy) change to `어려운 것 같아요` and `매운 것 같아요`.

For the copula `이다`, the stem `이` is followed by `ㄴ`, resulting in `인 것 같아요`. For example, `천재인 것 같아요` (I think [he] is a genius) or `미인인 것 같아요` (I think [she] is a beauty).

For action verbs in the present tense, the rule is to add `는` to the verb stem regardless of whether it has a patchim. Examples include `좋아하는 것 같아요` (I think [he] likes...), `잘하는 것 같아요` (I think [he] is good at...), and `먹는 것 같아요` (I think [he] eats...). For `ㄹ` irregular verbs, the `ㄹ` is dropped before adding `는`, such as `만드는 것 같아요` (I think [she] makes...) and `아는 것 같아요` (I think [you] know...).

---

### Nuance and Usage Comparison
The instructor concludes by comparing the two forms. While `고 생각해요` is grammatically correct and useful for stating a firm opinion, `것 같아요` is the "go-to" expression for most social interactions in Korea. Using `것 같아요` makes the speaker appear more humble and less likely to cause offense by being too "outspoken." The instructor uses a final complex example to show these rules in practice: `생각을 표현하는 건 중요하다고 생각해요` (I think expressing thoughts is important). She then demonstrates how to use `유익하다` (to be beneficial/helpful) to say `이 수업이 유익한 것 같아요` (I think this class is helpful).
