## Teaching Points

**1. Expressing Necessity: 필요하다 vs. 필요(가) 있다/없다**
*   **필요하다 (to be necessary):** A descriptive verb used primarily with nouns. While it can be negated as 필요하지 않다 or 안 필요하다, it is less common in spoken Korean.
*   **필요(가) 있다/없다 (There is/is no need):** This is the more natural way to express "need" or "don't need."
    *   **Structure:** `[Noun] + 필요(가) 있다/없다` or `[Verb Stem] + ㄹ/을 필요(가) 있다/없다`.
    *   On screen, Ji explains that 필요(가) 있다 feels more neutral, objective, and formal than 필요하다. It shifts the focus to the object of the need.
    *   Example: 걱정할 필요 없어 (There is no need to worry).

**2. Ability and Possibility: ㄹ/을 수 있다/없다**
*   **수** is a noun meaning "ability," "way," or "possibility."
*   **ㄹ/을 수 있다:** To be able to / There is a way to.
*   **ㄹ/을 수 없다:** To not be able to / There is no way to.
*   Ji emphasizes that ㄹ/을 수 없다 has a strong nuance of impossibility or something being out of one's control.
*   **Nuance with Particles:**
    *   **할 수가 있다/없다:** More emphatic/emotional.
    *   **할 수는 있다:** "I can do it, but..." (implies hesitation or contrast).
    *   **할 수도 있다:** "I might" or "I can also do this."
    *   **할 수밖에 없다:** "I have no choice but to..."

**3. Alternative Negations for Ability: 못 vs. 지 못하다**
*   **못 + Verb:** An adverb used for action verbs. It implies "can't" due to circumstances or lack of skill (e.g., 요리를 못 해요 - "I can't cook" or "I am bad at cooking").
*   **지 못하다:** A more formal and indirect version of "can't." Unlike 못, it can be used with descriptive verbs to imply that an effort was made but the result was not achieved (e.g., 완벽하지 못해요 - "It's not perfect [despite trying]").

**4. Contrastive Connectors: ~지만 vs. ~는데**
*   **~지만:** Derived from 하지만. It shows a clear, strong contrast, similar to "however." It is not 받침-sensitive.
*   **~는데 / ~은데 / ~ㄴ데:** Derived from 그런데. It provides a softer contrast or sets the background/setup for the next clause. It can be translated as "but," "and," or "yet."
    *   **Action Verbs:** Always use ~는데 (e.g., 가는데, 먹는데).
    *   **Descriptive Verbs:** Use ~은데 after 받침 (작은데) or ~ㄴ데 after a vowel (예쁜데).

## Lesson Flow

**I. Assignment Review**
Ji opens the lesson by reviewing Amira's handwritten sentences. 
*   **Correction 1:** Amira wrote "아빠는 요리하 도움이 필요하지만 필요하지 않아요." Ji explains that in Korean, we don't say "help with cooking" directly; instead, we use "when" (ㄹ/을 때). He corrects it to: 아빠는 요리할 때 도움이 필요해요 (When dad cooks, he needs help).
*   **Correction 2:** Amira used 마세요 (negative command) instead of 마셔요 (to drink) in the sentence "아무 때도 커피를 안 마세요." Ji clarifies the difference between 마시다 (to drink) and 말다 (to not do/stop).
*   **Correction 3:** Ji corrects the particle usage in "살아 과 같이." Since 살아 ends in a vowel, it should be 와, making it 살아와 같이. He notes that 와/과 is an exception to the usual pattern where 받침-ending words take the version starting with a silent letter.

**II. Necessity (필요)**
Ji transitions to the PowerPoint to explain 필요(가) 있다/없다. He shows how this form allows "need" to be used with verbs (ㄹ/을 필요), which 필요하다 cannot do. He provides several examples, such as "Is there a need to buy this?" (이걸 살 필요가 있어요?).

**III. Ability (Can/Can't)**
Ji introduces ㄹ/을 수 있다/없다. He explains the literal meaning of 수 (way/possibility). He highlights that this form is used for things out of one's control. He then introduces the common reactive expressions:
*   **그럴 수 있어:** "That can happen" / "I understand."
*   **이럴 수가!:** "No way!" / "How can this be!" (expressing shock).

**IV. Advanced "Can't" and Nuances**
Ji explains the use of 못 and 지 못하다. He compares them in a table, showing that 못 is the most versatile and casual, while 지 못하다 is formal. He specifically points out that adding particles to 지 못하다 (like 지는 못하다 or 지도 못하다) allows for more specific nuances like "I can't even..." or "I can't do [this specific thing], but...".

**V. Contrast (But/However)**
The final segment of this recording introduces ~지만 and ~는데. Ji explains that ~지만 is for "hard" contrast, while ~는데 is "soft" and often used to provide context. He begins explaining the conjugation rules for ~는데 before the recording ends.
