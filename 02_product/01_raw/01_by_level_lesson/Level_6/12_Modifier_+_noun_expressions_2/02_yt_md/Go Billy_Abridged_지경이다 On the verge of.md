## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the grammar form 지경이다, which is used to express that someone is on the verge of a certain state or action. The lesson focuses on the literal meaning of the noun 지경, which refers to a boundary or a set of circumstances, and how this translates into common English expressions like "to the point of," "nearly," or "on the brink of."

The learning arc begins with the basic construction and conjugation of the form, followed by a deep dive into the nuances of the word 지경. A critical rule is established early on: this specific grammar is almost exclusively used for negative or undesirable situations. Through various examples involving hunger, sadness, and even hyperbole like "touching the sky," the instructor demonstrates how to use this form to add dramatic emphasis to a negative condition.

---

### Construction and Meaning of 지경이다
To create this form, you take a verb stem and attach the future tense marker ~(으)ㄹ. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you add 을; if it ends in a vowel, you add the letter 릴 (ㄹ). This is then followed by the noun 지경 and the verb 이다 (to be). The instructor explains that the word 지경 literally means a boundary between two areas or states. Visually, he represents this on the board as a line separating two countries or conditions. When you use this grammar, you are saying that you have reached the very edge of that boundary but have not quite crossed over into the next state yet.

Beyond the meaning of "boundary," 지경 can also refer to a "circumstance" or "condition." Therefore, the expression implies that the current circumstances are so extreme that they are leading directly to a specific result. Common English translations include "on the verge of," "to the point of," "nearly," "to the degree that," or simply "that" in a resultative sense. For example, 울 지경이다 translates to "on the verge of tears" or "to the point of crying."

---

### The Negative Context Rule
A vital usage note emphasized by the instructor is that 지경이다 is specifically used for negative or bad situations. It is paired with verbs that describe difficult, stressful, or painful experiences. The instructor lists several common verbs used with this form on the board: 울다 (to cry), 죽다 (to die), 포기하다 (to give up), 넘어지다 (to fall over), and 쓰러지다 (to collapse). You would not use this form to say you are "on the verge of winning the lottery" or "on the verge of being happy," as those are positive outcomes.

---

### Practical Examples and Hyperbole
The instructor provides several sentences to illustrate how this form functions in context. In a workplace scenario, he describes a character named Chul-soo:

> **철수가 일을 너무 안 하고 있어서 잘릴 지경이었어요.** / Chul-soo was working so little that he was on the verge of being fired.

Another common usage involves the verb 죽다 (to die) to express extreme discomfort or hunger. The instructor notes that while the word "think" or "thought" isn't in the Korean sentence, it often translates that way in English:

> **너무 힘들어서 죽을 지경이었어요.** / It was so hard that I nearly died (or "I thought I was going to die").
> **배고파 죽을 지경이에요.** / I'm starving to death (literally: "I am on the verge of dying of hunger").

The form can also be used for hyperbolic descriptions. To describe a very tall building, one might say:

> **건물이 너무 높아서 하늘에 닿을 지경이에요.** / The building is so tall it's nearly touching the sky.

In a more complex example involving a sincere apology, the instructor uses the idiomatic expression "begging until your hands become feet" (손이 발이 되도록 빌다) to show that even extreme effort might not be enough:

> **여자친구에게 잘못한 게 너무 많아서 손이 발이 되도록 빌어도 모자를 지경이에요.** / I did so many bad things to my girlfriend that it won't be enough even if I begged on my hands and knees.
