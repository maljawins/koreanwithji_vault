## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the particle 뿐, which functions as a way to express "only" or "just." While it shares a basic definition with other common Korean words like 만 or 그냥, 뿐 carries a much stronger emphasis on the idea of "nothing more" or "nothing else." It suggests that the subject is the absolute limit of a situation, providing a sense of finality or exclusivity that simpler particles lack.

The lesson covers how to attach 뿐 to both nouns and verbs, including past tense constructions. A significant portion of the video is dedicated to the nuance of using 뿐이다 at the end of a sentence versus using it in the middle. Furthermore, the instructor highlights a specific negative or "downplaying" nuance that arises when 뿐 is combined with the verb 이다 (to be) as 일 뿐, which is often used when the speaker is complaining or minimizing the importance of something.

---

### Understanding the Nuance of 뿐
The instructor explains that 뿐 means "only and nothing more" or "just that and nothing else." On screen, he writes out these definitions to emphasize that it is more than a simple affirmative; it is an exclusionary statement. Because of this, it has a stronger emphasis than the particle 만. He also compares it to the adverb 그냥 (just), noting that while 그냥 is primarily informal or casual, 뿐 can be used at any speech level. However, 뿐 is less common in daily conversation and carries a slightly more "fancy" or written-style feel.

---

### Using 뿐 with Nouns
Attaching 뿐 to a noun is straightforward: you simply place it directly after the noun. If the expression ends the sentence, the verb 이다 (to be) is attached to form 뿐이다. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate the "nothing else" nuance:

> **이제 할 수 있는 건 기도뿐이에요.** / All I can do now is pray. (The only thing left is prayer and nothing else.)
> **어제 하루 종일 먹은 건 밥 한 그릇 뿐이었어요.** / All I ate yesterday all day was just one bowl of rice. (Implying nothing else was consumed.)
> **나를 믿어준 건 너 뿐이야.** / You're the only one who believed me. (And no one else did.)

---

### Using 뿐 with Verbs
When using 뿐 with verbs, the construction requires the future tense adjective form `-(으)ㄹ`. The rule is to take the verb stem and attach 을 if it ends in a consonant, or ㄹ if it ends in a vowel (or if the stem already ends in ㄹ, you leave it as is). This is followed by a space and then 뿐.

This can be applied to both present and past tense stems. For the past tense, the instructor demonstrates that you take the past tense stem (e.g., 했 from 하다) and attach 을 뿐. This results in the form 했을 뿐, meaning "only did [action]."

> **하라는 것만 했을 뿐인데, 상을 주셨어요.** / All I did was what I was told to do, but they gave me a prize.
> **저는 그 사람 이름만 들었을 뿐, 성별이 뭔지도 몰라요.** / I only heard that person's name; I don't even know what their gender is.
> **엄마가 여행 날짜만 알려줬을 뿐 구체적인 건 안 알려줬어요.** / Mom only told me the travel dates; she didn't tell me anything specific.
> **자기 직전에 물을 마셨을 뿐인데 덜 피곤해졌어요.** / All I did was drink water right before bed, but I felt less tired.

---

### The Specific Nuance of 일 뿐
The instructor explains a subtle but important distinction when using 뿐 with the verb 이다 (to be) in the form `Noun + 일 뿐`. While `Noun + 뿐` is a neutral statement of "only," adding the ㄹ modifier to 이다 creates a nuance that can feel negative, complaining, or dismissive. It is often used to "downplay" something as being less important than one might think.

> **그냥 취미일 뿐인데 돈이 많이 들어요.** / It's just a hobby, but it costs a lot of money. (Downplaying the importance of the hobby.)
> **그렇게 하면 시간 낭비일 뿐, 도움은 하나도 안 돼요.** / If you do that, it's only a waste of time; it's not helpful at all. (Expressing a negative result.)
> **제가 가수이긴 해도, 그냥 사람일 뿐인데, 평범하게 대해주세요.** / Even though I am a singer, I am just a person, so please treat me normally. (Minimizing their own status.)
