## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson focuses on two intermediate-level forms used to express the concept of "only" or "nothing but": 뿐이다 and 따름이다. While Korean has several ways to translate "only"—most notably the particles 만 and 밖에—these specific forms are used to add significant emphasis to a sentence. They convey a sense that there is absolutely nothing else besides the stated noun or action.

The instructor frames the lesson for intermediate learners, as the nuances and conjugation rules are more complex than basic particles. The arc of the lesson moves from using 뿐이다 with nouns, then with verbs, and finally introduces the more formal and emotive variant, 따름이다.

---

### Using 뿐이다 with Nouns
The most basic way to use this form is by attaching 뿐 directly to a noun without a space, followed by the verb 이다 (to be). This construction, Noun + 뿐이다, adds strong emphasis, effectively meaning "to be nothing but [Noun]."

A critical usage note provided is that 뿐이다 should generally be used in longer, more descriptive sentences rather than short ones. Using it in a very brief sentence without context can sound awkward because there is no clear reason for such strong emphasis. For example, the instructor demonstrates that while `지우개 뿐이에요` is grammatically correct, it feels incomplete without a surrounding context.

> **필통에 있는 건 지우개 뿐이에요.** / The only thing at all that's in my pencil case is an eraser.
> **우리에게 남은 것은 시간 뿐이에요.** / The only thing that we have left is time, and nothing else.
> **숙제를 다 한 건 철수뿐이었어요.** / It was only 철수 and nobody else who did all of the homework.

---

### Using 뿐이다 with Verbs
When expressing that one "only" performs a certain action or is "only" in a certain state, the form Verb Stem + (을/ㄹ) 뿐이다 is used. To conjugate this, take a verb stem and attach 을 if it ends in a consonant, or ㄹ if it ends in a vowel. This is followed by a space, then 뿐이다. This form can also be used in the past tense by attaching the (을/ㄹ) ending to the past tense stem (e.g., 하다 becomes 했을 뿐이다).

This verbal form carries a feeling similar to the adverb 그냥 (just/only). It suggests that there is only one option available or only one way to be. It is often paired with the adverb 그저 (only/just) to further reinforce that the subject is not doing anything else.

> **그저 기다릴 뿐이다.** / All I do is wait (and nothing else).
> **저는 들은 대로 말했을 뿐이에요.** / All I said was just what I had heard.
> **잠을 못 자서 피곤할 뿐이에요.** / I couldn't sleep, so all I am is tired.

Additionally, the instructor notes that you can combine this with the particle 만 after a noun earlier in the sentence to stack the emphasis.

> **손님이 몇 명이 올지 대략적으로만 알 뿐이에요.** / All I have is just a rough guess of how many customers will come here.

---

### The Specific Case of 일뿐이다
When the verb 이다 (to be) itself is used with this grammar, it becomes 일뿐이다 and is attached directly after a noun. This specific construction often carries a negative or defensive nuance, as if the speaker is complaining or downplaying something as being "nothing more than" that noun. It is very similar to using the adverb 그냥 before a noun.

> **우리는 친구일 뿐이에요.** / We are only friends (nothing more).
> **그냥 친구예요.** / We're just friends.

If a speaker wants to express "only" without this negative or complaining tone, they should use the standard Noun + 뿐이다 (without the 일) or simply use 그냥.

> **내가 원하는 게 너뿐이야.** / All that I want is you (and no one else).

---

### The Formal Variant: 따름이다
The form 따름이다 is functionally identical to 뿐이다 but is used in much more formal contexts. It is conjugated the same way as the verbal 뿐이다 form: Verb Stem + (을/ㄹ) 따름이다.

While the meaning is the same, 따름이다 is specifically common when the speaker is expressing strong emotions such as hope, regret, surprise, gratitude, or apology. It sounds more sophisticated and "heavy" than 뿐이다. The instructor suggests that if a learner encounters 따름이다 and finds it difficult, they can simply swap it with 뿐이다 in their mind to understand the core meaning.

> **이걸 혼자 다 했다니 놀라울 따름이네요.** / I have nothing but surprise to find out that you did all of this by yourself.
