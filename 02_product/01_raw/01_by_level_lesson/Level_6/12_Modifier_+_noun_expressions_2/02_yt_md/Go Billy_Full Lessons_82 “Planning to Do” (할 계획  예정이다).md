### Expressing Plans with 계획
To say you are planning to do something, you must combine a verb with the noun 계획 (plan) and the copula 이다 (to be). The instructor breaks down the construction into three steps. First, take the verb stem of the action you plan to perform. Second, attach the future tense attributive marker: use 을 if the verb stem ends in a consonant, or simply attach the consonant ㄹ to the bottom of the stem if it ends in a vowel. Third, add the word 계획 followed by the verb 이다, which is then conjugated to fit the desired politeness level (most commonly 이에요 in polite speech).

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this pattern. For a verb ending in a vowel like 하다 (to do), the stem 하 becomes 할, resulting in the sentence 숙제를 할 계획이에요 (I'm planning to do my homework). For a verb ending in a consonant like 먹다 (to eat), the stem 먹 becomes 먹을, as seen in the sentence 오늘 저녁에 초밥을 먹을 계획이에요 (I'm planning to eat sushi tonight). The instructor also demonstrates how to use this form in a question by changing the intonation and using the honorific suffix 시, as in 일찍 은퇴할 계획이세요? (Are you planning to retire early?).

---

### Formal Alternative: 예정
While 계획 is the standard word for "plan," the instructor introduces 예정 as a similar alternative. 예정 translates more closely to "schedule" or "prearranged plan." Using the form ~을/ㄹ 예정 이다 is considered a tad more formal than using 계획, though both are used frequently in daily conversation and writing.

The construction remains identical to the previous form, simply replacing the noun 계획 with 예정. The instructor provides examples such as 오늘 저녁을 같이 먹을 예정입니다 (I am planning to eat dinner together tonight), which uses the formal 입니다 ending. Another example shown is 주말에 뭐 할 예정이에요? (What are you planning to do on the weekend?), demonstrating its use in a standard polite question. Finally, the instructor shows the sentence 두 시 전에 집에 돌아갈 예정이에요 (I'm planning to return home before 2 o'clock) to show the form used with the verb 돌아가다 (to return).
