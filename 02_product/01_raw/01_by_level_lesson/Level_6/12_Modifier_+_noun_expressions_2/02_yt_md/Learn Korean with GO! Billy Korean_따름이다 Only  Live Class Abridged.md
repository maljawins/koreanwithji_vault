While it shares a primary meaning with the more common form 뿐이다, 따름이다 carries a more formal and emphatic nuance. The instructor highlights that while the form is less common in casual speech, understanding it is crucial for comprehending formal Korean and expressing deep emotions like gratitude, regret, or curiosity.

### Basic Meaning and Comparison with 뿐이다
따름 means "only" or "just," indicating that a situation is exactly as described and nothing else. The instructor notes that it is essentially the same as 뿐, a form he has taught previously. Both can be translated as "only/just and nothing else/more." However, 따름이다 is more emphatic and formal than 뿐이다. Because of its formal nature, it is less common in everyday casual conversation but appears frequently in written texts, speeches, or formal acknowledgments.

---

### Conjugation Rules
To use this form, you take a verb stem and attach either 을 or ㄹ depending on the ending of the stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 을; for example, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹을. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you attach the ㄹ letter; for example, 가다 (to go) becomes 갈. An exception exists for stems already ending in ㄹ, which simply keep that ㄹ.

After attaching 을/ㄹ, you add 따름이다. The 이다 portion must then be conjugated to match the desired politeness level or tense, such as 입니다, 이에요, 이었습니다, or 이었어요. This form can be used with both the regular verb stem (present/future intent) and the past tense verb stem. For the past tense, you use the stem found after the double-consonant conjugation (e.g., 했 from 하다) and always attach 을 to it, resulting in 했 을 따름이다.

---

### Nuance: Action Verbs vs. Descriptive Verbs
The instructor explains a critical distinction in nuance between action and descriptive verbs when using this form. When used with an action verb, the meaning is a literal "just that and nothing else." It functions similarly to the adverb 그냥 (just). For example, saying "I just did what I had to do" uses the action verb nuance to show there was no other motive or action involved.

When used with a descriptive verb—specifically those expressing emotion—the nuance shifts from "only" to "so much" or "full of." For instance, using it with 감사하다 (to be thankful) creates the meaning "I am just so thankful." Literally, it means "I am only thankful and nothing else," which pragmatically emphasizes that the speaker's entire state of being is filled with that emotion. Common emotional verbs used with this form include 고맙다/감사하다 (to be thankful), 안타깝다 (to be unfortunate/pity), 죄송하다 (to be sorry), 피곤하다 (to be tired), and 궁금하다 (to be curious).

---

### Usage Restrictions and Differences from 뿐이다
While 따름이다 can often replace 뿐이다, the reverse is not always true. A specific difference is that 뿐이다 allows for the construction 일 뿐이다 (using the verb 이다), whereas 따름이다 does not typically appear as 일 따름이다. This is because 따름이다 is fundamentally tied to expressing a state of emotion or a specific action, and the plain copula 이다 (to be) does not carry the necessary emotional weight to fit the "so much" nuance of the descriptive verb pattern.

---

### Example Sentences and Analysis
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate these points. In the sentence 친구들에게 고마울 따름입니다 (I have nothing but gratitude for my friends), the descriptive verb 고맙다 emphasizes a deep, singular feeling of thanks. Conversely, in the sentence 저는 기억나는 대로 말했을 따름이에요 (I only said what I remembered), the action verb 말하다 (to speak) in the past tense simply limits the action to exactly what was stated.

Other examples include:
> **저는 해야 할 일을 했을 따름입니다.** / I just did what I was supposed to do.
> **제가 도울 게 없다니 안타까울 따름이에요.** / It's nothing but unfortunate that there's nothing I can help with.
> **맛있게 드셨다니 감사할 따름이에요.** / I feel so grateful that you enjoyed the meal.
> **저 역시 죄송할 따름이에요.** / I am just so sorry as well.
> **잠이 부족해서 피곤할 따름이지만 괜찮아요.** / I'm just so tired because I'm not sleeping enough, but I'm okay.
> **다들 어떻게 공부를 하는지 궁금할 따름이었어요.** / I was just so curious how everyone is studying.
