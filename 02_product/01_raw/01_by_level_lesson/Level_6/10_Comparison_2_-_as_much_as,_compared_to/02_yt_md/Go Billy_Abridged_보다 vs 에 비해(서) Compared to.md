### Basic Adverbs: 더 and 덜
Before diving into complex comparisons, the instructor reviews the adverbs **더 (more)** and **덜 (less)**. These are placed directly before a verb or adjective to indicate a higher or lower degree of that action or state. For example, the sentence `김치를 더 주세요` means "Please give me more kimchi." While these adverbs function well on their own to indicate quantity or degree, they are also frequently used in conjunction with comparative particles to clarify the direction of the comparison.

---

### Comparing with 보다
The particle **보다** is the standard way to say "than" in Korean. It is attached directly to the noun that serves as the basis for comparison. The basic structure is `Noun + 보다`, which translates to "than [Noun]" or "compared to [Noun]."

The instructor uses the example of being smarter than a person named 철수. To say "than 철수," you simply attach the particle: `철수보다`. When integrated into a full sentence like `저는 철수보다 더 똑똑해요`, it translates to "I am smarter than 철수." A key point emphasized is that the adverb **더** is actually optional when using 보다. Saying `저는 철수보다 똑똑해요` still implies "I am smarter than 철수" because the comparison is already established by the particle. However, if you wish to say "less," the adverb **덜** is mandatory, as in `저는 철수보다 덜 똑똑해요` (I am less smart than 철수).

Other examples provided include:
- `피자는 햄버거보다 맛있어요.` / Pizza is tastier than hamburgers.
- `저는 빵보다 밥을 더 좋아합니다.` / I like rice more than bread.
- `이게 그것보다 더 나아요.` / This is better (preferable) than that. (Note: 나아요 comes from the verb 낫다, meaning to be preferable).

---

### Using 보다 with Action Verbs
When using 보다 with action verbs, the comparison can sometimes feel vague if only 더 is used. For instance, saying "I do more than 철수" doesn't specify if you work more often, work a larger amount, or work better. To resolve this, the instructor explains that it is common to add another adverb after 더 to clarify the nature of the comparison.

The instructor demonstrates this with two specific clarifications:
1. `저는 철수보다 더 많이 먹어요.` / I eat more (a larger amount) than 철수.
2. `저는 철수보다 일을 더 빨리 해요.` / I work faster (more quickly) than 철수.

By adding `많이 (a lot)` or `빨리 (quickly)`, the sentence becomes precise. Other options include `더 잘 해요 (do better)` or `더 많이 해요 (do more work)`.

---

### Comparing with 에 비해(서)
The second major form for comparison is **에 비해** or **에 비해서**. The syllable 서 at the end is optional and does not change the meaning. This form literally means "compared to" and is derived from the verb `비하다 (to compare)`. The instructor notes that 비하다 is a "special" verb not typically used on its own in general conversation; for the general action of comparing things, one should use the verb `비교하다`.

The usage of `Noun + 에 비해(서)` is very similar to 보다. For example, `저는 철수에 비해서 일을 더 빨리 해요` means "Compared to 철수, I work more quickly." While interchangeable with 보다 in most cases, 에 비해(서) is described as sounding slightly "fancier" or more formal. Crucially, it provides more emphasis on the act of comparison itself, highlighting the difference between the two items being compared.

---

### Comparing Actions and Hypotheticals
To compare actions using 에 비해(서), the action must first be nominalized using the `~는 것` form. The instructor provides the following examples:
- `이게 한국어를 배우는 것에 비해서 쉬워요.` / This is easy compared to learning Korean.
- `지하철을 타는 것은 걸어가는 것에 비해서 빨라요.` / Taking the subway is fast compared to walking.

Additionally, the instructor introduces **에 비하면**, which is the `~면 (if)` form of 비하다. This translates to "if you compare it to..." and is used for hypothetical comparisons. While less common than 비해서, it follows the same logic and usage patterns.
