### Overview
This lesson focuses on creating ordinal numbers in Korean using the suffixes 째 and 번째. While both are used to indicate order (first, second, third, etc.), they function differently in sentence structures and carry distinct nuances. The instructor explains how to attach these to Native Korean numbers, noting specific irregular forms and grammatical roles such as nouns, adjectives, and adverbs.

The learning arc begins with the basic construction of 째 for general listing, moves into the more versatile 번째 for counting occurrences and modifying nouns, and concludes with a specialized use of 째 after time counters to express duration. The instructor also clarifies the difference between the standalone adjective 첫 and the compound 첫 번째, highlighting fixed expressions where only the former is acceptable.

---

### Ordinal Numbers with 째
The most basic way to create an ordinal number is to take a Native Korean number and attach the suffix 째. Unlike when numbers are used with counters (where 하나 becomes 한, 둘 becomes 두, etc.), 째 typically attaches to the full, original form of the number. For example, "second" is 둘째 and "third" is 셋째. However, the number "one" is an exception; you do not say 하나째 or 한째. Instead, you use the special adjective 첫 (first) to form 첫째.

These forms are primarily used for general listing, such as identifying paragraphs in a text or steps in a process. They are not typically used to modify a noun directly (e.g., "the first person"). A unique social usage of this form is referring to children in a family by their birth order. For instance, a parent might say 우리 둘째는... `(As for our second [child]...)` to discuss their second-born.

---

### Counting and Modifying with 번째
For most everyday situations, especially when modifying a noun or counting the "number of times" something happens, 번째 is the standard choice. To form this, you use the shortened Native Korean number forms (한, 두, 세, 네, 스무) followed by a space and then 번째. As with the previous rule, "first" remains irregular, using 첫 번째 instead of anything based on 하나.

번째 is grammatically flexible and can function as a noun, an adjective, or an adverb. As a noun, it can be the subject or object of a sentence. As an adjective, it modifies a following noun, such as in the phrase 저의 첫 번째 여행이에요 `(It is my first trip)`. To use it as an adverb, the particle 로 is attached to the end. The instructor provides the example: 다섯 번째로 발표하신 분이 누구예요? `(Who is the person who presented fifth?)`. Here, 다섯 번째로 describes the order in which the action of presenting occurred.

---

### Expressing Duration with Counter + 째
A distinct usage of 째 occurs when it is attached directly to a time-related counter (like 시간 for hours, 일 for days, or 개월 for months). In this context, it describes how far into a continuous state or activity someone is. This is similar to the word 동안 (for/during), but while 동안 emphasizes the total duration, 째 emphasizes the current point reached in that duration.

The instructor demonstrates this with several examples. 한 시간째 공부하고 있어요 means "I have been studying for an hour," with the nuance of "I am now in my first hour of studying." Other examples include:
- 2년째 공부하고 있어요. / I am in my second year of studying.
- 너무 바빠서 잠을 3일째 못 자고 있어요. / I'm so busy that it's my third day of not being able to sleep.
- 이 집에서 산지 4개월째 되는 날부터 물이 세기 시작했어요. / Water started leaking from the day that it became the fourth month since living in this house.

---

### 첫 vs. 첫 번째
While 첫 번째 can function as an adjective, the word 첫 is a pure adjective on its own meaning "first." While they are often interchangeable when modifying a noun, 첫 is required for certain fixed compound words. The instructor lists several common expressions where 첫 번째 cannot be used:
- 첫사랑 (First love)
- 첫날 (First day)
- 첫인상 (First impression)
- 첫눈 (First snow / First sight)

The word 첫눈 is particularly interesting as it can refer to the first snowfall of the year or "first sight" in the context of falling in love at first sight. In these specific cultural and linguistic collocations, using 첫 번째 would sound unnatural.
