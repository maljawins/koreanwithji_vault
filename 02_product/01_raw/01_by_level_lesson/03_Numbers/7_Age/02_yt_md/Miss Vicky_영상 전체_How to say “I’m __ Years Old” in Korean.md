### Overview
In this lesson, the instructor explains how to express age in Korean, which involves navigating the distinction between Native Korean and Sino-Korean number systems. The primary method for stating age uses Native Korean numbers paired with the measure word 살 (years of age). However, the instructor also introduces a common alternative used by adults: stating one's birth year using Sino-Korean numbers.

The lesson covers essential conjugation rules for specific numbers, common sentence structures for both polite (존댓말) and casual (반말) speech, and the appropriate ways to ask others about their age depending on their social status. By the end of the video, learners are equipped to handle age-related conversations across various social contexts in Korea.

### Expressing Age with Native Korean Numbers
The standard way to say "I am [number] years old" uses the sentence structure 저는 [number] 살이에요 (polite) or 나는 [number] 살이야 (casual). The instructor emphasizes that while Korean has two number systems, Native Korean numbers must be used when counting years of age with the counter 살. For example, nineteen is 열아홉 in Native Korean, so "I am 19 years old" is 저는 열아홉 살이에요.

A critical grammar rule applies when Native Korean numbers are followed by a measure word like 살. The numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, and 20 change their form:
- 하나 (1) becomes 한
- 둘 (2) becomes 두
- 셋 (3) becomes 세
- 넷 (4) becomes 네
- 스물 (20) becomes 스무

The instructor demonstrates this by correcting a common mistake: thirty-one is 서른 하나, but "31 years old" must be 서른 한 살. Similarly, 22 years old is 스물두 살, 3 years old is 세 살, and 34 years old is 서른네 살. Other numbers like 다섯 (5), 여섯 (6), 일곱 (7), 여덟 (8), and 아홉 (9) do not change their form. The instructor notes that the change for 20 (스물 to 스무) only happens when it is the final figure; for example, 21 is still 스물한 살.

---

### Stating Birth Year with Sino-Korean Numbers
An alternative and very popular way for Koreans to share their age is by stating their birth year. This method uses the last two digits of the year followed by 년생 (year-born). Unlike the standard age expression, this format requires Sino-Korean numbers.

If someone was born in 1995, they would say 저는 구십오 년생이에요. The instructor breaks down the components: 년 means "year" and 생 means "birth." For those born in the year 2000 or later, there are two options. One can use the last two digits, such as 공공년생 (00-born) or 공사년생 (04-born), or the full year, such as 이천 년생 (2000-born) or 이천사 년생 (2004-born).

---

### Asking "How Old Are You?"
The instructor provides three distinct ways to ask for someone's age based on politeness levels and the age of the listener:
- **나이가 어떻게 되세요?** / What is your age? (Polite/Standard): This is the most common and softest way to ask someone you are not close with.
- **몇 살이세요?** / How many years old are you? (Polite): While correct, it is slightly less formal than the first option.
- **연세가 어떻게 되세요?** / What is your age? (Honorific): This version replaces 나이 (age) with the honorific term 연세 and is used strictly for elderly people, typically those over 65.

In casual situations (반말), such as children talking to each other or friends speaking to one another, the question is simplified to **몇 살이야?** (How old are you?).
