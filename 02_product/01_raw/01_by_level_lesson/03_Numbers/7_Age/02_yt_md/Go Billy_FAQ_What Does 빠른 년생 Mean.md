## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor explains the unique Korean cultural concept of **빠른 년생** (early year-born), a term used to describe individuals who entered school a year earlier than their same-year peers. This phenomenon arises from the intersection of the traditional Korean age system, the Western age system used for legal and academic purposes, and the specific timing of the Korean school year. The video explores how this concept functioned, the social complications it caused regarding politeness levels, and its eventual legal abolition.

---

### The Korean Age System vs. Western Age
To understand **빠른 년생**, one must first understand how Koreans calculate age. In most countries, a person is zero at birth and gains a year on their birthday. In Korea, everyone is considered one year old at birth. Furthermore, everyone gains a year simultaneously on January 1st, regardless of their actual birthday. The instructor illustrates this with an extreme example: a baby born on December 31st is one year old at birth and becomes two years old the very next day on January 1st. For legal and official matters, Koreans use **만** (Western age), which refers to the standard international age calculation.

---

### The Origin of 빠른 년생
The Korean school year begins in March. Historically, the school system determined enrollment based on **만** (Western age) rather than Korean age. This created a specific window for children born in January and February (and sometimes early March). Because these children reached the required Western age before the March start date, they were eligible to enroll in school with children born in the previous calendar year. 

The instructor uses the term **빠른 년생** (literally "early year-born") to describe these individuals. For example, someone born in January 1980 would be a **빠른 80년생**. Because they entered school "early," they grew up in a class where almost all their classmates were born in 1979. In the Western system, they are the same age as their classmates when school starts, but in the Korean age system, they are technically one year younger than their peers.

---

### Social Complications and Politeness Levels
The existence of **빠른 년생** created significant friction in Korean social hierarchy, which is heavily based on age. The instructor highlights the concept of **반말** (informal/half-speech), which is reserved for close friends of the same age or those younger than the speaker. Normally, one must use respectful speech with anyone even one year older. 

This led to a social dilemma: should a **빠른 년생** student use **반말** with classmates who are technically one year older in Korean age? The social consensus was yes, because they are in the same grade. However, this "age-jumping" caused confusion outside of school. If a **빠른 80년생** meets a "regular" **79년생** who was not in their class, the social hierarchy becomes ambiguous. This ambiguity often led to social friction and what the instructor describes as a form of age-based discrimination or confusion.

---

### The Abolition of the System
Due to these persistent social problems and the confusion regarding politeness levels, the South Korean government officially changed the school enrollment cutoff date to January 1st in 2009. Under the current law, children must be born in the same calendar year to be in the same grade. This change effectively destroyed the concept of **빠른 년생** for younger generations. 

The instructor notes that while the system is legally gone, the term remains common among adults born in the 1980s and 1990s who lived through the old system. He predicts that in another decade or so, the concept will likely disappear from the cultural lexicon entirely as the older generations age out.
