### Construction and Tense
To create this form, one simply takes a verb stem and attaches 지 followed by a space and the word 뭐. While 뭐 literally means "what," in this specific grammatical context, it functions more like "whatever." The instructor notes that the form can optionally end with a conjugation of the copula 이다 (to be), resulting in variations like 뭐예요, 뭐야, or 뭐니.

This ending is compatible with both action verbs and descriptive verbs. It can also be applied across different tenses by attaching it to the appropriate tense-conjugated stem. In the present tense, 하다 (to do) becomes 하지 뭐. In the past tense, it becomes 했지 뭐, and in the future tense, it becomes 하겠지 뭐. The inclusion of the 이다 ending is entirely optional; for example, one could say either 하지 뭐 or 하지 뭐야 depending on the desired nuance.

---

### Politeness and Social Nuance
The politeness level of the expression is determined by how it is ended. Using just ~지 뭐 is considered casual speech (반말). Adding the polite copula ending, such as ~지 뭐예요, makes the sentence sound more polite. In some cases, speakers may use ~죠 뭐 as a more polite alternative to ~지 뭐, though the instructor points out that ~지 뭐예요 is slightly less common in daily speech than the simpler versions.

Crucially, this form has an inherently playful or informal feel. Because it conveys a sense of "oh well" or "whatever," it is generally inappropriate for formal situations. The instructor illustrates this with a workplace scenario: if a boss asks about a late report, responding with ~지 뭐 would likely result in being fired because it sounds like the speaker doesn't care about the consequences.

---

### Meanings and Usage
The primary function of ~지 뭐 is to express that nothing else can be done or said about a situation. It is often translated into English as "well," "oh well," or "I guess." This "I guess" does not indicate a literal guess or uncertainty, but rather a resignation to fate. It is used when accepting an outcome, even if it is not ideal, or when noting that a bad situation could have been worse.

When using this form, the speaker is often implicitly looking for sympathy from the listener. It carries a "poor me" or "look at what I have to deal with" undertone. Additionally, it can be used when explaining something to a listener who is having trouble understanding; it signals that the speaker is just letting them know the reality of the situation and that they should just accept it.

### Examples and Shortened Forms
The instructor provides several examples to show the form in context. To express a casual "I'm sure I'll be able to do it," one might say:
> **할 수 있겠지 뭐.** / I guess I'll be able to do it.

When deciding on a simple course of action because there are no better options, a speaker might say:
> **그냥 피자나 시켜 먹지 뭐.** / I guess I'll just order pizza or something.

The form is also useful for admitting minor mistakes or explaining past actions:
> **잊었지 뭐야.** / I guess I forgot.
> **혼자 했지 뭐.** / I just did it myself.

A common variation involves combining this ending with the "must/should" grammar (~해야 하다). This often shortens in conversation. For example, 봐야 하지 뭐 (I guess I should see/taste it) shortens to 봐야지 뭐.
> **맛만 봐야지 뭐.** / I guess I should at least taste it.
> **그냥 포기해야지 뭐.** / I guess I better just give up.
