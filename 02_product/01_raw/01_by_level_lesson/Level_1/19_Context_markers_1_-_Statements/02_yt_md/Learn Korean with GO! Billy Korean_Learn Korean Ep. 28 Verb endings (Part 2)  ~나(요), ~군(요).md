## Teaching Points

### Overview
In this lesson, Billy introduces two common Korean verb endings that add specific nuances to sentences: `~나(요)` and `~군(요)`. These endings allow speakers to express curiosity or surprise, moving beyond basic statement or question forms. The lesson focuses on how these endings are attached to verb stems and the specific emotional or contextual "feeling" they convey to the listener.

The instructor uses a practical, conversational approach, demonstrating the endings through everyday examples like asking about someone's English ability or reacting to a situation. By the end of the lesson, learners should understand how to use these endings to sound more natural and expressive in Korean.

---

### The Question Ending ~나(요)
The first verb ending covered is `~나` or `~나요`. This ending is used exclusively with questions. To form it, you simply take the verb stem and attach `~나` for informal speech or `~나요` for polite speech. While it functions as a standard question marker, it carries a specific nuance of curiosity. Using this ending suggests that the speaker is especially interested or curious to know the answer.

The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this curious tone:
- `맛있나?` / Is it good? (Informal)
- `영어를 잘 할 수 있나요?` / Can you speak English well?
- `오늘 학교에 갔나요?` / Did you go to school today?
- `혹시 밥을 드셨나요?` / By chance, did you eat?

---

### The Exclamatory Ending ~군(요)
The second ending is `~군` or `~군요`, which is used to turn a sentence into an exclamation. This ending is particularly useful when the speaker has just realized something or is surprised by a new piece of information. It is described as being a much stronger exclamation than the `~네(요)` ending covered in previous lessons. In English, this can often be translated as "Oh, I see," "Wow," or "Oh really!"

To use this ending, you attach `~군` (informal) or `~군요` (polite) directly to the verb stem. The instructor demonstrates its use in various contexts:
- `맛있군.` / It's really good. (Expressing surprise at the taste)
- `벌써 갔군요.` / Oh, he already left. (Realizing someone is gone)
- `대학생이시군요.` / Oh, he's a college student. (Reacting to learning someone's status)
- `연필이 더 없군요.` / There aren't any more pencils. (Noticing a lack of supplies)
