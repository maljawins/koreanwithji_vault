## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson focuses on the sentence endings 군, 구나, and 구먼, which are used to express that the speaker has just realized or discovered something new. These endings function similarly to English exclamations like "Oh!", "I see," or "So it is!" The instructor explains how to conjugate these endings with both descriptive and action verbs, the differences in politeness levels between the forms, and provides historical and stylistic context for the less common variations.

---

### Conjugating and Using 군 and 군요

The first form introduced is 군, which can be made polite by adding the suffix 요. To conjugate this with a descriptive verb, you simply take the verb stem and attach 군 or 군요. For example, the verb 덥다 (to be hot) becomes 덥군요 (I see it's hot). However, action verbs require an additional step in the present tense: you must attach 는 before the ending. Therefore, the verb 하다 (to do) becomes 하는군요 (I see you are doing it). The instructor notes a specific phonological rule: if an action verb stem ends in ㄹ, that ㄹ must be removed before attaching 는, following the standard pattern for endings starting with the letter ㄴ.

For any tense other than the present, the distinction between descriptive and action verbs disappears; you simply attach 군 or 군요 directly to the past or future tense stem. Pragmatically, these endings signal a moment of realization. Common examples include 그렇군요 (Oh, I see) and 맛있군요 (I see it tastes good). The instructor also shares a Korean proverb to illustrate the usage: 원숭이도 나무에서 떨어지는군요, which translates to "So monkeys do fall from trees," used to express that even experts can make mistakes.

---

### Casual Realization with 구나

The ending 구나 is the casual (반말) equivalent of 군요. It follows the exact same conjugation rules as 군: descriptive verb stems take 구나 directly, while action verb stems in the present tense require 는구나. For example, the instructor demonstrates 그렇구나 (Oh, I see) as the casual version of 그렇군요.

While both 군 and 구나 are casual, the instructor emphasizes that 구나 is much more common in natural, everyday conversation with close friends. He recommends using 구나 as the default casual choice for expressing realization. On-screen examples include 드디어 눈이 오는구나! (Oh, it's finally snowing!) and 영희도 참 바쁘구나 (I see Yeonghui is also very busy).

---

### Stylistic Variations: 구먼 and 구만

The instructor introduces 구먼 as an older, more traditional style of the realization ending. It conjugates exactly like 군 and carries the same meaning but sounds more "old-fashioned" or like something an older person might say. 

A further variation is 구만, which is a non-standard, dialectical, or "slangy" version of 구먼. While it is frequently heard in casual speech and can add a fun or even sarcastic tone to a sentence, it is technically incorrect according to standard grammar rules. The instructor advises against using it in formal settings like news reports or writing, but notes that it can be used for comedic effect or to sound more like a native speaker in very informal contexts. He also warns that while 구먼 can take the polite 요 to become 구먼요, the non-standard 구만 is almost never used with 요. An example of this style is 그러구만! (I see it is so!).
