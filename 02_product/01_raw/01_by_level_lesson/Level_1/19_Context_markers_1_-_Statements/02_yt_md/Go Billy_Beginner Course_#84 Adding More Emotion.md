### Overview
This lesson introduces four specific verb endings used to convey various emotions or pragmatic nuances in Korean: surprise, a desire for agreement, curiosity, and realization. While standard polite endings like ~아요/어요 convey information, these endings allow a speaker to express their subjective reaction to the information being shared. The instructor covers the conjugation rules for each, including specific irregularities for action versus descriptive verbs and handling ㄹ-bottom stems.

The lesson follows a structured arc: first, the individual grammar points are explained with conjugation formulas; second, a set of isolated example sentences demonstrates each form; third, a full conversation practice integrates the forms into a natural dialogue; and finally, the instructor provides advanced notes on tense restrictions and common usage variations.

### Expressing Surprise with ~네요
The ending ~네요 is used when the speaker is surprised by something they are experiencing or observing in the moment. To conjugate, take the verb stem and attach ~네요. In casual speech (반말), the final 요 is dropped, leaving just ~네.

The instructor provides several examples of this in action. For instance, saying 이 김치가 정말 맵네요 `(This kimchi is really spicy)` implies that the speaker has just tasted the kimchi and is surprised by its heat. Similarly, 둘 다 맛있네요 `(Both taste good)` and 이 방법은 좀 느리네요 `(This method is a little slow)` both carry an underlying tone of unexpected discovery. A specific rule applies to verbs with an ㄹ-bottom stem, such as 멀다 `(to be far)`: the ㄹ is removed before attaching the ending, resulting in 머네요.

---

### Seeking Agreement with ~죠
When a speaker wants the listener to agree with them or confirm something they believe to be true, they use the ending ~죠. This is the contracted form of ~지요. In casual speech, the speaker uses just ~지. This functions similarly to tag questions in English like "right?" or "isn't it?"

Examples shown include 한국말이 어렵죠 `(Korean is difficult, right?)` and 하지만 이 문법은 쉽지 `(But this grammar is easy, isn't it?)`. It can also be used with the past tense, as in 화장을 했죠 `(You put on makeup, right?)`. The instructor notes that this ending is versatile and commonly used to soften the tone of a statement by inviting the listener into the conversation.

---

### Expressing Curiosity with ~나요 and ~ㄴ/은가요
To express that the speaker is especially curious to know the answer to a question, they use ~나요 or ~ㄴ/은가요. The conjugation depends on the type of verb. For action verbs, the speaker attaches ~나요 to the stem. For descriptive verbs, the speaker attaches ~은가요 if the stem ends in a consonant, or ~ㄴ가요 if it ends in a vowel. In casual speech, the 요 is removed.

Examples include 지금은 너무 이른가요? `(Is now too early?)`, 상관 없나요? `(Does it not matter?)`, and 오늘은 시간이 많은가요? `(Do you have a lot of time today?)`. Irregularities include stems ending in ㄹ: for descriptive verbs like 길다 `(to be long)`, the ㄹ is removed to become 긴가요; for action verbs like 살다 `(to live)`, it becomes 사나요. For ㅂ-irregular descriptive verbs like 맵다 `(to be spicy)`, the ㅂ is replaced with 우 before adding ~ㄴ가요, resulting in 매운가요.

---

### Expressing Realization with ~군요
The ending ~군요 is used when the speaker has just realized or discovered a new fact. To conjugate descriptive verbs, simply attach ~군요 to the stem. For action verbs in the present tense, the speaker must attach ~는군요. In casual speech, the 요 is dropped, leaving ~군 or ~는군.

Examples provided are 좀 이상하군요 `(That's a little strange — I just realized it)`, 그렇군요 `(It is so / I see)`, and 역시 오늘도 잊었군 `(Not surprisingly, you forgot today too — I just realized it)`. For action verbs with an ㄹ-bottom stem like 살다 `(to live)`, the ㄹ is removed before adding ~는군요, resulting in 사는군요.

---

### Advanced Notes on Tense and Usage
The instructor concludes with important clarifications regarding ~나요 and ~ㄴ/은가요. The distinction between action and descriptive verb conjugations for these endings only applies in the present tense. In the past tense, both action and descriptive verbs simply use ~나요 (e.g., 할머니가 아프셨나요? `Was grandmother sick?`). 

Additionally, it is common for descriptive verb stems ending in a consonant to use ~나요 instead of ~은가요 in modern speech. For example, 맵다 `(to be spicy)` can become either 매운가요 or 맵나요. However, because both forms are widely used, learners should be familiar with both.
