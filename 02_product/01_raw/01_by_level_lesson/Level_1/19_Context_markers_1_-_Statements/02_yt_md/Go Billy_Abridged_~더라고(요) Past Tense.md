## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the verb ending ~더라고(요), a past tense marker used to describe events or states that the speaker personally experienced, witnessed, or discovered. The core concept is "personal knowledge"—it signals to the listener that the speaker was present when the action happened or the state was observed. This form adds a layer of reflection or reminiscence to a sentence, making it sound more descriptive and subjective than the standard past tense.

The lesson covers the basic conjugation rules, the nuanced differences between using it with action verbs versus descriptive verbs, and the specific usage of the casual form ~더라. The instructor emphasizes that while it can be translated as "I recall that..." or "I found out that...", in most natural English contexts, it simply translates as a standard past tense (did, was, happened) but carries the unspoken weight of "I know this because I was there."

---

### Basic Meaning and Nuance
The grammar form is constructed by taking a verb stem and attaching ~더라고(요). The presence of the syllable 더 is a linguistic clue that the form relates to the past tense. Specifically, it indicates past tense that the speaker has personally experienced or witnessed. The instructor uses the analogy of "being there when it happened" to explain the nuance.

While a standard past tense sentence simply states a fact, ~더라고(요) implies the speaker is reflecting on a memory. It can convey a sense of reminiscing or explaining something based on firsthand evidence. For example, saying a cell phone was expensive using this form implies the speaker saw the price tag or felt the impact of the cost themselves, rather than just reporting a general fact.

---

### Conjugation Rules and Action vs. Descriptive Verbs
For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the rule is simple: attach ~더라고(요) directly to the verb stem. The instructor notes that you should never use a past-tense stem with descriptive verbs in this form. For example, to say it was cold, you use 춥더라고요, never 추웠더라고요.

For action verbs, the speaker has two choices depending on the desired emphasis. Attaching ~더라고(요) to the regular verb stem (e.g., 하더라고요) describes an action the speaker witnessed. However, attaching it to a past-tense stem (e.g., 했더라고요) emphasizes that the action had already been completed or was "completely in the past" at the time the speaker observed the situation. This is similar to the "had done" structure in English.

---

### Example Sentences and Contexts
The instructor provides several examples to illustrate these rules. When describing the sky as pretty, the sentence is:
> **하늘이 정말 예쁘더라고요.** / The sky was really pretty (I recall/saw it).

When describing the inability to see something:
> **어디 있는지 안 보이더라고요.** / I couldn't see where it was (I remember trying to look).

An example of the completed past with an action verb involves falling flowers:
> **바람이 많이 불어서, 꽃이 다 떨어졌더라고요.** / Because it was very windy, the flowers had all fallen off.

Other examples include:
> **많이 비싸더라고요.** / It was very expensive.
> **저는 조금 달더라고요.** / It was a little bit sweet for me (I found it to be sweet).
> **도저히 못 찾겠더라고요.** / No matter what, I just couldn't find it.
> **그 방은 조금 작더라고요.** / That room was a little small.

---

### The Casual Form ~더라 and Self-Talk
The form ~더라 is the casual (반말) version of ~더라고요. It cannot take the polite ending 요. While it can be used to talk to friends—such as praising a friend's presentation by saying **너 발표 잘 하더라?** (You were really good at your presentation)—it is actually more common in "self-talk."

When talking to oneself, ~더라 is often used as a question to jog one's own memory. It is used when the speaker knows the information but is struggling to recall it in the moment. A very common expression is:
> **뭐였더라?** / What was that? (I'm trying to remember).

Another example of this "memory-jogging" self-talk is:
> **오늘이 무슨 요일이더라?** / What day of the week was it today? (Wait, what day is it again?)
