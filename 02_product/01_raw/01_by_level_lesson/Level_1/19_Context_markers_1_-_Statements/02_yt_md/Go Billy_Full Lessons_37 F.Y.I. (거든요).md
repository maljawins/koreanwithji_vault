## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces a specific verb ending used to provide information that the speaker believes the listener does not already know. This ending functions similarly to English expressions like "FYI," "for your information," or "just so you know." While it is a common and useful form to recognize, the instructor emphasizes that it requires careful handling regarding social context and intonation to avoid sounding rude or condescending.

---

### Grammar Construction: 거든(요)
The construction of this form is straightforward. To create this ending, take the verb stem and add **거든요**. When speaking with close friends in a casual (반말) setting, the polite suffix **요** is dropped, leaving just **거든**. The instructor notes that even if a student chooses not to use this form themselves initially, they will frequently encounter it in natural Korean conversation.

---

### The Importance of Intonation and Attitude
A critical aspect of using **거든요** is the speaker's intonation, as it significantly alters the nuance of the sentence. A rising intonation at the end of the sentence can make the speaker sound rude, defensive, or as if they are talking down to the listener. Conversely, a falling intonation is generally acceptable for simply informing someone of a fact they might not know.

The instructor demonstrates this with the example **아니거든요** (No, it's not / For your information, it isn't). When said with a rising tone, it carries a sharp, argumentative feeling. Because of this potential for rudeness, the instructor advises against using the rising intonation in formal situations or with people you are not close to.

---

### Practical Examples and Contexts
The video provides several sentences to illustrate how **거든요** provides background information or clarification:

- **제가 미국 사람이거든요.** / Just so you know, I'm an American. (Used, for example, if someone compliments your English and you want to explain why it is good).
- **저는 생선을 싫어하거든요.** / For your information, I hate fish.
- **우리 사귀고 있거든요.** / For your information, we're dating.
- **내일부터 일하게 됐거든요.** / Just so you know, I'm going to be working from tomorrow.

The instructor also highlights a specific, potentially rude usage: **됐거든**. This is a blunt way to reject someone, equivalent to saying "No thanks" or "I'm done with this" in a dismissive manner. It is often used to shut down a conversation or an unwanted advance.
