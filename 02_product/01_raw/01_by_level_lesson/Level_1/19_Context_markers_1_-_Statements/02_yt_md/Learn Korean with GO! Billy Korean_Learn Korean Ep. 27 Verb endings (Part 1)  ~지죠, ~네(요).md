## Teaching Points

### Overview
In this lesson, the instructor introduces how Korean verb endings can be modified to add specific nuances or extra meaning to a sentence. While the Korean language possesses a vast array of these endings, this video focuses on two of the most common types: those used for seeking confirmation and those used for expressing surprise or exclamation.

The lesson is structured to show the grammatical construction for both casual and polite speech levels for each ending. By attaching these suffixes to the verb stem, speakers can move beyond simple statements to engage the listener more dynamically or express their personal reaction to information.

---

### Seeking Confirmation with 지 and 죠
The first verb ending discussed is used to request confirmation from the listener, functioning similarly to English tag questions like "right?", "isn't it?", or "aren't you?". To form this, the speaker takes the verb stem and attaches `지` for casual sentences or `죠` for polite sentences. This ending implies that the speaker already has an idea of the answer but wants the listener to agree or confirm.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate this usage across different contexts:
- **맛있지?** / It's good, right?
- **어제 학교에 안 갔죠?** / You didn't go to school yesterday, did you?
- **조금 더 빨리 먹지?** / Eat a little faster, okay? (Used here as a soft suggestion/confirmation of intent)
- **고양이는 물을 싫어하죠?** / Cats hate water, don't they?

---

### Expressing Surprise with 네 and 네요
The second verb ending acts as an exclamation marker, used when the speaker is impressed, surprised, or has just realized something. While English speakers often convey this through vocal intonation alone, Korean utilizes the specific endings `네` (casual) and `네요` (polite) attached to the verb stem.

This ending is particularly common when reacting to a new discovery or complimenting someone. The instructor illustrates this with the following examples:
- **맛있네!** / It's really good! (Expressing surprise at the taste)
- **한국말을 아주 잘 하네요!** / You're very good at Korean!
- **몰랐네요!** / I didn't know that! (Expressing a sudden realization)
- **열심히 공부했네!** / You studied really hard!
