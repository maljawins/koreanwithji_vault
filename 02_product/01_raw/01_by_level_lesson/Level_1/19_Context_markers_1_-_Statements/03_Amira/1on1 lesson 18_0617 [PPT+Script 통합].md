## Lesson Overview

This lesson marks a significant transition for Amira as she begins moving toward the intermediate level of Korean. The primary focus is on "Context Markers"—nuanced sentence endings that allow a speaker to express emotions, realizations, and intentions that are often lost in strictly formal speech. Ji emphasizes that while formal endings like ~ㅂ/습니다 are necessary, they can sound "robotic" because they function as their own context markers and do not allow for the additional emotional layering provided by the markers covered today.

A major pedagogical theme of this session is the concept of "Comprehensible Input," a hypothesis championed by linguist Stephen Krashen. Ji explains that language acquisition happens most effectively when learners are exposed to messages they can understand through context, even if the grammar is slightly above their current level (i.e., "i+1"). To support this, the lesson moves away from isolated sentence practice and toward listening-based training, using the newly learned context markers to help Amira decipher the speaker's subtle meanings and reactions in real-world scenarios.

The lesson covers a wide array of endings, including ~네(요) for immediate realizations, ~구나 for realizations after thought, ~지(요) for confirmation and suggestions, ~잖아(요) for stating obvious facts, and ~더라고(요) for recalling personal experiences. By understanding these "leaves" and "fruits" on the grammatical tree, Amira will be able to speak more naturally and better understand the emotional subtext of native Korean conversations.

### Slide 4 — Context Markers Overview
- **On screen:** Two tree diagrams illustrating sentence structure.

    - Right tree (Informal/Polite): 하 (Stem) + 네 (Leaf: Context - Immediate Reaction) + 요 (Fruit: Politeness). Result: 하네요.
    - Text highlights: Context markers only in informal speech (반말 & ~요 form). Formal ending ㅂ/습니다 is a context marker of its own; you can't have more than one.
- **Ji's teaching:** First, a quick reminder: these 'context markers' can only be used in informal speech, when you're using 반말 and the ~요 form. The formal speech ending, ~ㅂ/습니다, is a context marker of its own. The idea is that it already marks the “formal” context. And you cannot have more than one context marker at a time. This means you can’t add emotions to your sentences in formal speech. So, don’t overuse this form because you’ll sound like a robot.

### Slide 5 — ~네(요): Immediate Realization
- **On screen:** Meaning: Realized something JUST NOW; Immediate reaction; Similar to "Oh" or "Wow." 
    - Grammar: Add after stem; 받침 doesn't matter. 
    - Irregular ㄹ: Remove ㄹ then add 네(요) (살다 → 사네요). 
    - 이다: After 받침 → 이네요 (집이네요); After vowel → 네요 (친구네요). 
    - Tree diagram showing 하/먹 (Stem) + 네 (Leaf: Immediate Reaction) + 요 (Fruit: Respect).
- **Ji's teaching:** Let’s start with 네요. ~네(요) shows your immediate reaction to something you've just realized, or something that's just happened. It's a lot like saying 'Oh!' or 'Wow!' The keyword here is “Just now.” This is important because we have another form that also shows realization in a slightly different way. The rule is simple: just attach 네요 after the stem. One exception is with the ㄹ verbs, like 살다. You first remove ㄹ then add 네요, so 살다 becomes 사네요. You can also use this with the past tense stem, so 하다 would be 했네요. With 이다, after a consonant, you’d use 이네요. For example, "이제 집이네요" (Oh, we're home now!) when you just realized you’re almost home. After a vowel, you just use 네요, like "좋은 친구네요" (Oh, she's a good friend!) when you just found out something good about a friend.

It literally means “Yeah, it is so,” but it's used like “That’s right.” Then, he adds, "물고기가 많네." He just realized, in that moment, that there are a lot of them! ### Slide 8 — ~구나 / ~구만 / 군(요): Realization After Thinking
- **On screen:** Meaning: Thought about it THEN realized; Similar to "I see." 
    - Descriptive Verb: Stem + 구나 (예쁘구나, 있구나). 
    - Action Verb: Stem + 는구나 (가는구나, 먹는구나). 
    - Irregular ㄹ: Remove ㄹ + 는구나 (사는구나). 
    - Negative ~지 않다: Descriptive → 않군요; Action → 않는군요. 
    - Usage: 반말 uses 구나/구만/군 (구만/군 sound like talking to yourself). 존댓말 is only 군요.
- **Ji's teaching:** Now, these are the context markers that also express realization: ~구나, ~구만, and ~군(요). The key difference is that this one sounds like you've figured something out after giving it some thought. It's not an instantaneous, immediate reaction like ~네(요). In English, it has a similar vibe to “I see” or “Aha” rather than just a quick “Oh!” ~구나, ~구만, and ~군 are all 반말. ~구만 and ~군 sound a bit more like you're talking to yourself. For 존댓말, it’s only ~군요. The way you attach this is different for Descriptive vs. Action Verbs. For descriptive verbs, you simply attach it directly to the stem: 예쁘구나. For action verbs, you must insert 는 before the ending: 가는구나, 먹는구나. For the negative form ~지 않다, descriptive verbs become 않구나, while action verbs become 않는구나.

### Slide 9 — Past Tense + ~구나 / ~구만 / 군(요)
- **On screen:** Rule: Past Stem + 구나(만) / 군(요). No need to differentiate between A.V and D.V. 
    - Examples: 예뻤구나, 있었구나, 왔구나, 갔구나. 
    - Tree diagram: Stem + Branch (Past) + 군 (Leaf: I thought about it then realized) + 요 (Fruit: Respect).
- **Ji's teaching:** In the past tense, you don't need to differentiate between action verbs and descriptive verbs. You simply attach ~구나, ~군, or ~군요 directly to the past tense stem. For example, 예쁘다 becomes 예뻤구나 and 오다 becomes 왔구나.

Now, A processes this new information, connects it with the earlier observation, and says: "아~ TV에 나와서 그렇구나." This is what 구나 does. He is connecting two pieces of information (many people + the poster) and coming to a realization after thinking about it. It's that 'Aha! Now I understand!' moment.

### Slide 12 — ~지(요): Confirmation
- **On screen:** Keyword: Confirmation. 
    - Grammar: Stem + 지(요); 받침 doesn't matter. 
    - Contraction: 하지요 → 하죠, 먹지요 → 먹죠. 
    - As a question to others: 1. Yes/No (Right?, Isn't it?). 2. 5W1H (Untranslatable intonation change - implies you expect them to know). 
    - As a question to yourself: Only 5W1H (이제 뭐 하지? - What do I do now?). 
    - Tree diagram: Stem + 지 (Leaf: Confirmation) + 요 (Fruit: Respect).
- **Ji's teaching:** Next, we have 지(요). ~지 is 반말, and in 존댓말, it’s ~지요, though you’ll hear the contraction ~죠 much more often. You just add it after the stem; it doesn't matter if it's an action or descriptive verb or if there's a 받침. The keyword for this form is 'confirmation.' When used as a Yes/No question to others, you're asking for the listener's agreement, like "Right?" or "Isn't it?" It can also be used with 5W1H questions. In English, this is an intonation change. "Where did you put your card?" vs "Where’d you put your card, again?" The second one implies I expect you to know and I want you to confirm. That is what 죠 does. When asking yourself, you can only use it with 5W1H questions, like "어떡하지?" (What should I do?) or "이제 뭐 하지?" (What do I do now?).

### Slide 16 — ~지(요) As a Statement
- **On screen:** Usage: To confirm something yourself; To express an obvious conclusion. 
    - Meanings: "Certainly", "Definitely", "Absolutely." 
    - Vocabulary: 당연하다 (To be obvious). 당연한 결과 (Natural outcome), 당연히 해야 해요 (Supposed to do it), 당연하지 (Of course). 
    - Vocabulary: 혹시 (By any chance). 혹시 몰라 (You never know). 
    - Comic strip: "Did you get the passport?!" "당연하지! (Of course!)"
- **Ji's teaching:** When you use ~지(요) in a statement, you are essentially confirming something yourself. It's less about getting the listener's agreement and more about stating something that's already clear or obvious to you. It carries the feeling of 'Certainly' or 'Absolutely.' In this comic, one character asks if they got the passport, and the other replies "당연하지!" This is a perfect example. He’s confidently stating an obvious conclusion. Let's look at 당연하다. It means ‘to be obvious.’ 당연한 결과 is a natural outcome. 당연히 해야 해요 means "I'm supposed to do it." And 당연하지 means "Of course." Also, note 혹시 (by any chance). "혹시 몰라" means "You never know."

### Slide 18 — ~지(요) As a Suggestion
- **On screen:** Honorific + 지요 (죠) = Polite & Gentle Suggestion. 
    - Feels like [Let’s] + [Please] combined. 
    - Reminder: ~자 is only for 반말. ㅂ/읍시다 is technically formal but sounds rude (갑시다 is rude). 
    - Use ~(으)시죠 instead. 
    - Example: 장모님, 오사카로 가시죠 (Mother in law, let’s please go to Osaka).
- **Ji's teaching:** When 지요 comes after an honorific verb, it becomes a very polite and gentle way to suggest something. It feels like a combination of 'Let's...' and 'Please.' Look at the text: "장모님, 오사카로 가시죠." 장모님 means ‘mother-in-law.’ If there’s one person you do not want to disrespect, it’s her! 가다 becomes 가시다 in honorific form, then 가시죠. It's a very polite invitation. Remember: ~자 is only for 반말. ㅂ/읍시다 (like 갑시다) is technically formal but can sound rude, like a boss telling you what to do. Use ~(으)시죠 instead; it's the most natural and well-received option.

### Slide 20 — 겠지(요): Guessing
- **On screen:** 겠지(요) = 겠다 + 지(요). 
    - Meaning: Probably; I guess it would/will. 
    - Example: 그 사람은 지금 자겠지? (He’s probably sleeping now, right?). 
    - Poetic example: 내일은 내일의 해가 뜨겠지 (I guess the sun will rise again tomorrow). 
    - Sarcastic honorific: 그러시겠지 (I guess [your highness] would do/say so). 
    - Structure: Stem + 아/어야 + 겠지(요) (I guess/suppose it should). Example: 늦었으니 이제 집에 가야겠지 (Since it’s late, I guess I should go home now).
- **Ji's teaching:** Next is 겠지(요). Remember, ~겠 expresses intention or possibility, so this is about guessing and then confirming that guess. It means "Probably" or "I guess it would." For example, "그 사람은 지금 자겠지?" means "He's probably sleeping now, right?" You're seeking confirmation for your guess. Look at "내일은 내일의 해가 뜨겠지." This is a poetic saying: "I guess the sun will rise again tomorrow." Then there's "그러시겠지." 그러시다 is the honorific of 그렇다. It literally means "I guess your highness would do so," but look at the cat's face in the image—it's sarcastic! Like "Oh, of course you would say that." Finally, when you combine 야 (must/should) with 겠지, it means "I suppose it should." Like "집에 가야겠지" (I guess I should go home now).

### Slide 21 — 아/어야지(요): Certainty and Obligation
- **On screen:** Meaning: Without 겠, you sound more certain. 
    - 1st Person Subject: "I'm just gonna" (조금만 보고 자야지 - I'm just gonna watch only a little then sleep). 
    - 2nd/3rd Person Subject: "Should", "Got to" (일어나. 일해야지 - Wake up, you got work to do).
- **Ji's teaching:** While 겠지 involved guessing, 아/어야지(요) expresses more certainty or firm intention because you have 야 instead of 겠. For the first person, it expresses a firm decision you've made for yourself. "조금만 보고 자야지" means "I'm just gonna watch a little more then sleep." When the subject is the second or third person, it means "should" or "got to." In the image, one is waking the other up: "일어나.. 일해야지" (Wake up.. you got work to do). It's a reminder of duty.

### Slide 22 — ~잖아(요): "You Know"
- **On screen:** Meaning: You insist you're right and assume the listener knows; Similar to "You know ___________." 
    - Origin: From ~지 않아요 (but usage is different). 
    - Grammar: Add after stem; 받침 doesn't matter. 
    - Formal version: 잖습니까? (The only context marker in formal speech). 
    - Example: 시간이 많이 안 남았잖습니까? (You know there’s not much time left). 
    - Tree diagram: Stem + 잖아 (Leaf: You know ___) + 요 (Fruit: Respect).
- **Ji's teaching:** ~잖아(요) originated from the negative ~지 않아요, but the usage is completely different! Don't confuse it with negation. When you use this, you are insisting you're right and assuming the listener already knows the fact. It's like saying "You know..." in English. The rule is simple: add it directly to the stem. Occasionally you'll hear ~잖습니까. This is the formal version and is the only context marker allowed in formal speech. A sports commentator might say "시간이 많이 안 남았잖습니까?" (You know there's not much time left) because it's an obvious fact for the viewers.

### Slide 25 — ~거든(요): Background Information
- **On screen:** Meaning: Provides background information; "It's because ~", "Just so you know." 
    - Grammar: Add after stem; 받침 doesn't matter. 
    - Examples: 지금 한국 너무 덥거든요? (Just so you know, it’s super hot in Korea right now). 친구랑 싸웠거든요 (It’s because I fought with a friend). 
    - Tree diagram: Stem + 거든 (Leaf: Background info) + 요 (Fruit: Respect).
- **Ji's teaching:** ~거든(요) is used to provide background information or a reason. It's like saying "Hey, listen, let me explain something to you." You can use it to set the stage: "지금 한국 너무 덥거든요? 그러니까 그냥 가을에 오세요" (Just so you know, it's super hot in Korea, so come in autumn). Or use it as a direct reason: "오늘 기분이 안 좋네요. 친구랑 싸웠거든요" (I'm not feeling good; it's because I fought with a friend). Be careful, though—depending on your tone, it can sound a little condescending or rude.

### Slide 26 — ~더라고(요): Recalling Experience
- **On screen:** Meaning: Talking about something you've personally experienced; "I recall." 
    - Usage: 반말 is ~더라 or ~더라고. 존댓말 is only ~더라고요 (더라요 is X). 
    - Descriptive Verbs: Only use Present Stem. Example: 덥더라고요 (I recall it was hot). 
    - Action Verbs: Can use both Present & Past Stem. 
    - Tree diagram: Stem + 더라(고) (Leaf: I recall) + 요 (Fruit: Respect).
- **Ji's teaching:** The core meaning of ~더라고(요) is that you are talking about something you've personally experienced or witnessed. It's like saying "I recall that..." For descriptive verbs, you can only use the present stem. If you say "날씨가 너무 덥더라고요," you use the present stem 덥다 even if talking about the past, because the heat stays "hot" in your memory. If you used the past stem, it would imply the heat stopped being hot after you left, which is weird.

### Slide 27 — Descriptive Verb + 더라고(요)
- **On screen:** A: [내가 말한 그 피자집] 가봤지? (You’ve been to that pizza place, right?). 
    - B: 응, 맛있더라 (Yeah, I recall it IS good). 
    - Why is 맛있었더라 wrong? It will always be delicious in B's memory; it's expected to be delicious again; they keep making delicious pizza.
- **Ji's teaching:** Even if the food is gone, you use the present stem 맛있더라 because the characteristic of being delicious is a permanent impression in your memory. You expect it to still be delicious if you went back.

### Slide 28 — Action Verb + 더라고(요)
- **On screen:** Mom: 오빠는 뭐 하니? (What’s your brother doing?). 
    - A: 운동하더라고요 (I recall he’s working out - Probably still is). 
    - B: 운동했더라고요 (I recall he had worked out - Now done). 
    - Note: [Present Stem] is fine most of the time.
- **Ji's teaching:** For action verbs, you can use both. If you saw him in the middle of a workout, say "운동하더라고요." If you saw him just as he finished, say "운동했더라고요." Most of the time, the present stem is fine.

### Slide 29 — ~나요? ~가요?: Curiosity
- **On screen:** Meaning: Genuinely curious; Sounds polite and kind; "Could it be that?" "I wonder if." 
    - Nuance only works with 존댓말 (요). 
    - Descriptive Verb: Stem + ㄴ/은가요? (큰가요?, 작은가요?). 
    - Action Verb: Stem + 나요? (가나요?, 먹나요?). 
    - Irregular ㄹ: Remove ㄹ + 나요? (사나요?). 
    - Irregular ㅂ: Replace ㅂ with 우 + ㄴ가요? (매운가요?, 더운가요?). 
    - Exception: 있나요?, 없나요?.
- **Ji's teaching:** These markers are for questions and express "curiosity." It sounds like you're genuinely wondering. This nuance only works with the ~요 form. If you drop the 요, it sounds like you're questioning yourself. For action verbs, use ~나요? (not 받침 sensitive). For descriptive verbs, use ~은/ㄴ가요? (is 받침 sensitive). Irregular ㄹ verbs remove the ㄹ (살다 → 사나요?). Irregular ㅂ verbs change to 우 (맵다 → 매운가요?). Note that 있다 and 없다 always take ~나요? even though they are descriptive.

### Slide 30 — ~나요? Example: Bathroom
- **On screen:** Illustration of bathroom signs. 
    - "화장실이 어디예요?" vs "혹시 화장실이 어디인가요?" (More polite, kind). 
    - Commonly used with 혹시. 
    - 이다 + ㄴ가요? → 인가요?.
- **Ji's teaching:** "화장실이 어디예요?" is fine but can feel a bit cold, like "WHERE IS THE BATHROOM?". Adding 인가요 makes it sound like "Hey, I was wondering where the bathroom is," which is much kinder.

### Slide 31 — ~나요? Example: English Skills
- **On screen:** English-themed word cloud. 
    - 민수 씨는 혹시 영어를 잘 하나요? (Is Minsu good at English, by any chance?).
- **Ji's teaching:** This is a polite inquiry showing genuine curiosity about Minsu's skills.

### Slide 32 — ~니? ~냐?: Banmal Questions
- **On screen:** Questions in 반말. 
    - ~니?: 반말 version of 나요/가요; Sounds kind and feminine. Example: 너도 그렇니? (Could it be you feel the same?). 
    - ~냐?: Common among male friends; Super informal/thuggish; Sounds cool and masculine. Example: 야, 거기 너! 돈 있냐? (Hey, you there! You got money, huh?). 
    - Irregular ㄹ: Remove ㄹ (사니?, 사냐?).
- **Ji's teaching:** These are exclusively for 반말. ~니? is typically used by women or parents and has a kinder, softer feeling. It's the casual version of the curiosity markers. ~냐? is common among male friends. It's meant to sound masculine but is very blunt. Most Korean women don't like it when guys use ~냐? with them; it can sound aggressive. I only use it with my very close guy friends.

### Slide 33 — ~을/ㄹ까(요)?: Listener's Opinion
- **On screen:** Meaning: Asking for listener's opinion; "Mild suggestion" or "Soft wondering." 
    - Meanings: Shall I?, Should I/we?, How about?, Do you want me to?, Do you think ____?. 
    - Grammar: After 받침 → 을까(요)? (먹을까요?); After vowel → ㄹ까(요)? (갈까요?). 
    - Exceptions: ㄹ (알까요?), ㅂ (매울까요?), ㅎ (어떨까요?). 
    - Example: 제가 할까요? (Do you want me to do it?).
- **Ji's teaching:** The core function of ~까(요) is to ask for the listener's opinion. It's a mild suggestion. "제가 할까요?" means "Do you want me to do it?" For verbs ending in ㄹ like 알다, just add 까 (알까요?). For ㅂ irregulars, change to 우 (매울까요?). For ㅎ irregulars, remove the ㅎ (어떨까요?).

### Slide 34 — 을/ㄹ: The Future(?) Branch
- **On screen:** IMPORTANT NOTE: 을/ㄹ shows you're talking about something that hasn't happened yet. 
    - Implies possibility or future action. 
    - Not "future tense" by grammar definition, but we'll call it "the future branch." 
    - Used in: Future tense (할 거예요), Ability (할 수 있어요), Modifying nouns (할 것). 
    - Grammar: After 받침 → 을; After vowel → ㄹ.
- **Ji's teaching:** 을/ㄹ refers to something that hasn't happened yet—something "to be done." This is why you see it in future tense and ability forms. It's technically not a "future tense," but calling it the "future branch" makes it easier to understand.

## Teaching Points

### Context Markers (Sentence Endings)
These markers are "leaves" on the grammatical tree that add emotional context. They are used in informal (반말) and polite (~요) speech. Formal speech (~ㅂ/습니다) is its own context and cannot be combined with these markers.
- **Realization:** ~네(요) is for immediate, "just now" reactions. ~구나/군요 is for realizations after thinking or connecting information ("I see").
- **Confirmation:** ~지(요) is used to confirm information with the listener ("Right?") or to state something obvious ("Certainly").
- **Guessing:** 겠지(요) is for probable guesses. 아/어야지(요) expresses a stronger sense of obligation or firm intention ("I'm gonna" or "You should").
- **Background:** ~거든(요) provides the "why" or background info for a statement ("It's because...").
- **Curiosity:** ~나요? (Action) and ~은/ㄴ가요? (Descriptive) express gentle, polite curiosity.
- **Recalling:** ~더라고(요) is used to report something the speaker personally experienced or witnessed.

### The "Future Branch" (을/ㄹ)
The suffix 을/ㄹ indicates that an action has not happened yet. It is the building block for the future tense, expressing ability, and modifying nouns to show "things to be done." When combined with 까(요), it asks for the listener's opinion on a future possibility or suggestion.

### Compound Verbs (Supplemental)
Compound verbs follow the structure **[Stem] + 아/어 + [Another verb]**.
- **아/어하다:** Used to describe the feelings or thoughts of a third person (e.g., 슬퍼해요 - "He is being sad").
- **아/어 있다:** Indicates a completed action that continues in that state (e.g., 깨져 있어요 - "It is broken").
- **아/어 주다:** Doing something as a favor for someone. Using "좀" (short for 조금) with this form softens requests (e.g., 물 좀 주세요).
- **아/어 보다:** To try something to see what it's like (e.g., 먹어 봤어? - "Have you tried eating it?").
