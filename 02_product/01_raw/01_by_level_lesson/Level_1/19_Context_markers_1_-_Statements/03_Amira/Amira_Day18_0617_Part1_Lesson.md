## Teaching Points

Ji introduces "Context Markers" as endings used primarily in informal speech (반말 or 요 form). He explains that formal speech endings like 습니다 act as their own context markers, meaning they cannot be combined with the markers taught today.

**1. ~네요 (Immediate Realization)**
*   **Meaning:** Expresses a reaction to something just realized or something that just happened. Similar to "Oh!" or "Wow!" in English.
*   **Usage:** Attach directly to the stem.
    *   Action/Descriptive Verbs: Stem + 네요.
    *   Irregular ㄹ: Remove ㄹ then add 네요. e.g., 살다 (to live) → 사네요.
    *   이다 (to be): After consonant → 이네요; After vowel → 네요.
*   **Examples:**
    *   이제 집이네요. (Oh, we're home now.)
    *   좋은 친구네요. (Oh, she's a good friend.)
    *   그렇네. (That's right/I see—used to agree with someone).

**2. ~구나 / ~구만 / ~군(요) (Realization after Thinking)**
*   **Meaning:** Expresses a realization that comes after a moment of thought or connecting pieces of information. Similar to "I see" in English.
*   **Usage:**
    *   **Descriptive Verbs:** Stem + 구나/군/구만 (반말) or 군요 (존댓말). e.g., 예쁘다 → 예쁘구나.
    *   **Action Verbs:** Stem + 는구나/는군/는구만 (반말) or 는군요 (존댓말). e.g., 먹다 → 먹는구나.
    *   **Past Tense:** No distinction between action/descriptive. Past stem + 구나/군요. e.g., 예뻤군요, 먹었군요.
*   **Nuance:** In 반말, 구나/군/구만 are interchangeable, though 군 and 구만 often sound like talking to oneself. In 존댓말, only 군요 is used.

**3. ~지(요) / ~죠 (Confirmation/Obviousness)**
*   **Meaning:** Used for confirmation ("Right?", "Isn't it?") or to state something obvious.
*   **Usage:** Stem + 지(요). Often shortened to 죠 in speech.
*   **Functions:**
    *   **Question to others (Yes/No):** Seeking confirmation. e.g., 한국 사람이시죠? (You're Korean, right?)
    *   **Question to others (5W1H):** Asking for information the speaker expects the listener to know. e.g., 어디에 두셨죠? (Where did you put it [again]?)
    *   **Question to self:** Contemplating or wondering. e.g., 어떡하지? (What should I do?)
    *   **Statement:** Confirming an obvious conclusion. e.g., 당연하지! (Of course!/Absolutely!)

## Lesson Flow

The lesson begins with Ji explaining the theory of **Comprehensible Input**. He tells Amira that because she has a solid base, they will now focus on longer, more complex sentences where she can use context to figure out new meanings. He transitions into the main topic: **Context Markers**.

Ji starts with **~네요**. He uses a visual of a tree to show that the verb stem is the branch and the ending is the leaf. He explains the "just now" aspect of this marker. He shows a slide of a father and son looking at fish; the son says "있네요" (immediate sight) and the father agrees with "그렇네" and "많네."

Next, Ji introduces **~구나/군요**. He contrasts this with ~네요 by explaining it requires a "thought process." He provides a scenario: two people enter a busy restaurant. Person A says "많네요" (immediate reaction to the crowd). Then they see a poster of the owner on TV. Person A then says "그렇구나" (realization after connecting the crowd to the TV appearance). Amira asks if using these markers makes it sound like she is talking to herself. Ji clarifies that while some forms (like 군) sound like self-talk in 반말, using the polite 군요 usually implies speaking to someone else.

Ji then moves to **~지(요)**, which he calls the most versatile marker. He breaks it down into three uses: asking others for confirmation, asking oneself a question, and making a statement of fact. He explains the subtle difference between "어디에 두셨어요?" (Where did you put it?) and "어디에 두셨죠?" (Where did you put it again/I expect you to know).

Finally, Ji discusses the word **당연하다** (to be obvious) in the context of the ~지 marker. He explains that "당연하지" means "Of course" or "Absolutely," and provides other variations like "당연한 결과" (natural outcome) and "당연하게 생각하지 마" (don't take it for granted). The recording ends as Ji is about to move into more examples.
