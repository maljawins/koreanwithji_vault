## Teaching Points

Ji covers a wide range of context markers, explaining their grammatical rules, nuances, and social implications.

*   **~지(요) as a Statement and Suggestion:**
    *   Used to confirm something regardless of the listener's agreement or to express an obvious conclusion.
    *   Ji introduces **당연하다 (to be obvious)** and its common form **당연하지 (Of course/Absolutely)**.
    *   **혹시 (by any chance)** is introduced as a contextual adverb often used with these markers.
    *   As a suggestion, **~(으)시죠** is presented as the most polite and gentle way to make a proposal to elders or superiors, combining "let's" and "please." Ji contrasts this with the more informal **~자** and the potentially rude **~읍시다**.

*   **~겠지(요) and ~야지(요):**
    *   **~겠지(요)** is used for guessing and seeking confirmation (e.g., "I guess/probably").
    *   **~야지(요)** expresses obligation or firm intention. When the subject is the first person, it indicates a personal decision (e.g., "I'm gonna..."). When used with second or third persons, it implies a stronger sense of "should" or "got to."

*   **~잖아(요):**
    *   Used to insist the speaker is right and assume the listener already knows the fact (e.g., "You know...").
    *   Ji notes that **~잖습니까** is the only context marker that can be used in formal speech.

*   **~거든(요):**
    *   Provides background information or a reason (e.g., "It's because...", "Just so you know").
    *   Ji warns that the tone and context are crucial, as it can sound informative or condescending.

*   **~더라고(요):**
    *   Used to recall a personal experience, witness, or feeling.
    *   For descriptive verbs, only the present stem is used (e.g., **덥더라고요** instead of **더웠더라고요**) because the characteristic is perceived as continuing in the speaker's memory.
    *   For action verbs, both present and past stems can be used to indicate whether the action was ongoing or completed at the time of the experience.

*   **~나요? and ~가요?:**
    *   Expresses genuine curiosity and sounds polite and kind (e.g., "I wonder if...", "Could it be that?").
    *   **Action Verbs:** Stem + **나요** (not sensitive to 받침).
    *   **Descriptive Verbs:** Stem + **ㄴ/은가요** (sensitive to 받침).
    *   **Exceptions:** **있다** and **없다** always take **나요** (e.g., **있나요**, **없나요**).
    *   Ji introduces the informal versions **~니** (kind/feminine) and **~냐** (thuggish/masculine), advising Amira to use **~니**.

## Lesson Flow

The lesson begins with Ji continuing the explanation of **~지(요)** as a statement, using the example of **당연하지 (Of course)** and introducing the adverb **혹시 (by any chance)**. He then moves to a TOPIK exam scenario to show how **~죠** confirms a shared reality.

Ji transitions to making suggestions, emphasizing the politeness of **~(으)시죠** when speaking to a mother-in-law (**장모님**). He provides a "Reminder" slide to contrast this with **~자** (반말) and **~읍시다** (formal but potentially rude).

Next, Ji introduces **~겠지(요)** for guessing, using poetic examples like the sun rising tomorrow (**내일은 내일의 해가 뜨겠지**). He then explains **~야지(요)**, distinguishing between a first-person intention (watching a *mukbang* before bed) and a second-person obligation (waking someone up for work).

The lesson proceeds to **~잖아(요)**, where Ji explains its origin from negation (**~지 않아요**) but its current use as a marker of "I'm right and you know it." He highlights its unique formal version, **~잖습니까**.

Ji then covers **~거든(요)** for background information, using weather and social conflicts as examples. He warns Amira about the potential for sounding rude depending on the tone.

The focus shifts to **~더라고(요)** for recalling experiences. Ji spends significant time explaining why descriptive verbs typically use the present stem in this context, using the example of a pizza place being "always delicious" in memory.

Finally, Ji introduces question markers **~나요** and **~가요**, detailing the grammatical rules for action versus descriptive verbs and irregulars. He concludes by discussing the gendered nuances of the informal endings **~니** and **~냐**, advising Amira on the most appropriate choice for her.
