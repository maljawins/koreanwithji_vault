### Overview

The instructor, Hyunwoo, addresses a common concern for language learners: the fear of unintentionally sounding rude. While overt insults or a harsh tone are obviously disrespectful in any language, Korean has specific linguistic structures that can cause a learner to sound impolite even when they have no ill intent. The lesson focuses on two primary pitfalls: the misuse of one-word answers and the incorrect application of the second-person pronoun "you." By understanding these nuances, learners can navigate social interactions with Korean speakers more respectfully and effectively.

---

### The Danger of One-Word Noun Responses

The first way learners accidentally sound rude is by using single nouns as complete responses. In English, it is perfectly acceptable to omit "It is..." and simply say "Coffee" when asked what you are drinking. However, in Korean, stating a noun alone like `커피 (coffee)` is grammatically and socially categorized as **반말 (casual language)**. Using this with someone you have just met or someone who requires **존댓말 (polite/formal language)** will immediately sound disrespectful.

To rectify this, the instructor explains that you must add the polite ending **요** to the noun. For example, instead of just saying `커피`, you should say `커피요 (It's coffee)`. Alternatively, you can use the full polite sentence form `커피예요 (It is coffee)`. This rule applies to all nouns used as messages. If someone asks `이거 뭐예요? (What is this?)`, answering with just `이거 (this)` is rude; you must say `이거요 (this one)`. Even when repeating a word for clarification, such as asking "Tomorrow?" in response to `내일 시간 있어요? (Do you have time tomorrow?)`, you cannot simply say `내일?`. You must use the polite form `내일이요?`.

This principle extends beyond nouns to adverbs and interrogative words. For instance, the word `누구 (who)` cannot be used alone without sounding rude to a stranger or superior. It must be phrased as `누구요?`. Similarly, if you are asking "How?" in a polite context, you should say `어떻게요?` rather than just `어떻게`. The instructor emphasizes that as long as a word is delivered as a message in an interaction, it is treated as a sentence and must carry the appropriate politeness marker.

---

### Misusing the Word for "You"

The second major pitfall involves the literal translation of the English pronoun "you." Many learners find words like `너 (casual you)` or `그대 (poetic you)` in dictionaries or song lyrics. However, using these words in standard conversation is almost always considered rude or socially inappropriate. Korean society relies heavily on social hierarchy and titles rather than direct second-person pronouns.

The instructor provides three better alternatives to avoid sounding rude. First, if you know the person's name, use their name followed by the suffix **씨**, such as `현우 씨`. Second, use the person's social or professional title, such as `선생님 (teacher)` or `사장님 (business owner/boss)`. Third, the most common and safest method in Korean is to simply omit the subject of the verb entirely. Because the context of the conversation usually makes it clear who is being addressed, explicitly saying "you" is often unnecessary and potentially offensive.

---

### Summary and Recap

The lesson concludes with a summary of the two main points to ensure learners avoid accidental rudeness. First, never use a noun, adverb, or question word in isolation when speaking to someone who requires polite language; always attach **요** to the end to transform the one-word response into a polite sentence. Second, avoid literal translations for "you" like `너`. Instead, stick to using names with **씨**, social titles, or omitting the subject altogether. Following these two guidelines will help learners maintain a polite and respectful image while practicing their Korean.
