**Adjectives are Verbs in Korean**

That’s right. Adjectives ARE verbs in Korean.

Let me show you what I mean.

[Show - “The cat is quiet” VS “A quiet cat”]

In English, “quiet” doesn’t change no matter the usage. Now let’s look at Korean.

Show – 고양이: Cat / 조용하다: To be quiet

Show - 고양이가 조용해요 VS 조용한 고양이

As you can see, the part that corresponds to “quiet” changes its form in Korean.

This is why Korean adjectives are called “descriptive verbs” because they work exactly like verbs.

Let’s compare these two words.

[Show 공부하다: V. To study / 조용하다: Adj. To be quiet]

공부하다 means “To DO study”, it’s an action verb.

And 조용하다 means “To BE quiet”. And here, the state of being quiet itself is treated like one single verb.

But since both words end with 하다, they both conjugate exactly the same way in the present tense.

[Show 공부해요, I study / 조용해요, It is quiet.]

As you just saw, adjectives and verbs follow very similar conjugation rules in Korean. So I’m going to be using the term “descriptive verb” instead of “adjective” from here on out.

Now, these verbs do not have a one-to-one translation in English, which is why they deserve a whole separate lesson to be explained.

Let’s start with 나다.

Again, 나다 doesn’t have an equivalent English word. You can only “interpret” this word as “to happen” “to occur”. And it’s always about something happens from within then manifests itself outward.

I always use this example to explain the meaning of 나다.

빛 means light. And we have 빛나다 - “Light happens from within”.

Namely, “to shine”.

You can optionally use the subject marker after 빛 and say 빛이 나다.

Now, here’s a little something that might help you remember 나다 a little better if you’re a K-pop fan.

In the song “Solo” by Jennie, there’s a part that goes “빛이 나는 솔로”. Now, 솔로 is solo, but not like a guitar solo. In Korean, 솔로 is actually used to mean “Single”.

So, “빛이 나는 솔로” means “Single that shines”

The thing is that this also sounds a lot like “Bitch 나는 솔로” which means “Bitch, I’m single”.

I thought this was a brilliant wordplay. Next level, genius work avoiding being censored.
