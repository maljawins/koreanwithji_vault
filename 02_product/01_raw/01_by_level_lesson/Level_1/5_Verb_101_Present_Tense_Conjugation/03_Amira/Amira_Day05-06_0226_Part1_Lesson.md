## Lesson Overview
This session marks a significant expansion of Amira's foundational Korean, moving from basic "A is B" structures into a comprehensive study of pronouns and the nuanced world of Korean particles. In this first part, Ji introduces a wide array of pronouns—covering singular, plural, and honorific forms—while emphasizing the cultural necessity of distinguishing between people and objects. This part leads directly into a deep dive into the distinction between the topic marker 은/는 and the subject marker 이/가, setting the stage for the sentence feedback and SOV structure analysis in Part 2.

## Teaching Points
*   **Pronoun Variety and Usage:** Korean pronouns are categorized by both formality (Low vs. Honorific) and usage (Proper vs. Real Life). Words like 니 (informal 'you') are common in speech but avoided in writing.
*   **Plurality with 들:** The marker 들 indicates plurality (like 's' in English) but is often dropped when the context or the pronoun itself (e.g., 너희, 여러분) already implies a group.
*   **Human vs. Object Distinction:** It is crucial to use person-specific markers. Addressing a person as a thing (것) is considered rude; instead, the format "이/저/그 + 분 (Honorific)" or "이/저/그 + 사람" must be used.
*   **Possessive Particle 의:** Equivalent to 's' or 'of'. It is often pronounced as 에. When combined with pronouns, it is frequently shortened (나의 → 내, 저의 → 제) or omitted entirely, in which case the pronoun acts as a possessive adjective.
*   **Topic Marker (은/는) vs. Subject Marker (이/가):** Ji introduces these as "markers" rather than "particles" to clarify their roles. 은/는 marks the topic (what the sentence is about), while 이/가 marks the subject of the specific verb.
