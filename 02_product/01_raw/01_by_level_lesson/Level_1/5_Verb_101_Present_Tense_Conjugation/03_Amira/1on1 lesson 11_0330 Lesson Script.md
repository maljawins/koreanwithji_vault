**Now, let’s move on to irregular verbs.**

Korean irregular verbs are actually **more predictable** than English ones because they follow **specific patterns**.

Unlike English, where you have to memorize each irregular verb individually, Korean irregular verbs change according to set rules.

Slide 34

**There are 7 types of irregular verbs in Korean.**

Each type is based on **what happens to the last letter of the verb stem when conjugated**.
And there are only **three key things** to focus on:

1. **What causes the change?** – Is it a **vowel** or **consonant** that triggers the change?
2. **What happens to the stem?** – Does the **받침** (final consonant) or the entire syllable get **removed or replaced**?
3. **What happens to the suffix?** – Does the vowel suffix change? (For example, remember how **으** attaches to words ending in 받침, like **을 거예요**, but not to words ending in a vowel, like **ㄹ 거예요**?

Now, let’s talk about irregular ㄹ, which is the last type. We briefly touched on this in the last lesson.

All verbs with stems that end in ㄹ are irregular.

This is the only type of irregular verb that’s influenced by the following consonant.

When followed by a vowel, it follows the regular conjugation rule.

But when followed by these 4 consonants ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㄹ, and 으 the ㄹ 받침 is removed, and the vowel suffix is also dropped.

For example, *알다* (to know) is an irregular verb.

When *알다* is followed by 니 which adds a little bit of kind tone to the question, the ㄹ 받침 is dropped.

니 is a context marker for questions in 반말. Adds a little bit of kind tone to the question.

And when followed by ㅅ or ㅂ, In either, *알 + 습니다* or *아 + ㅂ니다*, either way, the ㄹ 받침 in the stem disappears.

When followed by *으* or *ㅅ*, the same rule applies.

*알 + 으세요* or *아 + 세요*. The ㄹ 받침 is removed, and the 으 suffix is dropped as well.

For future tense: *알 + 을 거예요* or *아 + ㄹ 거예요*, either way, the ㄹ 받침 is removed and the 으 suffix is also dropped.

When followed by any vowel other than 으, it follows the regular conjugation rule.
So, in the present tense, it’s *알아요* and *벌어요*.

Next example, we have formal high ending.

*달다* means "to be sweet."

It ends in ㄹ, so you might think it should be *달습니다*, but that's wrong.

The *스* in *습니다* cancels out the ㄹ 받침, leaving you with *답니다*.

Alternatively, you could say that since *ㅂ니다* starts with ㅂ, it cancels the ㄹ 받침 in *달*.
Either way, you get *답니다*.

Slide 71

In honorifics, the *으시* suffix cancels the ㄹ 받침 and also removes the 으.

So, *살다* (to live) in the future tense becomes *사실 거예요*, not *살으실 거예요*.

You could also say that the *ㅅ* in *시* cancels out the ㄹ. Either way, you end up with *사실 거예요*.

Slide 72

팔다 in regular future tense becomes 팔 거예요, not 팔을 거예요.

In some resources, they say you don’t have to add 을 and just add 거예요 to make a future tense with ㄹ irregular.

But what’s actually happening is 으 cancels ㄹ 받침 and cancels itself, and this new ㄹ 받침 moves over to the stem.

This is why they say that all you have to do is just add 거예요 after the stem but that’s actually not true.

So, 팔다 in future tense is 팔 거예요

Slide 73

Now, we’re going to learn 2 more particles 도 and 만.

도 means also or even and 만 means only.

Both 도 and 만 can be placed after ANY noun. And they can also be attached to other particles like 에, 에서, 부터, 까지, 에게, and 한테. And there are other grammar points where they can be attached to as well, which we haven’t covered so we’ll revisit them later.

Slide 74

Now, let’s first look at 도

In English, “I also study math” has 3 meanings.

First, is “I, too, study math” placing emphasis on “I”.

Second one is “I study physics and I study math, too”.

And lastly, it means “I do all these things and studying math is one of them”

So, it all depends on the context and how you say it in English but that is not the case in Korean.

도 is placed precisely after the noun that you’re referring to

So, 저도 수학을 공부해요 means I, too study math

And 저는 수학도 공부해요 means I study math, too.

And 저는 수학 공부도 해요 means I do math studies as well.

And this is also the case with 만 but let’s stick with 도 for now

Slide 75

Now, we just learned that 도 can be placed after any noun and some particles but it cannot be placed after 은는, 이가, and 을를. And instead, 도 just simply replaces them.

Slide 76

Now, let’s look at 만. Just like 도, 만 can also be placed after any noun but where you place 만 is really important. And it can be placed after all the particles that 도 can be placed after.

The only difference is that if you wanted to use 만 with subject, topic, and object marker, you should place 만 before subject topic and object marker so it becomes 만이 만은 만을.

This is not necessary for object marker and subject marker in most cases and you could just drop 이 은

But there is a difference between just 만 and 만은 because of the special ability 은 has which is to show contrast.

If you said 난 이 순간만 기억해, it just simply means this is the only moment I remember but if you said 만은 기억해, it would mean I could maybe forget other moments but at least not this moment.

So, the feeling and the nuance is completely different.

Slide 77

Now, there are lots of ways to say only in Korean and 밖에 is one of them.

밖에 literally means “outside of” and you could translate 밖에 to “Anything but”.

And it can only be used with “Negative verbs”, not “positive verbs”. So, the verb should always be in either 지 않다 and 안 form.

It could also be used to say something figuratively

For example, 제 친구는 남자친구밖에 몰라요 doesn’t mean the only person she knows is her boyfriend. Rather, it means she’s very loyal to him as in she would never cheat on him.

And 밖에 could also be attached to other particles as well. For example 에어컨이 사무실에 밖에 없어요 means I have an air conditioner only at the office, but not at home.

And when it’s attached to another particle, it should be spaced 에밖에 is wrong. It should 에 밖에, spaced.

Slide 78

Let’s compare 만 with 밖에.

Firstly, 밖에 cannot be used with the descriptive verb ‘아니다’.

So, 여기서 나밖에 남자가 아니야 is wrong and it should be 여기서 나만 남자야

And 밖에 cannot be used with propositive and imperative sentences.

So 이것밖에 하지 마 is wrong and it should be 이것만 해

오늘은 공부밖에 안 하자 is wrong it should be 오늘은 공부만 하자

Now, there are times when using 밖에 is more natural than 만

Let’s compare these two sentences A) 저는 채소만 먹어요 and B) 저는 채소밖에 안 먹어요. And B is more natural because 밖에 emphasizes the feeling of being not enough. So, if you were to say 저는 채소만 먹어요 would be like I only eat vegetables and I’m proud of it whereas 채소밖에 안 먹어요 could sound like I only eat vegetables. I should probably eat meat, too.

And 만 can be used in both positive and negative sentences whereas 밖에 can be used only with negative sentences.

집에 동생만 있어요 and 집에 동생밖에 없어요 are both correct and they both mean My brother’s the only one at home.

And 집에 동생만 없어요 means everyone else is home but not my brother and it’s correct but 집에 동생밖에 있어요 does not mean everyone else is home.
