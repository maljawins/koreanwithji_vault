## Teaching Points

Ji covers the mechanics of the remaining irregular verb categories in detail:

*   **ㅂ (Bieup) Irregular (Continued):** Ji explains that 돕다 (to help) and 곱다 (to be beautiful/pure) are the only two verbs where ㅂ changes to 오 instead of 우 when followed by a vowel. This results in 도와요 and 고와요. He also provides tips for recognizing irregulars: most descriptive verbs ending in ㅂ are irregular, while most action verbs (except 돕다, 눕다, 줍다, 굽다) are regular.
*   **ㄷ (Digeut) Irregular:** When followed by a vowel, the ㄷ 받침 is replaced by ㄹ.
    *   Example: 걷다 (to walk) → 걸어요; 듣다 (to listen) → 들어요.
    *   Ji notes that some verbs are homonyms where one meaning is irregular and the other is regular, such as 묻다 (to ask = irregular; to bury = regular).
*   **르 (Reu) Irregular:** This is the only type where an entire syllable is replaced. When followed by a vowel, 르 becomes double ㄹ (ㄹㄹ). One ㄹ moves to the previous syllable's 받침, and the other starts the next syllable.
    *   Example: 모르다 (to not know) → 몰라요; 기르다 (to grow/raise) → 길러요.
*   **ㅎ (Hieut) Irregular:** Primarily affects descriptive verbs (colors and states). The ㅎ is removed, and the vowel changes to 애 or 얘.
    *   Example: 빨갛다 (to be red) → 빨개요; 어떻다 (to be how) → 어때요.
    *   Ji emphasizes the importance of 어때요 (How is it? / What do you think?) and its honorific form 어떠세요.
*   **ㅅ (Siot) Irregular:** When followed by a vowel, the ㅅ is removed, but unlike other irregulars, the vowel suffix remains intact (it does not merge or disappear).
    *   Example: 낫다 (to recover) → 나아요 (not 나요); 짓다 (to build) → 지어요.
*   **ㄹ (Rieul) Irregular:** These are affected by following consonants (ㄴ, ㅅ, ㅂ, ㄹ, 으). When these consonants follow, the ㄹ in the stem is removed.
    *   Example: 알다 (to know) → 압니다 (formal), 아세요 (honorific), 알 거예요 (future).

## Lesson Flow

The lesson follows a chronological progression through the irregular verb table:

*   **ㅂ Irregular Exceptions & Recognition:** Ji starts by explaining the unique cases of 돕다 and 곱다. He then shows a slide categorized by vowel sounds (ㅏ, ㅓ, ㅗ, ㅜ) to help Amira identify which descriptive verbs are likely irregular. He clarifies that action verbs like 입다 (to wear) are regular.
*   **ㄷ Irregular Interaction:** Ji introduces ㄷ irregulars, using 걷다 (to walk) and 듣다 (to listen) as primary examples. He explains the concept of homonyms using 묻다, showing how "to ask" changes to 물어요 while "to bury" stays 묻어요.
*   **르 Irregular Mechanics:** Ji explains the "double ㄹ" rule. Amira recognizes the word 몰라요 from previous lessons, and Ji uses this to explain the underlying grammar she had been using intuitively.
*   **ㅎ Irregular & Practical Phrases:** Ji focuses on color words and the word 어떻다. He spends extra time on 어때요 and 어떠세요, explaining they are used daily to ask for opinions. Amira follows the multi-step conjugation logic (removing ㅎ, changing vowel to 애).
*   **ㅅ Irregular Confusion:** This section causes the most hesitation. Amira asks why 낫다 becomes 나아요 instead of just 나요. Ji explains that the vowel suffix must stay to distinguish it from other verbs like 나다. Amira notes it's like an "invisible" consonant is still acting on the word.
*   **ㄹ Irregular & Wrap-up:** Ji explains that ㄹ irregulars are unique because they are triggered by consonants, not vowels. He uses 알다 (to know) and 살다 (to live) to demonstrate the ㄹ dropping before 습니다 or 으세요.
*   **Closing & Homework:** Due to time constraints, Ji postpones the "도 & 만" particles to the next lesson. He introduces a comprehensive Excel sheet of irregular verbs and assigns Amira the task of writing five sentences for each of the seven irregular types.
