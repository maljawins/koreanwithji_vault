## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor, Hyunwoo, introduces the fundamental mechanics of the Korean present tense for absolute beginners. The present tense is used to describe actions happening now or general habits. In Korean, verbs and adjectives are conjugated into the present tense using three primary endings: 아요, 어요, and 해요. The choice between these endings depends entirely on the vowel found in the verb stem. The lesson follows a structured path: identifying the dictionary form, removing the suffix 다, and applying one of three specific conjugation rules based on the remaining vowel.

---

### The Dictionary Form and the First Rule: 아요

Every Korean verb and adjective is listed in the dictionary in a basic form that ends with the syllable 다. To begin conjugation, this 다 must be removed. The remaining part is called the verb stem. The first rule states that if the last vowel of the verb stem is either 오 or 아, you must add the ending 아요.

The instructor demonstrates this with the verb 가다 (to go). After removing 다, the stem is 가. Since the vowel is 아, the rule dictates adding 아요. However, because 가 already ends in the 아 sound, adding another 아 is redundant. Therefore, the two merge into the simplified form 가요. This single conjugated form can mean "I go," "you go," "she goes," or even a command like "go!" depending on the context.

Other examples of this rule include:
- **작다 (to be small):** The stem is 작. Since it contains the vowel 아, it becomes 작아요.
- **보다 (to see/watch):** The stem is 보. Since it contains the vowel 오, it is followed by 아요. When spoken quickly, 보 + 아요 contracts into 봐요.

---

### The Second Rule: 어요

The second rule applies to all other vowels that are not 오 or 아. If the verb stem ends in vowels such as 어, 우, 이, or 위, the ending 어요 is added instead. This choice is made primarily for ease of pronunciation.

The instructor provides several clear examples:
- **먹다 (to eat):** The stem is 먹. The vowel is 어, so it is followed by 어요 to become 먹어요.
- **밀다 (to push):** The stem is 밀. The vowel is 이, so it becomes 밀어요.
- **뛰다 (to run/jump):** The stem is 뛰. The vowel is 위, so it becomes 뛰어요.

Visually, the instructor uses a series of clips to show these actions—walking for 가다, pushing for 밀다, and running for 뛰다—to reinforce the connection between the word and the action.

---

### The Third Rule: The 하다 Exception

The final rule is an exception for a specific category of verbs known as 하다 verbs. On its own, 하다 means "to do." In Korean, many verbs are formed by combining a noun with 하다. For example, 공부 (studying) becomes 공부하다 (to study), and 준비 (preparation) becomes 준비하다 (to prepare).

The rule for these verbs is simple: any verb ending in 하다 changes to 해요 in the present tense. Therefore, 공부하다 becomes 공부해요 and 준비하다 becomes 준비해요.

---

### Sentence Building and Summary

Once a verb is conjugated into the present tense, it can be used to form complete sentences. The instructor explains that you can place a subject in front to specify who is doing the action, or an object in front to specify what is being acted upon. Adverbs like 지금 (now) or 빨리 (quickly) can also be added before the verb to provide more detail.

The core conjugation logic is summarized as follows:
- **Stem vowel is 오 or 아:** Add 아요.
- **Stem vowel is anything else:** Add 어요.
- **Verb ends in 하다:** Change to 해요.

The video concludes with a musical segment that repetitively chants these rules to help students memorize the patterns through rhythm and song.
