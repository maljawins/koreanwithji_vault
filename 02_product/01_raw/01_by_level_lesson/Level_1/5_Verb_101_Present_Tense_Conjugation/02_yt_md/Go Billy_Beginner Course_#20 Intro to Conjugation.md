## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the fundamental concept of verb conjugation in Korean, explaining that every verb must be changed from its dictionary form to be used in a sentence. While there are dozens of possible ways to conjugate a single verb based on tense and politeness levels, the focus of this lesson is the "Present Tense Informal" form, commonly known as the 요 form. This form is versatile, being polite enough for general use with strangers or acquaintances without being overly formal.

The lesson follows a structured path: first defining the "dictionary form" (ending in 다) and the "verb stem" (the part remaining after removing 다), then establishing the three basic rules for attaching the correct ending (아, 어, or 요). The instructor emphasizes that while there are several exceptions to these rules, most exist to make the language easier and faster to pronounce. By the end of the lesson, students are equipped to conjugate the majority of standard Korean verbs into the present tense.

---

### The Dictionary Form and Verb Stem
Every Korean verb is found in the dictionary in a form that ends with the syllable 다. Examples include 가다 (to go), 먹다 (to eat), 좋아하다 (to like), 보다 (to see), and 이다 (to be). To begin the conjugation process, one must identify the verb stem by removing the 다. For instance, the stem of 먹다 is 먹, and the stem of 가다 is 가. This stem is the base to which various grammatical endings are attached to express tense, mood, and social relationship.

---

### Basic Rules for the 요 Form
The instructor outlines three primary steps for conjugating into the present tense informal 요 form. First, identify the verb stem. Second, look at the last vowel in that stem. If the vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, attach the syllable 아. If the stem ends in any other vowel, attach the syllable 어. Third, attach the politeness marker 요 to the end.

The instructor demonstrates this with the verb 앉다 (to sit down). The stem is 앉, which contains the vowel ㅏ. Following the rule, we attach 아 to get 앉아, and then add 요 to result in 앉아요. A complete sentence would be:
> **저는 앉아요.** / I sit down.

Another example is 먹다 (to eat). The stem is 먹, and since the vowel ㅓ is not ㅏ or ㅗ, we attach 어 to get 먹어, then add 요 for 먹어요.
> **저는 피자를 먹어요.** / I eat pizza.

For the verb 만들다 (to make), the stem is 만들. The vowel in the last syllable is ㅡ, so we attach 어 to get 만들어, then add 요 for 만들어요.
> **저는 케이크를 만들어요.** / I make cake.

---

### Exception 1: Stems Ending in a Vowel (Contractions)
When a verb stem ends in a vowel with no final consonant (받침), the attached 아 or 어 will often combine or contract into the stem itself. This makes the word faster to say.

If the stem ends in ㅏ, such as 가다 (stem: 가), adding 아 results in 가아, which naturally contracts to just 가. Adding 요 gives 가요.
> **저는 가요.** / I go.

If the stem ends in ㅗ, such as 보다 (stem: 보), adding 아 results in 보아, which contracts into the single syllable 봐. Adding 요 gives 봐요.
> **저는 TV를 봐요.** / I watch TV.

If the stem ends in ㅣ, such as 마시다 (stem: 마시), adding 어 results in 마시어, which contracts to 마셔. Adding 요 gives 마셔요.
> **저는 차를 마셔요.** / I drink tea.

If the stem ends in ㅜ, such as 배우다 (stem: 배우), adding 어 results in 배우어, which contracts to 배워. Adding 요 gives 배워요.
> **저는 한국어를 배워요.** / I learn Korean.

---

### Exception 2: Stems Ending in the Vowel ㅐ
If a verb stem ends specifically in the vowel ㅐ, such as 보내다 (to send; stem: 보내), you simply add 요 directly to the stem. The instructor explains that adding an extra 어 would be redundant and long, so it is shortened to 보내요.
> **저는 이메일을 보내요.** / I send an email.

---

### Exception 3: The ㅡ Vowel Drop
When a verb stem ends in the vowel ㅡ and has no final consonant, the ㅡ is dropped before adding the ending. To decide whether to add 아 or 어, you must look at the vowel in the syllable immediately preceding the dropped ㅡ. If there is no preceding syllable, you default to adding 어.

For 쓰다 (to write/use), the stem is 쓰. Removing ㅡ leaves just the consonant ㅆ. Since there is no other syllable to look at, we add 어 to get 써, then add 요 for 써요.
> **저는 펜을 써요.** / I use a pen.

For 바쁘다 (to be busy), the stem is 바쁘. Removing the ㅡ leaves 바ㅃ. Looking at the preceding syllable 바, the vowel is ㅏ, so we attach 아 to get 바빠, then add 요 for 바빠요.
> **저는 바빠요.** / I am busy.

---

### Exception 4: The 르 Irregular
Verbs ending in the syllable 르 require a specific transformation. First, drop the ㅡ from 르. Second, add an extra ㄹ to the bottom of the preceding syllable. Third, look at the vowel of that preceding syllable to determine if you add 아 or 어.

For 부르다 (to sing/call), the stem is 부르. Dropping ㅡ and adding a ㄹ to the preceding syllable 부 results in 불ㄹ. Since the vowel in 부 is ㅜ, we attach 어 to get 불러, then add 요 for 불러요.
> **빌리는 노래를 불러요.** / Billy sings a song.

---

### Exception 5: The ㅎ Irregular
For certain descriptive verbs ending in ㅎ, the ㅎ is removed, and the final vowel of the stem changes to the sound ㅐ. The instructor uses 그렇다 (to be so) as an example. The stem 그렇 loses the ㅎ, and the ㅓ changes to ㅐ, resulting in 그래. Adding 요 gives 그래요.
> **그것은 그래요.** / That is so.

---

### Exception 6: 하다 Verbs
Any verb ending in 하다 (to do) or consisting of a noun plus 하다 always conjugates to 해요. The instructor explains that 하다 becomes 해, then you add 요. For example, 좋아하다 (to like) becomes 좋아해요.
> **저는 요리를 좋아해요.** / I like cooking.

---

### Exception 7: The ㅂ Irregular
Many verb stems ending in the consonant ㅂ follow a special rule: the ㅂ is removed and replaced with the syllable 우. Then, the standard rule for 우 is applied (adding 어), resulting in the combined sound 워.

For 맵다 (to be spicy), the stem is 맵. Removing ㅂ and adding 워 results in 매워. Adding 요 gives 매워요.
> **김치는 매워요.** / Kimchi is spicy.

For 부럽다 (to be jealous), the stem is 부럽. Removing ㅂ and adding 워 results in 부러워. Adding 요 gives 부러워요.
> **저는 부러워요.** / I am jealous.

For 춥다 (to be cold), the stem is 춥. Removing ㅂ and adding 워 results in 추워. Adding 요 gives 추워요.
> **저는 추워요.** / I am cold.

---

### Final Notes on Irregulars
The instructor concludes by noting that while these rules cover most cases, some "true" irregulars exist. For example, 이다 (to be) conjugates to 이에요 (after a consonant) or 예요 (after a vowel), which does not follow the standard 아/어 rules. Additionally, some stems ending in ㄷ change the ㄷ to ㄹ about half the time, while others do not. The instructor advises students not to stress over memorizing every exception immediately, as they will become intuitive through practice and exposure.
