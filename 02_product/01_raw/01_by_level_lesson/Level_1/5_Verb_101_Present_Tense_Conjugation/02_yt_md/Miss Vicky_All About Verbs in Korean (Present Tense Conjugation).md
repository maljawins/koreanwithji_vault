---

### Sentence Structure and the Base Form
In English, the sentence order is Subject-Verb-Object (SVO), such as "I know you." In Korean, the order is Subject-Object-Verb (SOV): 나는 너를 알아. Similarly, for "I am a student," the Korean order is Subject-Noun-Verb: 저는 학생이에요. The instructor emphasizes that all Korean verbs and adjectives have a base form ending in 다, such as 알다 (to know) or 먹다 (to eat). These base forms cannot be used directly in sentences; they must be conjugated into present, past, or future forms.

---

### The Standard Conjugation Rule: Vowel Harmony
The primary rule for present tense conjugation depends on the last vowel of the verb stem. If the stem ends in the vowels 아 or 오, the ending 아 is added. For example, the stem of 알다 is 알, which contains the vowel 아, so it becomes 알아. If the stem ends in any vowel other than 아 or 오, the ending 어 is added. For example, the stem of 먹다 is 먹, which contains the vowel 어, so it becomes 먹어. To make these polite (존댓말), the particle 요 is simply added to the end: 알아요 and 먹어요.

---

### Group 1: Stems Ending in 아 or 오
This group is further divided into three cases based on the presence of a final consonant (받침). In Case 1, where there is a 받침, the rule is straightforward: 살다 (to live) becomes 살아 and 놀다 (to play) becomes 놀아. In Case 2, where there is no 받침 and the stem already ends in 아, the added 아 is deleted because it is phonetically redundant. Thus, 가다 (to go) becomes 가, 만나다 (to meet) becomes 만나, and 자다 (to sleep) becomes 자. In Case 3, where the stem ends in 오 with no 받침, the 오 and the added 아 combine into the vowel 와. For example, 오다 (to come) becomes 와. For 보다 (to see/watch), both 보아 and the contracted 봐 are acceptable, though 봐 is more common.

---

### Group 2: Stems Ending in Other Vowels
This group covers stems ending in vowels like 어, 이, or 우. Case 1 involves stems with a 받침, which follow the standard rule: 읽다 (to read) becomes 읽어. Case 2 involves stems with no 받침 ending in 에, 어, or 여. In these instances, the added 어 is deleted. For example, 지내다 (to stay/do) becomes 지내, 서다 (to stand) becomes 서, and 켜다 (to turn on) becomes 켜. Case 3 involves stems ending in 우 with no 받침, where 우 and 어 combine to form 워. Thus, 배우다 (to learn) becomes 배워 and 주다 (to give) becomes 줘. Case 4 involves stems ending in 이 with no 받침, where 이 and 어 combine to form 여. For example, 마시다 (to drink) becomes 마셔 and 가르치다 (to teach) becomes 가르쳐.

---

### Group 3: 하다 Verbs and Group 4: 이다
Verbs ending in 하다 are the simplest to conjugate: 하다 always changes to 해. This applies to all compound verbs like 말하다 (to speak) becoming 말해, 공부하다 (to study) becoming 공부해, and 일하다 (to work) becoming 일해. The copula 이다 (to be) has its own unique forms. In casual speech (반말), it becomes 야 after a vowel (제프야) and 이야 after a consonant (공책이야). In polite speech (존댓말), it becomes 에요 after a vowel (의사예요) and 이에요 after a consonant (선생님이에요). Note that while written as 예요, it is often pronounced as 에요.

---

### Irregular Verbs and Adjectives
Some verbs do not follow the standard patterns. For 듣다 (to listen), the ㄷ 받침 changes to ㄹ before adding 어, resulting in 들어요. For verbs ending in the vowel ㅡ, the ㅡ is often deleted. In 쓰다 (to write), ㅡ is replaced by 어 to become 써. However, in 아프다 (to be sick), the ㅡ is replaced by 아 because of the preceding 아 vowel, resulting in 아파. For verbs like 모르다 (to not know) and 고르다 (to choose), an extra ㄹ is added to the preceding syllable and the ㅡ changes to ㅏ or ㅓ, resulting in 몰라 and 골라.

---

### Adjectives and the ㅂ Irregular
Adjectives follow the same conjugation rules as verbs. 작다 (to be small) becomes 작아 and 멀다 (to be far) becomes 멀어. A specific rule applies to many adjectives with a ㅂ 받침. In these cases, the ㅂ is deleted and 워 is added. Examples include 귀엽다 (to be cute) becoming 귀여워, 쉽다 (to be easy) becoming 쉬워, 어렵다 (to be difficult) becoming 어려워, and 춥다 (to be cold) becoming 추워.

---

### Usage of the Present Tense
The instructor notes that the present tense in Korean is versatile. It is used to describe what someone is currently doing or what they do generally. It can also be used to make suggestions, offers, or commands, similar to the English imperative. Furthermore, the present tense can often indicate future intent or scheduled events, such as 내일 눈 와요 (It will snow tomorrow) or 너무 비싸면 안 사요 (If it's too expensive, I won't buy it).

In this case, ㅂ 받침 gets deleted and you put 워. 귀엽다 becomes 귀여워. 강아지가 귀여워요. The puppy is cute. 쉽다 means easy, so 쉬워. 한글은 쉬워요. Hangeul is easy. 어렵다 becomes 어려워. 한국어는 어려워요. Korean is difficult. If you want to negate it, you can just put 안: 한국어는 안 어려워요. 춥다 means to be cold, so 추워. 날씨가 추워요. The weather is cold.
