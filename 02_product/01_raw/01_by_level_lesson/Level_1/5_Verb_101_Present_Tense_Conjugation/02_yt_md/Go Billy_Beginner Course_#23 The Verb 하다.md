## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the versatile action verb **하다**, which primarily means "to do." This verb is fundamental in Korean because it can function as a standalone verb or combine with a wide variety of nouns to transform those nouns into action verbs. The lesson focuses on the mechanics of this noun-verb combination, the role of the object marker in these constructions, and important exceptions to the rule.

---

### Creating Verbs with 하다
The verb **하다 (to do)** can be attached to the end of many nouns to create a corresponding action verb. The instructor illustrates this using the noun **요리 (cooking)**. By combining **요리** and **하다**, you get the verb **요리하다**, which means "to cook" or literally "to do cooking." This pattern is extremely common and allows learners to expand their vocabulary rapidly by applying **하다** to nouns they already know.

The instructor provides a list of common nouns for practice:
- **공부** (study) → **공부하다** (to study)
- **숙제** (homework) → **숙제하다** (to do homework)
- **말** (words, speech) → **말하다** (to speak)
- **일** (work, job) → **일하다** (to work)
- **운동** (exercise) → **운동하다** (to exercise)
- **샤워** (shower) → **샤워하다** (to shower)

---

### The Role of the Object Marker
When a noun is combined with **하다**, that noun is technically the object of the verb "to do." Therefore, it is grammatically correct to place an object marker (**을/를**) between the noun and **하다**. For example, **요리하다** can also be expressed as **요리를 하다**. The instructor emphasizes that while the object marker is technically present, it is often optional in casual speech and writing. Both forms—the combined version and the version with the object marker—carry the same meaning.

This is demonstrated in several example sentences:
> **저는 한국어를 공부하고 싶어요.** / I want to study Korean.
> **저는 샤워를 하고 싶어요.** / I want to take a shower.
> **저는 일을 하고 싶어요.** / I want to work.
> **저는 운동을 하고 싶어요.** / I want to exercise.

---

### Important Exceptions: 좋아하다 and 싫어하다
The instructor highlights a common point of confusion for beginners: the verbs **좋아하다 (to like)** and **싫어하다 (to dislike)**. Although these verbs end in **하다**, they do not follow the noun + **하다** construction. In these cases, **좋아** and **싫어** are not nouns. Consequently, it is grammatically incorrect to insert an object marker and say **좋아를 하다** or **싫어를 하다**. These verbs derive from a different grammatical form that is not covered in this specific lesson, and they should be treated as single, inseparable verb units.
