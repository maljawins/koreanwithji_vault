### Conjugating the Plain Form: Present Tense
The conjugation of the plain form depends strictly on whether the verb is an action verb or a descriptive verb. For action verbs, the instructor explains a two-part rule based on the verb stem's ending. If the stem ends in a vowel, you attach `ㄴ` followed by `다`. For example, `하다 (to do)` becomes `한다`. If the stem ends in a consonant, you attach `는다`. For example, `먹다 (to eat)` becomes `먹는다`. There is a special rule for stems ending in `ㄹ`: you must remove the `ㄹ` before attaching `ㄴ` and `다`. Thus, `살다 (to live)` transforms into `산다`.

Descriptive verbs are much simpler to handle in the present tense. The instructor notes that for descriptive verbs, you use the unconjugated dictionary form exactly as it is. This means the word will always end in `다`. For example, `좋다 (to be good)` remains `좋다` in the plain form.

---

### What Is the Plain Form?
The plain form is unique because it is only used to make statements. It is never used to ask questions, make suggestions, give commands, or make requests. Its usage is split between speaking and writing. In spoken Korean, the plain form is used to "announce" something out loud to anyone nearby, but this is strictly limited to casual speech contexts. It is also used when talking to oneself, where the speech isn't directed at a specific listener.

In written Korean, the plain form is the standard for content not directed at a specific person. This includes essays, newspaper articles, and personal journal or diary entries. The instructor emphasizes that the plain form is also a prerequisite for several complex grammar forms that will be taught in future lessons.

---

### Plain Form in Other Tenses
While the present tense has specific rules for action versus descriptive verbs, other tenses in the plain form are more uniform. These forms always end in `다`. To create the past tense plain form, you take the standard past tense conjugation and remove the polite ending. For instance, `했어요` becomes `했다`. 

For the future tense, the instructor shows two paths. You can use the `겠다` ending, such as `하겠다`, or the `ㄹ 거다` ending (derived from `ㄹ 것이다`), such as `할 거다`. The progressive tense also follows this pattern: the standard `하고 있어요` is stripped of its polite ending to become `하고 있다`.

---

### Usage Notes and Examples
The instructor highlights a critical pronoun rule: when using the plain form, you must use the casual first-person pronoun `나 (I)` instead of the polite `저`. This applies regardless of whether you are writing a diary or speaking to yourself. 

The lesson provides several example sentences to illustrate these rules:
> **모든 사람이 그 영화를 좋아한다.** / Everyone likes that movie.
> **책이 정말 두껍다.** / The book is really thick.
> **나는 소풍 안 간다.** / I'm not going on the picnic.
> **난 약속한다.** / I promise.
> **우리 아들은 어렸을 때 날씬했다.** / When my son was young, he was slim.
> **나는 이 컴퓨터를 사용하지 않는다.** / I'm not using this computer.
> **나도 콘서트에 가고 싶었다.** / I also wanted to go to the concert.
> **예슬 씨는 천사처럼 아름답다.** / Yeseul is beautiful like an angel.

The instructor points out that the negative construction `지 않다` also follows the action/descriptive split: it becomes `지 않는다` for action verbs and stays `지 않다` for descriptive verbs.

### Conversation Practice and New Vocabulary
The lesson concludes with a dialogue between two friends, Moonbyul and Hwasa, demonstrating how the plain form is used for exclamations and self-talk within a casual conversation.

> **문별: 난 요즘 일 때문에 너무 바빠. 진짜 그만두고 싶다.** / I'm so busy these days because of work. I really want to quit.
> **화사: 나도야. 대부분은 그래. 세상에는 쉬운 게 없지. 대부분의 일은 똑같다.** / Me too. Most people are like that. There's nothing easy in the world, right? Most work is the same.

Key vocabulary and grammar points from the dialogue include:
- `그만두다 (to quit)`
- `대분분 (most/most people)`: Can be used with the particle `의` before a noun to mean "most of [noun]."
- `똑같다 (to be exactly the same)`
- `거든(요)`: Attached to a verb stem to mean "just so you know" or "for your information." In casual speech, the `요` is removed.
- `힘내다 (to cheer up / have strength)`
