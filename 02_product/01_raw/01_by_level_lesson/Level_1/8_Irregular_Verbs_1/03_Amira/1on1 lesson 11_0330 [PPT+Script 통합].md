## Lesson Overview
This lesson focuses on refining the student's understanding of Korean verb conjugation, specifically addressing common pitfalls with the future tense and the verb 하다. Ji begins by reviewing the student's previous assignment, correcting spelling errors, and explaining the nuances of word order, honorifics, and the placement of negative particles like 안. A significant portion of the review is dedicated to the "Tree" metaphor for conjugation, which helps visualize how stems, tense markers, and context suffixes interact.

The core of the lesson is a comprehensive guide to the seven types of Korean irregular verbs: ㅡ, ㅂ, ㄷ, 르, ㅎ, ㅅ, and ㄹ. Ji explains that unlike English irregulars, Korean irregular verbs follow predictable patterns based on whether the following suffix begins with a vowel or a consonant. The lesson details what happens to the stem (removal or replacement) and how the vowel suffixes are affected in each case.

Finally, the lesson introduces the particles 도 (also/even), 만 (only), and the negative construction 밖에 (nothing but). Ji explains how these particles can be combined with other markers to change the focus and nuance of a sentence, particularly how 은는 can be used to show contrast when attached to other particles.

### Slide 4~5 — Present Tense: 놀다 and 먹다
- **On screen:** A diagram of a tree. The "Stem" (놀 or 먹) is the trunk. The "Branch" represents the tense (empty for present). The "Leaf" is the "Context: Plain" (아 or 어). The "Fruit" is the "Respect" marker (요). 
- **Ji's teaching:** We learned about the 아 and 어 suffixes, which we call plain context markers. Since the stem 놀 ends in the vowel ㅗ, it should be 놀아요 in the present tense. Similarly, 먹다 conjugates to 먹어요 because the stem 먹 does not end in ㅏ or ㅗ.

### Slide 6 — Past Tense: 놀다 and 먹다
- **On screen:** The tree diagram now shows the "Branch" as the "Past tense," indicated by adding the ㅆ 받침. The formula shows: 놀 + 았 + 어 + 요 and 먹 + 었 + 어 + 요.
- **Ji's teaching:** For the past tense, you simply add the ㅆ 받침 to the plain context markers 아 or 어, forming 았 or 었. Then, you always add 어 as the plain context marker after that, making it 놀았어요 or 먹었어요.

### Slide 7 — Future Tense: 먹다
- **On screen:** The tree diagram shows the "Branch" as "Future tense" (을 거). A note states: "The suffix 어 is NOT included when conjugating future tense." The correction shows 먹을 거예요, not 먹얼 거예요.
- **Ji's teaching:** In the future tense, you don’t add that 어 suffix we used in the past and present. That’s why it’s 먹을 거예요, not 먹얼 거예요.

### Slide 8~10 — Conjugating 하다
- **On screen:** Tree diagrams for 하다. 
    - Present: 하 + 여 = 해(요). Note: 하다 is the only verb taking 여 as a plain context marker; 해 is a shortened 하여.
    - Past: 하 + 였 + 어요 = 했어(요). Note: 했 is shortened 하였.
    - Future: 하 + ㄹ 거 + 예요 = 할 거예요. Note: 여 is NOT included in future tense; this is why it is 할, not 핼.
- **Ji's teaching:** Now, let’s talk about 하다. First, 해 is just a shortened form of 하여, and 여 works like the 아/어 suffixes. However, 하다 is the only verb that takes 여 as its plain context marker. That’s why 하다 in the present tense is 해요. The same rule applies to the past tense: 했 is just a shortened version of 하였, which is why it’s 했어요. Just like how we don’t add 어 in the future tense of 먹다, we don’t include 여 in the future tense of 하다 either. That’s why it should be 할 거예요, not 핼 거예요.

### Slide 33~35 — Introduction to Irregular Verbs
- **On screen:** A table of 7 types of irregular verbs: ㅡ, ㅅ, ㅂ, ㅎ, ㄷ, 르, ㄹ. Three things to look at: 1. What causes the change? 2. What happens to the stem? 3. What happens to the vowel suffix?
- **Ji's teaching:** Korean irregular verbs are actually more predictable than English ones because they follow specific patterns. There are 7 types based on the last letter of the verb stem. We focus on what triggers the change (vowel or consonant), what happens to the stem (removed or replaced), and what happens to the suffix (like whether 으 is dropped). I've organized these into a table for you to reference.

### Slide 36~38 — Irregular ㅡ
- **On screen:** Examples: 바쁘다 → 바빠요, 크다 → 커요. Rule: ㅡ is dropped when followed by a vowel. 아/어 responds to the vowel before ㅡ. Future tense: 쓰다 → 쓸 거예요 (ㅡ is not dropped before a consonant).
- **Ji's teaching:** The ㅡ vowel is only dropped when followed by a vowel. 아/어 responds to the vowel in the syllable before the ㅡ. For example, 바쁘다 becomes 바빠요 because of the ㅏ in 바. If there's no vowel before it, or it's not ㅏ/ㅗ, we use 어, so 크다 becomes 커요. However, when followed by a consonant like the ㄹ in the future tense, the ㅡ stays put: 쓰다 becomes 쓸 거예요.

### Slide 39~46 — Irregular ㅂ
- **On screen:** Rule: ㅂ is replaced by 우 when followed by a vowel. The vowel suffix 으 is removed. Examples: 춥다 → 추워요, 돕다 → 도와요 (Exception: uses 아). Tables show descriptive verbs (mostly irregular) vs. action verbs (mostly regular).
- **Ji's teaching:** For ㅂ irregulars, the ㅂ is replaced with 우 when a vowel follows. Because 우 ends in a vowel, the 으 in suffixes like 을 거예요 is dropped. So 춥다 becomes 추워요. Note that 돕다 and 곱다 are special—they take 아 instead of 어, becoming 도와요 and 고와요. Most descriptive verbs ending in ㅂ are irregular, while most action verbs (except 눕다, 돕다, 줍다, 굽다) are regular.

### Slide 47~52 — Irregular ㄷ
- **On screen:** Rule: ㄷ is replaced by ㄹ when followed by a vowel. Examples: 걷다 (walk) → 걸어요, 듣다 (listen) → 들어요. Note: ㄷ does not change before a consonant (듣습니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** In ㄷ irregulars, ㄷ changes to ㄹ only when followed by a vowel. For example, 걷다 (to walk) becomes 걸어요. But be careful: some verbs like 받다 (to receive) are regular. Also, homonyms exist: 묻다 (to ask) is irregular, but 묻다 (to bury) is regular. When followed by a consonant, like in 듣습니다, the ㄷ remains unchanged.

### Slide 53~56 — Irregular 르
- **On screen:** Rule: 르 is replaced by ㄹㄹ when followed by a vowel. One ㄹ moves to the previous syllable's 받침, the other starts the next. Examples: 모르다 → 몰라요, 기르다 → 길러요.
- **Ji's teaching:** When a 르 verb meets a vowel, the 르 syllable is replaced by ㄹㄹ. One ㄹ becomes the 받침 of the preceding syllable, and the other stays with the suffix. So 모르다 becomes 몰라요. This doesn't happen before consonants, so in the future tense, it remains 기를 거예요.

### Slide 57~63 — Irregular ㅎ
- **On screen:** Rule: ㅎ is removed when followed by a vowel. 아/어 changes to 애. Examples: 빨갛다 → 빨개요, 어떻다 → 어때요. Honorific: 어떠세요 (ㅎ removed, 으 dropped).
- **Ji's teaching:** This applies to descriptive verbs (mostly colors). When a vowel follows, the ㅎ is dropped and the vowel changes to 애. 빨갛다 becomes 빨개요. For 어떻다, it becomes 어때요. In honorifics, the 으 in 으시 is a vowel, so it triggers the removal of ㅎ, and then the 으 itself is dropped because the stem now ends in a vowel: 어떠세요.

### Slide 64~67 — Irregular ㅅ
- **On screen:** Rule: ㅅ is removed when followed by a vowel, but the vowel suffix (like 으) remains intact. Examples: 낫다 → 나아요 (not 나요), 잇다 → 이을 거예요.
- **Ji's teaching:** For ㅅ irregulars, the ㅅ is removed before a vowel, but unlike other rules, the vowel suffix stays. So 낫다 becomes 나아요. It is very important to keep that extra vowel, otherwise, it sounds like the verb 나다 (to emerge). In the future tense, it is 나을 거예요.

### Slide 68~72 — Irregular ㄹ
- **On screen:** Rule: ㄹ is removed when followed by ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㄹ, or 으. Examples: 알다 → 압니다, 아세요, 알 거예요.
- **Ji's teaching:** All verbs ending in ㄹ are irregular. The ㄹ is removed when followed by specific consonants or the vowel 으. For example, 알다 becomes 압니다 (before ㅂ) or 아세요 (before 으/ㅅ). In the future tense, the 으 in 을 거예요 is dropped, and the ㄹ in the stem is also dropped, effectively leaving you with just the stem + ㄹ 거예요: 알 거예요.

### Slide 73~81 — Particles: 도, 만, 밖에, and 은는 (Visuals not provided)
- **Ji's teaching:** (Note: script markers 73-81 discuss particles). 도 means "also" and 만 means "only." They replace markers like 이/가 or 을/를 but attach after particles like 에 or 에서. 도 is placed precisely after the noun it refers to. 만 is similar, but if used with subject/object markers, it usually comes before them (만이, 만을). 

밖에 means "nothing but" and must be used with a negative verb. It emphasizes that something is "not enough." For example, 채소밖에 안 먹어요 sounds like "I only eat vegetables (and I should probably eat more variety)," whereas 채소만 먹어요 is a neutral "I only eat vegetables."

Finally, the topic marker 은는 can be attached to other particles to show contrast. For example, 12시에는 수업이 있어요 implies "I have class at 12 (but I might be free at other times)."

## Teaching Points

- **The Tree Metaphor:** A helpful way to remember conjugation order: Stem (Trunk) → Tense (Branch) → Context/Plain Marker (Leaf) → Respect Level (Fruit).
- **Future Tense of 하다:** Always remember it is 할 거예요, never 핼 거예요. The 여 marker used in present/past is dropped in the future.
- **Irregular Verb Triggers:** Most irregular changes are triggered by a following **vowel**. The ㄹ irregular is unique because it is also triggered by specific **consonants** (ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㄹ).
- **Replacement vs. Removal:** 
    - **Replacement:** ㅂ → 우, ㄷ → ㄹ, 르 → ㄹㄹ.
    - **Removal:** ㅎ, ㅅ, ㄹ are removed from the stem.
- **Particle Nuances:**
    - **도 (Also):** Replaces subject/object markers.
    - **만 (Only):** Can be used in positive or negative sentences.
    - **밖에 (Nothing but):** Only used with negative sentences; emphasizes insufficiency.
    - **Contrastive 은는:** When added to particles like 에 or 에서, it implies a contrast with other times or locations.
