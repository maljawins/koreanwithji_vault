### Colors as Descriptive Verbs vs. Nouns
In English, the word "red" can be an adjective (red car) or a noun (I like red) without changing its form. In Korean, colors like red, blue, and yellow are primarily descriptive verbs. For example, 빨갛다 means "to be red." To use this as an adjective, it must be conjugated to 빨간, as in 빨간 차 (red car). If used as a sentence predicate, it conjugates to 빨개요, as in 차가 빨개요 (the car is red). The instructor notes that many color verbs end in the consonant ㅎ, which often triggers a vowel change to 에 during conjugation.

Conversely, some colors are inherently nouns. An example is 초록색 (green). Because it is a noun, it does not require conjugation. It can be used as a direct object, 초록색을 좋아해요 (I like green), or placed directly before another noun to act as an adjective, 초록색 차 (green car). It can also end a sentence using the copula 이다, resulting in 차가 초록색이에요 (the car is green).

### Vocabulary List and the Noun Shortcut
The instructor provides a list of common colors, distinguishing them by their grammatical category. Verbs are identified by the "to be" prefix in English, while nouns often end in the syllable 색.

- **빨갛다** / to be red (Verb)
- **파랗다** / to be blue (Verb)
- **노랗다** / to be yellow (Verb)
- **하얗다** / to be white (Verb)
- **까맣다** / to be black (Verb)
- **초록색** / green (Noun)
- **주황색** / orange (Noun)
- **보라색** / purple (Noun)
- **회색** / gray (Noun)
- **갈색** / brown (Noun)

A key tip shared is that any descriptive verb color can be turned into a noun by taking its adjective form and adding 색. Thus, 빨갛다 becomes the noun 빨간색. This allows learners to use the "noun rules" for almost any color. For instance, "red car" can be expressed as either 빨간 차 (using the conjugated verb) or 빨간색 차 (using the noun form). Both are natural and correct.

### Nuances of Black and White
The instructor highlights specific choices for the color black. 까맣다 (to be black) and its derivatives 까만 or 까만색 are typically used for natural things, such as hair, animals (a black cat), or the sky. In contrast, 검정색 is a noun usually reserved for man-made or "painted" black objects, like a black car or black clothing. However, the instructor clarifies that these are often interchangeable in casual speech.

Example sentences provided include:
> **저의 머리가 까매요.** / My hair is black.
> **저는 까만 머리가 좋아요.** / I like black hair.
> **하얀색 티셔츠를 사고 싶어요.** / I want to buy a white T-shirt.
> **노란 병아리가 다섯 마리 있어요.** / There are five yellow chicks.
> **초록색 가방을 찾고 있어요.** / I'm looking for a green bag.

The instructor also references a famous Korean nursery rhyme line: 원숭이 엉덩이는 빨개요 (The monkey's butt is red), illustrating the use of the conjugated verb 빨개요 in a natural context.
