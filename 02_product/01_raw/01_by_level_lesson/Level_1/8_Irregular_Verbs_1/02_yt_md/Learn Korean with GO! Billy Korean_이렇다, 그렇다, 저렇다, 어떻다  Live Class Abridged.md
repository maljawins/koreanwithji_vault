## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson covers a group of special descriptive verbs and their related forms: 이렇다 (to be this way), 그렇다 (to be that way), 저렇다 (to be that way over there), and 어떻다 (to be how). These words are foundational to Korean communication as they allow speakers to refer to the state or manner of things based on their proximity to the speaker and listener. The instructor emphasizes that these are not just physical distances but can also refer to ideas or thoughts.

The learning arc begins with a review of the basic demonstrative adjectives 이 (this), 그 (that), and 저 (that over there) to establish the spatial logic used throughout the lesson. From there, the instructor introduces the descriptive verbs, their unique conjugation patterns, their adverbial forms ending in 게, and their adjective forms ending in ㄴ. Finally, the lesson touches upon action verb counterparts and common idiomatic expressions like 어떡해요 (what to do/oh no).

---

### The Foundation: 이, 그, and 저
Before diving into the verbs, it is essential to understand the three basic demonstrative adjectives. 이 (this) refers to something close to the speaker. 그 (that) refers to something close to the listener. 저 (that) refers to something far from both the speaker and the listener. These concepts extend beyond physical objects to include abstract concepts like thoughts or ideas. For example, 이 생각 (this thought) refers to the speaker's own idea, while 그 생각 (that thought) refers to the listener's idea.

These adjectives form the basis for several common nouns. Adding the noun 것 (thing) creates 이것 (this thing), 그것 (that thing), and 저것 (that thing over there). In spoken Korean, these are frequently shortened to 이거, 그거, and 저거. Similarly, they form location nouns: 여기 (here), 거기 (there), and 저기 (over there).

---

### Descriptive Verbs: 이렇다, 그렇다, and 저렇다
The verb 이렇다 means "to be this way" or "to be like this." It is used for things close to the speaker. When conjugated into the polite 요 form, these 렇다 verbs follow a special irregular pattern where the bottom ㅎ is dropped and the vowel changes, resulting in 이래요. An example sentence is:
> **문이 왜 이래요?** / Why is the door like this?

The verb 그렇다 means "to be that way" or "to be like that," referring to something near the listener or something just mentioned. It conjugates to 그래요. This is often used to explain a situation:
> **처음이라서 그래요.** / It is like that because it is the first time.

The verb 저렇다 means "to be that way" or "to be like that" for things far from both parties. It conjugates to 저래요.
> **저 꽃은 왜 저래요?** / Why is that flower (over there) like that?

---

### Adverb Forms: 이렇게, 그렇게, and 저렇게
By changing the ending to 게, these verbs become adverbs meaning "in this/that way" or "so." 이렇게 (in this way/like this) is often used to emphasize a degree:
> **왜 이렇게 맛있어요?** / Why is it this delicious? (Why is it so delicious?)

그렇게 (in that way/like that) is used when referring to the listener's actions:
> **그렇게 하면 다쳐요.** / If you do it like that, you will get hurt.

저렇게 (in that way/like that over there) refers to an action happening at a distance:
> **저렇게 해도 돼요?** / Is it okay to do it like that (over there)?

---

### Questioning and Problem Solving: 어떻다 and 어떡하다
어떻다 means "to be how" or "to be what way." It conjugates to 어때요 and is commonly used to ask for opinions or the state of something:
> **이건 어때요?** / How is this? (How about this?)

The adverb form 어떻게 (how/in what way) is used to ask about the method of an action:
> **어떻게 만들어요?** / How do you make it?

A related but distinct word is the action verb 어떡하다, which is a contraction of 어떻게 하다 (to do how). The conjugated form 어떡해요 is used to ask "What should I do?" or "What are you going to do?" In many contexts, it functions as an exclamation of sympathy or concern, similar to "Oh no!" or "I'm so sorry to hear that."
> **생일을 모르면 어떡해요?** / What do you do (Oh no) if you don't know the birthday?

---

### Adjective Forms: 이런, 그런, 저런, and 어떤
When these verbs are used to modify a noun directly, they take the form 이런 (this kind of), 그런 (that kind of), 저런 (that kind of over there), and 어떤 (what kind of). These must be followed by a noun.
> **이런 건 처음 봤어요.** / I saw this kind of thing for the first time.
> **그런 말은 믿으면 안 돼요.** / You should not believe that kind of talk.
> **저런 사람은 만나지 마세요.** / Don't meet that kind of person.
> **어떤 선물을 샀는지 궁금해요.** / I am curious what kind of present you bought.

---

### Intermediate Note: 이러다, 그러다, and 저러다
There is another set of action verbs: 이러다, 그러다, and 저러다. These mean "to do like this/that" and are often used as shorthand for "to say like this/that." For example, 그러지 마세요 means "don't do that." In reported speech, 그랬어요 is frequently used instead of 말했어요 (said) to mean "they said that" or "they went like that."
> **새로운 빵집이 내일 문을 열 거라고 그랬어요.** / They said the new bakery will open tomorrow.
