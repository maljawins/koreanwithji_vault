The core of the lesson distinguishes between color nouns and color descriptive verbs. While many colors are simple nouns that behave like their English counterparts, several primary colors are actually verbs that require specific conjugation rules to be used as predicates or adjectives.

### Color Nouns
The word for color is 색깔 or simply 색. Most colors in Korean are nouns and are used exactly like in English. They can function as the subject or object of a sentence, or be placed directly before another noun to act as an adjective. When used at the end of a sentence to say "Something is [color]," these nouns are paired with the copula verb 이다 (to be).

The instructor provides a list of common color nouns:
- **검정색** / black
- **주황색** / orange
- **초록색** / green
- **보라색** / purple
- **회색** / gray
- **갈색** / brown

To say "The bag is black," you simply combine the noun for bag, 가방, with the color noun and the conjugated copula: `가방이 검정색이에요`. To use it as an adjective, you place it before the noun: `검정색 가방을 샀어요 (I bought a black bag)`. As a pure noun, it can be the object of a preference: `검정색을 좋아해요 (I like black)`.

---

### Color Descriptive Verbs and the ㅎ Irregular
Some primary colors are not nouns but descriptive verbs. In Korean, a descriptive verb's dictionary definition is "to be [color]." Because they are verbs, they cannot be used as-is; they must be conjugated based on their position in the sentence. These verbs typically end with the consonant ㅎ.

The instructor uses 파랗다 (to be blue) as the primary example. He notes that the ㅎ followed by ㄷ in the dictionary form creates a aspirated ㅌ sound: 파라타. To use this at the end of a sentence (e.g., "The bag is blue"), you must conjugate it. The rule for these ㅎ-ending color verbs is to remove the ㅎ and change the final vowel to 에.

> **가방이 파래요.** / The bag is blue.

Note that in this construction, the verb 이다 is not used because 파랗다 already contains the meaning "to be." To use these verbs as adjectives (e.g., "a blue bag"), you remove the ㅎ and attach the consonant ㄴ to the bottom of the stem.

> **파란 가방** / a blue bag

The most common descriptive verb colors include:
- **파랗다** / to be blue (also used for green traffic lights)
- **빨갛다** / to be red
- **노랗다** / to be yellow
- **하얗다** / to be white
- **까맣다** / to be black

The instructor distinguishes between the noun 검정색 and the verb 까맣다 for "black." While often interchangeable, 까맣다 is more common for natural things like animals (e.g., `까만 고양이` for a black cat), whereas 검정색 is used for manufactured items, though both are generally acceptable for either.

---

### The "색" Cheat Code
To simplify using colors, the instructor introduces a "cheat code": take the adjective form of a color verb (ending in ㄴ) and attach the word 색 (color) to it. This creates a noun that can be used exactly like the color nouns discussed earlier.

For example, taking the adjective 파란 (blue) and adding 색 results in 파란색 (blue color). Once you have this noun form, you can use it in three ways without further conjugation:
1. As a predicate with 이다: `가방이 파란색이에요.` (The bag is blue.)
2. As an adjective: `파란색 가방을 샀어요.` (I bought a blue bag.)
3. As a noun: `파란색을 좋아해요.` (I like blue.)

This method allows learners to bypass the mental effort of conjugating verbs like 파랗다 or 빨갛다 in the middle of a conversation.

---

### Additional Color Vocabulary and Modifiers
The instructor provides a supplementary list of colors and nuances:
- **핑크 / 분홍색**: Both mean pink. 핑크 (from English) is often used for artificial items like an iMac, while 분홍색 feels more natural.
- **흰색**: Another word for white (noun form).
- **쥐색**: Literally "mouse color," used for a dark gray.
- **녹색**: Another word for green, though 초록색 is more common.
- **붉다**: To be dark red.
- **푸르다**: To be blue or green. It is specifically used for nature, such as the sky, the ocean, or lush green mountains.
- **살구색**: Literally "apricot color," used to describe skin tones.
- **연두색**: Light green / lime green.
- **남색**: Navy blue / dark blue.
- **하늘색**: Sky blue (하늘 means sky).

To further modify colors, two descriptive verbs are used:
- **연하다**: To be light/pale (not strong). As an adjective: **연한**.
- **진하다**: To be dark/deep/strong (thick in intensity, not consistency). As an adjective: **진한**.

These can be placed before any color noun to specify the shade:
> **진한 보라색** / dark purple
> **연한 노란색** / light yellow
