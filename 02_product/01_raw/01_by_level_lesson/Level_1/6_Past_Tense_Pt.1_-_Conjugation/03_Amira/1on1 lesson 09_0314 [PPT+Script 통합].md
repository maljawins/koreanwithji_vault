## Lesson Overview
In this lesson, Ji introduces the fundamentals of the Korean past tense, expanding on the verb conjugation rules learned in previous sessions. The primary focus is on how to transform verb stems into the past tense using the suffixes 았 and 었, and how these forms interact with different speech levels (informal low, informal high, and formal high). The lesson also covers honorific past tense forms, ensuring that respect is properly conveyed even when speaking about the past.

Beyond conjugation, the lesson explores the versatile descriptive verbs 있다 (to be/to have) and 없다 (to not be/to not have). Ji clarifies the distinction between using these verbs for existence versus possession, which is often a point of confusion for learners. This leads into a detailed breakdown of time and location particles, specifically 에 and 에서. Students learn how to specify when an action occurs and where it takes place, as well as how to indicate movement toward a destination.

Finally, the lesson introduces the particles 도 (also) and 만 (only), showing how they can be attached to nouns and other particles to add nuance to sentences. By the end of the session, the goal is for the student to be able to construct complex sentences that include time, location, and specific emphasis, all while correctly applying past tense conjugation.

### Slide 11 — Past Tense Rules
- **On screen:** STEM + 았 + ending OR 었 + ending. Rule 1: If stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ, add 았. Otherwise, add 었. Rule 2: Same as present tense, but with ㅆ받침. Rule 3: Add speech level endings (어, 어요, 습니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** Past tense conjugation is actually very simple. The only difference is that past tense adds ㅆ받침 to 았 or 었—everything else follows the same rules as the present tense. By nature, past tense cannot have propositive or imperative sentences because you can’t suggest or command something in the past.

### Slide 12 — Past Tense: ㅏ and ㅗ Stems
- **On screen:** Rules for stems ending in ㅏ or ㅗ. If there is a 받침, add 았 (아 + ㅆ). Examples: 남다 → 남았, 좋다 → 좋았. If it ends in a vowel, 았 merges. Examples: 가다 + 았 = 갔, 오다 + 았 = 왔. Notes: 보다 → 보았/봤 (both OK), 쏘다 → 쏘았/쐈 (both OK), 오다 → 오았 (Incorrect).
- **Ji's teaching:** In the present tense, if the verb stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ, the "branch" is 아, right? In the past tense, it becomes 았 (with the ㅆ받침). After 았, it’s followed by 어 or 습니다. 어 is a context marker for "plain" context, while 습니다 is for formal high speech. Formal high endings are context markers by themselves—they mark the formality of the sentence. That’s why formal speech doesn’t take additional context markers. When 았 is added to 오 or 아, it merges naturally: 가다 becomes 갔어, 오다 becomes 왔어. This merging isn’t always mandatory; for 보다, both 보았어 and 봤어 are correct. However, 오았 is strictly incorrect—it must be 왔어.

### Slide 25 — Past Tense: Other Vowels
- **On screen:** Rules for stems NOT ending in ㅏ or ㅗ. The branch is 었. If there is a 받침, add 었 (어 + ㅆ). Examples: 먹다 → 먹었, 길다 → 길었. If it ends in a vowel, 어 cancels out or merges. Examples: 서다 → 섰, 두다 → 뒀, 내다 → 냈, 되다 → 됐. Note: 주다 → 주었/줬 (both OK), 배우다 → 배웠 (배우었 is incorrect). Vowel math: ㅚ + ㅓ = ㅙ.
- **Ji's teaching:** Just like in the present tense, if the verb stem ends in any vowel other than ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 었 (어 plus ㅆ). When the stem ends in 받침, you simply add 었, like 먹었 and 길었. When the stem ends in a vowel, the 어 in 었 cancels out or merges. This follows the same rule as the present tense, except the ㅆ받침 is added. For example, 서다 becomes 섰, 두다 becomes 뒀, and 내다 becomes 냈. With 주다, both 주었 and 줬 are correct, but with 배우다, only 배웠 is correct. For 내다, which has the ㅐ vowel, the 어 cancels out but the ㅆ받침 remains, so 내 becomes 냈. Lastly, 되다 (to become) is very important. If you break down 되, it’s ㅗ + ㅣ. When combined with 었, it becomes 됐 because ㅣ + ㅓ equals ㅐ.

- **Ji's teaching:** 재다 means to measure and it ends in ㅐ with no 받침. Now, 었 can be broken into 어 and ㅆ받침, right? What did we learn last time with verbs that end in ㅐ? That 어 cancels out. ### Slide 41 — Honorific Past Tense Rules
- **On screen:** The branch is always (으)시 + 었 = (으)셨. Ending vowel doesn't matter; only 받침 matters. If stem has 받침, add 으셨. If no 받침, add 셨.
- **Ji's teaching:** Honorific conjugation is very similar to the present tense as well. It’s either 으시 or 시 plus 었, which then becomes 으셨 or 셨 depending on whether the stem ends in 받침. If you say 시었 quickly, it sounds like 셨.

### Slide 42~44 — Honorific Conjugation: 남다

So, it becomes 남으셨어 in informal low speech: 사장님이 가게에 남으셨어 (*The owner stayed at the store*). In informal high, it's 남으셨어요: 목사님이 교회에 남으셨어요 (*The pastor stayed at the church*). In formal high speech, 습니다 follows 었, so it becomes 남으셨습니다: 선생님이 학교에 남으셨습니다 (*The teacher stayed at the school*).

### Slide 45~47 — Honorific Conjugation: 배우다

### Slide 51 — 있다 & 없다
- **On screen:** Both are descriptive verbs. 있다 has 2 meanings: 1. To be (existence), 2. To have (possession). 없다 is the opposite. Example box: "I have money = There is money to me = Money is in possession of me."
- **Ji's teaching:** Before we move on to time and location, we’re first going to look at 있다 and 없다, which allow you to say something is somewhere or something isn’t somewhere. You’ll see these words everywhere; they’re arguably some of the most important descriptive verbs in Korean. Technically, 있다 means to be, as in to exist, but because it’s a descriptive verb, it’s better to think of it as “to be in existence.” It can also mean to have depending on the particle and context. 없다 is simply the opposite. When 있다 means “to have,” you can think of it as “to be in possession of.” It’s helpful to adopt a fresh perspective: consider it as "money is in possession of me" or "I have money" in a more abstract sense.

### Slide 52 — Distinguishing by Particles
- **On screen:** Comparison: 나는 집이 있어요 (I have a house - Subject marker 이) vs. 나는 집에 있어요 (I am at home - Location marker 에).
- **Ji's teaching:** If you look at these two sentences, they can mean completely different things. In 나는 집이 있어요, 집 (house) is marked with the subject marker 이, indicating possession: "I have a house." In 나는 집에 있어요, 에 is a location marker. This sentence means you are physically present at home: "I'm at home."

### Slide 53 — 가지다 vs. 있다
- **On screen:** 가지다 is an action verb meaning to have/take/bring/hold. Note: It cannot be used in present tense to say "I have something." Incorrect: 집을 가져요.
- **Ji's teaching:** There’s also the verb 가지다, which is an action verb that can be translated as "to have." We’ll revisit it later, but for now, just remember that 가지다 cannot be used in the present tense to say "I have something." So, 집을 가져요 is just incorrect.

### Slide 54 — Time & Location Particles: 에 & 에서
- **On screen:** 에 marks both TIME & LOCATION. 에서 marks LOCATION. Examples: 아침에 (Time), 책상에 (Location), 학교에 가요 (Location/Movement), 학교에서 공부해요 (Location/Action).
- **Ji's teaching:** We’ve seen these particles in some of our past example sentences. A lot of learners get confused between 에 and 에서, so we’re going to go over each one step by step. First of all, 에 can mark both TIME and LOCATION, while 에서 can mark only the location.

### Slide 55 — 에 Marking Time
- **On screen:** 에 marks specific time. Example: 오후 열두시에 점심을 먹어요. Rule: Add 에 to the final noun only. Incorrect: [오후에 열두시에]. Correct: [오후 열두시에]. Example: 금요일 밤에 만나자.
- **Ji's teaching:** 에 is used to mark a specific time when an action occurs. So you would say 저는 오후 열두시에 점심을 먹어요, meaning "at 12 o’clock in the afternoon." 오후 means afternoon. You only add the particle to the final noun. So it’s not 오후에 열두시에; it’s just 오후 열두시에. The same thing happens with 금요일 밤에 만나자 (*Let's meet on Friday night*).

### Slide 56 — Words that don't take 에
- **On screen:** A table of adverbs: 오늘, 내일, 어제, 매일, 항상, 자주, etc. Note: These words are treated like ADVERBS. Examples show 에 crossed out: 어제(에), 항상(에).
- **Ji's teaching:** There are some words where you shouldn’t add 에 to. That’s because these words are treated like adverbs, which means you don’t need 에 to indicate when it is happening. So you wouldn’t say 어제에 한국어를 공부했어요; it's just 어제 한국어를 공부했어요. Same with 항상; it’s not 항상에, it’s just 항상, so it’s 저는 항상 일찍 자요.

### Slide 57 — 에 Marking Location
- **On screen:** [MOVEMENT TO A LOCATION]: 가게에 가요, 옷장에 넣었어요. [EXISTENCE - NO ACTION]: 책상에 열쇠가 있었어요, 식당에 사람이 많아요. Note: Descriptive verbs are often used when 에 responds to existence.
- **Ji's teaching:** Next, let’s look at when 에 marks the location. First, when there’s movement involved to a certain location, it’s very similar to “TO” in English. In the first sentence, I am moving to the store, so it’s 저는 가게에 가요. In the second sentence, the pants are moving into the closet, so it’s 옷장에 넣었어요. Secondly, 에 is used to note a state of being or existence. It’s often used in pair with descriptive verbs. In 책상에 열쇠가 있었어요 (*The key was on the desk*), nothing happened; the key was just there. Similarly, in 식당에 사람이 많아요 (*There's a lot of people in the restaurant*), people are just there.

### Slide 58 — When 에 is Optional
- **On screen:** Table: 어디, 여기, 저기, 거기. 에 is optional when clear from context. Example: 나 병원 가 (O). Note: 에 is necessary in "집에 있어요" to avoid confusion with "집 있어요" (I have a house).
- **Ji's teaching:** After these four words (어디, 여기, 저기, 거기), you don’t need to add 에 because they are obviously words for locations. You can also drop 에 in a sentence like 나 병원 가 because 가다 indicates the movement. But on the other hand, you need 에 in the sentence 나는 집에 있어요 because, as we learned, 집 있어요 could mean "I have a house." So, 에 is optional only when it’s clear from context.

### Slide 59 — 에서 Marking Location
- **On screen:** [WHERE THE ACTION TAKES PLACE]: 집에서 저녁을 먹었어요, 공항에서 만나자, 시드니에서 아미라를 만났어요. [FROM / OUT OF]: 책에서 배웠어요, 10에서 7을 빼요, 주머니에서 열쇠를 꺼내라.
- **Ji's teaching:** What if something IS happening? Then, you use 에서. For example, 집에서 저녁을 먹었어요 (*I ate dinner at home*). The eating took place at home. Similarly, 공항에서 만나자 (*Let's meet at the airport*) or 저는 시드니에서 아미라를 봤어요. Meeting Amira happened in Sydney. 에서 can also mean “from” or "out of." Although 책 (book) is technically not a location, figuratively, it could be treated like one: "I learned English from a book."

### Slide 60 — 에서 Abbreviated
- **On screen:** Table showing 어디에서 → 어디서, 여기에서 → 여기서, etc. Example: 어디서 왔어요? vs. 저는 호주에서 왔어요.
- **Ji's teaching:** 에서 could be abbreviated to just 서 with certain words like 어디서 or 여기서. But when marking a specific location like 호주 (Australia), you would have to say 호주에서, not 호주서.

### Slide 61 — 에 vs. 에서 Comparison
- **On screen:** Table comparing usage. 에: Time, Existence, Movement (To). 에서: Occurrence (Action), From. Note: The VERB determines which particle to use.
- **Ji's teaching:** This is the comparison of 에 and 에서. Although both mark locations, only 에 can mark the time. 에 as a location marker marks where something exists or the movement of a noun. 에서 as a location marker marks where something is happening, and it could also mean "from."

### Slide 62 — Sentence Structure (S T L O V)
- **On screen:** S T L O V structure. Example 1: 저는 아침에 욕실에서 샤워를 했어요. Example 2: 저는 저녁에 집에서 양말을 세탁기에 넣었어요. Color-coded: Time (Yellow), Location (Blue/Green).
- **Ji's teaching:** Now, you can make complex sentences like these using the S T L O V (Subject, Time, Location, Object, Verb) structure. For example: "I took a shower in the bathroom in the morning" or "I put the socks in the washing machine at home in the evening."

### Slide 63 — 까지 (Until, Up to, By)
- **On screen:** 까지 marks both TIME & LOCATION. Examples: 저녁까지 (Time), 세시까지 (Time), 회사까지 (Location), 거기까지 (Location). Note: 까지 can also mark Subject/Object (Even/Too), but that's for later.
- **Ji's teaching:** 까지 means "until," "up to," or "by." It can mark both time and location. For example: "The professor works until the evening" (저녁까지) or "I take the subway from home to work" (회사까지). It can also mean "even" or "too" in some contexts, but we'll learn more about that later.

### Slide 64 — From A to B
- **On screen:** A에서 B까지 [Location only]. A부터 B까지 [Both Time & Location]. Examples: 시드니에서 브리스번까지 (Location), 여기부터 저기까지 (Location), 밤 11시부터 아침 6시까지 (Time).
- **Ji's teaching:** When saying "From A to B," use 에서 for location only, but use 부터 for both time and location. For example, "from Sydney to Brisbane" is 시드니에서 브리스번까지. But for "from here to over there" or "from 11 PM to 6 AM," you use 부터.

### Slide 65~69 — Combining Particles (도, 만, 는)
- **On screen:** Noun + 에/에서/부터/까지 can be followed by 는, 도, and 만. Examples: 주말에는 (As for the weekend), 화요일에도 (On Tuesday too), 집에만 (Only at home). Slide 69: 에는 (As for this place), 에도 (This place also).
- **Ji's teaching:** Particles like 도 (also) and 만 (only) can be placed after any noun, but they can also be placed after 에 and 에서. This creates forms like 에도, 에만, 에서도, or 에서만. This specifies "also in that location" or "only at that time." For example: "I met a friend on Tuesday, too" (화요일에도) or "I stayed only at home on the weekend" (집에만 있었어요). 에 can also combine with 는 to imply contrast: 거기에는 아무것도 없어요 (*As for there, there is nothing*) implies the thing might be somewhere else.

## Teaching Points
- **Past Tense Conjugation:** The choice between 았 and 었 depends on the last vowel of the verb stem. If it's ㅏ or ㅗ, use 았; otherwise, use 었. These merge with stems ending in vowels (e.g., 가다 → 갔다, 서다 → 섰다).
- **Honorific Past Tense:** Always uses the branch (으)셨 (which is (으)시 + 었). The choice between 으셨 and 셨 depends solely on whether the stem has a 받침.
- **있다 vs. 없다:** These descriptive verbs cover both existence ("There is") and possession ("I have"). Possession is typically marked with the subject marker 이/가 on the object possessed, while existence uses the location marker 에.
- **에 vs. 에서:**
    - **에:** Used for specific time (except for adverbs like 오늘/내일), destination of movement (with verbs like 가다/오다), and location of existence (with verbs like 있다/없다/많다).
    - **에서:** Used for the location where an action occurs and to mean "from" or "out of."
- **Particle Combinations:** Particles like 도 (also) and 만 (only) can follow location/time particles (에, 에서, 부터, 까지) to add specific emphasis or contrast to the sentence.
