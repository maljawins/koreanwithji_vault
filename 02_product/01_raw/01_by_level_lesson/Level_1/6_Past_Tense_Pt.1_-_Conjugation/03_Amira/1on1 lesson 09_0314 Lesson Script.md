Okay, past tense conjugation Past tense conjugation is actually **very simple.**

The **only difference** is that past tense adds **ㅆ받침** to **았** or **었**—everything else follows the same rules as the present tense.

💡 **By nature, past tense cannot have propositive or imperative sentences** because you can’t suggest or command something **in the past.**

**3️⃣ Key Points Before Looking at Examples**

✔ **In the present tense**, if the verb stem **ends in ㅏ or ㅗ**, the "branch" is **아** right?
✔ **In the past tense**, it becomes **았** (with the ㅆ받침).

After **았**, it’s either followed by **어** or **습니다**, depending on speech level:

* **어** → Context marker that indicates **no specific context** (*we’ll call this "plain."*)
* **습니다** → **Formal high speech level**, which **cannot** have an additional context marker.

💡 **Remember**:
**Formal high endings are context markers by themselves**—they mark the **formality** of the sentence. That’s why **formal speech doesn’t take additional context markers.**

✔ **When 았 is added to 오 or 아, it merges naturally:**

* **가다 (to go)** → **갔어 / 갔어요 / 갔습니다**

This **merging** isn’t **always mandatory** with certain verbs.
