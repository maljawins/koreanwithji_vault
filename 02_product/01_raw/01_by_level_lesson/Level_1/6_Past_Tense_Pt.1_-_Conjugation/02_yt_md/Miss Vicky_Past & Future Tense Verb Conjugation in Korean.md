### Past Tense Conjugation Rules
The past tense is formed by adding specific suffixes to the verb stem, which is the part of the verb remaining after removing the dictionary ending `다`. The instructor identifies three primary categories for conjugation. The first category includes stems ending in the vowels `아` or `오`. For these, the suffix `았다` is used for written forms, while `았어요` is used for polite conversational speech. Examples include `알다 (to know)` becoming `알았어요` and `살다 (to live)` becoming `살았어요`. The instructor notes that `오다 (to come)` and `보다 (to see)` are slightly irregular or abbreviated; `오다` becomes `왔어요` (not `오았어요`), and `보다` can be either `보았어요` or the more common abbreviated form `봤어요`.

The second category consists of stems ending in any vowels other than `아` or `오`. These stems take the suffix `었다` for written forms or `었어요` for conversational speech. For instance, `되다 (to become)` becomes `되었어요` (often abbreviated to `됐어요`), and `주다 (to give)` becomes `주었어요` (abbreviated to `줬어요`). Other examples include `울다 (to cry)` becoming `울었어요`, `있다 (to be/have)` becoming `있었어요`, and `없다 (to not be/not have)` becoming `없었어요`. The instructor points out the irregular verb `듣다 (to listen)`, where the `ㄷ` in the stem changes to `ㄹ` before adding the suffix, resulting in `들었어요`.

The third category covers all verbs ending in `하다`. This is the simplest group because it follows a consistent rule without exceptions: `하` is always replaced with `했`. Therefore, `했다` is used for written forms and `했어요` for conversational speech. Examples provided include `공부하다 (to study)` becoming `공부했어요`, `말하다 (to speak)` becoming `말했어요`, and `보답하다 (to repay/give back)` becoming `보답했어요`.

### Descriptive Verbs and Irregularities
In Korean, adjectives function similarly to verbs when they act as the predicate of a sentence, leading the instructor to refer to them as "descriptive verbs." When an adjective like `작다 (to be small)` is used to say "it was small," it follows the standard past tense rules based on its stem vowel. Since `작` contains the vowel `아`, it becomes `작았어요`. The instructor clarifies that while adjectives have different forms when modifying a noun directly (e.g., `작은`), they conjugate exactly like action verbs when ending a sentence.

Several irregular past tense forms are highlighted as essential for memorization. Beyond the `ㄷ` to `ㄹ` change in `듣다`, the instructor introduces `갖다 (to have)`, which becomes `가졌어요` rather than following the standard `아` vowel rule. Another significant irregular is `모르다 (to not know)`, which transforms into `몰랐어요`. The instructor explains that these forms do not follow a strict logic and must be learned through practice.

---

### Future Tense Conjugation Rules
The future tense is generally easier to master than the past tense, as it depends primarily on whether the verb stem ends in a vowel or a consonant. The instructor outlines two main categories for this conjugation. The first category applies to stems ending in a vowel or the consonant `ㄹ`. For stems ending in a vowel, the suffix `ㄹ 거예요` is attached directly to the stem. For example, `보다 (to see)` becomes `볼 거예요` and `공부하다 (to study)` becomes `공할 거예요`. If the stem already ends in `ㄹ`, such as `살다 (to live)` or `만들다 (to make)`, you simply add `거예요` without adding an extra `ㄹ`, resulting in `살 거예요` and `만들 거예요`.

The second category applies to stems ending in any consonant other than `ㄹ`. In these cases, the suffix `을 거예요` is added to the stem. Examples include `있다 (to be)` becoming `있을 거예요`, `없다 (to not be)` becoming `없을 거예요`, and the descriptive verb `작다 (to be small)` becoming `작을 거예요`. The irregular verb `듣다 (to listen)` appears again here; the `ㄷ` changes to `ㄹ`, and because the original stem ended in a consonant, it follows this second rule to become `들을 거예요`.

---

### Sentence Structure and Particles
The instructor concludes by demonstrating how to build full sentences using the newly learned tenses, reinforcing previous lessons on particles. When using the verb `되다 (to become)`, the instructor reminds students that the object of the "becoming" takes the subject markers `이/가` rather than the object markers `을/를`. For example, "I became a fan" is translated as `저는 팬이 되었어요`. 

The lesson also touches upon the placement of adverbs, noting that time adverbs like `어제 (yesterday)` or `내일 (tomorrow)` and manner adverbs like `열심히 (hard/diligently)` should be placed before the verb, often near the beginning of the sentence. For instance, "I studied Korean yesterday" is `저는 어제 한국어를 공부했어요`. Finally, the instructor explains that in sentences like "I did it" (`내가 했어`), the object "it" is often omitted in natural Korean conversation if the context makes it obvious what is being referred to.
