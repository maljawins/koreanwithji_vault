### Overview
The instructor introduces the past tense in Korean, contrasting it with the present tense covered in previous lessons. While the present tense describes actions happening now or general habits, the past tense is used for events that have already concluded. The lesson focuses on the informal polite speech level (존댓말 ending in 요), demonstrating that past tense conjugation is a simple three-step extension of the present tense rules students already know.

The learning arc begins with a review of present tense sentences to establish a baseline. It then moves into the mechanical rules for transforming these into the past tense, covers the specific conjugation of the copula 이다 (to be), provides a guided practice session with six sentences, and concludes with a brief introduction to how the honorific suffix (으)시다 functions in the past tense.

### Present Tense vs. Past Tense
To frame the lesson, the instructor displays several present tense examples on screen. These include:
- **저는 야구를 봐요.** / I watch baseball.
- **저는 농구를 좋아해요.** / I like basketball.
- **골프가 더 재밌어요.** / Golf is more entertaining.
- **TV를 보고 싶으세요?** / Do you want to watch TV?

The instructor explains that to change these to the past tense, the English equivalents would become "watched," "liked," "was," and "did you want to." In Korean, this change is handled through a specific suffix added to the verb or adjective stem.

---

### How to Conjugate the Past Tense
The instructor outlines a three-step process for conjugating any regular verb or adjective into the past tense. First, conjugate the word into the informal present tense as usual, but stop before adding the final **요**. Second, attach a double **ㅅ** (ssang-siot) to the bottom of that conjugated form as a batchim. Third, attach **어요** to the end.

Visual examples on screen demonstrate this transformation:
- **가다 (to go):** The present tense form (minus 요) is **가**. Adding the double **ㅅ** results in **갔**, and adding **어요** results in **갔어요** (went).
- **먹다 (to eat):** The present tense form (minus 요) is **먹어**. Adding the double **ㅅ** results in **먹었**, and adding **어요** results in **먹었어요** (ate).

The instructor also highlights the change in time markers, showing how **오늘 운동을 해요** (I exercise today) becomes **어제 운동을 했어요** (I exercised yesterday) by changing both the adverb and the verb ending. A vocabulary note defines **어제** as "yesterday."

---

### The Past Tense of 이다 (To Be)
The verb **이다** follows a slightly different pattern based on whether the preceding noun ends in a vowel or a consonant. In the present tense, it becomes **이에요** after a consonant or **예요** after a vowel. In the past tense, this shifts to:
- **이었다** → **이었어요** (after a consonant)
- **였다** → **였어요** (after a vowel)

The instructor provides a comparison using the word **고양이** (cat) and **동물** (animal). In the present tense, one says **고양이에요** (It is a cat). In the past tense, because **고양이** ends in a vowel, it becomes **고양이였어요** (It was a cat). Conversely, if describing a general animal, **거기에 동물이 있었어요** (There was an animal over there) uses the past tense of **있다** (to exist/be there), which is **있었어요**.

### Practice and Common Expressions
The lesson includes a practice segment where the viewer is asked to convert six sentences. Through these examples, the instructor provides additional linguistic nuances:
- **날씨가 아주 더웠어요.** / The weather was very hot. (From **덥다**)
- **제 여자친구는 친절했어요.** / My girlfriend was kind. (From **친절하다**)
- **제 남자친구를 빨리 보고 싶었어요.** / I wanted to see my boyfriend quickly.
- **여기에 사과가 있었어요.** / There was an apple here.
- **저는 한국어를 가르쳤어요.** / I taught Korean. (From **가르치다**)
- **빌리 씨의 집에 갔어요.** / I went to Billy's house.

The instructor notes that **빨리** can mean "quickly," "as soon as possible," or "right away." Furthermore, he explains that the phrase **보고 싶다** (literally "to want to see") is the standard way to express "to miss someone." Therefore, **보고 싶었어요** can mean "I wanted to see you" or "I missed you."

---

### Honorific Past Tense: (으)시다
The instructor briefly introduces how to use the honorific suffix **(으)시다** in the past tense. This form is used when the subject of the sentence is someone deserving of high respect, such as a grandparent or a teacher.
- After a consonant: **으셨어요**
- After a vowel: **셨어요**

Examples shown on screen include:
- **우리 할머니도 중국어를 공부하셨어요.** / My grandmother also studied Chinese.
- **선생님은 나가셨어요?** / Did the teacher go outside?

The instructor concludes by mentioning that more practice with honorifics will be provided in future lessons.
