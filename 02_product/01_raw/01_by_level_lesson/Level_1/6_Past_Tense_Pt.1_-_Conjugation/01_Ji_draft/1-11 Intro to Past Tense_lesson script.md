**Intro**

In a “conventional” Korean curriculum, they don’t usually teach you the past tense this early on. But I believe the best time to learn it though is right after learning the present tense because it follows a grammar rule that’s almost identical.

All you have to do is just add a ㅆ 받침 to either 아 or 어.

But this time, you’d add it to the “Branch” part where it represents the tense of the verb if you remember the plant analogy from the last lesson.

And the standard context marker that comes in the leaf part is always 어 for informal speech and 습니다 in formal speech.

Let’s get right in.

**When the stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ The branch is 았**

✔ **So, in the present tense**, if the verb stem **ends in ㅏ or ㅗ**, it’s **아** right?
✔ **In the past tense**, it becomes **았** (with the ㅆ받침).

After **았**, it’s either followed by **어(요)** or **습니다**, depending on speech level.

**When to just add 았 after the stem**

When the stem ends in ㅗ or ㅏ and has a 받침, you simply add 았.

**[Show 작다 – Small]**

작다 means small and the stem ends in 받침.

작 has ㅏ so, you add 았 in the branch and 어 afterwards so it becomes 남았어 for informal low speech level.

[Show : 강아지가 정말 작았어요] - The puppy was really small.

In formal speech, the formal context marker 습니다 takes place after 았. So it’s 작았습니다.

[Show: 신발이 너무 작았습니다] – The shoes were too small.

**[Show 놓다 – To put]**

놓다 ends in ㅗ and has 받침. So it becomes 놓았어(요) and 놓았습니다. But you can also shorten them to 놨어요 and 놨습니다. However, 놓다 is the only word that does this.

[Show 좋다 – good]

For example, [좋았어요 – It was good] cannot be shortened to 좠어요.

책상에 연필을 놓았어요 / 놓았습니다. (놨어요 / 놨습니다 O) – I put the pencil on the desk

고객들의 반응이 좋았어요 / 좋았습니다. (좠어요 / 좠습니다 X) – Customers’ reaction was good [We had a good customer review]

**When 았 merges with the stem**

When the stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ with no 받침, 았 merges with the stem.

[Show 가다 – to go and 오다 – to come]

가았 is redundant so 았 merges with 가 and becomes 갔.

So 가다 becomes 갔어 / 갔어요 / 갔습니다.

**어제 친구네 집에 갔어요 / 갔습니다 – I went to my friend’s house yesterday.**

Again, “에” is a location marker.

And similarly, 오다 becomes 왔어 / 왔어요 / 왔습니다.

오다 ends in ㅗ with no 받침 so 았 merges with 오 and becomes 왔 and followed by 어. it’s 왔어.

Remember 왔어? “Did you come?” To greet friends? This is how it’s done.

**어제 비가 왔어요 / 왔습니다 – It rained yesterday. In Korean we say “the rain came” literally. We don’t have a word like “it” in Korean.**

**When the stem doesn’t end in ㅏ or ㅗ**

Now, just like in the present tense, if the verb stem ends in any vowel other than ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 었, which is 어 plus ㅆ.

[Show 입다 – To wear (clothing)]

입 ends in a 받침 so it becomes 입었어 / 입었어요 / 입었습니다.

언니가 드레스를 입었어요 – My older sister wore a dress.

남자가 쟈켓을 입었습니다. – The man wore a jacket.

When the stem DOESN’T end in a 받침, 어 in 었 either cancels out or merges with the stem. However, ㅆ remains intact and then goes right under the original stem. And the rest is the same.

[Show 서다 – To stand]

서었 is redundant so it becomes 섰. And then you get 섰어 / 섰어요 / 섰습니다.

**물병이 똑바로 섰어요 – The water bottle stood upright.**

배우다 means to learn. 배우었 becomes 배웠. Then you get 배웠어 / 배웠어요 / 배웠습니다.

**저는 한국어를 배웠어요 – I learned Korean (language).**

[Show 재다 – To measure / 세다 – To count]

재다 means to measure and it ends in ㅐ with no 받침. And we learned that ㅓ cancel out because it’s already in the letter ㅐ, alphabetically speaking – And the same goes for ㅔ.

So 재다 becomes 쟀. Then you get 쟀어 / 쟀어요 / 쟀습니다.

And 세다 becomes 셌. Then you get 셌어 / 셌어요 / 셌습니다.

**Example sentences with 재다 and 세다 in past tense**

Next, we have 되다 which means to become.

[Show the movement of the letters in the video – 되었 = 되+ㅓ+ㅆ = 됐]

Now, when 되, is combined with 었, ㅆ remains intact as always and ㅓ merges with ㅚ making it ㅙ

This is how 되었 turns into 됐. Then you get 됐어 / 됐어요 / 됐습니다.

[Show 되었어 / 되었어요 / 되었습니다]

Note that 되었 is not “WRONG” but it’s way too formal and almost never used in spoken Korean. So I recommend you just stick with 됐.

**저는 한국어 선생님이 됐어요. – I became a Korean teacher.**

**Past Stem**

So we learned all about 았 and 었 rule. I’m sure it wasn’t too hard to follow because it’s very similar to the present tense we learned in the last lesson.

Now, up to 았 and 었 part, we’re going to call this the past stem. This will become more relevant later on in level 2 when we learn about more advanced concepts.

For now, all you need to know is that you can use a lot of different endings and forms to express different ideas after the past stem.
