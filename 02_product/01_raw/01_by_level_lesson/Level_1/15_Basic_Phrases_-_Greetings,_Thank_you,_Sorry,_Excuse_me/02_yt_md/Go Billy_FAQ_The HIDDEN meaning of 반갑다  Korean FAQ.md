## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor explores the deeper nuances of the Korean verb 반갑다, which most beginners encounter early on in the fixed expression 만나서 반갑습니다 (Nice to meet you). While textbooks often translate this phrase as a single unit, the instructor explains that 반갑다 is actually a descriptive verb with a broader range of meanings related to feeling glad, happy, or delighted. By understanding the core definition of the word, learners can use it in various contexts beyond initial introductions, such as greeting old friends, visiting nostalgic locations, or expressing joy at a long-awaited event.

---

### The Literal Meaning of 반갑다
The instructor clarifies that 반갑다 is a descriptive verb meaning "to feel happy," "to feel glad," "delighted," or "pleasant." He notes that the standard dictionary definition involves feeling happy or glad to meet someone you have missed, or when something you desired is accomplished or happens. Therefore, the common translation "Nice to meet you" is actually a contextual application of the verb rather than its literal definition. The phrase 만나서 반갑습니다 literally breaks down into 만나서 (because I meet you/meeting you) and 반갑습니다 (I am glad).

---

### Using 반갑다 with People You Already Know
A common misconception among learners is that 반갑다 can only be used when meeting someone for the first time. However, because the word signifies being glad to see someone you missed, it is perfectly appropriate to use with friends or acquaintances you haven't seen in a while. In casual situations, one can say 반가워요 or simply 반가워 to a friend. In this context, the meaning shifts from "Nice to meet you" to "Nice to see you" or "I'm glad to see you again."

---

### Past Tense: 반가웠어요
The instructor demonstrates how to use the verb in the past tense to express that a previous encounter was pleasant. By using 반가웠어요 (It was nice to see you), a speaker can wrap up a meeting with a friend or acquaintance. He provides an example sentence for hanging out with a friend:
> **오늘 진짜 반가웠어.** / It was really nice to see you today.

---

### Non-Human Applications of 반갑다
Because the core of 반갑다 is the feeling of being glad that something you wanted has happened, it can be applied to locations, weather, and specific situations. If a person returns to their hometown after ten years, they can use 반갑다 to express their happiness at being back in a place they missed. Similarly, if it hasn't rained in a long time and it finally starts to pour, a speaker might say it is 반갑다 to see the rain.

The instructor also provides a situational example: if a non-native speaker living in Korea has been speaking only Korean for a year and finally meets someone to speak English with, they might feel 반갑다 to use their native language again. In all these cases, the word conveys a sense of "I've missed this" or "I'm so glad this is happening."
