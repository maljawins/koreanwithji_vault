### Overview
This lesson focuses on common ways to ask "How are you?" and "Are you doing well?" in Korean. The instructor introduces the core verb used for these expressions and explains how to conjugate it for different levels of politeness. Students will learn not only how to initiate these greetings but also how to respond naturally to them.

The lesson covers the transition from asking if someone is doing "well" to asking "how" they are doing by swapping adverbs. Additionally, the instructor introduces a common phrase for reuniting after a long time and provides a full conversation practice to demonstrate these phrases in a realistic social context, including honorific variations for addressing elders or parents.

---

### The Core Verb: 지내다
To ask how someone is doing or to describe one's own state, learners must first understand the verb `지내다`. While it has several meanings, in the context of greetings, it means "to spend" or "to pass time." For example, the phrase `시간을 지내다` means "to pass time." When used in a greeting, it implies passing time while living your life.

---

### Asking "Are You Doing Well?"
By combining the adverb `잘 (well)` with the verb `지내다`, you get the expression `잘 지내다`, which literally means "to pass the time well." The instructor provides two polite versions of this question based on the relationship with the listener. For close acquaintances who are the same age or older, the standard polite form is `잘 지내요?`. For strangers or situations requiring extra politeness, the honorific form `잘 지내세요?` is used.

---

### Asking "How Are You Doing?"
To ask specifically "how" someone is doing rather than just if they are doing "well," the adverb `잘` is replaced with `어떻게 (how)`. This results in the phrase `어떻게 지내다`. Following the same politeness rules, the instructor presents `어떻게 지내요?` for close acquaintances and `어떻게 지내세요?` for strangers or those requiring higher respect.

---

### Responding to the Greeting
When someone asks how you are, you can use `잘 지내다` to reply. A simple affirmative response is `네, 잘 지내요 (Yes, I am doing well)`. For a more nuanced response that implies you have been doing well continually up to the present moment, you can use the continuous form: `네, 잘 지내고 있어요 (Yes, I am and have been doing well)`.

---

### Long Time No See
The instructor introduces the expression `오랜만이다`, which literally means "to be a long time since something." In a social setting, this is conjugated as `오랜만이에요` to mean "Long time no see." To reply to this, it is common to simply say `네` and repeat the expression: `네, 오랜만이에요`.

---

### Conversation Practice and Honorifics
The video concludes with a dialogue between two people, 희준 and 우혁. The conversation demonstrates the flow of these greetings:
> **희준: 안녕하세요! 오랜만이에요.** / Hello! Long time no see.
> **우혁: 네, 오랜만이에요. 잘 지내세요?** / Yes, long time no see. Are you doing well?
> **희준: 네, 아주 잘 지내고 있어요. 우혁 씨는요?** / Yes, I'm doing very well. How about you, 우혁?
> **우혁: 저도 잘 지내고 있어요.** / I am also doing well.

A critical grammar point is introduced regarding third parties, such as parents. When asking if someone's parents are doing well, the `고 있다` (continuous) form is upgraded to the honorific `고 계시다`.
> **희준: 부모님들도 잘 지내고 계세요?** / Are your parents also doing well?
> **우혁: 네, 잘 지내고 계세요.** / Yes, they are doing well.
