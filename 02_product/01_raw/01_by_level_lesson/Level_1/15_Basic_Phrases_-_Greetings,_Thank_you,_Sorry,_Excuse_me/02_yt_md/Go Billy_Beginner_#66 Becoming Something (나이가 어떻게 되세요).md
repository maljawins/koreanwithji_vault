### The Primary Meaning: To Become

The verb 되다 is used to say that a noun becomes another noun. In the polite present tense, it conjugates as 돼요, and in the past tense, it becomes 됐어요. When used in this sense, the noun that someone or something is becoming is typically marked with the subject marker 이 or 가. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this transformation. For instance, to ask what someone wants to be, one would say 뭐가 되고 싶어요? (What do you want to be/become?). A response might be 저는 더 친절한 사람이 되고 싶어요 (I want to be a nicer person).

Other examples shown on screen demonstrate various tenses and intentions. 현우 씨는 나중에 부자가 될 거예요 (Hyunwoo will be rich later) uses the future tense. 저는 한국어 선생님이 되고 싶어요 (I want to be a Korean teacher) expresses a desire. The past tense is used in 우리는 결국 친구가 됐어요 (We eventually became friends). Finally, a negative sentence is shown: 저는 이 차의 주인이 되고 싶지 않아요 (I don't want to be the owner of this car).

---

### Permission and Functionality

The verb 되다 has a secondary meaning of "to be okay," which is frequently used when asking for or giving permission. This is often seen in the grammatical structure -아/어/여도 되다. The instructor highlights examples such as 정말 가도 돼요? (Is it really okay to go? / Can I really go?) and the negative response 안 돼요 (It's not okay / You can't). Another example provided is 제가 그렇게 만들어도 돼요? (Is it okay to make it like that?).

Additionally, 되다 is the standard verb used to say that a machine or device "works" or functions properly. If a machine is broken or not functioning, Koreans say it "doesn't become." Examples include 이 기계가 지금 안 돼요 (This machine doesn't work now) and 프린터가 갑자기 안 돼요 (The printer suddenly doesn't work). Conversely, to say something works well, one would say 제 핸드폰이 잘 돼요 (My cell phone works well).

---

### Polite Inquiries with 어떻게 되다

A very common and polite way to ask for information in Korean is to use the adverb 어떻게 (how) with 되다. While a beginner might use 뭐예요? (What is it?) to ask for a name, using 어떻게 돼요? is significantly more natural and polite in social settings. This structure essentially asks "How does [the information] become?" but functions as "Tell me about..." or "What is...".

The instructor lists several essential polite questions using this format. 가족이 어떻게 되세요? is used to ask about family size or details. 취미가 어떻게 돼요? asks about hobbies. For names, 성함이 어떻게 되세요? is much more polite than 이름이 뭐예요?. Similarly, for age, 연세가 어떻게 되세요? is the appropriate honorific way to ask an older person, whereas 나이가 어떻게 돼요? is used for those of a similar or younger age.

---

### Demonstrative Descriptive Verbs: This and That

The lesson expands on the descriptive verb 어떻다 (to be how) by introducing its counterparts: 이렇다 (to be like this), 그렇다 (to be like that), and 저렇다 (to be like that over there). These follow the same conjugation patterns as 어떻다. For each verb, the instructor demonstrates four forms: the adverbial "like this/that" (-게), the noun-modifying "this/that kind of" (-ㄴ), the present tense "it is like this/that" (-애요), and the past tense "it was like this/that" (-앴어요).

- **이렇다 (Like this):** 이렇게, 이런, 이래요, 이랬어요.
- **그렇다 (Like that):** 그렇게, 그런, 그래요, 그랬어요.
- **저렇다 (Like that over there):** 저렇게, 저런, 저래요, 저랬어요.

The instructor notes that 그래요? is commonly used to ask "Is that so?" or "Really?", and the non-question form 그래요 can be used to answer "It is so" or "Yeah." These forms are essential for comparing things or referring back to previously mentioned ideas in a conversation.
