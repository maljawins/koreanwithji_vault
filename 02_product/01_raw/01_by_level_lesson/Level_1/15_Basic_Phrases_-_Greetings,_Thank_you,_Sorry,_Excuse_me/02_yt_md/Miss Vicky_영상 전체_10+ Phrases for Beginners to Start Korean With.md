### Understanding Speech Levels: 존댓말 and 반말
Before diving into specific phrases, the instructor establishes the two main categories of Korean speech. **존댓말** is the formal or polite form, typically used with people older than the speaker, strangers, or in formal settings like giving a speech. Visually, the instructor notes that 존댓말 often ends with the syllables **요** or **~습니다**. **반말** is the informal or casual form, used with close friends, siblings, or people younger than the speaker. Unlike the formal form, 반말 does not have one fixed ending.

---

### Greetings and Farewells
The most basic greeting is **안녕하세요? (Hello)**. The instructor breaks this down: **안녕** means "peace," and **하세요** comes from the verb "to do," essentially asking if the person is being peaceful. In casual settings, this is shortened to **안녕**.

Farewells in Korean depend on who is staying and who is leaving. **안녕히 가세요** literally means "peacefully go" and is said to the person who is leaving. **안녕히 계세요** means "peacefully stay" and is said to the person who is staying. If both parties are leaving, they both say **안녕히 가세요**. In casual speech, the single word **안녕** serves as both "hi" and "bye."

### Affirmation, Negation, and Understanding
For "yes" and "no," the instructor provides the following pairs:
- **네** (Yes - formal) / **응** (Yes - informal)
- **아니요** (No - formal) / **아니** (No - informal)

To express "I understand" or "Okay," there are several variations. **알겠습니다** and **알겠어요** are polite ways to acknowledge information or instructions. The informal versions are **알겠어** or **알았어**. The instructor demonstrates this with a short dialogue where a mother tells a child to do the dishes (**설거지 좀 해**), and the child responds with **알겠어요**.

---

### Thank You and Sorry
There are four common ways to say "thank you" in polite speech, ranging in degrees of formality:
- **감사합니다** (Most formal/polite)
- **고맙습니다** (Very polite)
- **감사해요** (Polite)
- **고마워요** (Polite/friendly)
The informal version is **고마워**.

For apologies, the word **죄송** is generally more polite than **미안**.
- **죄송합니다** (Most formal)
- **죄송해요** (Polite)
- **미안해요** (Polite)
The informal version is **미안해**.

---

### Excuse Me and Navigating Language Barriers
While there is a literal translation for "excuse me," Koreans often use situational alternatives. To get through a crowd, one says **잠시만요 (Just a moment)** or **지나갈게요 (I will pass through)**. To get someone's attention, such as a waiter or a stranger on the street, the standard phrase is **저기요 (Over there / Hey there)**.

For beginners who cannot yet sustain a conversation, the instructor provides survival phrases:
- **저 한국어 잘 못해요.** / I can't speak Korean very well.
- **저 한국어 잘 몰라요.** / I don't understand Korean well.
In casual speech, these become **나 한국어 잘 못해** and **나 한국어 잘 몰라**.

---

### Nationality and Names
To state your nationality, use the pattern: **저는 [Country] 사람이에요**.
- **저는 미국 사람이에요.** / I am an American.
- **저는 필리핀 사람이에요.** / I am a Filipino.
In this pattern, **저** is the formal "I," **는** is a subject marker, and **사람** means "person." For the informal version, **저** changes to **나**, and the ending **이에요** changes to **야**, resulting in **나는 [Country] 사람이야**.

To state your name, use: **제 이름은 [Name]이에요/예요**.
The choice between **이에요** and **예요** depends on the final consonant (받침) of the name. If the name ends in a consonant, like Rachel (**레이첼**), add **이** before the ending: **제 이름은 레이첼이에요**. If the name ends in a vowel, like Vicky (**비키**), use **예요**: **제 이름은 비키예요**. For informal speech, use **내 이름은 [Name]이야**.
