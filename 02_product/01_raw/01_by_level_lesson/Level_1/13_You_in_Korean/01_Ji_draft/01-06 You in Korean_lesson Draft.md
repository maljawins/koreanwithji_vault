
**Introduction**

So far, we've learned: I, this & that, and here & there.

And in this lesson, we’re going to learn all about “you” in Korean.

So, how DO you say “you?”

Short answer, it’s 너 in 반말.

But in 존댓말, well, it’s not so simple.

Here’s what I mean.

In the lesson “How Korean works”, we learned that Koreans drop the subject in their sentences when the it’s clear from context.

[Show the video of a receptionist asking a young Korean man “어디 가세요?” in an office building lobby]

어디 means “Where” and “가세요?” is “go” so literally, it means “Where go?”

And here, you don’t need “you” because it’d be weird to ask about anyone else in this situation because she’s obviously asking HIM where HE’s going.

And 세요 in 가세요 makes it honorific. And honorific verbs are ONLY used to elevate others, not yourself.

So, since she’s talking to HIM, and using honorifics, this makes the subject “you”

Now, this is how most, if not all Koreans communicate politely without saying “you” directly.

You simply omit the subject and use the honorific verb.

**You in Korean**

But there are times when you do want to specifically say YOU. And that’s what we’re going to cover in this lesson.

Now, this can be a quite tricky because it’s a very cultural thing. So, it’s not as simple as you might think.

First, we have 너. Now, 너 does mean you but it’s only for close friends of the same age or younger.

By “same age” I mean “Korean age”, people who’re born in the same year as you.

However, same age doesn’t mean you can start using ‘너’ right away. You have to establish some type of connection FIRST and mutually agree to speak in 반말 with each other. So, ‘너’ can be rude in many cases.

We have an expression especially for that and that is “말을 놓다” which means “to drop speech”.

So, 너 is very restrictive.

**Older people that are close to you**

So, what about people who are close to you but are older?

Let’s learn that first.

Think of how you call your parents. You never call them by their first name, right? It’s basically the same idea. So, think “who are they TO YOU?” and simply substitute that word where “YOU” would be placed.

For example, “What about you?” to your mom would be “엄마는(요)?” and “아빠는(요)?” to your dad. Again, we’ll talk about the particle 는 in the lesson 은/는 & 이/가.

**오빠, 언니, 형, 누나**

For friends who are older, it depends on which gender you are.

And both parties have to agree to call or to be called as such so you need to be close to each other to a certain degree.

If you're a **woman**, and you could call an older male friend 오빠 – older brother and an older female friend 언니 - older sister

On a side note, some people think 오빠 means “honey” or “babe” and it feels cringey for some reason. But in fact, it’s a perfectly normal word.

If you're a guy, you would say 형 to an older male friend and 누나 to an older female friend.

This is why one of the first things Koreans ask you is **“How old are you?”** - “몇 살이세요?” or more politely, “나이가 어떻게 되세요?”

Note there is no “You” in these questions again.

I know this can be rude in Western culture but don’t be afraid to ask in Korean because it’s perfectly normal.

**Older People Who Are Not Close to You**

And what about people who’re NOT so close to you?

Well, some textbooks will teach you that it’s 당신 but let me tell you “당신” is just WRONG. It does mean “you” but it’s only used in very specific situations.

One, for married couples typically middle-aged or older to address their spouse. My parents use 당신 to address each other but this probably doesn’t concern you unless you have a middle-aged Korean partner.

And two, when “you” is completely unknown. You don’t know any information about them and you don’t care like in the advertisement. And it’s rude to say 당신 in person, as it implies “I don’t care who you are, but you right there”. So, don’t say 당신 unless you want to start a fight.

**What to Say Instead: Titles and Names**

So what DO you say?

The most common ways to say "you" are with titles—like job titles—and names.

You can add 씨 or 님 after their name. It can loosely translate to "mister" or "miss."

My Korean name is 지균, so I would be 지균씨 or 지균님.

씨 is a bit more casual than 님. 님 is actually an honorific suffix.

So you would use 님 in professional settings after titles instead of their name.

For example: 선생님, 매니저님, 교수님, 팀장님, 사장님.

**Strangers**

But what if you’re talking to a stranger? You don’t know their name, title, age, nothing.

Then you have 2 options. And it pretty much all comes down to age.

You kind of have to guess their age and requires a deeper understanding of the culture. Note that Koreans are much more lenient to non-Koreans on this whole “politeness” thing so you don’t have to worry too much about it.

But I’ll show you some example words, any ways.

You can address older people as 선생님. Now, we just learned that 선생님 means teacher but in this context, it’s more like sir or ma’am. The idea is they have more experience so you can learn a thing or two from them.

And when you’re talking to someone who looks relatively younger or possibly around the same age as you, you can say address them with 그 쪽, which means “that side”.

**When in Doubt**

I know this can be confusing, I mean it’s sometimes hard even for us Koreans.

So when in doubt, just ask them. “호칭을 어떻게 할까요?” which translates to “What should I call you?”

And they’ll kindly tell you their preferred “title”, so to speak.

So most Koreans, myself included just drop the subject in the sentence altogether and use honorific verbs and that’s the easiest way around all this.

**How to Call Your Friend by Name**

One last thing before we wrap up this lesson.

In Korean, we don't just say 태형! or 윤기!

We say 태형아 and 윤기야.

This only applies to close friends you can use 반말 with.

In 존댓말, you'd simply say 태형님 or 태형씨.

**받침-Sensitive**

아 and 야 are also 받침-sensitive.

아 is for names that end in 받침, and 야 is for names that end in a vowel.

**Using Names in Sentences**

When you use your friend's name in a sentence, and the name ends in 받침, you always add an extra syllable: 이.

For example: 태형이는 제 친구예요.

But if the name doesn't end in 받침, you don't add 이.

For example: 윤기는 제 친구예요.
