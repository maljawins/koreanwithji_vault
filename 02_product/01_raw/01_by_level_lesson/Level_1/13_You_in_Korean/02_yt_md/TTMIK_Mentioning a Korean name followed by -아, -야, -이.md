## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common question from learners regarding the suffixes 아, 야, and 이 that are often attached to Korean names. Many students mistakenly believe these markers are related to the gender of the person being addressed. However, the instructor clarifies that these attachments are strictly determined by the phonology of the name—specifically whether it ends in a vowel or a consonant—and the social relationship between the speakers. These markers are used exclusively in casual, informal speech (반말) among close friends and family members.

---

### Formal vs. Informal Addressing

Before diving into the specific suffixes, the instructor establishes the baseline for addressing people in Korean. In formal or social settings where politeness is required (존댓말), names are typically followed by titles such as 씨 (Mr./Ms.), 선생님 (Teacher), or specific job titles like 과장님 (Section Chief). For example, the instructor is commonly addressed as 현우 씨 or 현우 선생님. Other examples shown include 경은 언니 (used by younger females to address an older female) or 진석진 고객님 (used in customer service contexts). These formal titles are safe to use with strangers or colleagues.

---

### Calling Someone's Name: The Vocative Markers -아 and -야

When you are trying to get someone's attention or calling out to them directly in a casual context, you must use a vocative marker. The choice between 아 and 야 depends entirely on the final sound of the name. If a name ends in a vowel (no 받침), you add 야. If a name ends in a consonant (has a 받침), you add 아.

The instructor provides several examples using the names of TTMIK teachers:
- **현우** ends in a vowel, so it becomes **현우야**.
- **경화** ends in a vowel, so it becomes **경화야**.
- **경은** ends in a consonant, so it becomes **경은아**.
- **석진** ends in a consonant, so it becomes **석진아**.

The instructor notes that a mother would likely address her son as 석진아 rather than just 석진. This usage signals a close, informal relationship where 반말 is appropriate.

---

### Talking About Someone: The Suffix -이

A separate rule applies when you are talking about a person as the subject or object of a sentence, rather than addressing them directly. In casual speech, if a person's name ends in a consonant, the suffix 이 is attached to the name before any other grammar particles are added. If the name ends in a vowel, nothing is added.

The instructor demonstrates this with a list:
- **현우** (vowel) stays **현우**.
- **경은** (consonant) becomes **경은이**.
- **주연** (consonant) becomes **주연이**.
- **경화** (vowel) stays **경화**.

Once this 이 is attached (if necessary), you then add standard particles like 는/은 (topic), 가/이 (subject), 를/을 (object), 한테 (to/from), or 하고 (with). For example, you would say 주연이한테 (to Juyeon) or 경은이가 (Gyeongeun [subject]). The instructor emphasizes that saying just 주연한테 or 경은가 sounds very unnatural in a casual context.

Interestingly, these "casual" name forms can appear in sentences that otherwise use polite endings (존댓말). For instance, saying "현우가 할 거예요" (Hyunwoo will do it) or "경은이가 할 거예요" (Gyeongeun will do it) indicates that the speaker uses casual language with Hyunwoo or Gyeongeun personally, even if they are speaking politely to the current listener.

---

### Exceptions and Nuances with Foreign Names

The rules for foreign names are more flexible and depend on personal preference. When calling for someone with a foreign name like James (제임스), a speaker might simply say "제임스!" or add the casual marker to say "제임스야!" Adding the marker makes the address feel significantly more casual and "Koreanized."

The instructor also points out a specific nuance regarding the suffix 이가. While the rule states that 이 is added to consonant-ending names, adding both 이 and the subject marker 가 to a foreign name (e.g., 로렌이가 할 거예요) can sometimes make the person sound much younger or like a child. This "double marker" effect is occasionally used when talking down to someone or referring to small children.

---

### Common Misconception: -이 vs. Subject Marker -이

The instructor concludes by warning beginners not to confuse the name suffix 이 with the subject marker 이. Although they look and sound identical, they serve different functions. The name suffix 이 is an attachment used to make the name function naturally within a casual sentence, whereas the subject marker 이 is a functional particle that identifies the subject of a verb.
