### Overview

The instructor, Billy, is joined by guest Minji from "Minji Teaches Korean" to discuss the evolving usage of the Korean honorific suffixes 님 and 씨. Billy notes that when he began learning Korean in 2005, 씨 was the standard polite suffix used after names, while 님 was largely reserved for specific titles. However, linguistic trends have shifted significantly. Today, 님 has become the dominant, safest way to address others politely in a wide variety of contexts, including online interactions and service industries, whereas 씨 has taken on a more nuanced role that can sometimes imply a lower social standing or a lack of full respect if used incorrectly. The lesson aims to clarify these distinctions to help learners navigate social hierarchies and avoid accidental rudeness.

---

### The Traditional and Modern Use of 님

Historically, 님 was primarily attached to professional titles or social roles to show high respect. Common examples include 선생님 (teacher), 사장님 (CEO/boss), 대표님 (representative/CEO), 부장님 (department head), 대리님 (assistant manager), and 교수님 (professor). Minji explains that these titles are used based on hierarchy; for instance, a student must call their professor 교수님, but two professors of equal rank might refer to each other as 교수 without the suffix, or use 김교수 (Professor Kim). 

A critical rule mentioned is that you should never use 님 when referring to yourself. Saying 저도 교수님이에요 (I am also a professor) is grammatically and socially awkward because you are effectively granting yourself an honorific title. The only major exception to the title rule is 선생님, which is used so universally that it is often applied to oneself or to strangers regardless of their actual profession.

In the modern era, 님 has expanded beyond titles to be used directly after names or usernames. This is especially prevalent online. Minji notes that addressing someone by just their username without 님 is considered aggressive or rude, similar to how omitting the suffix in a game like Overwatch can feel like an insult. In physical settings like hospitals, staff will almost always call patients using their full name plus 님, such as 김민지 님.

---

### Understanding the Nuances of 씨

While 씨 is still a polite suffix, its perceived level of respect has lowered compared to 님. It is now most commonly used by someone in a slightly higher or equal position to address someone younger or lower in rank politely. For example, a manager in their 30s might address a new employee as 민지 씨. It signals a "polite but not extra polite" vibe.

The instructors emphasize that 씨 should only be used with names, never with titles. While you can say 교수님, saying 교수 씨 or 사장 씨 is considered highly offensive and rude. Furthermore, using 씨 to address a teacher or someone significantly older is inappropriate. Billy shares that while he understands when students call him 빌리 씨, it is technically incorrect and should be avoided in favor of 빌리 쌤 or 빌리 선생님.

---

### Social Contexts and Safety Rules

When deciding between the two, 님 is generally the "safe" choice for most polite interactions. If you are meeting someone for the first time or are in a professional setting (사회 친구), using 님 is the best way to ensure you aren't being rude. 씨 is acceptable among people of a similar age (또래), but even then, 님 is becoming more common to show mutual respect.

The transition to casual speech (반말) usually requires a mutual agreement. Until that agreement is made, suffixes should be maintained. Minji explains that once a way of speaking is established with someone, it tends to stick. Therefore, if you start a relationship using 님, you will likely continue using it unless the relationship becomes significantly closer, such as becoming best friends or starting to date.
