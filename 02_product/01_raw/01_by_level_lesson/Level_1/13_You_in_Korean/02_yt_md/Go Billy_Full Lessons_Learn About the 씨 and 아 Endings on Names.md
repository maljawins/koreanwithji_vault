### The Polite Suffix 씨
The instructor explains that `씨` functions similarly to English titles like Mr., Mrs., or Miss. In Korean social dynamics, it is generally considered rude to call someone by their name alone unless you are very close friends. For example, if a person is named `철수`, calling him just `철수` is only appropriate among intimate friends. In more formal or less familiar settings, attaching `씨` to the end of the name makes the address polite.

The instructor demonstrates that `씨` can be attached to either a full name or just the first name. One might say `김 씨` to address someone by their surname in a polite manner, or `철수 씨` to address them by their given name. While it translates to Mr. or Mrs., its primary function is to signal a baseline level of respect and social distance.

---

### Casual Vocative Markers: 아 and 야
The second part of the linguistic explanation covers how to call out to friends in casual Korean. Unlike English, where one simply says the name (e.g., "Hey, Jimin"), Korean requires a vocative suffix. The choice between `아` and `야` depends entirely on whether the name ends in a consonant or a vowel.

If a name ends in a consonant (a `받침`), the suffix `아` is used. The instructor provides the example of the name `지민`, which ends in the consonant `ㄴ`. To call out to this person, one must say `지민아`. Conversely, if a name ends in a vowel, the suffix `야` is used. Using the name `영희` as an example, since it ends in a vowel, the correct form is `영희야`. Both suffixes carry the same meaning and are strictly reserved for casual, non-honorific speech among friends or toward those younger than the speaker.
