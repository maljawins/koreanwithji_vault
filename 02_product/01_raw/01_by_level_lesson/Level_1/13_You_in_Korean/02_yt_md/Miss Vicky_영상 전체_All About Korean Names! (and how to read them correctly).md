### Structure of Korean Names

The fundamental rule of Korean names is the order: **Family Name (성) + Given Name (이름)**. The term 이름 can refer specifically to the given name or to the full name as a whole. Most Koreans have a three-syllable name consisting of a one-syllable family name and a two-syllable given name, such as the instructor's name, 심효정 (Shim Hyo-jung), where 심 is the family name and 효정 is the given name.

However, variations exist. Some people have two-syllable names, such as 김솔 (Kim Sol), where both the family name and given name are one syllable each. Four-syllable names are less common but occur in two ways. First, a one-syllable family name followed by a three-syllable given name, like the actress 황우슬해 (Hwang Woo-seul-hye). Second, a two-syllable family name followed by a two-syllable given name. Common two-syllable family names include 남궁 (Namgung), 선우 (Seonwoo), 제갈 (Jegal), and 황보 (Hwangbo).

This can lead to ambiguity. For a name like 황보영, there are two possibilities: the family name is 황 and the given name is 보영, or the family name is 황보 and the given name is 영. Because two-syllable family names are rare, it is socially acceptable to politely ask for clarification.

### Pronunciation and Romanization

The instructor highlights that standard English romanization often misrepresents actual Korean pronunciation. For the family name 김 (Kim), the initial sound is closer to a "G" than a "K," making it sound like `Gim`. The family name 이 (Lee) is pronounced simply as `Ee`, similar to the sound in "Hee." The name 박 (Park) should be read as `Bak`, without the "r" sound present in the English spelling.

Specific phonetic shifts also occur within given names for ease of pronunciation. In the name 박신혜 (Park Shin-hye), the character 혜 contains the vowel sound `ye` (예), but it is actually pronounced as `e` (에). Therefore, it is read as `Shin-he` rather than `Shin-hye`. This same rule applies to the common name 지혜 (Jihye), which means "wisdom" and is pronounced `Ji-he`.

---

### Calling Someone by Name: 아 and 야

In English, you can address someone by their first name directly (e.g., "Jennifer, help me"). In Korean, while not strictly wrong to use just the name, it is standard and friendlier to add a vocative particle: **아** or **야**. The choice depends on whether the name ends in a final consonant (받침).

- If the name ends in a **받침**, add **아**: `효정 (Hyo-jung) → 효정아`, `남준 (Nam-joon) → 남준아`, `지은 (Ji-eun) → 지은아`.
- If the name ends in a **vowel** (no 받침), add **야**: `신혜 (Shin-hye) → 신혜야`, `지혜 (Ji-hye) → 지혜야`, `윤기 (Yun-gi) → 윤기야`, `지수 (Ji-soo) → 지수야`.

These particles indicate friendliness and are used among friends or with people younger than the speaker.

---

### Social Etiquette of Full Names

Using a person's full name to address them is common in Korea, but it is strictly reserved for people who are extremely close. Calling someone by their full name, such as `심효정, 가자!` (Shim Hyo-jung, let's go!), is acceptable between best friends. However, doing this to a stranger or someone you just met is considered very rude and socially awkward. When using the full name to call someone, the particles 아 and 야 are not added.
