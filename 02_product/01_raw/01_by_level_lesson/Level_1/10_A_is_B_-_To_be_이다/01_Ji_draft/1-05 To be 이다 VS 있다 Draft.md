**A is B**

In this lesson, we’re going to learn about “이다” - “To be”.

Before we get started, I’m going to need you to start thinking of the concept of “Be” in a different light because the word “Be” in English is very versatile.

Let’s look at these sentences

I am a man – 이다 (A is B)

저는 남자예요

I am (at) home – 있다 (be, exist)

저는 집에 있어요

I am kind – 친절하다 (To be kind)

저는 친절해요

As you can see, “am” in each sentence has completely different functions.

And Korean expresses these three meanings with different words.

And what we need here is the first one is **이다**.

**A is B – 이다 Conjugation**

이다 is a unique verb, which means it doesn’t follow the “regular” conjugation rules.

So, this is something you have to memorize.

**이다 Conjugation**

이다 is 받침-sensitive, which means the way it conjugates *changes* depending on whether the noun being described ends in a 받침 or not.

Here’s how to say “I am a student” in 3 different speech levels.

Casual: 나는 학생이야.

Polite: 저는 학생이에요.

Formal: 저는 학생입니다.

As you can see, 학생 meaning student ends in a 받침. So it’s 이야 in 반말, 이에요 in polite speech, and 입니다 in formal speech.

Both ‘나’ and ‘저’ mean “I” but “저” is the humble, polite version. So, ‘저’ goes hand in hand with 존댓말 instead of ‘나’.

는 is called the "topic marker." It marks who or what the sentence is about.

We also have 은 for words that end in a 받침—like 이 사람은, meaning "this person."

Note that there's absolutely no way to translate this because it simply doesn't exist in English.

There are other particles you can add after the subject of the sentence, and we're going to learn about them very soon.

For now, just know that 은 and 는 are the most commonly used particles when introducing yourself.

And let’s look at when there’s no 받침.

“I am a singer”

나는 가수**야**저는 가수**예요**저는 가수**입니다.**

이다 becomes 야 in casual speech, 예요 in polite speech and 입니다 in formal speech.

But in Koreans actually say these like this

1. We say 에요 instead of 예요
- 에요 is easier to say than 예요 right? So we’d say 가수에요 not 가수예요.

2. Secondly, instead of 가수입니다, we’d say 가숩니다

This makes it flow better. But you’d have to use the correct spelling in writing.

**This and That**

Now let’s learn how to say **“this”** and **“that”** in Korean to say **"This is A. That is B."**

In English, **“this”** and **“that”** can be both **nouns and adjectives**. However, in Korean, they are **always adjectives**.

So, if you **must** add a noun **‘것’ (thing) or 거 -the shortened version. And we almost always just say “거” in spoken Korean rather than “것”.**

So we have 이거 저거 and 그거

이 means "this." Anything close to the speaker, you'd say 이거. You can also say 요거, but this only happens in speech, not writing. Fun fact - 요거 comes from the 전라 regional dialect, but now it's used everywhere in the country.

And we have 2 version for “that”.

저 is for something you can see but is far away from both the speaker and the listener like “that tree over there” - “저 나무” If the tree is really far away, you could say “쩌어~ 나무”.

And 그 is used in 2 cases.

First, we use it for anything that’s not close enough to be called “this” like in this picture (show a picture of a staff holding a product).

And second, when you’re talking about something that is not in your sight like “that guy I met yesterday” You could also say “고거”. And just like “요거”, this is only used in informal situations.

**Here and There**

I want you to pay attention to the consonants of these words because the same logic applies to “here and there”.

So, here is 여기, Over there is 저기 and 거기 would be just “there”.

This is a very important concept to remember so make sure to make a note of this.

**This and That**

Back to This and that. We just learned that 것 can shortened to 거.

And when combined with the topic marker, **것은 becomes 거는 and 거는 becomes 건**.

Here are some examples:

**이것은 의자예요 / 이거는 의자예요 / 이건 의자예요**

**저것은 책상이에요 / 저거는 책상이에요 / 저건 책상이에요**

**그것은 공책이에요 / 그거는 공책이에요 / 그건 공책이에요**

**What is your name?**

Before we learn how to say “My name is”, let’s first learn “What is your name?”

We have two versions to say this in Korean.

The first one is “성함이 어떻게 되세요?” This is the politest way to ask someone their name.

And the second one is “이름이 뭐예요?” 이 표현은 두 가지 조건이 충족되지 않으면 다소 무례하게 느껴질 수도 있다. 첫번째는 처음 만나는 사람의 나이가 네 나이와 비슷해보일 때이다.

두번째는 만난 장소가 파티나 지인을 통해 누군가를 소개받는 자리와 같이 사적인 자리일 때이다.

So, you would never say this to someone like a 60 year old 아저씨 or your customer even if they look younger than you.

Why do they look completely different? That’s because the first one is an idiom which makes no sense in English when translated literally.

The second version though, is pretty straight forward. 이름 means name. And 뭐 means what. And we just learned 예요. Altogether, “The name what is?”.

Now, let’s look at the first phrase.

[Show 성함이 어떻게 되세요?]

성함 is the honorific version of 이름. And “어떻게 되세요?” literally means “How does it become?”

So, “how does the name become?” See, it makes no sense in English.

The best way to think of 어떻게 되세요 is to simply regard it as the polite form of "what is your [noun]...? So, yes, you’re going to have to learn this phrase as a whole chunk.

Here’s a few more examples

나이가 어떻게 되세요? – 나이 means age. So, this means “How old are you?”

In 반말 though, it would be “너(는) 몇 살이야?” By the way, do you see the irony here? Asking for someone’s age in 반말?

I mean, age is the most important information to speak 반말 in the first place.

그래서 누가 봐도 너보다 어린 꼬마 아이들을 빼고는 네가 누군가에게 반말로 나이를 묻는 일은 없을 것이다.

직업이 어떻게 되세요? – 직업 is job or profession. So, this one means “What do you do for a living?”

In 반말, you’d say “너(는) 직업이 뭐야?”

**My name is**

Now, here’s how you say “My name is” Note you’d never use 성함 to refer to your own name. It’s always 이름.

Polite – 제 이름은 Name 이에요 / 예요.

Casual – 내 이름은 Name 이야 / 야.

Formal – 제 이름은 Name 입니다.
