## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the distinction between the different ways to express "to be." In English, the single verb "to be" covers identity, equality, and location. In Korean, these concepts are split between two distinct verb pairs: 이다/아니다 for identity and equality, and 있다/없다 for existence and possession. The lesson breaks down the grammatical rules, particle requirements, and semantic nuances for each pair, providing clear examples of how they function in both standard and casual speech.

---

### Identity and Equality: 이다 and 아니다

The verb 이다 means "to be" in the sense of "to be equal to." It is used when you are identifying what something is. For example, if you want to say "That is a cat," you use 이다 because you are stating that "that" is equal to the category of "cat." The instructor provides the sentence 그것은 고양이에요 (That is a cat) to illustrate this. A key grammatical point is that 이다 does not require a subject marker (이/가) on the noun it follows.

The opposite of 이다 is 아니다, which means "to not be" or "to not be equal to." Unlike 이다, 아니다 requires the use of the subject marker 이 or 가 to mark the noun that is being negated. The instructor demonstrates this with the sentence 그것은 고양이가 아니에요 (That is not a cat), highlighting the inclusion of the marker 가. In casual or slang speech, this marker is sometimes omitted, as seen in the example 난 바보 아니야 (I'm not stupid), where 난 is a contraction of 나는. Another formal example provided is 저는 좋은 학생입니다 (I am a good student).

---

### Existence and Possession: 있다 and 없다

The verb 있다 means "to exist." While it often translates to "is" or "are" in English, its core meaning is "there is" or "something exists." This is the primary source of confusion with 이다, as both can translate to "is," but 있다 never means "is equal to." Instead, it is used to describe the location of something or the fact that something exists in a certain place. For example, 그는 학교에 있다 (He is at school) uses 있다 because it describes his existence at the location of the school (marked by the location particle 에). Using 이다 here would incorrectly imply that the person is equal to the school.

있다 and its opposite 없다 (to not exist/there is not) are also the standard way to express "to have" and "to not have" in Korean. The instructor explains that a sentence like 저는 돈이 많이 있어요 literally translates to "As for me, there is a lot of money," but it is the natural way to say "I have a lot of money." The adverb 많이 (a lot) is used here to modify the quantity. Conversely, 저는 돈이 많이 없어요 means "I do not have a lot of money." When using 있다 and 없다, the item that exists or is possessed is typically marked with the subject marker 이 or 가.

---

### Advanced Usage and Vocabulary

The instructor introduces more complex sentences to show these verbs in context. To express that something is completely lacking, the phrase 한 벌도 없어요 (not even one article) is used in the sentence 깨끗한 옷이 한 벌도 없어요 (I don't have even one clean article of clothing). This example introduces the descriptive verb 깨끗하다 (to be clean), the noun 옷 (clothes/article of clothing), and the counter 벌, which is used specifically for counting items of clothing.

The verb 있다 can also be transformed into an adjective form, 있는, to describe a person or thing. This is shown in the sentence 그는 항상 학교에 있는 사람이에요 (He is a person who is always at school). Here, 항상 (always) is an adverb modifying the state of being at school. Finally, the instructor shows how 있다 is used for scheduled events with the example 일주일 뒤에 시험이 있어요 (There is a test after one week), introducing the vocabulary 일주일 (one week), 뒤에 (after), and 시험 (test).
