### Cultural Etiquette: Bowing and Handshaking

Before speaking, a proper introduction in Korea begins with a bow. The instructor explains that bowing is a sign of respect used when meeting someone for the first time or after a long period of absence. The depth and duration of the bow are proportional to the level of respect shown. In formal or business settings, a 45-degree bow is standard. Handshaking is also acceptable in these formal contexts, but it follows specific rules: one should use both hands, typically by placing the left palm under the right elbow while shaking with the right hand. For extra respect, such as meeting a celebrity, one can use two full hands. In everyday polite interactions, a slight head bow is sufficient, while close friends do not need to bow at all and can simply wave or say hi.

---

### Sentence Structure: "I Am [Name]"

To state your name politely, the instructor introduces the formula: **저는 [Name] 이에요/예요.** This structure consists of three parts. First, **저는** comes from **저 (I/me)** and the topic marker **는**, literally meaning "as for me." Second is the person's name. Third is the polite ending **이에요** or **예요**, which both come from the verb **이다 (to be)**. This verb functions like an equal sign, essentially saying "I equal [Name]."

The choice between the two endings depends on the final sound of the name:
- Use **이에요** if the name ends in a consonant (e.g., **석진이에요**).
- Use **예요** if the name ends in a vowel (e.g., **빌리예요**).

The instructor notes that while **예요** is the correct spelling after a vowel, it is often pronounced simply as **에요**. Additionally, while casual versions like **나는** exist, they are discouraged for first-time meetings to avoid being accidentally rude.

---

### Expressing "Nice to Meet You"

The instructor teaches two main ways to say "Nice to meet you," both derived from the verb **반갑다 (to be happy to see someone)**. For people of a similar age or in casual atmospheres, **반가워요** is appropriate. In more formal situations or when showing higher respect, **반갑습니다** is used. These phrases are versatile; they are used both when meeting someone for the first time and when seeing someone again after a long time, effectively meaning "Nice to see you" as well.

---

### Responding and Basic Affirmations

When someone says "Nice to meet you," the natural Korean response is to acknowledge it with "Yes, nice to meet you too." This is done by adding **네 (yes)** to the beginning of the phrase: **네, 반갑습니다** or **네, 반가워요**. The instructor explains that **네** is not just a literal "yes" but also functions as an acknowledgment like "all right" or "okay." To provide a complete set of basic responses, the instructor also introduces the opposite of **네**, which is **아니요 (no)**.

---

### Advanced Notes and Variations

The lesson concludes with several "special notes" for more natural speech. First, **저는** is frequently shortened to **전** in spoken Korean (e.g., **전 빌리예요**). Second, the word **만나서 (meeting/having met)** can be added before the greeting to form **만나서 반가워요**, though this is only used for the very first meeting. Third, a very formal but less common expression is **처음 뵙겠습니다**. Finally, the instructor introduces an alternative way to state one's name: **제 이름은 [Name] 이에요/예요**, which literally means "My name is [Name]."
