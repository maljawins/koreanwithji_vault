### Refresher: The Verb 이다 (To Be)
Before diving into demonstratives, the instructor reviews the copula 이다 (to be). In polite spoken Korean, this verb takes two forms: 이에요 and 예요. The choice depends on the preceding sound: use 이에요 after a consonant and 예요 after a vowel. Note that while written as 예요, it is often pronounced as 에요.

The instructor explains that 이다 functions like an "equals sign" in a sentence. For example, in the sentence 저는 빌리예요 (I am Billy), the structure implies "I = Billy." This understanding is crucial because when saying "This is a movie," the Korean sentence structure will literally be "This thing = movie."

---

### Demonstrative Adjectives: 이, 그, and 저
Korean uses three distinct words to express "this" and "that" as adjectives. These words must always be followed by a noun and a space.

**이 (This)** is used for objects close to the speaker. Examples include:
- **이 영화** / this movie
- **이 티비** / this television
- **이 음악** / this music

**그 (That)** is used for objects close to the listener, but far from the speaker. Examples include:
- **그 영화** / that movie
- **그 티비** / that television
- **그 음악** / that music

**저 (That over there)** is used for objects far from both the speaker and the listener. It is important not to confuse this adjective 저 with the pronoun 저 (I). Examples include:
- **저 영화** / that movie (over there)
- **저 티비** / that television (over there)
- **저 음악** / that music (over there)

---

### Demonstrative Nouns: 이것, 그것, and 저것
When you want to use "this" or "that" as a standalone noun (e.g., "This is a movie" or "I want that"), you combine the adjectives above with the word 것, which means "thing."

- **이것** (This thing): Close to the speaker.
- **그것** (That thing): Close to the listener.
- **저것** (That thing over there): Far from both.

Unlike the adjectives 이, 그, and 저, these are full nouns and can take particles like the topic marker (은/는) or the object marker (을/를). The instructor provides several examples of these nouns used with the topic marker:
- **이것은 영화예요.** / This (thing) is a movie.
- **그것은 티비예요.** / That (thing) is a television.
- **저것은 음악이에요.** / That (thing over there) is music.

---

### Conversation Practice: Pizza Cake
The instructor uses a roleplay between characters Billy and Kyeungeun to demonstrate these words in context. In the first scenario, Billy points to a cake near Kyeungeun and says:
> **저는 그 케이크를 갖고 싶어요.** / I want to have that cake (the one near you).

Kyeungeun, referring to the cake now close to her, replies:
> **이 케이크는 피자 케이크예요.** / This cake (near me) is a pizza cake.

In the second scenario, Billy points to a cake far away from both of them:
> **저는 저 케이크를 먹고 싶어요.** / I want to eat that cake (over there).

Kyeungeun confirms:
> **저 케이크는 피자 케이크예요.** / That cake (over there) is a pizza cake.

The same logic applies when using the noun forms. Billy might say **저는 그것을 갖고 싶어요** (I want to have that thing) and Kyeungeun would respond with **이것은 피자 케이크예요** (This thing is a pizza cake).

---

### Usage Notes and Spoken Contractions
The instructor emphasizes the grammatical difference: 이, 그, and 저 are adjectives and cannot stand alone with particles (e.g., "이는" is incorrect). They must always modify a noun. Conversely, 이것, 그것, and 저것 are nouns and can be used freely as subjects or objects.

In casual spoken Korean, the noun forms are frequently shortened. The final consonant 'ㅅ' is dropped, resulting in:
- **이것** → **이거**
- **그것** → **그거**
- **저것** → **저거**

While both the full and shortened versions are correct and widely used, learners will encounter the shortened versions more often in daily conversation.
