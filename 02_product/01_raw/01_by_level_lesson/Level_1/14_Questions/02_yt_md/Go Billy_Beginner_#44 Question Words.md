### Overview
The instructor introduces the essential Korean question words equivalent to "who, what, where, when, why, and how." While basic yes/no questions are formed by changing intonation, these question words allow for more detailed inquiries. The lesson covers the specific vocabulary for each question word, their grammatical categories (nouns vs. adverbs), and important morphological changes that occur when certain particles are attached.

The learning arc begins with individual word breakdowns, followed by a conversation practice to see the words in context. Finally, the instructor provides critical notes on sentence structure, specifically regarding word order for emphasis and the preference for subject markers over topic markers when using question words.

### Who: 누구 and 누가
The word for "who" is 누구, which functions as a noun. It can be used with the object marker 를 as in `누구를 만났어요? (Who did you meet?)`. However, a special rule applies when "who" is the subject of the sentence. Instead of saying 누구가, the word transforms into 누가. This is a mandatory contraction similar to how the first-person pronoun 저 becomes 제가 when followed by the subject marker 가. Examples of this subject form include `누가 요리했어요? (Who cooked?)` and `누가 한국어를 가르쳐요? (Who teaches Korean?)`.

---

### What: 무엇 and 뭐
The formal word for "what" is 무엇, which is primarily used in formal writing. In everyday speech, it is almost always shortened to 뭐. For example, `이게 뭐예요? (What is this?)` uses the shortened form. When "what" is used with the object marker 를, it can appear as 무엇을 (formal), 뭐를 (casual), or the most common spoken contraction 뭘. The instructor demonstrates this with `뭘 공부하고 싶어요? (What do you want to study?)`. Additionally, when combined with the verb 하다 (to do), it becomes `뭐 하다`, such as in the sentence `이제 뭐 할 거예요? (What will you do now?)`.

---

### Where and When: 어디 and 언제
Both "where" (어디) and "when" (언제) are nouns in Korean. For "where," the instructor notes that the location particle 에 is often optional in spoken Korean. One can say `어디에 갈 거예요?` or simply `어디 갈 거예요? (Where will you go?)`. It can also take the subject marker, as in `어디가 좋아요? (Where is good?)`. Similarly, 언제 is used to ask about time: `언제 만날 수 있어요? (When can we meet?)` or `언제가 좋아요? (When is good?)`. The instructor provides several examples including `언제 결혼할 거예요? (When will you get married?)` to show its versatility.

---

### Why and How: 왜 and 어떻게
Unlike the previous words, "why" (왜) and "how" (어떻게) function as adverbs. 왜 is straightforward and typically placed before the verb: `이걸 왜 먹었어요? (Why did you eat this?)` or `한국어를 왜 공부하세요? (Why do you study Korean?)`. 어떻게 is used to ask about the manner or method of an action. Examples include `그건 어떻게 달라요? (How is that different?)` and `서울역에 어떻게 가요? (How do I go to Seoul Station?)`.

---

### Word Order, Emphasis, and Markers
The instructor explains that while question words typically appear directly before the verb, they can be moved to the very beginning of a sentence to add emphasis. Comparing `피자를 언제 먹었어요?` and `언제 피자를 먹었어요?`, both mean "When did you eat the pizza?", but the latter emphasizes the "when." 

A crucial grammatical rule is that question word nouns (who, what, where, when) typically do not take the topic marker (은/는). Instead, the subject marker (이/가) is used. For instance, to ask "What is expensive?", one must say `뭐가 비싸요?` rather than 뭐는 비싸요. This is because the question word represents unknown information, which is the functional domain of the subject marker rather than the topic marker.
