## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the distinction between the question words 무슨, 어느, and 어떤. While all three can translate to "what" or "which" in English, they function as adjectives that modify a following noun and carry specific nuances regarding the nature of the question being asked. The lesson moves from basic "what" questions using the noun 뭐 to more specific inquiries that require these adjective forms.

The learning arc begins with a review of basic question structures and then systematically breaks down each word. 무슨 is introduced for general "what" questions about identity; 어느 is presented for "which" questions involving choices or unspecified entities; and 어떤 is explained as a word for "what kind of" traits, though it often overlaps with 어느 in spoken Korean. The video concludes with a comprehensive quiz to test the learner's ability to choose the correct word based on context.

### From 뭐 to 무슨
The instructor starts by reviewing the basic word for "what," which is 뭐. This is used as a noun in simple questions like 뭐 읽고 있어요? (What are you reading?) or 뭐 좋아해요? (What do you like?). However, when the question becomes more specific and includes a noun—such as "What book are you reading?"—using 뭐 책 is grammatically incorrect. Instead, the adjective form 무슨 must be used to modify the noun.

무슨 is used when asking about the identity or name of something. For example, 무슨 책 읽어? (What book are you reading?) is asking for the specific title. Other common uses include 무슨 음식 (what food), 무슨 영화 (what movie), and 무슨 노래 (what song). The instructor notes that 무슨 is also used for specific time-related questions like 무슨 요일 (what day of the week) and idiomatic expressions like 무슨 사이 (what kind of relationship/relation).

---

### 어느: Choosing from Options
어느 is primarily used to mean "which" when there is a limited or specific set of choices. The instructor demonstrates this with the phrase 어느 거 (which one). If you are asking someone to choose between specific items, you would ask 어느 거 먹을래? (Which one do you want to eat?) or 어느 책 읽을래? (Which book do you want to read?).

A specific rule mentioned is that 어느 is the standard word for asking about nationality: 어느 나라 사람이에요? (Which country person are you / Where are you from?). Additionally, 어느 can refer to an unspecified or unidentified person or day, similar to "a certain" or "some" in English. For example, 어느 날 (one day/a certain day) or 어느 여자 (some woman I don't know).

---

### 어떤: Traits and Spoken Nuance
어떤 is perhaps the most versatile and confusing of the three. Its primary meaning is "what kind of," asking about the traits, characteristics, or personality of a subject. While 무슨 책 asks for a title, 어떤 책 asks about the genre or quality (e.g., "a touching book" or "a novel"). When asking about a person's character, 어떤 사람이에요? (What kind of person is he?) is the correct form.

The instructor highlights that in modern spoken Korean, 어떤 has largely begun to overlap with 어느. It can mean "which one" (어떤 거) and can also refer to unspecified entities (어떤 남자 - some guy). In many casual contexts, 어떤 is actually more natural and common than 어느, which can sound slightly formal.

---

### Summary and Comparison
The instructor provides a visual summary to help organize the rules:
- **무슨:** "What" (adjective); asks for the name or identity of a noun.
- **어느:** "Which"; used for choices or unspecified/unidentified things.
- **어떤:** "What kind of" (traits/personality); also used interchangeably with 어느 for "which" or "unspecified" in spoken Korean.

To illustrate the difference between 무슨 and 어떤, the instructor compares two questions: 무슨 영화 좋아해요? (What movie do you like? - asking for a title) versus 어떤 영화 좋아해요? (What kind of movie do you like? - asking for a genre like action or comedy).
