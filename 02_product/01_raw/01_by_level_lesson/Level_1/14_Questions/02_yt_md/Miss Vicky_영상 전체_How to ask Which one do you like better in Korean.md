### The Basic Choice Pattern: Noun 이/가 좋아요
The simplest and most common way to ask someone to choose between two things is to repeat the phrase `좋아요 (is good/to like)` after each noun. The basic structure shown on screen is `Noun 이/가 좋아요, noun 이/가 좋아요?`. While `좋아요` literally means "is good," in this context, it functions as "do you like."

The instructor provides the classic example:
> **엄마가 좋아, 아빠가 좋아?** / Who do you like more, mom or dad?

In this example, the instructor uses the informal `반말` form `좋아`. For the polite `존댓말` form, one simply adds the suffix `요`. The choice of subject marker `이` or `가` depends on whether the preceding noun ends in a final consonant (받침). For example, `고양이가 좋아 (Do you like cats)` uses `가` because `고양이` ends in a vowel, whereas `매운 음식이 좋아 (Do you like spicy food)` uses `이` because `음식` ends in a consonant.

---

### Adding Nuance with 더 and 아니면
To make the question more specific or natural, speakers often add the adverb `더 (more)` or the conjunction `아니면 (or/if not)`. Adding `더` emphasizes the comparative aspect of the question: `고양이가 더 좋아요, 강아지가 더 좋아요? (Do you like cats more or dogs more?)`.

The word `아니면` can be placed between the two choices to clearly separate the options. The instructor demonstrates that these can be used individually or combined into a single sentence:
- **Using 더:** `매운 음식이 더 좋아요, 단 음식이 더 좋아요?`
- **Using 아니면:** `매운 음식이 좋아요 아니면 단 음식이 좋아요?`
- **Using both:** `매운 음식이 더 좋아요 아니면 단 음식이 더 좋아요?`

---

### Advanced Structures and Different Verbs
The choice pattern is not limited to the verb `좋다`. It can be applied to any verb or adjective to ask "Which one is more [adjective]?" or "Which one do you [verb] more?". For example, using the adjective `어렵다 (to be difficult)`, one can ask `한국어가 어려워요, 영어가 어려워요? (Which is more difficult, Korean or English?)`.

A more advanced and highly natural structure introduced is `Noun(이)랑 noun 중에 뭐가 더 [Verb/Adj]?`. This translates to "Between Noun and Noun, which one is more [Verb/Adj]?".
> **한국어랑 영어 중에 뭐가 더 어려워요?** / Between Korean and English, which one is more difficult?

The instructor also applies this to the verb `잘하다 (to be good at/to do well)`. To ask which language someone speaks better, the structure becomes `한국어를 더 잘해요, 영어를 더 잘해요?` or the combined form `한국어랑 영어 중에 뭘 더 잘해요?`. Note that when using `잘하다`, the object markers `을/를` are used instead of the subject markers `이/가`.

---

### Natural Ways to Answer
The lesson covers several ways to respond to these choice questions beyond just picking one option.
- **Choosing both:** `둘 다 좋아요 (I like both)`.
- **Choosing both equally:** `둘 다 똑같이 좋아해요`. The word `똑같다` means to be the same, and `똑같이` means equally.
- **Choosing one with a slight preference:** `둘 다 좋아요. 근데 고양이가 조금 더 좋아요 (I like both, but I like cats a little more)`.
- **Choosing neither:** `아무것도 안 [Verb]`. For example, `아무것도 안 마실래요 (I don't want to drink anything/neither)`.

---

### Common Mistake: (이)나 vs. 거나
A major point of confusion for learners is trying to use `(이)나` or `-거나` (both meaning "or") in these choice questions. The instructor explicitly states that you cannot use these in questions asking someone to make a single choice. Saying `엄마나 아빠가 좋아요?` sounds awkward and does not mean "Do you like mom or dad better?".

Instead, `(이)나` (for nouns) and `-거나` (for verb/adjective stems) are used when listing potential choices or possibilities where a final decision hasn't been made or doesn't matter.
- **Noun (이)나:** `저는 재즈나 힙합을 좋아해요 (I like [things like] jazz or hip-hop)`.
- **Verb -거나:** `음악을 듣거나 자요 (I listen to music or sleep [when I'm free])`.

In these cases, the speaker is providing a list of examples rather than asking for a specific selection between two distinct items.
