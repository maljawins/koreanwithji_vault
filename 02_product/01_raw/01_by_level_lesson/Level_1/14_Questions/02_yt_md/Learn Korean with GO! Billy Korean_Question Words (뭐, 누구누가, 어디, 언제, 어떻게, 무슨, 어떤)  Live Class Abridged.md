### Who: 누구 and 누가
The word for "who" is `누구`. When "who" is the object of a sentence, it is used with the object marker as `누구를`. For example, `누구를 좋아해요? (Who do you like?)` asks about the person receiving the action of liking. However, a critical grammatical rule applies when "who" is the subject of the sentence. While one might expect `누구` + `가` (the subject marker), the form `누구가` is never used. Instead, it always shortens to `누가`.

The instructor illustrates the difference in meaning between these forms. `누구를 만났어요?` means "Who did you meet?" where the "who" is the person being met. Conversely, `누가 만났어요?` means "Who met (someone)?" where the "who" is the person performing the action of meeting. While `누구` can be used with other particles like `누구에게 (to whom)`, `누구한테 (to whom)`, or `누구랑 (with whom)`, the subject form must always be `누가`.

---

### What: 무엇 and 뭐
The formal, standard word for "what" is `무엇`, but in nearly all common speech, it is shortened to `뭐`. These are identical in meaning. When used with particles, they become `뭐를` (object) or `뭐가` (subject). A specific spacing rule is noted: when `뭐` is used with the verb `하다 (to do)`, it is often written without a space as `뭐 하다 (to do what)`. Examples include `뭐 하고 있어요? (What are you doing?)` and `뭐 좋아해요? (What do you like?)`.

---

### Where, When, and Why: 어디, 언제, 왜
`어디` means "where." It can be used alone or with the location particle as `어디에`. The instructor notes that `어디 가고 싶어요?` and `어디에 가고 싶어요?` both mean "Where do you want to go?" though `어디` is more common in casual speech. `언제` means "when," used in sentences like `언제 가요? (When are you going?)` or `언제 갈까요? (When should we go?)`.

`왜` means "why" and functions as an adverb. An example provided is `왜 안 갔어요? (Why didn't you go?)`. Interestingly, `왜` can sometimes translate to "what" in English contexts. If someone is staring at you, a Korean might ask `왜요?` or `저를 왜 봐요?`. While this literally means "Why (are you looking)?", the functional English equivalent is "What? / What are you looking at?". Using `뭐` in this specific context would sound overly aggressive, like "What's your problem?".

---

### How: 어떻게 vs 어떡해
The word for "how" is `어떻게`, which functions as an adverb. It is important to distinguish this from the verb `어떡해`, which is a contraction of `어떻게 해`. While they sound similar, `어떡해` means "What do I do?" or "What should we do?" and is used as an exclamation when facing a problem.

`어떻게` literally means "how," as in `한국어를 어떻게 배웠어요? (How did you learn Korean?)`. However, it translates to "what" in two specific instances:
1. **Opinions:** When asking "What do you think?", Koreans say "How do you think?" using `어떻게 생각해요?`. To ask about a specific topic, use the structure `[Noun]에 대해(서) 어떻게 생각하세요? (What do you think about [Noun]?)`.
2. **Structural Makeup:** When used with the verb `되다`, `어떻게` asks for the "makeup" or details of a noun. This is the polite way to ask for information:
   - `가족이 어떻게 되세요?` (What is your family [makeup]? / Tell me about your family.)
   - `성함이 어떻게 되세요?` (What is your name? - Polite)
   - `연세가 어떻게 되세요?` (What is your age? - Polite)
   - `취미가 어떻게 되세요?` (What are your hobbies?)

---

### Sentence Structure and Emphasis
Question words can appear either at the beginning of a sentence or directly before the verb. The difference is purely one of emphasis.
- **Option A:** `왜 피자를 좋아해요?` (Emphasis on "Why")
- **Option B:** `피자를 왜 좋아해요?` (Emphasis on "Pizza")
Both sentences mean "Why do you like pizza?", but placing the question word directly before the verb (Option B) is very common and sounds natural.

---

### Indefinite Pronouns: Someone, Something, Somewhere, Someday
By adding `~ㄴ가` to question words, they become indefinite pronouns:
- `누군가`: Someone
- `뭔가` (or `무엇인가`): Something
- `어딘가`: Somewhere
- `언젠가`: Someday

Examples include `누군가를 만날 거야? (Will you meet someone?)` and `언젠가 또 만나요 (Let's meet again someday)`. However, in casual speech, the base question words (`뭐, 누구, 어디, 언제`) are often used to mean these indefinite pronouns. The distinction is made through intonation:
- **Interrogative Meaning:** Emphasize the question word. `뭐 먹었어요?` (WHAT did you eat?)
- **Indefinite Meaning:** Emphasize the verb. `뭐 먹었어요?` (Did you eat SOMETHING?)
If a sentence is a statement rather than a question (e.g., `뭐 만들었어.`), it can only mean the indefinite version ("You made something") because "what" requires a question.

---

### Questioning Adjectives: 무슨 and 어떤
Both words translate to "what" but function as adjectives that must precede a noun.
- `무슨`: Used when asking "what [noun]" in a general sense or when choosing from options. `무슨 음식을 좋아해요? (What food do you like?)`
- `어떤`: Literally means "what kind of." It is derived from the adjective `어떻다 (to be how)`. `어떤 사람이에요? (What kind of person is he?)` or `어떤 음악을 좋아해요? (What kind of music do you like?)`. While `무슨` is common, `어떤` is often more natural when asking for a description or type.
