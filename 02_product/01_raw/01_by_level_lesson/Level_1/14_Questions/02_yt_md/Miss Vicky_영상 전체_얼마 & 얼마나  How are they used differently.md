### Asking for Price with 얼마

The word **얼마** is primarily used when asking about the price of something. To form a question, **얼마** is typically followed by the verb **이다 (to be)** or **하다 (to do)**. The most common polite form is **얼마예요? (How much is it?)**. The instructor notes that while **얼마예요?** is the standard, you will also frequently hear **얼마해요?** used in the same context.

When asking for the price of a specific item, the noun comes first. For example, to ask about two apples, you say **사과 두 개 얼마예요? (How much are two apples?)**. Other examples include **이 옷 얼마예요? (How much is this clothing?)** and **요즘 아이폰 얼마예요? (How much is an iPhone these days?)**. To ask for the total price of multiple items, you can use **전부 (all/everything)** or **다 (all)**, resulting in phrases like **이거 전부 얼마예요?** or **이거 다 얼마예요? (How much is all of this?)**.

---

### Expressing Degree with 얼마나 and 얼만큼

While **얼마** focuses on price, **얼마나** (which can be used interchangeably with **얼만큼**) asks about the degree or extent of an adjective or verb. When followed by an adjective, it translates to "how [adjective]." The instructor demonstrates this with several common adjectives:

- **얼마나 예뻐요?** / How pretty is it?
- **얼마나 비싸요?** / How expensive is it?
- **얼마나 커요?** / How big is it?
- **얼마나 작아요?** / How small is it?

A specific application is asking about height. In Korean, the phrase **키가 크다** literally means "height is big," so to ask how tall someone is, you say **키가 얼마나 커요?**. The instructor reminds viewers that height in Korea is measured in centimeters, not feet, and is expressed using Sino-Korean numbers, such as **백칠십 센티미터예요 (It is 170cm)**.

---

### Using 얼마나 with Verbs and Adverbs

When **얼마나** is used with a verb, it typically asks about the progress or duration of an action. In these cases, it often appears with past tense verbs. For example, **숙제 얼마나 했어요? (How much homework have you done?)** or **영화 얼마나 봤어요? (How much of the movie have you watched?)**.

The instructor explains that while **얼마나** can be followed directly by a verb, it is grammatically more accurate to place an adverb in between to specify whether you are asking about quantity or time.
- **많이 (a lot)**: **공부를 얼마나 많이 했어요?** (How much [quantity] did you study?)
- **오래 (for a long time)**: **공부를 얼마나 오래 했어요?** (How long [time] did you study?)

Without these adverbs, the meaning is usually determined by context. For instance, **남자친구랑 얼마나 만났어?** naturally asks how long a couple has been dating.

---

### Comparison and Degree Particles: 만큼 and 하나도

To answer "how much" questions, the instructor introduces the particle **만큼**, which means "as much as [noun]." This allows for comparative answers:
- **꽃만큼 예뻐요.** / You are as pretty as a flower.
- **저만큼 커요.** / He is as tall as me.
- **동남아시아만큼 더워요.** / It is as hot as Southeast Asia.

For negative responses, the instructor provides a scale of intensity:
1. **별로 안 [adjective/verb]**: Not that much. (**별로 안 더워요** / It's not that hot.)
2. **얼마 안 [verb]**: Haven't done much. (**얼마 안 했어요** / I haven't done much.)
3. **하나도 안 [adjective/verb]**: Not at all / Not even one. (**하나도 안 했어요** / I didn't do any of it; **하나도 안 귀여워** / It's not cute at all.)

---

### Summary of Response Expressions

The instructor highlights several useful expressions for answering questions about progress or degree:
- **다 했어요.** / I finished it all.
- **거의 다 했어요.** / I'm almost finished.
- **엄청 / 진짜 / 아주 / 정말**: Various ways to say "very" or "really." **엄청** is noted as a more slang-like, emphatic version of "very."
- **하늘만큼 땅만큼 사랑해요.** / I love you as much as the sky and the earth (a "cheesy" but classic expression equivalent to "to the moon and back").
