## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the distinction between 무슨, 어느, and 어떤. While all three can translate to "which" or "what" in English, they are not interchangeable. Each word carries a specific nuance regarding the speaker's knowledge of the options available and the type of information they are seeking.

The lesson is structured into two main parts. First, the instructor explains how these words function within questions, highlighting the differences between general inquiries, choices from a known set, and requests for descriptive qualities. Second, the video explores how these same words change meaning when used in non-question statements, such as in slang expressions, fairy tales, or when referring to unspecified "certain" things.

---

### 무슨 — Asking "What"
The word 무슨 is an adjective form of the noun 뭐 (what). Because it is an adjective, it must always be followed by a noun. It is primarily used to ask "what" in a general sense when the speaker has no specific options in mind. Common examples include:
- **무슨 책?** / What book?
- **무슨 색?** / What color?
- **무슨 말이에요?** / What are you talking about? (Literally: What words are those?)

While 무슨 can sometimes translate as "which" in English—such as when looking at a pile of pens and asking "What/Which pen can I pick?"—it fundamentally remains a "what" question. There are specific grammatical restrictions where 무슨 cannot be used before certain nouns: 것 (thing), 곳 (place), and 쪽 (direction). For these nouns, 어느 is required instead.

---

### 어느 — Choosing "Which"
어느 translates most accurately to "which." It is used when there is a limited set of options to choose from or when comparing specific items. The instructor notes that using 어느 often sounds more polite than 무슨 because it implies the speaker is aware of the available choices. For example, asking **어느 영화** (which movie) suggests there is a specific list of movies being considered, whereas **무슨 영화** (what movie) is a completely open-ended question.

Beyond its use as a question word, 어느 appears in fixed expressions related to quantity, such as **어느 정도** or **어느 만큼**, both meaning "how much" or "to what extent." The instructor recommends learning these as independent vocabulary items rather than trying to apply the "which" logic to them.

---

### 어떤 — Describing "What Kind Of"
어떤 is used to ask for details about the type, kind, or quality of a noun. It is derived from the descriptive verb 어떻다 (to be how), which is also the root of the common expression 어때요? (how is it?). When a speaker asks **어떤 영화**, they are not asking for a specific title or choosing from a list; they are asking for the genre or the nature of the movie, such as a comedy or a horror film.

---

### Usage in Non-Question Statements
When these words are used in sentences that are not questions, their meanings shift significantly.

무슨 can be used as a slang expression to indicate disbelief or to talk down about something. It functions similarly to the English phrase "my eye!" or "what the heck." A common usage is **무슨 소리야?**, which translates to "What the heck are you talking about?" or "What kind of nonsense is that?"

어느 and 어떤 both take on the meaning of "a certain" or "some" in statements. 어느 is more formal and is frequently found in written stories and fairy tales to introduce a setting or character without specific details. For example:
> **옛날 어느 작은 마을에...** / A long time ago, in a certain small village...

어떤 is more common in everyday speech and implies even less information than 어느. It suggests that the speaker doesn't know much about the subject they are mentioning. For instance, using **어떤 이상한 사람** (some weird person) implies the speaker encountered someone but has no further identifying information about them.
