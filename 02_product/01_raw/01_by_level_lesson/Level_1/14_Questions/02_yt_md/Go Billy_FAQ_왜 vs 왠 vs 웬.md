## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor addresses a common point of confusion for Korean learners: the difference between 왜, 왠, and 웬. While all three are related to the concept of "why" or "what" in certain contexts, they function as different parts of speech and carry distinct nuances. The lesson moves from the most basic and versatile term to more specific expressions used to convey uncertainty or surprise.

The core of the lesson focuses on distinguishing between the standard adverb 왜, the specific contraction 왠지, and the adjectival form 웬. By understanding the grammatical roles—adverb vs. adjective—and the emotional weight of each word, learners can avoid common spelling mistakes and sound more natural in casual conversation.

---

### 왜: The Standard Adverb for "Why"
The word 왜 is the most common and standard adverb used to ask "why." It is versatile and can be used in almost any general situation to seek a reason or cause. The instructor demonstrates this with a simple example: 피자를 왜 좋아해요? (Why do you like pizza?). On screen, the instructor circles 왜 within the sentence to emphasize its role as the primary word for "why" in general contexts.

---

### 왠 vs 왠지: Contraction and Uncertainty
The instructor clarifies a major spelling trap: the syllable 왠 is almost never used by itself. If you see 왠 written alone, it is likely a misspelling of 웬. The only correct and common usage of this spelling is in the word 왠지. 

The instructor explains the etymology of 왠지 through a visual breakdown on screen: 왜 (why) + 이다 (to be) + 지 (modifier indicating "whether" or "if"). Therefore, 왠지 is a shortening of 왜인지. It is used to express a sense of "for some reason" or "I don't know why." It typically appears in sentences alongside verbs of knowing or not knowing, such as 왠지 알아요 (I know why) or 왠지 몰라요 (I don't know why). This form specifically signals uncertainty.

---

### 웬: The Adjective of Surprise
Unlike 왜, the word 웬 functions as an adjective. This means it must always be placed before a noun. While it is often translated as "why" in English sentences, its literal meaning is closer to "what" or "what kind of." The instructor compares it to the word 무슨 (what/what kind of). For example, while 무슨 일이야? means "What's up?" or "What's the matter?", 웬 일이야? carries the same basic meaning but adds a layer of surprise or being taken aback.

The instructor highlights that 웬 is primarily used in casual, spoken Korean among friends and is not suitable for formal situations. A very common idiomatic expression shown is 웬 떡이야? (What rice cake is this?). This is used when something unexpectedly good happens, similar to saying "What a windfall!" or "What a pleasant surprise!"

---

### Using 웬 to Express "Why" with Emotion
The instructor provides further examples to show how 웬 replaces 왜 when the speaker wants to emphasize their surprise at a situation. In the sentence 밖에 웬 강아지가 이렇게 많아? (Why are there so many dogs outside?), 웬 acts as an adjective modifying "dogs." While the meaning is identical to using 왜, the use of 웬 signals that the speaker is shocked by the number of dogs.

Another example provided is 갑자기 웬 선물이야? (Why a present all of a sudden?). Again, this could be phrased with 왜, but using 웬 highlights the unexpected nature of receiving the gift. Visually, the instructor writes these sentences out, circling the nouns being modified to reinforce that 웬 is an adjective that changes the "feeling" of the sentence rather than just its literal meaning.
