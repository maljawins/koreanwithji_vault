### What: 무엇 and 뭐

The standard word for "what" is **무엇**. The instructor references a famous children's song that asks **무엇이 똑같을까? (What is the same?)** to illustrate its use. While **무엇** is the full form often found in written materials like books or articles, it is almost always shortened to **뭐** in daily conversation. Common examples include **뭐 해요? (What are you doing?)**, **이름이 뭐예요? (What is your name?)**, and **뭐 먹을래요? (What do you want to eat?)**.

---

### Who: 누구 and 누가

The word for "who" is **누구**. It is used in simple questions like **누구세요? (Who is it? / Who are you?)** or **누구 좋아해요? (Who do you like?)**. When "who" is the subject of the sentence, it must be combined with the subject marker **가**. The instructor explains that **누구** plus **가** becomes **누가**. Using "누구가" is grammatically incorrect. An example of this is **누가 했어요? (Who did it?)**. Additionally, the instructor notes that **누구랑 (with whom)** uses the particle **랑**, which is synonymous with **와** and **과**.

---

### Where: 어디

**어디** means "where." It is frequently used with the location particle **에서** to ask where an action takes place. Examples shown include **어디에서 일해요? (Where do you work?)** and **어디에서 샀어요? (Where did you buy it?)**. The instructor mentions that in certain contexts, especially with verbs of movement or residence like **가요 (to go)** or **살아요 (to live)**, the particles **에** or **에서** can be omitted in casual speech, resulting in phrases like **어디 살아요? (Where do you live?)**.

---

### When: 언제

The question word for "when" is **언제**. It can be used simply as **언제예요? (When is it?)** or attached to specific subjects like **시험이 언제예요? (When is the exam?)**. The instructor introduces the particles **부터 (from)** and **까지 (until/to)**, which can be attached to **언제**. **언제부터 배웠어요?** means "Since when did you learn?" while **언제까지 공부할 거예요?** means "Until when will you study?" The instructor notes that **부터** and **까지** apply to both time and location.

---

### How: 어떻게 and 어때

The word for "how" is **어떻게**. The instructor highlights a specific pronunciation rule: because the **ㅎ** batchim in **어떻** meets the **ㄱ** in **게**, it produces a strong **ㅋ** sound, resulting in the pronunciation **어떻게**. 

A critical distinction is made between **어떻게 (how)** and the expression **어떡해 (What should I do?)**. Although they sound identical, **어떡해** is a contraction of **어떻게 해** and is used to express anxiety or worry rather than asking a "how" question. For example, if someone says they lost their wallet, a listener might exclaim **어떡해!** to mean "Oh no, what are you going to do?" Context is the only way to distinguish them in speech. Finally, **어때요?** is introduced as a way to ask "How is it?" or "What do you think about...?", such as **달고나 커피 어때요? (How is the Dalgona coffee?)**.

---

### Why: 왜

The word for "why" is **왜**, which the instructor notes is easy to remember because it sounds somewhat similar to the English word "why." Common uses include **왜 안 왔어요? (Why didn't you come?)** and **왜 웃어요? (Why are you laughing?)**. Unlike English, the word order in Korean does not change when turning a statement into a "why" question; one simply adds **왜** to the beginning of the sentence.

---

### Homework Review and Nuances

During the homework review, the instructor clarifies several advanced points. For the verb **배우다 (to learn)**, the past tense **배웠어요** is a contraction of **배우** + **었어요**. Regarding the sentence **엄마 언제 와요?**, the instructor explains it has two possible meanings depending on context: it could be a direct question to one's mother (**Mom, when are you coming?**) or a question to a third party about her (**When is Mom coming?**). 

Finally, the instructor explains a unique cultural usage regarding the verb **늦다 (to be late)**. In Korean, even when saying "I am late" in the present state, the past tense **늦었어요** is typically used. Therefore, "Why are you late?" is translated as **왜 늦었어요?**.
