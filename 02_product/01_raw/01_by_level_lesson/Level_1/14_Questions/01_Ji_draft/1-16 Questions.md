**Intro**

In this section of level 2, we’re going to learn about different sentence types.

As a reminder, there are 4 sentence types in Korean - statements, questions, suggestions, and commands.

**We’ve already learned how to make statements in level 1 so, let’s first with the questions**

**Questions**

Unlike English, where you have to change the word order to form a question (e.g., *“Do you like cats?”*), Korean keeps the same **S-O-V (Subject-Object-Verb)** structure.

So instead of **"Do you like cats?"**, in Korean, it would be **"You cats like?"**

Let’s see what that looks like in each speech level.

반말 (Casual) – [너는] 고양이[를] 좋아해?

존댓말 (Informal) – [Name 씨는 / Title 님은] 고양이[를] 좋아하세요?

존댓말 (Formal) - [Name 씨는 / Title 님은] 고양이[를] 좋아하십니까?

The first one is casual, second is polite and the last one is formal.

Three things to notice here.

First, the “You part” or the name or the title part in 존댓말 is optional.

Liking something is a feeling. And the rule of three tells us that **the only person I can ask is YOU.**

And the honorifics in 존댓말 - 좋아하**세요? And** 좋아하**십니까?** also make it clear that “You” are the one I’m asking the question since I’m elevating “You”

Second, the object marker (를) is optional because it’s obvious that 고양이 is the object.

And you would either answer with 네 or 아니요/아닙니다 in 존댓말. Or 응 or 아니 in 반말.

Alright, now let’s learn question words:

**Who 누구, 누가**

**When 언제**

**Where 어디**

**What 무엇, 뭐 무슨,**

**Which 어느, 어떤**

**How 어떻게**

**How many 몇**

**How much 얼마, 얼마나, 얼만큼**

**Why 왜**

These question words go exactly where they belong in a sentence in the STLOHV position.

So again, the word order stays the same. So, instead of **“Who do you like?”** in English, in Korean, it’d be **“You who like?”**

Let’s go over each one

**Who 누구 / 누가**

They both mean who. But there is a difference.

Let me explain 누가 first.

누가 is 누구 plus 가. Yes, the subject marker. But 누구가 is incorrect. It’s always 누가. So, we use 누가 to say “who” as the subject like “Who did this?” - “이거 누가 했어?”

In all other cases, it’s 누구. Like in this sentence.

Example sentence 업데이트 (레벨 1에서 배운 내용 토대로 현재형 / 과거형 섞어서)

(너는) 누구(를) 좋아해? – Who do you like?

**And (를)** is also optional because 누구 "who" is clearly the object.

Another example, 누구야? 누구세요? “Who are you?” Or “Who is it?”.

**언제** means **"when"** and is treated like a noun.

“그게 언제야?” “그게 언제예요?”

Example sentence 업데이트 (레벨 1에서 배운 내용 토대로 현재형 / 과거형 섞어서)

They both mean “When is that?” Remember 그게 is shorted from 그것이.

And since 언제 is a noun, it can also be the subject and all you have to do is just add 가 after 언제

언제가 좋아? or 언제가 좋으세요? “When is good?”

“When would be a good time for you?”

This is a very common phrase for setting up a date or a meet up.

**Where (어디)**

And where is 어디.

어디야? 어디예요? Means “where is it?” Or “where are you?” depending on the context.

어디 is also a noun and you can add location particles like 에, 에서 and say 어디에 and 어디에서.

And 어디에 is often shortened to just 어디.

And 어디에서 is shortened to 어디서.

Example sentence 추가 (레벨 1에서 배운 내용 토대로 현재형 / 과거형 섞어서)

**What (무엇 / 뭐 / 무슨)**

무엇 and 뭐 are the same thing. It means “what” as a NOUN.

And 무엇 is common in writings. And 뭐 is much more common in conversation.

And since they’re both nouns, when it’s a subject, you add the subject marker say 무엇이 or 뭐가 and when it’s an object, you can add the object marker and say 무엇을 or 뭐를.

Example

무엇이 / 뭐가 문제예요? What is the problem?

무엇을 / 뭐를 찾으세요? What are you looking for?

However, 무슨 is an adjective that directly describes a noun. You can think of it like “What kind of” so you always need to add a noun right afterwards.

“무슨 영화 좋아하세요? What (kind of) movie do you like?”

**Which (어느, 어떤)**

Let’s talk about “which”. Both 어느 and 어떤 are adjectives. So there should always be a noun after these words.

First, 어느 is used when there’s one to choose from many. Like “which of these”.

Example : 어느 쪽이에요? 쪽 means side or direction. So this would mean “Which way is it?”

Next, 어떤. 어떤 comes from “어떻다” which means “to be a certain way”. So 어떤 could roughly translate to “Which KIND of”

Note that the usages of 무슨 and 어떤 sometimes overlap and they’re “almost” interchangeable.

Let me explain.

If you get asked "이거 무슨 책이에요?", you’d respond with an answer that’s related to a "name" to the book, Like its title, genre, or even its author... like "It's science fiction.", or "It's a Stephen King novel."

But if you get asked "이거 어떤 책이에요?" you’d most likely respond with more of the characteristics of the book, eg. "It's an exciting mystery novel." or "It's very easy to read."

**How (어떻게)**

“어떻게” ALSO comes from “어떻다”. And this suffix “게” makes 어떻다 into an adverb. So 어떻게 means how, but specifically “how” as in “in what way”

Here’s a phrase you’ve probably heard a million times – 어떡해?

어떡해? is actually just a shortened version of 어떻게 해? – How do? Or in natural English? “What do I do?”

It’s important to note that the way Korean uses 어떻게 is different from the way you’d use “how” in English.

생각하다 which means to think. Or to be more precise. “to do thinking.”

So now we can make “어떻게 생각하세요?” – “How do you think?”

To say the same in English, you would say “What do you think?”, not “HOW do you think?” like “What do you think about this idea?”

If you were to go word for word from English to Korean using 뭐, you would get “뭐를 생각하세요?”

But this is just awkward. And nobody ever says it.

And here’s another phrase you’ll hear very often. “무슨 생각하세요?” “What thought are you doing?” This translates to “What are you thinking ABOUT?” naturally.

너의 레벨이 점점 높아지고 많은 input들을 접하면 자연스럽게 이런 뉘앙스의 차이점들을 느끼고 “습득”할 수 있을 것이다.

**How much 얼마, 얼마나, 얼만큼**

얼마 is how much. And it’s treated like a noun. So how much is it? asking the price of something in Korean would be 얼마예요?

To use it like “How much so and so”, you have to use 얼마나 or 얼만큼. They’re interchangeable but 얼마나 is much more common than 얼만큼.

음식을 얼마나 먹었어요? How much (food) did you eat? 음식 means food and you can omit it the same way food in English could be omitted.

Note that in English “How much” is an adjective modifying the object “food” directly. However in Korean, 얼마나 is an adverb modifying the verb 먹다 to eat.

시간이 얼만큼 걸려요? Means how much (time) does it take? Note that the subject marker “이” is used after 시간 indicating that 시간 is the subject of the verb 걸리다 which means to take.

**Why 왜**

And lastly, we have 왜 which means “why”. And 왜 can come in 2 places in Korean.

It can either come at the very beginning of the question or right before the verb.

So, we can have two versions of “Why do you like cats?” in Korean.

“고양이를 왜 좋아하세요?”

“왜 고양이를 좋아하세요?”

Both versions are perfectly natural but there can be slight difference in the nuance. Remember I said the most important information typically comes right before the verb?

So in the first sentence, “왜” is placed right before the verb part. And so here, “why” is the most information.

And the same can said about the second sentence. This time, “고양이를” is placed right before the verb. So this can sound like “Why do you like CATS?” Like, out of all the animals you can like, why cats?

But if you think about it, “the reason” and “cats” carry equally important significance. So whether you say the first one or the second one, you can expect the same answer.

**Question words = Some(?)**

Let me tell you something very important to remember about the question words before we end the lesson.

In Korean, question words can also mean “some + noun.”

We learned that “누구 좋아해?” means “Who do you like?”

But this sentence can also mean “Do you like someone?”

And the difference is, it all comes down to HOW you say it.

I’ll read it out loud twice in each tone so pay close attention to the intonation.

누구 좋아해? – Who do you like?

누구 (pause) 좋아해? – Do you like someone?

Notice the brief pause in the second version and the different tone of 좋아해?

Another example.

어디 가? – Where are you going?

어디 (pause) 가? – Are you going somewhere?

The difference in the tone is very clear so it shouldn’t be too hard to tell one usage apart from the other.
