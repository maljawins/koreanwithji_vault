## Lesson Overview

This lesson focuses on mastering the "big picture" of Korean verb conjugation in the present tense. The primary objective is to move beyond simple sentence construction and understand the systematic way Korean verbs change across four sentence types (Declarative, Inquisitive, Propositive, and Imperative) and three speech levels (Informal Low, Informal High, and Formal High), including honorific forms. Ji emphasizes that while it may be tempting to jump into advanced topics like past tense or complex markers, mastering these 18 basic present-tense combinations is the essential foundation for all future progress.

The lesson provides a deep dive into the conjugation of the branch 어. Building on previous knowledge of the branch 아, students learn the specific rules for when the 어 suffix is added, when it cancels out due to redundancy, and when it merges with the stem vowel to create a new sound. This is illustrated through a variety of action and descriptive verbs, such as 서다 (to stand), 보내다 (to send), 쓰다 (to write), and 고치다 (to fix).

Finally, the lesson introduces verb "honorification" using the suffixes 시 and 으시, and explains how to form suggestions (Propositive) and commands (Imperative). By the end of the session, the student is equipped to handle approximately 80% of present-tense verb endings, excluding only irregular verbs and specific context markers.

### Slide 21 — Branch 아
- **On screen:** Rule: If stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 아. 
    - With 받침: 남다 -> 남아, 좋다 -> 좋아.
    - No 받침: 가다 + 아 = 가 (redundant), 보다 + 아 = 봐 (ㅗ + ㅏ = ㅘ).
- **Ji's teaching:** Previously, we learned that the branch changes depending on the final vowel of the verb stem. If the stem ends in ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 아.

### Slide 22 — Branch 어
- **On screen:** Rule: If stem ends in a vowel OTHER than ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 어.
    - With 받침: 적다 -> 적어, 입다 -> 입어, 읽다 -> 읽어.
    - No 받침: 서다 + 어 = 서 (redundant).
- **Ji's teaching:** If the stem ends in any other vowel, the branch is 어. Now, let’s take a deeper look at how the 어 suffix works. If a verb stem ends in a consonant and its final vowel is not ㅏ or ㅗ, you simply add 어 after the stem. But in some cases, 어 gets canceled out or merges with the stem depending on the ending.

### Slide 23 — Formal High Speech Type
- **On screen:** Rule: Vowel doesn't matter, only 받침 matters.
    - Yes 받침: 습니다 (좋습니다, 적습니다, 읽습니다).
    - No 받침: ㅂ니다 (갑니다, 핍니다, 섭니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** For formal high speech, the vowel doesn’t matter—only whether the verb stem has a 받침 (final consonant) or not.

### Slide 24~26 — 적다 (to write down / to be little)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram showing the conjugation of 적다.
    - Slide 24 (Informal low): 적 + 어 = 적어. Example: 국이 너무 적어 (The soup is too little).
    - Slide 25 (Informal high): 적 + 어 + 요 = 적어요. Examples: 나는 종이에 번호를 적어요; 학교에 학생이 적어요.
    - Slide 26 (Formal high): 적 + 습니다 = 적습니다. Example: 차에 기름이 적습니다 (The car is running low on gas).
- **Ji's teaching:** In our last lesson, we covered 적다 (to write down). For informal low, since it has a 받침 and the vowel is ㅓ, we add 어 to get 적어. For informal high, all you have to do is just add 요 to the end (the "fruit" part of the verb tree). For formal high, the vowel is irrelevant; since it has a 받침, it becomes 적습니다.

### Slide 27 — Other Verbs like 적다
- **On screen:** 있다 (있어, 있어요, 있습니다), 먹다 (먹어, 먹어요, 먹습니다), 입다 (입어, 입어요, 입습니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** These verbs follow the same pattern as 적다 because their stems end in a consonant and the vowel is not ㅏ or ㅗ.

### Slide 28 — 어 Cancels Out or Merges
- **On screen:** 
    - Cancels out: ㅓ, ㅕ, ㅐ, ㅔ (서다 -> 서, 켜다 -> 켜, 보내다 -> 보내, 세다 -> 세).
    - ㅡ is placeholder: 어 takes over (쓰다 -> 써).
    - ㅣ or ㅜ: merges (치다 -> 쳐, 주다 -> 줘).
- **Ji's teaching:** When the stem ends in a vowel other than ㅏ or ㅗ and has no 받침, 어 is either removed or merges. If the stem ends in ㅓ or ㅕ, the 어 sound becomes redundant, so it cancels out. If the stem ends in ㅐ or ㅔ, 어 is also canceled out. Technically, 보내어 and 세어 are grammatically correct, but they sound very poetic and are mostly used in songs. Remember ㅡ, the neutral placeholder vowel? When 어 is added, it takes over and ㅡ disappears. If the stem ends in ㅣ or ㅜ, 어 merges with the stem and becomes 여 and 워. 이 + 어, if you say it fast, is 여. 우 + 어, if you say it fast, is 워.

### Slide 29~31 — 서다 (to stand / to stop)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for 서다.
    - Slide 29 (Informal low): 서 + 어 = 서. Example: 나는 똑바로 서 (I stand up upright).
    - Slide 30 (Informal high): 서 + 어 + 요 = 서요. Example: 차가 갑자기 서요.
    - Slide 31 (Formal high): 서 + ㅂ니다 = 섭니다. Example: 버스가 정류장에 섭니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** 서다 means to stand or to stop physically. Since 서 and 어 create a redundant sound, 어 cancels out, and 서다 simply becomes 서. For informal high, just add 요. For formal high, since 서다 doesn’t end in 받침, it conjugates to 섭니다.

### Slide 32 — Other Verbs like 서다
- **On screen:** 펴다 (펴, 펴요, 폅니다), 켜다 (켜, 켜요, 켭니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** For these verbs, the same rule applies. 펴어 and 켜어 sound redundant, so 어 gets cancelled out.

### Slide 33~35 — 보내다 (to send / to spend)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for 보내다.
    - Slide 33 (Informal low): 보내 + 어 = 보내. Example: 나는 오늘 돈을 보내.
    - Slide 34 (Informal high): 보내 + 어 + 요 = 보내요. Example: 저는 가족과 시간을 보내요.
    - Slide 35 (Formal high): 보내 + ㅂ니다 = 보냅니다. Example: 저는 아이를 학교에 보냅니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** 보내다 means to send or to spend time. The stem ends in ㅐ, so 어 is removed. We say 보내, not 보내어. For formal high, because 보내 doesn't have a 받침, it becomes 보냅니다.

### Slide 36~38 — 세다 (to count)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for 세다.
    - Slide 36 (Informal low): 세 + 어 = 세. Example: 나는 숫자를 세.
    - Slide 37 (Informal high): 세 + 어 + 요 = 세요. Example: 저는 날짜를 세요.
    - Slide 38 (Formal high): 세 + ㅂ니다 = 셉니다. Example: 직원이 돈을 셉니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** Same rule applies for 세다. The stem is ㅔ, and 어 cancels out. 세다 becomes just 세. In formal high, it becomes 셉니다.

### Slide 39 — Other Verbs like 보내다, 세다
- **On screen:** 지내다 (지내, 지내요, 지냅니다), 없애다 (없애, 없애요, 없앱니다), 메다 (메, 메요, 멥니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** 지내다 becomes 지내, 지내요, 지냅니다. We learned this word in "How are you doing?" (어떻게 지내?). 없애다 means to remove something, and 메다 means to carry. I recommend you get all these words down on your vocabulary list. The only way you’re going to remember words is by using them, not just "memorizing."

### Slide 40~42 — 쓰다 (to write / to use / to be bitter)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for 쓰다.
    - Slide 40 (Informal low): 쓰 + 어 = 써. Example: 나는 일기를 써 (I write a journal).
    - Slide 41 (Informal high): 쓰 + 어 + 요 = 써요. Example: 차가 정말 써요 (The tea is really bitter).
    - Slide 42 (Formal high): 쓰 + ㅂ니다 = 씁니다. Example: 남동생이 화장실을 씁니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** 쓰다 is a homonym; it primarily means to write, but can also mean to use or to be bitter. The stem ends in ㅡ, the neutral vowel. Because it’s neutral, 어 takes over the ㅡ and it becomes 써. In formal high, it becomes 씁니다. Note that 차 is also a homonym meaning car or tea.

### Slide 43 — Other Verbs like 쓰다
- **On screen:** 트다 (터, 터요, 틉니다), 크다 (커, 커요, 큽니다), 예쁘다 (예뻐, 예뻐요, 예쁩니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** There are not that many verbs with stems that end in ㅡ, but all these words work the same way.

### Slide 44~46 — 고치다 (to fix)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for 고치다.
    - Slide 44 (Informal low): 고치 + 어 = 고쳐. Example: 나는 기계를 고쳐.
    - Slide 45 (Informal high): 고치 + 어 + 요 = 고쳐요. Example: 남자가 핸드폰을 고쳐요.
    - Slide 46 (Formal high): 고치 + ㅂ니다 = 고칩니다. Example: 아버지가 지붕을 고칩니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** If you say 치 and 어 fast, it becomes 여. So 어 merges with the stem and becomes 쳐. 고치다 means to fix. In formal high, 치 has no 받침, so it becomes 고칩니다. (note: script marker says Slide 46 says "고치다 - To hit", but the example is "Father fixes the roof").

### Slide 47 — Other Verbs like 고치다
- **On screen:** 치다 (쳐, 쳐요, 칩니다), 드리다 (드려, 드려요, 드립니다), 당기다 (당겨, 당겨요, 당깁니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** 치다 means to hit. 드리다 means to give, but it’s an honorific word of the verb 주다. 당기다 means to pull.

### Slide 48~50 — 주다 (to give)
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for 주다.
    - Slide 48 (Informal low): 주 + 어 = 줘. Example: 나한테 가방을 줘.
    - Slide 49 (Informal high): 주 + 어 + 요 = 줘요. Example: 저는 남동생한테 돈을 줘요.
    - Slide 50 (Formal high): 주 + ㅂ니다 = 줍니다. Example: 저는 여자친구한테 선물을 줍니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** Remember the compound vowel ㅝ? If you say 주어 quickly, it becomes 줘. 한테 is a particle meaning "to" (for people). In formal high, the stem 주 doesn't have a 받침, so it becomes 줍니다.

### Slide 51 — Other Verbs like 주다
- **On screen:** 두다 (둬, 둬요, 둡니다), 배우다 (배워, 배워요, 배웁니다), 감추다 (감춰, 감춰요, 감춥니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** These verbs follow the same merging rule as 주다.

### Slide 52 — Verb "Honorification"
- **On screen:** Suffix 시 or 으시 comes right after the stem (First Branch).
    - Vowel ending: 시 (주다 -> 주시다).
    - 받침 ending: 으시 (읽다 -> 읽으시다).
    - Always followed by 어 (Second Branch): (으)시 + 어 = (으)셔.
- **Ji's teaching:** Let’s move onto verb "honorification." Just like formal high speech, the only thing that matters is the 받침. If it ends in a vowel, it’s 시. If it ends in a 받침, it’s 으시. These suffixes always come right after the stem; I call this the "first branch." The vowel of the stem does not matter with honorific verbs because it’s always followed by 어 as the "second branch." 어 merges with 으시/시 to become 으셔 or 셔.

### Slide 53 — 은/는 & 이/가 Review
- **On screen:** Table showing silent letter ㅇ rule. 은 and 이 are for words with 받침. 는 and 가 are for words with no 받침. Note: "Applies to not only particles, but also verbs (Branch, Leaf)."
- **Ji's teaching:** Remember, any suffix that starts with the silent letter ㅇ, like 으시, is for words with a 받침. If it doesn’t start with the silent letter, like 시, it’s for words with no 받침. We went over this when we first learned about 은/는 and 이/가.

### Slide 54~56 — 보다 -> 보시다
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for honorific 보다.
    - Slide 54 (Informal low H): 보시 + 어 = 보셔. Example: 사장님이 티비를 보셔 (The owner watches TV).
    - Slide 55 (Informal high H): 보시 + 어 + 요 = 보셔요 or 보세요. Examples: 아버지가 영화를 보셔요; 어머니가 창밖을 보세요.
    - Slide 56 (Formal high H): 보시 + ㅂ니다 = 보십니다. Example: 할머니께서 아기를 보십니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** 보 has no 받침, so it’s followed by 시. The second branch is always 어, so it becomes 보셔. For informal high, you can say 보셔요 or 보세요. Both are correct, but 보세요 is more common these days. Honorific formal high is always ㅂ니다 because 시 has no 받침. Remember 께서? It’s the honorific of the subject marker 이/가.

### Slide 57 — Other Verbs like 보다 (Honorific)
- **On screen:** 가다 (가셔, 가셔요/가세요, 가십니다), 즐기다 (즐기셔, 즐기셔요/즐기세요, 즐기십니다), 서다 (서셔, 서셔요/서세요, 서십니다), 지내다 (지내셔, 지내셔요/지내세요, 지내십니다).
- **Ji's teaching:** When it comes to verb honorification, all that matters is whether the stem ends in a 받침 or not. These verbs all end in a vowel, so they follow the same rule.

### Slide 58~60 — 적다 -> 적으시다
- **On screen:** Tree diagram for honorific 적다.
    - Slide 58 (Informal low H): 적으시 + 어 = 적으셔. Example: 어머니가 가격을 적으셔.
    - Slide 59 (Informal high H): 적으시 + 어 + 요 = 적으셔요 or 적으세요. Examples: 선생님이 문제를 적으셔요; 아버지가 번호를 적으세요.
    - Slide 60 (Formal high H): 적으시 + ㅂ니다 = 적으십니다. Example: 교수님께서 점수를 적으십니다.
- **Ji's teaching:** The stem of 적다 ends in a 받침, so the honorific suffix is 으시. 적다 becomes 적으셔. For informal high, it’s 적으셔요 or 적으세요. The suffix 으시 meets the formal high ending ㅂ니다 to become 적으십니다.

### Slide 61 — Other Verbs like 적다 (Honorific)
- **On screen:** 읽다, 좋다, 받다, 잡다.
- **Ji's teaching:** These words have a 받침 in their stem and follow the same rule regardless of the vowel.

### Slide 62 — Propositive (Suggestions)
- **On screen:** "Let's do it."
    - Informal low: Stem + 자.
    - Informal high: Stem + 으시/시 + 지 + 요.
    - Formal high: Stem + 읍시다 / ㅂ시다.
- **Ji's teaching:** Propositive endings are inherently context markers indicating a suggestion. The informal high propositive form includes honorifics and is the most polite way to make a suggestion. The formal high ending can sometimes come off as rude, so I recommend sticking with the informal high form.

### Slide 63~64 — Propositive Informal Low
- **On screen:** 
    - Slide 63: 가 + 자 = 가자. Example: 집에 가자.
    - Slide 64: 앉 + 자 = 앉자. Example: 자리에 앉자.
- **Ji's teaching:** Informal low is the easiest. It doesn’t matter if the stem ends in a 받침 or not; you just add 자.

### Slide 65~66 — Propositive Informal High (H)
- **On screen:** 
    - Slide 65: 가 + 시 + 지 + 요 = 가시죠. Example: 사무실에 가시죠.
    - Slide 66: 앉 + 으시 + 지 + 요 = 앉으시죠. Example: 소파에 앉으시죠.
- **Ji's teaching:** We just learned honorifics. 가 doesn't have a 받침, so 시 comes in. 지 is a context marker that marks a suggestive context here. So 가+시+지+요 becomes 가시죠. For 앉다, which has a 받침, we use 으시 to get 앉으시죠.

### Slide 67~68 — Propositive Formal High
- **On screen:** 
    - Slide 67: 가 + ㅂ시다 = 갑시다. Example: 지하철역으로 갑시다.
    - Slide 68: 앉 + 읍시다 = 앉읍시다. Example: 의자에 앉읍시다.
- **Ji's teaching:** The formal high suffix for suggestion is ㅂ시다 for stems with no 받침 and 읍시다 for stems with a 받침. Recognize the pattern? 읍 starts with the silent letter ㅇ.

### Slide 69 — Imperative (Commands)
- **On screen:** "Do it." Comparison table between Declarative and Imperative. Note: "Informal high (H) is the most common."
- **Ji's teaching:** In English, the verb doesn’t change for imperatives, and Korean verbs don’t change much either. They are almost the same as declarative verbs, but because of the culture of respect, honorific verbs are most commonly used. Formal high is the only speech level that has a different ending for imperatives compared to declaratives.

### Slide 70 — Imperative Informal Low
- **On screen:** 하 + 여 = 해(라). Example: 그냥 해(라) (Just do it). Note: "해라 sounds more intense than 해."
- **Ji's teaching:** Remember how 하다 turns into 해? If you add the context marker 라 after 해, it becomes much more forceful. It can sound like giving an order. Repeat after me: 그냥 해라.

### Slide 71 — Imperative Informal High (H)

- **Ji's teaching:** Remember the 세요 ending from 안녕하세요? Repeat after me: 나중에 하세요. Unlike declarative sentences, 셔요 does NOT work for the imperative.

### Slide 72 — Imperative Formal High
- **On screen:** 하 + 십시오 = 하십시오. Example: 운동을 하십시오 (Exercise). Note: "앉으십시오 for stems with 받침."
- **Ji's teaching:** Formal high is the only speech type with a different ending for imperatives. The ending is 십시오 if the stem has no 받침 and 으십시오 for stems with a 받침.

## Teaching Points

### Verb Conjugation: The Branch 어
- **Rule:** If the verb stem ends in any vowel other than ㅏ or ㅗ, the branch is 어.
- **Canceling Out:** The 어 suffix is removed if the stem ends in ㅓ, ㅕ, ㅐ, or ㅔ and has no 받침 (e.g., 서다 -> 서, 보내다 -> 보내).
- **Merging:** 
    - If the stem ends in ㅣ, it merges with 어 to become ㅕ (e.g., 고치다 -> 고쳐).
    - If the stem ends in ㅜ, it merges with 어 to become ㅝ (e.g., 주다 -> 줘).
    - If the stem ends in ㅡ, the ㅡ disappears and is replaced by 어 (e.g., 쓰다 -> 써).

### Verb Honorification
- **Suffixes:** 시 (for stems ending in a vowel) or 으시 (for stems ending in a 받침).
- **Placement:** These suffixes are the "first branch" and come immediately after the verb stem.
- **Conjugation:** Honorific verbs are always followed by the branch 어.
    - 시 + 어 = 셔.
    - 으시 + 어 = 으셔.
- **Informal High Note:** In the informal high level, both 셔요 and 세요 are grammatically correct, but 세요 is much more common.

### Sentence Types
- **Declarative:** Standard statements.
- **Inquisitive:** Questions. In Formal High, the ending 다 changes to 까? (e.g., 갑니다 -> 갑니까?).
- **Propositive:** Suggestions ("Let's...").
    - Informal Low: Stem + 자.
    - Informal High: Honorific + 지요 (e.g., 가시죠).
    - Formal High: ㅂ시다 / 읍시다.
- **Imperative:** Commands.
    - Informal Low: Add 라 for forcefulness (e.g., 해라).
    - Informal High: Use the 세요 ending (셔요 is not used for commands).
    - Formal High: 십시오 / 으십시오.

### General Grammar Rules
- **Formal High Consistency:** In the Formal High speech level, the stem's vowel is irrelevant; only the presence of a 받침 determines the ending (습니다/습니까/읍시다/으십시오 vs ㅂ니다/ㅂ니까/ㅂ시다/십시오).
- **Silent Letter Rule:** Suffixes starting with the silent letter ㅇ (은, 이, 으시, 읍시다, 으십시오) are used for words ending in a 받침.
