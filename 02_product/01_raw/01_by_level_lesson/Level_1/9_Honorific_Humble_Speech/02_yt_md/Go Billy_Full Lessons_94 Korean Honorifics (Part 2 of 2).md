## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson continues the exploration of Korean honorifics, shifting the focus from showing respect to others (honorific speech) to showing humility for oneself (humble speech). The instructor explains that while honorific speech elevates the person being spoken about, humble speech achieves a similar effect of respect by figuratively lowering the speaker. The video covers specific humble verb forms and honorific particles that replace standard markers in polite conversation.

---

### Humble Speech vs. Honorific Speech

The instructor defines humble speech as a way to show humility from the perspective of the person speaking. On screen, a comparison is drawn: honorific speech shows extra respect toward the person who is being spoken about, whereas humble speech shows humility by lowering oneself to the person who is being spoken about. These two systems are often used together in the same sentence to maximize the level of politeness and social propriety.

---

### Humble Verb Substitutions

Certain common verbs have specific humble counterparts that must be used when the speaker is performing an action for someone of higher status. The instructor highlights two primary examples:

**1. 주다 (to give) → 드리다**
When giving something to a person who deserves respect, such as a teacher or an elder, the verb `주다 (to give)` is replaced by its humble form `드리다`. The instructor demonstrates this with a comparison:
> **내일 선물을 줄 거예요.** / I will give you a present tomorrow. (Standard/Polite)
> **내일 선물을 드릴 거예요.** / I will give you a present tomorrow. (Humble/Polite)
The second sentence is more appropriate when the recipient is someone like a teacher, as `드리다` shows the speaker's humility in the act of giving.

**2. 보다 (to see) → 뵙다 / 뵈다**
When meeting or seeing someone of higher status, `보다 (to see)` is replaced by `뵙다` or `뵈다`. The instructor notes that these are particularly common in greetings and farewells.
> **내일 봐요.** / I'll see you tomorrow. (Standard/Polite)
> **내일 봬요.** / I'll see you tomorrow. (Humble/Polite)
The form `봬요` is a contraction of `뵈어요`. Using this humble form indicates that the speaker is showing respect by lowering their own status relative to the person they are meeting.

---

### Honorific and Humble Particles

Beyond verbs, certain grammatical particles must be swapped for honorific versions when referring to or addressing a person of higher status. The instructor provides a chart of these transformations:

- **Subject Markers:** `이/가` becomes `께서`.
- **Topic Markers:** `은/는` becomes `께서는`.
- **Dative Markers (to/from):** `에게` and `에게서` are replaced by `께`.

The instructor provides example sentences to illustrate these particles in context:
> **아버지께서 집에 안 계세요.** / Dad is not at home.
In this sentence, the standard subject marker `가` is replaced by `께서` because the subject is `아버지 (father)`. Additionally, the honorific verb `계시다` (the honorific form of `있다`) is used.

Another example demonstrates the combination of honorific particles and humble verbs:
> **이제 할머니께서는 주무실 거예요.** / Grandma will sleep now.
Here, `께서는` replaces `는`, and the honorific verb `주무시다` (to sleep) is used to show respect to the grandmother.

Finally, an example using the dative particle `께` and the humble verb `드리다`:
> **선생님께 선물을 드렸어요.** / I gave the teacher a present.
This sentence uses `께` instead of `에게` and `드렸어요` instead of `줬어요`. This combination shows both extra respect toward the teacher and humility from the speaker.
