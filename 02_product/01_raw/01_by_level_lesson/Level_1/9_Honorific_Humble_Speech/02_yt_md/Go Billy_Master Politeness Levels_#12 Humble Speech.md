## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces humble speech, known in Korean as 낮춤말. While honorific speech (존댓말) and humble speech both function to show extra respect to others, they achieve this through opposite mechanisms. Honorific speech elevates the person being spoken about, metaphorically placing them on a pedestal. In contrast, humble speech functions by lowering the speaker themselves in front of the other person. These two forms are frequently used in tandem to maximize politeness. This lesson focuses on the core verbs and nouns required to master this "lowering" aspect of Korean politeness, which is essential in professional environments and when interacting with older members of society.

---

### Core Humble Verbs: 드리다 and 뵈다

The instructor identifies two primary verbs that form the foundation of humble speech. The first is 드리다, which is the humble version of 주다 (to give). It is used when the speaker is giving something to someone of higher status. Visually, the instructor suggests imagining a person kneeling down and offering an object with both hands.

> **제가 이걸 드리고 싶었어요.** / I wanted to give this to you.
> **이건 선생님께 드릴 편지예요.** / This is a letter I'll give to the teacher.
> **할머니께 안 드렸어요?** / You didn't give it to your grandmother?

The second core verb is 뵈다, the humble version of 보다 (to see/meet). This verb is used when the speaker is the one doing the seeing or meeting of a superior. In the 요 form, it conjugates as 봬요 or 뵈어요.

> **내일 봬요.** / See you tomorrow.
> **아침에 봬도 될까요?** / Could I see you in the morning?
> **언제 뵐 수 있을까요?** / When could I see you?

---

### Humble vs. Honorific: 드리다 vs. 주시다 and 뵈다 vs. 보시다

A common point of confusion for learners is the distinction between humble and honorific versions of the same action. The instructor clarifies that 드리다 is humble (speaker gives to superior), while 주시다 is honorific (superior gives to speaker). It is incorrect to use 드리다 when a superior is giving something to you, as that would imply they are lowering themselves. However, a superior (like a parent) might use 드리다 when instructing a subordinate (a child) to give something to another superior (a teacher).

Similarly, 뵈다 is used when the speaker sees a superior, whereas 보시다 is used when the superior is the one doing the seeing. Using standard 보다 in a professional context, such as saying 내일 봐요 to a boss, sounds too casual and potentially impolite. In such cases, 뵈다 is the appropriate choice to maintain the "talking upward" feel of the conversation.

---

### The Extra Humble Verb 뵙다

The instructor introduces 뵙다 as a variant of 뵈다. While they share the same meaning (to see/meet), 뵙다 carries an even more humble tone. It is less common in daily speech than 뵈다 but appears in specific formal set phrases. Interestingly, despite the different base form, it conjugates identically to 뵈다 in the 요 form (봬요).

> **내일 선생님을 뵙고 싶어요.** / I want to see the teacher tomorrow.
> **만나 뵙게 되어 반갑습니다.** / It is nice to be able to meet you.
> **처음 뵙겠습니다.** / Nice to meet you (literally: I see you for the first time).

The instructor notes that 처음 뵙겠습니다 is a more traditional, older expression, and modern speakers often prefer 만나서 반갑습니다 or simply 반갑습니다.

---

### Humble Nouns: 저 and 저희

Humble speech extends beyond verbs to the nouns used for self-reference. The common word 저 (I/me) is actually a humble noun. By using 저 instead of the plain 나, the speaker is actively lowering themselves relative to the listener. This is why 저 is standard for strangers and superiors, while 나 is reserved for close friends.

The plural version is 저희 (we/us). This is also a humble noun used to lower the speaker's group. However, the instructor notes that 저희 is typically reserved for extra polite or formal situations. In everyday speech with strangers, the standard noun 우리 (we/us) is perfectly acceptable and more common.

---

### Additional Polite and Humble Substitutions

The instructor provides a list of other common substitutions used to increase the politeness or formality of speech. Some are classified as honorific/humble, while others simply sound more refined.

- **말 (speech/word)** becomes **말씀**. This can be used both for the speaker's own words (humble) or the other person's words (honorific).
- **밥을 먹다 (to eat a meal)** is often replaced by **식사를 하다**. While not strictly a humble/honorific pair, 식사를 하다 sounds more formal. A very high honorific version of 밥 is **진지**, though this is becoming less common.
- **데리다 (to bring/accompany someone)** becomes **모시다** when bringing a superior.
  > **도서관에 할아버지를 모시고 갔어요.** / I brought my grandfather to the library.
- **물어보다 (to ask)** becomes **여쭈다** or **여쭙다** when asking a superior.
  > **제가 하나만 여쭤보겠습니다.** / Let me ask you just one thing.

---

### Grammar Notes and Usage Restrictions

The instructor concludes with two technical notes regarding how these humble verbs interact with auxiliary grammar patterns. First, 드리다 can replace 주다 in the ~아/어 주다 (do something for someone) pattern. For example, 해 줄게요 (I'll do it for you) becomes the more polite 해 드릴게요.

However, a similar substitution does not work for 뵈다. While 보다 is used in the ~아/어 보다 (try doing something) pattern, you cannot substitute 뵈다 into this grammar. Therefore, an expression like 해 볼게요 (I'll try doing it) cannot be changed to 해 뵐게요.
