## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the concept of humble speech, known in Korean as 낮춤말. He explains that while honorific speech (존댓말) shows respect by metaphorically elevating the person being spoken about, humble speech achieves the same goal of showing respect by lowering the speaker. By making oneself "lower," the other person naturally becomes "higher" in the social hierarchy of the conversation. This lesson covers the specific verbs, nouns, and pronouns used to implement this humble register.

---

### Understanding 낮춤말

The term 낮춤말 comes from the causative verb 낮추다, which means "to make something low" or "to lower." The instructor emphasizes that humble speech and honorific speech are two sides of the same coin; they are often used together because they serve the same purpose of expressing respect. You typically use humble speech when speaking to teachers, bosses, coworkers (if not close), leaders, grandparents, other people's family members, and strangers. It is a way to signal that you recognize the other person's higher rank or the need for social distance and politeness.

---

### Humble Verbs: 드리다 and 뵈다/뵙다

The first major humble verb is **드리다**, which is the humble equivalent of **주다 (to give)**. The instructor describes the feeling of 드리다 as kneeling down and offering something up with both hands. This verb can replace 주다 in any context, including auxiliary verb constructions.

- **주다** (to give) → **드리다** (to humbly give)
- **도와주다** (to help) → **도와드리다** (to humbly help)
- **만들어 주다** (to make for someone) → **만들어 드리다** (to humbly make for someone)

Example sentences provided:
> **선생님께 드리고 싶었어요.** / I wanted to give it to the teacher.
> **도와드리고 싶어요.** / I want to help (you).
> **이것도 드릴까요?** / Shall I give you this too?
> **제가 만들어 드릴게요.** / I will make it for you.

The second set of verbs is **뵈다** and **뵙다**, which are humble versions of **보다 (to see/meet)**. While they have the same meaning, 뵙다 is slightly more formal but less common than 뵈다. They both conjugate to **봬요** in the polite present tense. The instructor notes that while these replace 보다 when meeting a person, they cannot replace the auxiliary verb 보다 (used for "trying" something).

- **보다** (to see/meet) → **뵈다 / 뵙다** (to humbly see/meet)

Example sentences provided:
> **처음 뵙겠습니다.** / Nice to meet you (Literally: I see you for the first time).
> **내일 봬요.** / See you tomorrow.
> **그럼 언제 다시 뵐 수 있을까요?** / Then when can we meet again?

A critical grammar rule mentioned is that humble verbs cannot be combined with the honorific marker **-(으)시-**. For example, you cannot say **들이시다** or **뵈시다** because it is logically inconsistent to lower yourself and raise yourself at the same time in the same word.

---

### Humble Nouns and Pronouns: 저 and 저희

Humble speech also requires using specific pronouns to refer to oneself or one's group. Using these instead of their plain counterparts is essential for maintaining a humble tone.

- **나** (I - plain) → **저** (I - humble)
- **우리** (We/Us/Our - plain) → **저희** (We/Us/Our - humble)

The instructor notes that **저** is not just "polite" but specifically "humble" because it lowers the speaker's status relative to the listener.

---

### Advanced Humble Verbs: 모시다 and 여쭈다

For more advanced learners, the instructor introduces two additional humble verbs: **모시다** and **여쭈다/여쭙다**.

**모시다** is the humble version of **데리다 (to take/bring a person)**. It is used when you are accompanying someone of higher status to a location.
- **데리고 가다/오다** → **모시고 가다/오다** (or **모셔 가다/오다**)

Example sentence:
> **공항에 사장님을 모시고 갔어요.** / I took my boss to the airport.

**여쭈다** (sometimes **여쭙다**) is the humble version of **묻다 (to ask)**. It is used when asking a question to someone you respect.
- **묻다** → **여쭈다 / 여쭙다**

Example sentence:
> **선생님께 여쭤봤어요.** / I asked the teacher.
