### Overview

The instructor introduces the word 부탁, which is a common noun meaning "a request" or "a favor." While many learners encounter this word through the set phrase 잘 부탁드립니다, the video explores how to use it more broadly as a verb to make polite or formal requests. The lesson distinguishes between the standard verb 부탁하다 and the humble version 부탁드리다, while also clarifying when these expressions are appropriate versus when they sound awkward.

---

### 부탁 as a Noun and Verb

The word 부탁 functions primarily as a regular noun. A speaker can use it to state that they have a favor or a request to ask of someone. However, when used to actively ask a favor, it combines with the verbs 하다 or 드리다. The instructor explains that 부탁하다 is the standard form used for everyday polite requests, whereas 부탁드리다 is a humble verb (존댓말). Because 드리다 is the humble version of "to give," using 부탁드리다 literally means "to give a request" in a way that lowers the speaker's status, making it sound much more formal and polite.

---

### Making Polite Requests with Nouns

To make a polite or formal request for a specific action or item, the speaker takes a noun, optionally attaches the object marker 을/를, and follows it with 부탁하다 or 부탁드리다. The instructor provides several examples of this pattern:
- **자기소개(를) 부탁드립니다** / Please introduce yourself (Literally: "I request a self-introduction").
- **양해(를) 부탁드립니다** / Please understand (Literally: "I request your understanding").
- **박수(를) 부탁드립니다** / Please applaud (Literally: "I request applause"). This is a common phrase used by hosts on TV shows to encourage the audience to give a round of applause.
- **도움(을) 부탁드립니다** / Please help (Literally: "I request help").
- **애교를 부탁해요** / Please show us some charm/acting cute.

---

### Usage Restrictions and Awkwardness

A critical distinction is made regarding the scope of these verbs. 부탁하다 and 부탁드리다 are reserved for favors or formal requests, not for simple daily transactions. The instructor warns against using these words for mundane things like asking for water. Saying **물 부탁드립니다** would be equivalent to saying "Might I please ask of you some water," which sounds overly dramatic and awkward in a casual or restaurant setting. In such cases, the standard command **물 주세요** (Please give me some water) is the correct choice.

---

### 부탁 vs. 제발

The instructor clarifies a common point of confusion between the verb "to please" (requesting) and the adverb "please." While 부탁해요 can be translated as "please," it is a verb used for making a request. In contrast, **제발** is an adverb used when the speaker is almost begging or pleading with someone. They are not interchangeable; 부탁 is for requests, while 제발 is for desperate begging.

---

### The Expression 잘 부탁드립니다

The video revisits the common phrase **잘 부탁드립니다**, which literally translates to "Please take good care of this for me" or "I request your favor." This is most frequently used during self-introductions to mean "Please look favorably upon me" or "I look forward to working with you." For slightly less formal situations, one can use **잘 부탁해요**. The instructor notes that while 드립니다 and 해요 are the most common endings because of the polite nature of the word, other endings like 드려요 or even the casual 해 can be used depending on the social context.
