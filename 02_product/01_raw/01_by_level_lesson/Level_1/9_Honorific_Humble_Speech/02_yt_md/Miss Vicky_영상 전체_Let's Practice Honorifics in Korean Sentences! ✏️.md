### Forming the Honorific Base Form
The instructor explains that every Korean verb and adjective has an honorific form. To create the base honorific form, one must identify the verb stem and add the suffix -(으)시다. If the stem ends in a vowel, only -시다 is added; if it ends in a consonant (받침), -으시다 is added. For example, 가다 (to go) becomes 가시다, while 넣다 (to put in) becomes 넣으시다.

Several irregular patterns are highlighted during this process. For stems ending in ㄹ, such as 놀다 (to play) or 만들다 (to make), the ㄹ is dropped before adding -시다, resulting in 노시다 and 만드시다. For ㅂ irregular verbs like 눕다 (to lie down) or 덥다 (to be hot), the ㅂ is removed and replaced with 우 before adding -시다, resulting in 누우시다 and 더우시다. The ㄷ irregular is also addressed: for 듣다 (to listen), the ㄷ changes to ㄹ before adding -으시다, resulting in 들으시다. Interestingly, the instructor notes that 닫다 (to close) is regular and simply becomes 닫으시다.

---

### Conjugating Honorifics in Different Tenses
Once the honorific base form is established, it must be conjugated for use in sentences. The instructor emphasizes a specific quirk in the present tense: while grammatically -셔요 is the direct conjugation of -시다, Koreans almost exclusively use the ending -세요. Therefore, 가시다 becomes 가세요 and 들으시다 becomes 들으세요.

For the past tense, the -시다 ending follows standard rules to become -셨어요. For instance, 기다리시다 (to wait) becomes 기다리셨어요. In the future tense, the stem of the honorific form is combined with -(으)ㄹ 거예요, resulting in the ending -실 거예요. For example, 바쁘시다 (to be busy) becomes 바쁘실 거예요. The instructor provides a visual chart showing these transformations across various verbs to reinforce the patterns.

### Unique Honorific Verbs: Group 1
Some common verbs have unique honorific forms that do not use the -(으)시다 suffix. The instructor introduces the first group: 먹다 (to eat) and 마시다 (to drink) both share the honorific form 드시다. The verb 자다 (to sleep) becomes 주무시다. For 말하다 (to speak), the noun 말 is upgraded to the honorific 말씀, resulting in 말씀하시다. These unique forms follow the same conjugation rules as regular honorifics (e.g., 드세요, 주무셨어요, 말씀하실 거예요).

---

### Unique Honorifics with Contextual Meanings: 있다 and 주다
The instructor explains that some verbs have two different honorific forms depending on the intended meaning. The verb 있다 has two functions: possession (to have) and existence/location (to be somewhere). When expressing possession, the honorific form is 있으시다 (e.g., "Do you have a book?" = 책 있으세요?). When expressing location or presence, the form is 계시다 (e.g., "The teacher is in the classroom" = 선생님께서 교실에 계세요). 계시다 is also used for the honorific present progressive form -고 계시다.

The verb 주다 (to give) also has two forms: 주시다 and 드리다. The choice depends on who is being honored. 주시다 is used when the subject (the giver) is the person being honored (e.g., "Grandfather gave me money" = 할아버지께서 용돈을 주셨어요). Conversely, 드리다 is used when the receiver of the action is the person being honored, even if the subject is the speaker (e.g., "I gave the teacher a book" = 저는 선생님께 책을 드렸어요). This distinction is critical for showing proper respect in social interactions.

---

### Honorifics for Sickness and Identification
The instructor briefly covers 아프다 (to be sick/hurt). While 아프시다 is the standard honorific, 편찮으시다 is a unique honorific used for general illness. Generally, 아프시다 is used for specific body parts (e.g., "Grandmother has a headache"), while 편찮으시다 refers to a person being unwell overall. Finally, the instructor explains how to use 이다 (to be) in honorifics for identification. The form becomes -이세요 (or -세요 after a vowel). This is commonly used when asking "Who is this?" (누구세요?).
