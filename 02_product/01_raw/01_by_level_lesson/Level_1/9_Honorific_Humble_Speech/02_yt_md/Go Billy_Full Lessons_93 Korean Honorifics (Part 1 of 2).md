## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces honorific speech as a distinct component of the broader Korean politeness system. While many learners are familiar with speech levels like the polite 요 form, the formal 니다 form, and the casual 반말, honorifics serve a different purpose: they show respect to the person being spoken about, rather than just the person being spoken to. This lesson focuses on the mechanics of elevating a subject through specific verb endings, unique honorific verbs, and honorific nouns.

The learning arc begins by clarifying the difference between speech levels and honorifics. The instructor demonstrates that these two systems can be mixed—for example, using casual speech with a friend while simultaneously using honorifics to discuss that friend's father. The lesson then moves into the practical application of the 시다 suffix, the memorization of irregular honorific verbs, and the necessity of using honorific nouns to maintain a consistent level of respect.

---

### Honorifics vs. Politeness Levels

Honorific speech is often confused with general politeness levels, but the instructor clarifies that they are separate systems. Politeness levels like the 요 form and 니다 form are used to show respect to the listener. In contrast, honorific speech shows respect to the person you are speaking about. This means you can use casual Korean (반말) with a close friend while using honorifics to refer to a third party, such as the friend's parents.

The instructor provides a comparison using the sentence "Where is dad?" A standard polite version is 아버지가 어디에 있어요? Here, the 요 form shows respect to the listener, but not specifically to the father. To show respect to the father, the verb must be changed to an honorific form. If speaking to a close friend about their father, the sentence becomes 아버지가 어디에 계셔? This combines the honorific verb 계시다 (to exist/be) with a casual ending (어) because the listener is a friend, but the subject (dad) deserves respect.

---

### The Honorific Suffix (으)시다

The most common way to create an honorific verb is by adding the suffix 시다 to the verb stem. If the stem ends in a consonant, 으시다 is used; if it ends in a vowel, simply 시다 is added. This suffix can then be conjugated into various speech levels. The instructor displays a chart of common transformations:

- **하다 (to do)** → 하세요, 하십니다, 하셔, 하시면, 하셨네
- **믿다 (to believe)** → 믿으세요, 믿으십니다, 믿으시고, 믿으셨어요
- **이다 (to be)** → 이세요, 이십니다, 이시죠, 이셨는데
- **알다 (to know)** → 아세요, 아십니까, 아시나요, 아실까요

The instructor highlights that the ending 세요 is a contraction of 시어요. While often used for polite commands like 주세요 (Please give me) or 하세요 (Please do it), it is also a standard honorific statement ending. For example, 선생님이 뭐 하세요? means "What is the teacher doing?" using the honorific form to respect the teacher.

---

### Unique Honorific Verbs

Some common verbs do not use the 시다 suffix but instead have entirely different honorific counterparts. The instructor emphasizes that if a unique honorific form exists, it must be used; using the standard verb with 시다 (like 먹으시다) is grammatically incorrect. The following essential pairs are presented:

- **있다 (to exist/be)** → 계시다
- **없다 (to not exist/be)** → 안 계시다
- **먹다/마시다 (to eat/drink)** → 드시다 (or 잡수시다, though 드시다 is more common)
- **자다 (to sleep)** → 주무시다
- **죽다 (to die)** → 돌아가시다 (literally "to go back")

Example sentences shown on screen demonstrate these in context: 어머니는 지금 집에 안 계세요 (Mom is not at home now) and 선생님은 이미 점심을 드셨어요 (The teacher ate lunch already).

---

### Honorific Nouns

To speak naturally, honorific verbs should be paired with honorific nouns when referring to respected individuals. Using an honorific verb with a plain noun can sound inconsistent. The instructor provides a list of common noun substitutions:

- **사람 (person)** → 분
- **집 (house)** → 댁
- **이름 (name)** → 성함
- **나이 (age)** → 연세
- **말 (speech/words)** → 말씀
- **밥 (meal)** → 진지

The instructor illustrates this with the sentence 선생님이 좋은 분이세요 (The teacher is a good person). This sentence is superior to using 사람 because it matches the honorific verb 이세요 with the honorific noun 분. Another example provided is 연세가 어떻게 되세요? (How old are you?), which uses the honorific noun for age and the honorific verb 되시다 to show high respect to an elder.
