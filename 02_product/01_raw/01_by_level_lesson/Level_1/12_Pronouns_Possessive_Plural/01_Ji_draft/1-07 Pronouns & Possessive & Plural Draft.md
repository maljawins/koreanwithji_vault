**Introduction**

So far, we've learned how to say "I", "you", “this and that”.

And now it’s time to learn other pronouns.

But before we start: Pronouns in Korean do not change depending on whether they're used as a subject or an object—like "I" changes to "me" in English.

**I & My & Mine**

We learned that the standard version of "I" is 나, and 저 is the humble version.

Example: 나는 학생이야. / 저는 학생이에요.

So how do you say "my"?

Simple. You just add the possessive marker 의 after the pronoun. Think of it like apostrophe-S in English.

So you get 나의 and 저의.

의 is known to be one of the hardest sounds to make, but when it's used as a possessive marker, it's pronounced "에"—which is a lot easier.

But Koreans almost never say 나의 and 저의. We contract them to 내 and 제.

Examples:

이건 내 핸드폰이야.
저건 제 가방이에요.
그건 제 커피예요.

**"Mine"**

To say "mine," all you need to do is add 것.

But most of the time, 것 becomes 거—yes, that's the same word in 이거, 저거, and 그거.

When 거 comes after possessives, it's always pronounced "꺼."

So you get 내 거 (꺼) and 제 거 (꺼).

Example: 이건 내 거야. / 저건 제 거예요.

**We & Our & Ours**

"We" also has two versions: standard and humble.

우리 and 저희 for the humble version.

저희 is pronounced "저히."

Example: 우리는 학생이야. / 저희는 학생이에요.

You can also add the plural marker 들, which is equivalent to "s" or "es" in English, and say 우리들 and 저희들.

But 들 is optional, so 우리 and 저희 will do in most cases.

**Plural in Korean**

There something you need to know about plurals in Korean. Korean doesn't emphasize plural as much as English does.

For example, in English, you say "Cats are cute."

But in Korean, we just say 고양이는 귀여워요.

So unless you want to emphasize that there are multiple units, you don't need to attach 들 every time.

In fact, if you say 고양이들, it has a nuance similar to "THE cats" in English.

By the way, Korean doesn't have articles like "a" or "the."

**"Our" and "Ours"**

For "our," you can optionally attach 의 and say 우리의 and 저희의, but 우리 and 저희 are perfectly fine as is.

Example: 이 분은 우리(의) 엄마예요.

To say "ours," you just add 거.

Example: 이 그림은 우리 거야.

**"My" vs. "Our" in Korean Culture**

Now that we know how to say “my” and “our”, let’s talk about something Koreans do that confuses the hell out of non-Koreans.

우리/저희 남편 – “OUR” husband?

우리/저희 아내 – “OUR” wife?

First of all, no, we don’t share our spouses. What we really mean is “MY husband” and “MY wife”.

This is a very cultural thing. Korean culture tends to prioritize the group over the individual, and this really affects the way we use 우리.

The most classic example is 우리 나라 (our country). 제 나라 (my country) sounds really unnatural.

So we use 우리 for things or people that are part of a community—like home, company, school—or family members. For example, "my mom," "my brother," and "my dog" would be "our mom," "our brother," and "our dog."

And this is why we say 우리 남편 and 우리 아내 because husband and wife are a part of OUR family.

But this doesn’t happen until marriage. It’s MY girlfriend and MY boyfriend like 제 여자친구 or 제 남자친구.

So, 우리 and 저희 are for community and 내 and 제, on the other hand, are used for things that are more personal and exclusive.

**Your & Yours**

In the last lesson, we learned about 너—the casual "you."

"Your" in Korean is 너의, which is contracted to 네, but it's pronounced "니" to avoid confusion with 내.

Most 한글 is pronounced as written, but this is an exception to remember.

"Yours" is 네 거 (꺼).

Examples:

이거 네 물이야?
저거 네 거야?

**Plural "You" - Casual**

There are quite a few plural forms of 너—like "you guys" in English.

We have 너희(들) and 너네(들). They're interchangeable. But 너네 is more common than 너희 in spoken Korean. By the way 너희 is pronounced “너히”

You might also hear 니들, 니네(들), but these are only used informally.

The Plural Marker 들 is optional except for of course, 니들.

So 너희 and 너희들 are the same thing

**About 네 in 너네**

네 in 너네 is not "yes," but another plural suffix meaning "people of the same kind, class, or group."

While 들 can be used for all nouns like 연필들 - pencils, 네 can only come after pronouns.

네 can also function as a possessive marker instead of 의.

And 네 as the possessive marker has that same “your community” or “your group” type of feeling like

너네 가족 – Your family, 너네 집 – Your house, 너네 회사 – Your company

**Plural "You" - Polite**

In the last lesson, we learned about “title + 님”. So, you can simply attach 들 at the end—like 선생님들 (the teachers), 교수님들 (the professors).

But what if these people don’t have the same title?

You only have word to remember – 여러분(들).

Unlike singular **‘you,’ ‘여러분’ by itself is inherently polite and respectful.**

* **‘여러’** means **‘several’ or ‘multiple’**
* **‘분’** is the honorific form of **‘명’**, the counter for people.
* Together, they form **‘여러분’** to address **a group of people respectfully.**

**3rd person Pronouns in Korean**

Let’s talk about **3rd-person pronouns now.**

Pronouns in Korean are gender-neutral.

Because of this, most pronouns follow 이/저/그 + people format

Just like it’s rude to misgender people in English these days, in Korean, it’s crucial to **distinguish people from objects.** So, we should say “This person is my boss” instead of “This is my boss”

**3rd person (He, his & She, her)**

First things first, some textbooks will teach you that “he” is 그 and “she” is 그녀. But we never use these words in real life.

They’re only used in written texts, especially in a poetic way like in a song. Using them in speech will make you sound cringey. So, please don’t use them.

Use these instead.

**Casual Third Person**

If you're referring to a close friend, you use:

얘 (this person)
쟤 (that person)
걔 (that person)

**얘는 제 친구예요 / 이 펜은 쟤 거에요 / 거기는 걔네 집이에요.**

These come from 이, 저, 그 plus 아이, which means "kid.". But you can use 얘 쟤 걔 for adults too.

**Honorific Third Person**

For honorific, We simply add 분 after 이, 저, and 그. It’s the same 분 from 여러분.

So we get 이 분, 저 분, 그 분.

이 분은 제 선생님이에요.
저 분은 제 상사예요.
그 분은 제 교수님이에요.

**3rd person – plural (They, their)**

**"They" - Casual**

For casual "they," all you have to do is add the plural marker.

So we get:

얘들, 쟤들, 걔들

Or optionally: 얘네(들), 쟤네(들), 걔네(들)

Example: 얘들은 내 친구야. / 쟤네는 학생이야.

As you can see, you can use either 네 or 들, or both, to indicate plurality. The order is 네들, not 들네.

When you’re using 네, 들 is optional. 얘네(들) is slightly more commonly used than 얘들, by the way.

**"They" - Polite**

For polite "they," you can only add 들—not 네—after 분.

So it goes like: 이 분들, 저 분들, 그 분들.

Examples:

이 분들은 제 동료(들)입니다.
이 차는 저 분들 거야.
그 분들은 여러분(의) 손님(들)이에요.

Optional words are in parentheses.

Similarly, to say "their" you just add 의

and for "theirs," you add “의 거” or just “거”. So, 의 is optional for “theirs”.

**이들, 저들, 그들**

We also have 이들, 저들, and 그들. These are very neutral—neither casual nor polite. It’s sounds like “I don’t know them, they don’t know me and I don’t care.”

So, you can hear these words in formal settings like on the news or in a book, but they're not common in everyday speech.

이들은 한국 사람입니다.
