**Suggestions**

So, we’re talking about sentences like “Let’s go” or “Let’s sit”.

And there’s something you need to know before we get started.

Endings in suggestive sentences are inherently context markers because suggestion IS a context in an of itself.

And you’d use a different one for each speech level.

Let’s start with the easiest one. Casual speech.

All you have to is just add 자 after the verb stem.

It doesn’t even matter if the stem ends 받침 or not.

For example, 가다 to go becomes 가자 – Let’s go.

And 앉다 to sit becomes 앉자 – Let’s sit.

Now, for polite speech, it’s Honorific + 지(요) or just 죠 in short. Obviously, Koreans use the second more often. So, 가다 (gada - to go) becomes 가시다 (gasida) in its honorific form. And 가시다 becomes 가시죠.

And 앉다 becomes 앉으시다 then becomes 앉으시죠.

Let’s look at this book. The title is **'장모님, 오사카로 가시죠’**

**장모님 means ‘mother in law’ from the husband’s side. By the way, if you’re a girl, you’d call your husband’s mother ‘시어머님’.**

So altogether it means is 'Mother-in-law, let's go to Osaka respectfully.' - a very polite way to invite her to the trip.

Lastly, formal. Formal ending is 받침-sensitive. So it’s ㅂ시다 after a vowel.

가다 becomes 갑시다.

And it’s 읍시다 after a 받침. So, 앉다 becomes 앉읍시다.

Now, this form is “supposed to be” formal but I advise you. Don’t use it because it can sound rude. I don’t know why most Korean textbooks never talk about this. So, let me tell you how it exactly sounds.

This is something your boss would say to “politely suggest” that you do some type of work instead of just straight up telling you to do it when in fact, they’re actually TELLING you to do it. It’s practically a command.

So, what you should do instead is just stick to “~시죠” - the second one. It's the politest option.

**ㄹ/을까(요)?**

Now, while we’re still at suggestions, there’s one more form to talk about.

This form is used to ask about the listener’s opinion.

Depending on its usage, it has a similar tone to all these expressions like

“Shall I?”

“Should we?”

“How about doing?”

“Do you want to?”

“Do you think?”

This form is also 받침-sensitive.

가다 to go ends in a vowel so it becomes 갈까요? – Shall we go?

앉다 to sit ends in a 받침 so it becomes 앉을까요? – Shall we sit?

Let’s move on to commands.

**Commands**

How you make commands in Korean is not so different from making statements.

This is exactly like how commands in English work. In English, the verb doesn’t change for imperatives. “Go” is just “go,” right?

There’s only a very few minor differences for each speech level.

**Commands (Casual)**

So in 반말, 하다 turns into just 해.

But if you add **라**, it becomes much more forceful like you’re giving an order. So, depending on how you say it, it can sound rude. Note 라 only works for 반말.

**그냥 해(라) – Just do it**

**Commands (Polite)**

The way you make it into a polite speech is

First you make the verb into an honorific, and then conjugate that just like you would for a regular present tense.

So it ends in 세요.

나중에 하세요

**Commands (Formal)**

For formal commands, you make the honorific verb then you’d add ㅂ시오.

So, 가다 to go becomes 가시다 in honorific then it becomes 가십시오.

And 받다 to receive becomes 받으시다 then to 받으십시오.

This 십시오 ending though, is very rarely used in in real-life situations, so you don’t have to worry too much about it. I can’t even remember the last time I personally said it.

It’s almost strictly for making public formal announcements or written instructions.

**Example - Casual**

벗다 means to take off

The stem ends in ㅓ so the branch is 어 and 라 is optional so it’s 벗어라

Repeat after me 코트를 벗어

**Example - Polite**

모자를 벗으세요

벗다 becomes 벗으시다 in honorific since 벗 ends in a 받침, so it becomes 벗으세요

모자를 벗으세요

**Example – Formal**

안경을 벗으십시오.

으십시오 is the formal high suffix for imperative so it’s 벗으십시오

안경을 벗으십시오

**Negative Commands**

Now, Let's learn how to say **"Don't do something"** in Korean!

Take a **verb stem** and attach **지 말다** and then conjugate 말다

**💡 Note: What is 말다?**

* **말다** is a **helping verb**, which means it **cannot be used by itself**.
* It’s always used as part of another grammar form, like **지 말다**.

Its meaning is:
→ to stop
→ to cease
→ to not do (something)

**🧩 Conjugation of 말다**

**말다** is an **irregular verb.**

And we’re going to learn about irregular verbs properly later. So, for now I just want you to learn these by heart.

“Don’t do it” in 3 speech levels

Casual – 하지 마

Polite – 하지 마세요

Formal – 하지 마십시오

Again, the formal version is very uncommon in real life.

So just know that ~지 마세요 is the most commonly used and the most polite one.

And also, you might occasionally come across ~지 말아요 as if it were a regular verb but this one sounds poetic.

So, you’ll get away with using **마세요** the most in everyday polite speech.
