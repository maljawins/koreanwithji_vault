## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the various ways to make suggestions in Korean, focusing on the English equivalents of "let's" and "shall we." The lesson emphasizes that making a suggestion is essentially proposing an action to be done together with the listener. Because Korean relies heavily on social hierarchy and politeness levels, the instructor covers three distinct ways to phrase these suggestions: a standard polite version for acquaintances, a more inquisitive "shall we" form, and a formal version used by higher-ranking individuals.

The learning arc begins with the most common way to say "let's" in everyday polite situations, moves into the more nuanced "shall we" question form (including its irregular conjugations), and concludes with the formal command-style suggestion. Throughout the video, visual charts and example sentences demonstrate how verb stems transform when these endings are attached.

---

### Making Suggestions with "Let's" (Polite Level)
For most non-formal situations with acquaintances, the simplest way to say "let's" is to use the adverb 같이 (together) combined with the standard present tense 요 form. Visually, the instructor displays this as `같이 + 요 Form`. This literally translates to "we do [verb] together." The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this pattern:

- **같이 가요.** / Let's go together.
- **같이 밥 먹어요.** / Let's eat together.
- **한국어를 같이 공부해요.** / Let's study Korean together.
- **영화를 같이 봐요.** / Let's watch a movie together.

---

### Asking "Shall We?" with (으)ㄹ까요
A more common way to suggest an action is to phrase it as a question, equivalent to "shall we?" or "shall I?" in English. This is formed by attaching (으)ㄹ까요 to the verb stem. The instructor outlines the conjugation rules: attach 을까요 if the verb stem ends in a consonant, and attach ㄹ까요 if it ends in a vowel.

- **어디 갈까요?** / Where shall we go?
- **과일을 먹을까요?** / Shall we eat fruit?
- **뭘 만들까요?** / What shall we make?
- **제가 먼저 볼까요?** / Shall I watch it first?

The instructor also notes two important exceptions. First, if a verb stem ends in ㄹ, you simply attach 까요 (e.g., 만들다 becomes 만들까요). Second, for ㅂ-irregular verbs, the ㅂ is removed and replaced with 우 before adding ㄹ까요.

---

### Extra Polite and Honorific Suggestions
To be even more polite, especially when suggesting something to someone older or of higher status, the honorific infix (으)시 can be combined with the "shall we" ending. The instructor compares three levels of the same suggestion:

1. **같이 밥 먹으러 가요.** / Let's go eat a meal together. (Standard)
2. **같이 밥 먹으러 갈까요?** / Shall we go to eat a meal together? (More suggestive)
3. **같이 식사하러 가실까요?** / Shall we go to eat a meal together? (Extra polite/Honorific)

In the third example, the instructor points out the use of 식사(를) 하다 (to have a meal) as the honorific version of 밥(을) 먹다.

---

### Formal Suggestions with (으)ㅂ시다
The final form taught is the formal ending (으)ㅂ시다. This is constructed by attaching 읍시다 to stems ending in a consonant or ㅂ시다 to stems ending in a vowel. Examples shown include:

- **지금 합시다.** / Let's do it now.
- **갑시다.** / Let's go.

The instructor provides a critical social note: this form is primarily used when a higher-ranking person is speaking "down" to a lower-ranking person or a group. For instance, a group leader might use this with their members. It is generally not appropriate to use this toward someone of higher status than yourself.
