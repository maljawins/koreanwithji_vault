### Basic Request Pattern: Noun + 주세요
To ask for an item politely, the speaker follows a three-step process shown on screen. First, say the noun for the object desired. Second, attach the appropriate object marker, which is 을 if the noun ends in a consonant or 를 if it ends in a vowel. Finally, add the phrase 주세요. The resulting formula is **Noun + (을/를) 주세요**.

The instructor emphasizes that this expression is appropriate for anyone you are not close with or anyone older than you. It is a polite, standard way to conduct transactions or ask for favors. To help learners internalize this, a vocabulary list is provided for practice:
- **메뉴판** (menu)
- **파스타** (pasta)
- **샐러드** (salad)
- **후식** (dessert)
- **음료** (drink/beverage)

### Softening Requests with 조금 and 좀
The lesson reviews the adverb **조금**, which means "a little" or "kind of," as in the sentence **한국어를 조금 할 수 있어요** (I can speak a little bit of Korean). The instructor then introduces **좀**, which is the informal and shortened version of 조금. While they share the same literal meaning, **좀** has a special pragmatic function when used with requests like 주세요.

Using **좀** in a request has a "softening effect." It makes the command sound less harsh and more polite, similar to saying "Could you please..." instead of just "Give me..." in English. For example, while **물을 주세요** (Give me water) is grammatically correct and polite, **물을 좀 주세요** (Could you please give me some water) sounds more natural and gentle. This makes the listener more likely to want to fulfill the request.

### Conversation Practice: Ordering at a Restaurant
A sample dialogue between characters James and Jinyoung demonstrates these concepts in a real-world setting. James uses several variations of the request pattern:
> **저기요, 빵을 더 주세요.** / Excuse me, give me some more bread.
> **아, 물도 주세요.** / Ah, give me some water, too.
> **김치도 좀 주세요.** / Could you please also give me some kimchi?

In these examples, James uses **더** (more) and **도** (also/too) alongside the basic 주세요 structure. The final sentence uses **좀** to demonstrate the softening effect discussed previously.

---

### Grammar Notes and Speech Levels
The instructor explains that **주세요** originates from the action verb **주다** (to give). The **세요** ending is a specific honorific piece of speech used to show respect to the listener. Because Korean culture places high importance on politeness—especially when asking for favors—this honorific form is the standard for requests.

However, speech levels change based on the relationship. When speaking to close friends of the same age or younger, the polite **주세요** is replaced with the casual form **줘**. For example, one might say **피자를 줘** (Give me pizza) to a best friend. The instructor warns that using **줘** with anyone else would be considered very impolite.
