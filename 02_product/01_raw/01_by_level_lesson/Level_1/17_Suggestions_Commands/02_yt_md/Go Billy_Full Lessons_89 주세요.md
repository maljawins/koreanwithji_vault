### Overview

---

### The Basic Structure of 주세요

The word 주세요 literally translates to "give me," but in a social context, it functions as a polite way to say "please give me." To form a request, a speaker takes the noun for the item they want and attaches the appropriate object marker. If the noun ends in a consonant, the marker 을 is used; if it ends in a vowel, 를 is used.

The instructor emphasizes that while English speakers might use long, indirect phrases like "Could you get me some..." or "Can I have...", Korean speakers can simply use the noun + object marker + 주세요. This is perfectly acceptable and polite in almost any standard situation, such as ordering at a restaurant. For example, to ask for a menu, one says 메뉴를 주세요 (Please give me a menu). To ask for water, one says 물을 주세요 (Please give me water).

---

### Modifying Requests with 더, 많이, and 조금

To make requests more specific regarding quantity, several adverbs can be placed directly before 주세요. The word 더 means "more." When placed before the verb, it creates a request for an additional amount of an item. For instance, 물을 더 주세요 means "Please give me more water."

If a speaker wants to specify a large or small amount, they use 많이 (a lot) or 조금 (a little). Placing 많이 before the verb, as in 김치를 많이 주세요, translates to "Please give me a lot of kimchi." Conversely, using 조금 as in 밥을 조금 주세요 translates to "Please give me a little bit of rice."

---

### Combining Quantity Adverbs

It is possible to combine certain quantity modifiers to be even more precise. The instructor demonstrates combining 조금 (a little) with 더 (more) to mean "a little more." An example of this is 고기를 조금 더 주세요 (Please give me a little bit more meat). However, there is a grammatical restriction: 많이 (a lot) cannot be combined with 더 (more). To express a desire for a lot more, a speaker should simply use 더 by itself to indicate "more" or 많이 by itself to indicate "a lot," depending on the context.
