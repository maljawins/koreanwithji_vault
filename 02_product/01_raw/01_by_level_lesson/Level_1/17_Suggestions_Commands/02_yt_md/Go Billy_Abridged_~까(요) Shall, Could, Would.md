## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the beginner-level grammar form ~(으)ㄹ까요, which is primarily used to ask for the listener's opinion by suggesting a possibility. The lesson covers the mechanical conjugation rules for both action and descriptive verbs, addresses common irregulars, and explores how the English translation shifts based on the type of verb used. While action verbs often translate as "shall we" or "should I," descriptive verbs typically translate as "do you think" or "could it be."

The learning arc begins with the "how-to" of conjugation, moves into the nuance of meaning for different verb classes, provides practical example sentences, and concludes with a specific idiomatic usage involving nouns. By the end of the lesson, students should understand that ~(으)ㄹ까요 is not a simple question about facts, but a way to probe the listener's thoughts on a potential scenario.

---

### Conjugation Rules
To create this form, one must start with the verb stem, which is the unconjugated verb minus the 다 ending. The instructor emphasizes that you cannot find a verb stem from a already conjugated form like 해요; you must go back to the dictionary form 하다. The attachment rules depend on the final letter of the stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach 을까요. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, or specifically the letter ㄹ, you attach ㄹ까요.

The instructor demonstrates these rules with three examples on the whiteboard. For the verb 하다 (to do), the stem 하 ends in a vowel, so it becomes 할까요. For the verb 먹다 (to eat), the stem 먹 ends in a consonant, resulting in 먹을까요. For the verb 살다 (to live), the stem already ends in ㄹ, so you simply add 까요 to get 살까요. For casual speech, the final 요 can be dropped, leaving just ~(으)ㄹ까.

---

### Irregular Exceptions
A specific exception is highlighted for descriptive verbs ending in the consonant ㅂ. In these cases, the ㅂ is removed from the stem and replaced with the syllable 우 before finishing the conjugation. The instructor uses the example 맵다 (to be spicy). The stem 맵 loses the ㅂ, becomes 매우, and then follows the vowel rule to become 매울까요. This pattern is consistent for most ㅂ irregular descriptive verbs.

---

### Meaning and Usage: Action Verbs
When paired with action verbs, ~(으)ㄹ까요 functions as a suggestion or a request for the listener's preference. It translates naturally into English as "Shall I/we...?", "Should I/we...?", "Do you want to...?", or "How about...?" The instructor explains that the literal Korean logic is "I am suggesting a possibility; what do you think about it?"

The subject of the sentence determines whether the suggestion involves just the speaker or the group. Adding the pronoun 제가 (I) makes it clear the speaker is asking "Shall I...?", whereas omitting the subject often implies "Shall we...?" depending on context. Examples provided include:
> **내일 같이 갈까요?** / Shall we go together tomorrow?
> **제가 아침에 전화할까요?** / Shall I call in the morning?
> **그럼 다음 주에 영화 볼까요?** / Then shall we watch a movie next week?

---

### Meaning and Usage: Descriptive Verbs
With descriptive verbs, the form shifts from making a suggestion to asking for the listener's conjecture or wonder. It translates as "Do you think...?", "Could it be...?", or "Would it be...?" The instructor notes that even some action verbs, like 어울리다 (to match/suit), translate better this way. The nuance is different from a standard question; instead of asking "Is it hot?", the speaker is asking "Do you think it might be hot?" Examples include:
> **커피가 뜨거울까요?** / Do you think the coffee is hot?
> **내일은 더 더울까요?** / Do you think tomorrow will be hotter?
> **빨간색은 어울릴까요?** / Do you think red would suit/match?
> **이 옷은 작을까요?** / Do you think these clothes would be small?

---

### Noun Usage: ~(이)랄까
The instructor introduces a related shortened form used with nouns: ~(이)랄까. This is a contraction of ~(이)라고 할까, where 할까 comes from the verb 말하다 (to speak/say). It literally means "Should I say...?" or "How should I put it?" and is used when the speaker is searching for the right word or expressing a tentative feeling. If the noun ends in a consonant, use 이랄까; if it ends in a vowel, use 랄까. An example sentence is provided:
> **오래 알고 지낸 느낌이랄까?** / Should I say it's a feeling like we've known each other for a long time?
