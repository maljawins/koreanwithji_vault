## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces four distinct ways to express "let's" in Korean, ranging from very casual to polite and honorific. The lesson is structured as a progression through speech levels, starting with the most informal way to make suggestions to friends and ending with a polite form that can be used with strangers or older individuals. Each form is explained through its conjugation rules, social nuances, and specific usage cases, including how to handle negative suggestions.

---

### Casual Suggestions with ~자

The most basic and casual way to say "let's" is by using the ending **~자**. This is strictly reserved for **반말 (casual speech)** and should only be used with friends of the same age, people younger than the speaker, or those with whom a casual speech agreement has been established. To conjugate, one simply takes the action verb stem (removing the **다**) and attaches **자**. The instructor emphasizes using the regular verb stem rather than a past tense stem.

> **같이 밥 먹으러 가자.** / Let's go eat a meal together.
> **만나서 얘기하자.** / Let's meet and talk.

For negative suggestions (let's not...), the instructor notes that the standard **안** negation is not used. Instead, the ending **~지 말다** is employed. The **다** is removed from **말다** and replaced with **자**, resulting in **~지 말자**.

> **가지 말자.** / Let's not go.

---

### Using the ~요 Form for Suggestions

A slightly more polite but still informal way to suggest doing something together is to simply use the standard **~요** form of an action verb. While this uses the polite ending, the instructor cautions that it is still not appropriate for formal situations, strangers, or people significantly older, such as a boss. It feels "softly" polite but remains relatively casual. Adding the word **같이 (together)** is recommended to clarify that the speaker is making a suggestion for a group activity rather than just stating their own intention.

> **같이 점심 먹어요.** / Let's eat lunch together.
> **드라마 같이 봐요.** / Let's watch a drama together.
> **같이 놀러 가요.** / Let's go hang out together.

---

### Formal Suggestions with ~(으)ㅂ시다

The ending **~(으)ㅂ시다** is a formal way to say "let's," but it carries a specific social nuance. The instructor explains that this form is often used by a higher-ranking person addressing a lower-ranking group—essentially "talking down" in a polite manner. Because of this authoritative undertone, it is generally avoided with strangers or superiors. 

The conjugation rules are as follows:
- If the verb stem ends in a consonant, attach **~읍시다**.
- If the verb stem ends in a vowel, attach **~ㅂ시다**.
- If the verb stem ends in **ㄹ**, remove the **ㄹ** and attach **~ㅂ시다**.

> **빨리 갑시다.** / Let's hurry up and go.
> **지금 공부합시다.** / Let's study now.
> **밥 먹읍시다.** / Let's eat.

---

### Polite Honorific Suggestions with ~(으)시죠

The most polite and "soft" way to make a suggestion, suitable for use with strangers or elders, is **~(으)시죠**. This form is conjugated by adding **~으시죠** to stems ending in a consonant or **~시죠** to stems ending in a vowel (with the same **ㄹ** removal rule as above). 

The instructor reveals a "dark secret" about this form: it is technically a polite, honorific command. It combines the honorific marker **~(으)시** with the **~죠** (from **~지요**) ending, which seeks agreement. Therefore, it literally means "Do [action], okay?" However, in practice, it functions as a very respectful "let's."

> **다음에 같이 낚시하러 가시죠.** / Let's go fishing together next time. (Literally: Go fishing together next time, okay?)
> **앉으시죠.** / Let's sit down. / Please have a seat.

A critical exception occurs with the verb **먹다 (to eat)**. Because **~(으)시죠** is an honorific form, one must use the honorific verb **드시다** instead of **먹다**, resulting in **드시죠**. This is often used to tell an older person to start eating so that the rest of the group may begin.

> **드시죠.** / Let's eat. / Please start eating.
