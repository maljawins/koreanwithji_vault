## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor introduces the "Shall we?" or "Shall I?" form in Korean, which is used to make suggestions or ask for someone's opinion on a future action. The lesson covers the grammatical construction of this form, how to adjust it for different politeness levels, and an important secondary meaning involving speculation or "wondering" about a situation. The video uses a mix of live-action skits and on-screen text to demonstrate these concepts in context.

---

### How to Form "Shall we?" / "Shall I?"

The instructor explains that this form is simple to create by manipulating the verb stem. The basic formula displayed on screen is: **v.s. + (을/ㄹ) + 까(요)**.

To conjugate a verb into this form, you first take the verb stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, you add **을까**. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, you add **ㄹ까** directly to the bottom of the stem. If the verb stem already ends in **ㄹ**, you simply delete that **ㄹ** before adding the ending. To make the expression polite, you add the particle **요** at the end.

The instructor provides a practical example in a casual context, shown through a skit where he sits on stairs and contemplates his weight. He asks himself:
> **운동을 할까?** / Should I do some exercise?

Because there is no **요** at the end of this sentence, it is strictly for casual situations, such as talking to oneself or speaking to a very close friend.

---

### The "Wondering" Meaning: "Could it be...?"

Beyond making suggestions, this grammatical form has a second major function: expressing wonder or speculation about a possibility. In this context, it translates closely to the English "Could...?" or "I wonder if...?" The instructor clarifies that this is not the "could" of physical ability or capability, but rather the "could" of uncertainty and questioning.

The screen transitions to a blue background to list several examples of this speculative usage:

> **그 사람이 집에 있을까요?** / Could he be at home?
> **내일 비가 올까요?** / Could it rain tomorrow?
> **정말 그럴까요?** / Could it really be so?
> **지금 뭘 하고 있을까요?** / What could he be doing now?

In these examples, the speaker is not suggesting an action but is instead wondering aloud about a state of affairs or a future event. The addition of **요** in these examples makes them appropriate for polite conversation.
