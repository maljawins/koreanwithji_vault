**Verb Conjugation: The ‘어’ Suffix**

Previously, we learned that **the branch** changes depending on **the final vowel** of the verb stem.

* If the stem ends in **ㅏ** or **ㅗ**, the branch is **아**.
* If the stem ends in **any other vowel**, the branch is **어**.

For **formal high speech**, the **vowel doesn’t matter**—only whether the verb stem **has a 받침 (final consonant) or not.**

Now, let’s take a deeper look at how the **어** suffix works.

In our last lesson, we covered **적다** (*to write down*).

If a verb stem **ends in a consonant** and its final vowel **is not** ㅏ or ㅗ, you simply **add 어** after the stem.

✔ **있다 → 있어**
✔ **먹다 → 먹어**
✔ **입다 → 입어**

But in some cases, **어 gets canceled out or merges with the stem** depending on the ending.

**When 어 Cancels Out or Merges**

If the verb stem **doesn’t** end in ㅏ or ㅗ **and has no 받침**, **어** is either removed or merges with the stem.

✔ **If the stem ends in ㅓ or ㅕ:**

* The **어** sound becomes redundant, so it cancels out.
* **서다 → 서 (not 서어)**
* **켜다 → 켜 (not 켜어)**

✔ **If the stem ends in ㅐ or ㅔ:**

* **어** is also canceled out.
* **보내다 → 보내 (not 보내어)**
* **세다 → 세 (not 세어)**

💡 **Technically,** 보내어 and 세어 are grammatically correct, but they sound very poetic and are mostly used in **songs** or literary writing.

**What Happens When the Stem Ends in ㅡ?**

Remember **ㅡ** (*the neutral placeholder vowel*)?

When **어** is added, it **takes over** and **ㅡ disappears**:

✔ **쓰다 → 써 (not 쓰어)**

**When the Stem Ends in ㅣ or ㅜ**

어 merges with the stem and becomes 여 and 워 just like 보다 previously.

이 + 어, if you say it fast, it’s 여

우 + 어, if you say it fast, it’s 워
