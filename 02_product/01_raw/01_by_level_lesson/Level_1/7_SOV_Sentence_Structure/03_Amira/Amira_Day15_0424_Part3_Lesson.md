## Teaching Points

The lesson focuses on the nuanced application of the `~야` suffix and its role in expressing necessity.

*   **The "Only If" Logic of `~야`**: Ji explains that `~야` functions as a strict conditional. It identifies the *only* case or condition under which the following statement can be true or acceptable.
    *   **Structure**: Verb Stem + `아/어/여` + `야`.
    *   **Example**: `영어를 배워야 호주에서 편하게 살 수 있어요.` (Only if you learn English can you live comfortably in Australia / You need to learn English in order to live comfortably in Australia.)
    *   Ji highlights that while English might use a variety of words (need to, must, in order to), Korean uses the succinct `~야` to convey this entire sense of prerequisite necessity.

*   **Interchangeability of `하다` and `되다`**: When expressing obligation (must/should), `~야 하다` and `~야 되다` are often interchangeable. Ji notes that `~야 하다` is slightly more formal and less common in casual spoken Korean than `~야 되다`.

*   **Adverbial Usage**: The lesson briefly touches on the adverb `편하게` (comfortably), derived from `편하다` (to be comfortable), used within the context of the main example sentence.
