## Teaching Points

*   **Natural Phrasing vs. Literal Translation:**
    *   In English, one says "to see a doctor," but in Korean, the natural expression is `병원에 가다` (to go to the hospital/clinic). Using `의사를 만나다` (to meet a doctor) sounds like a business meeting or social engagement rather than a medical visit.
    *   `열이 나다` (to have a fever): Ji highlights the versatility of the verb `나다` (to occur/happen/break out), noting it is as fundamental as `하다` and `되다`.
*   **Sentence Structure (STLOHV):**
    *   Korean sentences move from **Broad to Specific**.
    *   **Word Order Flexibility:** While the verb must stay at the end, other elements can shift. Placing a noun immediately before the verb emphasizes it as the most important information. For example, `집에서 한국어를 공부해요` (What do you study at home? -> Korean) vs. `한국어를 집에서 공부해요` (Where do you study Korean? -> At home).
*   **Particle Review:**
    *   **Topic (`은/는`) vs. Subject (`이/가`):** `은/는` introduces a new topic, states general facts, or implies contrast. `이/가` marks the subject of the specific verb and answers "Who/What did it?"
    *   **Contractions:** In spoken Korean, `나는` becomes `난`, `너는` becomes `넌`, and `것이` becomes `게`.
    *   **Special Pronoun Changes:** When followed by `가`, `나` becomes `내가`, `저` becomes `제가`, and `너` becomes `네가` (often pronounced `니가` to avoid confusion).
    *   **Object Marker (`을/를`):** Used only with action verbs. Descriptive verbs (like `좋다`, `있다`, `필요하다`) cannot take objects.
*   **Verb Categories:**
    *   **Passive vs. Active:** `쓰다` (to write) vs. `쓰이다` (to be written).
    *   **Transitive vs. Intransitive:** `달리다` (to run - no object) vs. `좋아하다` (to like - takes an object).
    *   **Causative:** `깨다` (to wake up) vs. `깨우다` (to wake someone up).
*   **The `~서` Connector:**
    *   Expresses reason (because/so).
    *   Expresses a "continual state" or "connected purpose." `친구랑 만나서 영화관에 갔어요` implies meeting the friend and going to the theater *together*, whereas `친구랑 만나고 영화관에 갔어요` implies meeting the friend, finishing that action, and then going to the theater (possibly alone).

## Lesson Flow

*   **Homework Correction:** Ji opens by reviewing Amira's sentence `열이 나면 의사를 만나야 합니다`. He explains that while grammatically correct, `병원에 가다` is the culturally natural way to express seeking medical help. He then corrects a spelling mistake in `빨리` (fast) and suggests using the topic marker `는` in `필요는 없어요` to add a nuance of contrast.
*   **Contrast and Tense:** Moving to the sentence `저는 김치를 좋아하지만 김밥을 싫어해요`, Ji suggests using `김밥은` to make the contrast more "vibrant." In the sentence about being late to school, he explains that the tenses must match: `일찍 일어났지만 학교에 늦었어요` (I woke up early but was late). He introduces the "fancier" verb `지각하다` (to be late for school/work).
*   **Advanced Connectors:** Ji praises Amira for using `~서` in `너무 뜨거워서 못 먹어요`. Amira mentions she sees it often in example sentences. Ji uses this to explain the difference between `~고` (sequential/separate actions) and `~서` (connected actions/shared results).
*   **Speech Level Consistency:** Ji corrects `저도 쉴 수 있으면 좋겠어`, noting that if the humble `저` is used, the sentence must end with the polite `요`. Alternatively, she can use the casual `나` with the casual ending.
*   **The STLOHV Framework:** Ji transitions to a PowerPoint presentation. He explains the philosophy of Korean word order, arguing that "Time" and "Space" are objectively broader than the "Self," which is why `아침에 저는` (In the morning, I...) is a logical starting point.
*   **Deep Dive into Particles:** The lesson moves into a systematic review of particles. Ji uses a Venn diagram to show the overlap between Topic and Subject markers. He warns Amira about a common pitfall: using object markers with descriptive verbs like `필요하다`.
*   **Verb Nuances:** Ji briefly introduces active/passive and transitive/intransitive concepts. He uses a humorous example with the verb `묻다`: `그분을 물어볼게요` could mean "I will ask him" (irregular) or "I will bite him" (regular), depending on the particle and conjugation.
