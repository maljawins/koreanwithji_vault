## Teaching Points

Ji provides an in-depth explanation of future tense variations and conditional connectors, integrating visual aids from his PowerPoint slides and live annotations:

*   **Future Tense Suffix `ㄹ/을`:** Ji explains that this suffix signals potential, intention, or something "yet to happen." It is the root for several grammar forms:
    *   `ㄹ/을 거다`: The standard future tense, also used for conjecture (e.g., "I guess he will...").
    *   `ㄹ/을게(요)`: The "Promise Form." Used only with 1st person subjects (`I` or `We`). It implies a favor or a considerate notification to the listener (e.g., `저녁은 우리가 살게` / We will buy dinner).
    *   `ㄹ/을래(요)`: The "Intention Form." It is more assertive and used to state what the speaker wants to do or to ask the listener's intention in an inquisitive sense (e.g., `커피 마실래?` / Do you want to drink coffee?).
*   **The Connector `~야` (Only if):** Ji introduces `~야` as a more emphatic version of `~면` (if). 
    *   On screen, he shows the conjugation rule: Stem + `아/어/여` + `야`.
    *   Example: `매워야 맛있어요` (It is tasty only if it is spicy).
*   **Expressing Obligation with `~야 되다/하다`:** Since Korean lacks a single word for "must" or "should," Ji explains that combining the "only if" connector with `되다` (to be okay) or `하다` (to do) creates the meaning of obligation.
    *   `~야 되다`: "It is acceptable only if [action]," which translates to "must" or "have to."
    *   Example: `병원에 가야 돼` (I have to go to the hospital).
*   **Comparison of `~면` vs. `~야`:** Ji uses the example of tea temperature to show the difference. `차가 뜨거우면 조심하세요` (If the tea is hot, be careful) is a general condition, whereas `차가 뜨거워야 조심하세요` (Only if the tea is hot, be careful) emphasizes that the heat is the specific reason for caution.

## Lesson Flow

The lesson proceeds through a series of comparative explanations and student-led inquiries:

1.  **Future Tense Nuances:** Ji opens by addressing Amira's reliance on the `~ㄹ 거예요` ending. He uses a PowerPoint table to compare `~ㄹ 거다`, `~ㄹ게`, and `~ㄹ래`. He explains that `~ㄹ게` is "considerate" (like a promise), while `~ㄹ래` is "affirmative" (stating intention).
2.  **Contextual Examples:** To clarify the difference, Ji types out a scenario: `오늘은 집에 있을래` (I'm staying home—assertive) versus `오늘은 그냥 집에 있을게` (I'll just stay home—apologetic/promise). Amira asks which one to use when inviting someone over, and Ji corrects her to use the inquisitive `~ㄹ래요?` form (`우리 집에 올래?`).
3.  **Introduction of `~야`:** Ji moves to the connector `~야`. He explains it follows the same conjugation rules as the `~아/어/여요` present tense. He uses the example of `뜨겁다` (to be hot) becoming `뜨거워야` (only if it's hot).
4.  **Obligation and `되다`:** Ji explains how `~야` combines with `되다` to mean "must." He provides the example `병원에 가야 돼` (I have to go to the hospital). He explains that `되다` literally means "to be acceptable," so the phrase means "It is only acceptable if I go to the hospital."
5.  **Student Practice and Refinement:** Amira asks how to warn someone about hot tea. Ji helps her construct `차가 뜨거우면 조심해야 돼요` (If the tea is hot, you must be careful), showing how both a standard conditional (`~면`) and an obligation (`~야 돼요`) can exist in one sentence.
6.  **Cultural Context:** Ji uses food examples, like Somali soup or Korean stews (`국` or `탕`), to explain that some things are only "tasty" (`맛있어요`) if they are "hot" (`뜨거워야`).
