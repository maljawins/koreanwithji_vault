### 반말 vs 존댓말 — The Two Ways to Speak Korean

The instructor explains that there are two primary ways to speak Korean: **반말 (casual)** and **존댓말 (formal)**. The choice between these two is not just about grammar but about the relationship between the speaker and the listener.

- **존댓말 (Formal):** This is the polite way of speaking. A beginner can easily recognize it by looking at the end of a sentence. If a sentence ends in **요** or **니다**, it is almost certainly formal.
- **반말 (Casual):** This is used with people you are close to. While it has many forms, a general rule of thumb is that 반말 is always shorter than its 존댓말 equivalent.

The instructor clarifies a common misconception: it is not strictly about age. While you generally use formal speech for those older and casual for those younger, intimacy and social context matter more. For instance, the instructor speaks casual Korean with her parents (high intimacy) but uses formal Korean with younger coworkers (professional context).

---

### Social Rules for Politeness Levels

The video outlines three basic rules for when to use each level:
1. **Strangers:** Use **존댓말** with people you have just met or strangers, regardless of their age (except for very small children).
2. **Age/Status:** If you are a minor or a student, you must use **존댓말** to adults.
3. **Intimacy:** Use **반말** only with people you personally know well or those with whom you have a mutual agreement to speak casually.

### Formal Self-Introductions and Greetings

The instructor demonstrates how to introduce oneself formally using the polite word for "I," which is **저**.

**The "I am..." Sentence Structure:**
- **저는 [Name/Noun] 입니다.** (Very formal)
- **저는 [Name/Noun] 이에요 / 예요.** (Standard polite)

The choice between **이에요** and **예요** depends on the final consonant (받침) of the preceding noun:
- If the word ends with a **받침** (consonant): use **이에요**.
  > **저는 선생님이에요.** / I am a teacher.
- If the word ends **without a 받침** (vowel): use **예요**.
  > **저는 여자예요.** / I am a woman.

**Formal Greetings:**
- **안녕하세요.** / Hello (Polite).
- **만나서 반갑습니다.** / Nice to meet you (Formal).
- **만나서 반가워요.** / Nice to meet you (Polite).
- **감사합니다.** / Thank you (Formal).
- **고마워요.** / Thank you (Polite).

---

### Casual Self-Introductions and Greetings

In casual settings (**반말**), the word for "I" changes from 저 to **나**.

**The "I am..." Sentence Structure:**
- **나는 [Name/Noun] 이야 / 야.**

Similar to the formal version, the ending changes based on the final consonant:
- If the word ends with a **받침**: use **이야**.
  > **나는 사람이야.** / I am a human.
- If the word ends **without a 받침**: use **야**.
  > **나는 비키야.** / I am Vicky.

**Casual Greetings:**
- **안녕.** / Hi / Bye (Casual).
- **고마워.** / Thanks (Casual).

---

### The "You" Problem in Korean

The instructor highlights a critical difference between English and Korean: there is no fixed, safe word for "you" in formal Korean. While **너** exists for "you" in casual speech, using a direct translation of "you" in a formal context can often sound rude or aggressive.

Instead of saying "you," Koreans typically:
1. Use the person's **Title** (e.g., **선생님** for teacher).
2. Use the person's **Name + 씨**.
3. Omit the subject entirely if context makes it clear who is being addressed.

The instructor provides a dialogue example where a student addresses a teacher:
> **선생님, 안녕하세요.** / Teacher, hello. (Instead of "Hello, you.")
> **선생님은요?** / How about you, teacher? (Literally: "As for teacher?")
