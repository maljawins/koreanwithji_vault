## Teaching Points

### Overview

The instructor focuses on the nuances of addressing others in casual speech (반말), specifically how to say "you." While previous lessons covered honorific and polite ways to address people, casual speech introduces unique terms and social rules. A core principle of Korean remains: the word "you" is often omitted because the subject is assumed through context or honorific titles. However, in casual settings where titles are dropped, speakers must know how to use direct address correctly to avoid sounding rude or aggressive.

---

### Using (or Not Using) 너

The most common casual word for "you" is **너**. While it is the standard dictionary translation, its usage is strictly limited by social hierarchy. It can only be used with close friends who are either the same age as the speaker or younger. It is never appropriate to use **너** toward someone older, even if the relationship is very close and the older person has given permission to speak casually. Using **너** incorrectly, such as toward a stranger or a senior, can sound incredibly offensive, almost like a swear word, because it carries a nuance of "talking down" to someone.

Couples are a notable exception where **너** might be used as they are considered equals, though many couples still prefer specific titles like **오빠 (older brother)** or **누나 (older sister)** depending on their dynamic. For older friends, speakers should stick to familial titles like **형 (older brother for males)**, **오빠 (older brother for females)**, **누나 (older sister for males)**, or **언니 (older sister for females)** even when using casual grammar.

---

### Asking What to Call Someone

Because the rules for addressing others can be complex even for native speakers, it is common to ask directly how someone would like to be referred to. The instructor provides three casual ways to ask this:

> **내가 어떻게 부르면 좋을까?** / How should I refer to you?
> **내가 뭐라고 부를까?** / What shall I call you?
> **내가 뭐라고 부르면 돼?** / What should I call you?

To respond to such a question casually, one can use the person's name followed by the particle **라고** (after a vowel) or **이라고** (after a consonant). For example:

> **빌리라고 불러줘.** / Please call me Billy.

---

### Using Names and the 이 Suffix

When talking about a friend who is the same age or younger, using their name is often considered friendlier and softer than using **너**. When referring to such a friend in a sentence, the suffix **이** is attached to names ending in a consonant, while names ending in a vowel remain as-is. For example, a friend named **민우** is just **민우**, but a friend named **백현** becomes **백현이**.

This naming convention is strictly for friends and should never be used for superiors like a boss. Additionally, names can be combined with familial titles for older friends, such as **민우 형** or **백현이 오빠**.

---

### Calling Out to Friends (Vocative Particles)

The rules change slightly when calling out to someone to get their attention. In these cases, the **이** suffix is not used. Instead, speakers use the vocative particles **아** or **야**.

- If the name ends in a vowel, attach **야**: **민우야!**
- If the name ends in a consonant, attach **아**: **백현아!**

The instructor notes that this particle **야** is distinct from the exclamation **야!**, which is a strong, often rude way to yell "Hey!" at someone.

---

### Listening Practice and Summary

The lesson includes a dialogue between two friends, **우진** and **태민**, meeting by chance. **우진** calls out using the vocative particle: **어? 태민아, 어디 가? (Huh? Taemin, where are you going?)**. **태민** responds using the name of a mutual friend with the **이** suffix: **나 수연이 만나러 가. (I'm going to meet Suyeon.)**. He then uses **너** to address **우진**: **너도 같이 갈래? (Do you want to go together too?)**.

The instructor concludes by advising learners not to overstress about perfect politeness levels. Native speakers generally appreciate the effort and understand that learners may make mistakes. However, mastering these distinctions demonstrates a deeper understanding of Korean language and culture.
