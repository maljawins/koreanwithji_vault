### Overview

The instructor introduces the most formal speech level used in modern-day Korean, commonly referred to as the 니다 form. While many learners are introduced to polite speech (존댓말) through the 요 form, this lesson focuses on the higher tier of politeness used in official, professional, and highly respectful contexts. The learning arc covers the mechanical conjugation of both statements and questions, the specific social situations that demand this level of formality, and how it can be combined with honorific suffixes for maximum respect.

---

### Conjugating the 니다 and 니까 Forms

The 니다 form is the standard conjugation for statements in formal speech. To create it, one takes a verb stem and attaches either ㅂ니다 or 씁니다. If the verb stem ends in a vowel, ㅂ니다 is attached directly to the bottom of the stem. If the verb stem ends in a consonant, 씁니다 is added. A special rule applies to stems ending in the consonant ㄹ: the ㄹ must be removed before attaching ㅂ니다. The instructor provides three visual examples: 가다 (to go) becomes 갑니다, 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹습니다, and 살다 (to live) becomes 삽니다.

When asking a question in this formal level, the ending changes to 니까. The conjugation rules remain identical to the statement form: use ㅂ니까 for vowel stems, 씁니까 for consonant stems, and remove the ㄹ from stems ending in that letter before adding ㅂ니까. Using the previous examples, 가다 becomes 갑니까?, 먹다 becomes 먹습니까?, and 살다 becomes 삽니까?. Both the 니다 and 니까 endings are classified as 존댓말 (polite speech), but they sit at a higher level of formality and respect than the 요 form.

---

### Defining Formal Speech and Common Phrases

Formal speech carries an "official" or "public" tone. Because of its high level of respect and social distance, it is typically not used with friends, family members, or regular acquaintances. However, several fixed expressions in Korean default to this formal level regardless of the specific relationship, simply because they are standard social formulas. The instructor lists several on screen: 감사합니다 (thank you), 만나서 반갑습니다 (nice to meet you), 잘 먹겠습니다 and 잘 먹었습니다 (used before and after meals), and 생일 축하합니다 (happy birthday).

---

### When to Use Formal Speech

A common misconception among learners is that formal speech is reserved only for the most extreme or rare ceremonies. In reality, it is used whenever a speaker wants to imbue their words with a strong, respectful, or official feel. It signals that the speech has a larger or more important purpose beyond casual conversation. Common situations include making official announcements, giving business presentations, writing formal letters, and speaking in the Korean military. It is also the appropriate choice when meeting a new boss, a new teacher, or one's future parents-in-law for the first time.

The instructor emphasizes that using the 요 form in these specific high-formality situations can actually sound too informal and, by extension, impolite. For instance, saying 만나서 반가워요 to a new boss instead of 만나서 반갑습니다 would be considered rude. While the 요 form is polite, the 니다 form shows the extra layer of respect required by these specific social hierarchies.

---

### Combining Formal and Honorific Speech

Formal speech (how you speak to someone) can be combined with honorific speech (how you speak about someone) for maximum politeness. This is achieved by adding the honorific suffix 으시다 to the verb stem before applying the 니다 or 니까 endings. The instructor demonstrates this with a comparison: asking a boss "Did you receive the project files?" using only formal speech would be 프로젝트 파일을 받았습니까?. To make it even more respectful toward the boss, the speaker should use the honorific version: 프로젝트 파일을 받으셨습니까?. This combination of the 니다 form and the honorific suffix is the highest level of politeness in standard modern Korean.

---

### Summary and Speech Level Classification

In linguistic terms, the 니다 and 니까 forms are part of a speech level called 하십시오체 or 합쇼체. This is the most formal type of 존댓말 used today. To summarize the distinctions: formal speech (하십시오체) shows respect toward the person you are speaking to, while honorific speech (으시다) shows respect toward the person you are speaking about. These two systems work together to navigate the complex social hierarchies of Korean culture.
