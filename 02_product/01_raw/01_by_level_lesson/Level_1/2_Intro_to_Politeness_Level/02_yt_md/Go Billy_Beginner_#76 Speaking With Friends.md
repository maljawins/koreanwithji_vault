### Basic Rules of Casual Korean (반말)
To speak casually, the speaker should conjugate a verb into its standard polite form and then simply drop the 요 at the end. Additionally, the speaker must avoid using honorific speech about the listener or humble speech about themselves. This logic applies to nearly all grammar forms previously learned in the course. The instructor emphasizes that while being too polite might be slightly awkward, being too casual can be perceived as rude, so 반말 should be reserved for close friends of the same or younger age.

---

### Casual Pronouns: "I" and "You"
In casual speech, the humble pronoun 저 (I/me) is replaced by the casual pronoun 나. This change affects how the word combines with various particles. The instructor provides a breakdown of these transformations:
- **나 + 의 (possessive):** becomes 나의 or the contracted 내 (my).
- **나 + 가 (subject):** becomes 내가 (I).
- **나 + 는 (topic):** becomes 나는 or the contracted 난 (I).
- **나 + 를 (object):** becomes 나를 or the contracted 날 (me).

Addressing the listener as "you" is more complex. For friends of the same age or younger, the instructor recommends using their name. If the name ends in a consonant, attach 이 to the end (e.g., 백현 becomes 백현이). While the word 너 means "you," it can sound like the speaker is "speaking down" to someone, so it should only be used in very close relationships. For older friends, learners should continue using titles like 형, 오빠, 누나, or 언니 rather than casual pronouns.

The pronoun 너 also has specific particle combinations:
- **너 + 의:** becomes 너의 or 네. Because 네 (your) sounds identical to 내 (my), it is often pronounced as 니 in spoken Korean, though the spelling remains the same.
- **너 + 가:** becomes 네가. Similarly, to distinguish it from 내가 (I), it is frequently pronounced as 네가 (with a distinct 'e' sound) or even 니가 in speech.
- **너 + 는 / 너 + 를:** become 너는/넌 and 너를/널 respectively.

---

### Yes, No, and the Verbs "To Be"
The standard affirmative 네 and negative 아니요 change in casual settings. "Yes" becomes 응 (or the more common 어), and "No" becomes 아니. The verbs 이다 (to be) and 아니다 (to not be) also have unique casual conjugations:
- **이다:** becomes 이야 after a consonant (e.g., 밥이야) and 야 after a vowel (e.g., 김치야).
- **아니다:** becomes 아니야 regardless of the preceding sound (e.g., 빌리가 아니야).

---

### Dropping the Object Marker
In casual speech, the object markers 을 and 를 are often dropped to make speaking faster and easier. This is acceptable as long as the sentence's meaning remains clear. For example, "한국어를 공부하고 있어요" can become "한국어 공부하고 있어." While this can also happen in polite speech, doing so frequently makes the speaker sound less formal or even "slangy."

---

### Suggestions and Commands
The instructor introduces the casual suggestion form 자, which means "let's." This is created by attaching 자 directly to an action verb stem. Examples include:
- **저녁 먹으러 가자** / Let's go eat dinner.
- **조금 더 공부하자** / Let's study a little bit more.
- **같이 놀자** / Let's hang out together.

For commands, casual speech simply uses the standard conjugated verb form without the 요. The instructor compares three levels of commands:
- **Honorific:** 빨리 하세요 / 빨리 해주세요 (Please do it quickly).
- **Informal (Polite):** 빨리 해요 / 빨리 해줘요 (Do it quickly).
- **Casual:** 빨리 해 / 빨리 해줘 (Do it quickly).

---

### Conversation Practice: 지수 and 제니
The lesson concludes with a dialogue between two friends of the same age. The conversation demonstrates the use of casual endings (만났어?, 안 만났는데), casual pronouns (내 전화), and the casual "yes" (응). It also introduces the adverb 그냥, which means "just" or "nothing special." The instructor notes that 그냥 is common in casual talk but should be avoided in formal speech.
