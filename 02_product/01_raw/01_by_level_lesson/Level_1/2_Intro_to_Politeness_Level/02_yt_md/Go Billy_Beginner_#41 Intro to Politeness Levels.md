### Review of the (으)세요 Ending
The instructor reviews that the ending (으)세요 is used when talking about someone else to be extra polite. This applies whether one is making a statement about that person or asking them a question. For example, a regular polite question would be 김치를 좋아해요? (Do you like kimchi?), while the extra polite version is 김치를 좋아하세요? (Do you like kimchi?).

A critical rule highlighted on screen is that (으)세요 is only used for people, never for objects. The instructor provides a visual example: while 이것이 김치예요? (Is this kimchi?) is correct, one must never say 이것이 김치세요?. Using the honorific ending in that context would be incorrectly showing respect toward the kimchi itself rather than a person.

---

### Conjugation Exceptions with (으)세요
The lesson outlines specific rules for verb stems ending in 비읍 (ㅂ) or 리을 (ㄹ). For stems ending in 비읍, the ㅂ is removed and replaced with 우 followed by 세요. The instructor demonstrates this with the verb 굽다 (to bake), which transforms into 구세요. For stems ending in 리을, the ㄹ is simply removed before attaching 세요. An example shown is 팔다 (to sell), which becomes 파세요. The instructor notes that while other exceptions exist, these are the primary ones for beginners to master.

---

### Understanding Honorific Speech
Honorific speech is used to show honor or respect to the subject of the sentence. Crucially, the person being spoken about is not always the person being spoken to. The instructor uses the example 우리 어머니는 친절해요 (My mom is kind) versus the more respectful 우리 어머니는 친절하세요 (My mother is kind). Both are appropriate for speaking to peers or elders, but the latter shows specific respect toward the mother.

The instructor emphasizes that failing to use honorific speech when discussing a teacher or boss—even when speaking to a close friend—can be perceived as disrespectful. This highlights that politeness levels in Korean are about the relationship between the speaker and the subject, as well as the speaker and the listener.

---

### Politeness Levels and Social Hierarchy
Korean has several essential speech levels. The instructor compares the honorific 하세요 with the informal polite 해요. Future lessons will cover casual speech (반말) used with close friends. The core logic remains consistent: age and social rank are the primary factors. In Korea, one must speak more politely to or about someone older.

However, this politeness is often one-sided. While a younger person uses (으)세요 to address an elder, the elder may respond using standard informal speech or even casual speech. This non-reciprocal nature of Korean politeness is a key cultural element that learners must navigate.

---

### Conversation Practice: 아니요 and 네
A practice dialogue between two co-workers, Jin-hee and Jin-young, illustrates these concepts. Jin-hee addresses her older colleague as 언니 (older sister/friend) and uses the honorific 안녕하세요 (Hello). Jin-young asks if Jin-hee is busy using the standard polite form 바빠요?. Jin-hee responds with 아니요 (No), which is the polite way to say "no" (the opposite of 네). Jin-hee then asks if the older colleague is busy using the honorific 언니는 바쁘세요?.

---

### The Dictionary Form: (으)시다
The instructor explains that the (으)세요 ending actually originates from the form (으)시다. While (으)시다 is the non-conjugated form found in dictionaries, it follows the same ㅂ and ㄹ exceptions as (으)세요. When used to make a request or a polite present-tense statement, (으)시다 conjugates to (으)세요. For example, 읽다 (to read) becomes the honorific 읽으시다, which then conjugates to 읽으세요. The instructor notes that (으)시다 will conjugate differently in past or future tenses, which will be covered in later lessons.
