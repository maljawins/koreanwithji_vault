## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the question ending ~냐, along with its more formal or traditional variants 느냐 and 으냐. While modern spoken Korean primarily uses the simplified ~냐 form for almost all verbs, understanding the full grammatical breakdown is essential for reading older literature, watching historical dramas, or understanding the speech of older characters in media.

The lesson follows a specific arc: first, teaching the simplified modern usage that applies to both action and descriptive verbs; second, explaining the social nuances and "talking down" nature of this ending; and finally, detailing the technically "correct" but less common rules for separating action verbs (느냐) and descriptive verbs (으냐/냐).

---

### Basic Construction and Modern Usage
In modern everyday Korean, the most common way to use this ending is simply attaching 냐 to a verb stem. The instructor demonstrates this with the verb stem + 냐 pattern. For most verbs, there are no complex changes. However, there is one consistent exception: if a verb stem ends in the consonant ㄹ, that ㄹ must be removed before attaching 냐. For example, the verb 살다 (to live) becomes 사냐. The instructor explains that ㄹ and ㄴ do not "get along," necessitating this drop.

This ending is strictly 반말 (casual speech). However, it carries a much stronger, more blunt nuance than the standard casual endings like ~어/아 or even the question ending ~니. While ~니 has a soft, almost feminine or gentle nuance, ~냐 sounds strong and can even come across as obnoxious or "talking down" to someone. Because of this, it is only used with people of the same age or younger. Even if an older friend gives you permission to speak casually, using ~냐 might still feel too strong or disrespectful; it is best reserved for very close friends of the same age or subordinates.

Examples of modern usage:
- **춘야?** / Are you cold? (From 춥다)
- **좋아하냐?** / Do you like it? (From 좋아하다)
- **어디 사냐?** / Where do you live? (From 살다)
- **내일이 무슨 요일이냐?** / What day of the week is tomorrow? (From 이다)
- **다 했냐?** / Did you finish it? / Did you do it all? (Past tense stem 했 + 냐)

---

### Technical Rules for Action Verbs (느냐)
While the simple 냐 is common today, the technically complete grammar rule dictates that action verbs should use the ending 느냐. This form is frequently found in older movies, literature, or when an older character is speaking with authority. The ㄹ-drop rule still applies here: 살다 (to live) becomes 사느냐.

The instructor notes two specific exceptions that always use the 느냐 pattern: the verbs 있다 (to be/to have) and 없다 (to not be/to not have). These become 있느냐 and 없느냐 respectively.

Examples of action verb usage:
- **무슨 색을 좋아하느냐?** / What color do you like?
- **시험은 언제 보느냐?** / When are you taking the test?
- **하나만 사느냐?** / Are you only buying one?
- **혼자 왔느냐?** / Did you come here alone? (Past tense stem 왔 + 느냐)

---

### Technical Rules for Descriptive Verbs (으냐/냐)
For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the technical rule depends on whether the stem ends in a consonant or a vowel. If the stem ends in a consonant, you attach 은야. If it ends in a vowel, you attach 냐.

Specific exceptions for descriptive verbs include:
- **ㄹ-drop:** For verbs like 멀다 (to be far), remove ㄹ and attach 냐 to get 머냐.
- **ㅂ-irregular:** For verbs like 더럽다 (to be dirty) or 춥다 (to be cold), the ㅂ changes to 우. Since 우 ends in a vowel, you then attach 냐, resulting in 더러우냐 or 추우냐.

A critical distinction is made for tenses: the 은야/냐 distinction for descriptive verbs only applies to the present tense. If you are using the past tense or future tense of a descriptive verb, the grammar "reverts" to using 느냐.

Examples of descriptive verb usage:
- **지금 어디냐?** / Where are you now? (어디 ends in a vowel + 냐)
- **발이 괜찮으냐?** / Is your foot okay? (괜찮다 ends in a consonant + 은야)
- **이 길은 항상 더러우냐?** / Is this street always dirty? (Technically correct form of 더럽냐)
- **바빴느냐?** / Were you busy? (Past tense of 바쁘다 uses 느냐)
- **선생님이었느냐?** / Was [your dream] to be a teacher? (Past tense of 이다 uses 느냐)
