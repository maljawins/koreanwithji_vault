## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson covers three specific verb endings used exclusively for asking questions: **~나(요)**, **~가(요)**, and **~니**. While standard question endings like **~어/아(요)** are functional, these three endings provide a distinct "soft" nuance. They signal that the speaker is extra curious or interested in the answer, often translating to "by chance" or "I wonder if..." in English. The instructor explains the grammatical rules for attaching these to action and descriptive verbs, handles various irregular stem changes, and clarifies the social contexts—ranging from extra polite to friendly or even condescending—where each ending is appropriate.

---

### The Action Verb Ending: ~나(요)

The ending **~나** (casual) or **~나요** (polite) is used primarily with action verbs. To conjugate, you take the action verb stem and attach the ending directly. The instructor demonstrates this with **하다 (to do)**, which becomes **하나요?**. The primary nuance of this form is "softness." Instead of a direct question like **한국에 살아요? (Do you live in Korea?)**, using **한국에 사나요?** sounds more like "By chance, do you live in Korea?" or "I'm curious, do you live in Korea?" This added layer of curiosity makes the question sound more polite and less abrupt.

There is one major exception for verb stems ending in the consonant **ㄹ**. In these cases, the **ㄹ** must be removed before attaching the ending. For example, **살다 (to live)** has the stem **살**. After removing the **ㄹ**, you attach **나**, resulting in **사나요?**.

The instructor provides several examples of **~나요** in use:
- **지금 가도 되나요?** / Would it be okay if I left now? (Softer than **지금 가도 돼요?**)
- **무슨 일로 오셨나요?** / What are you here for? (Using the past tense honorific stem **오셨**)
- **김치를 좋아하나요?** / Do you, by chance, like kimchi?

---

### The Descriptive Verb Ending: ~가(요)

While action verbs use **~나(요)**, descriptive verbs (adjectives) typically use **~은/ㄴ가(요)**. If the descriptive verb stem ends in a consonant, you attach **~은가(요)**; if it ends in a vowel, you attach **~ㄴ가(요)**. For example, **크다 (to be big)** becomes **큰가요?**.

This form follows several irregular rules:
- **ㄹ stems:** Just like with action verbs, the **ㄹ** is removed. **멀다 (to be far)** becomes **먼가요?**.
- **ㅂ stems:** The **ㅂ** is removed and replaced with **우**. **춥다 (to be cold)** becomes **추운가요?**.
- **ㅎ stems:** The **ㅎ** is often removed. **그렇다 (to be so)** becomes **그런가요?**.
- **있다 and 없다:** Although these function as descriptive verbs, they conjugate like action verbs using **~나(요)**. Therefore, you say **있나요?** and **없나요?**. This applies to compound forms like **~고 있다**, resulting in questions like **뭐 하고 있나요? (What are you doing, by chance?)**.

The instructor notes a "real-world" shortcut: in casual conversation (though not on official tests), many Koreans use **~나요** even for descriptive verbs that end in a consonant. While **작은가요? (Is it small?)** is technically correct, **작나요?** is also commonly accepted and sounds natural. Similarly, **춥나요?** and **괜찮나요?** are frequently used instead of **추운가요?** and **괜찮은가요?**.

---

### Tense and Honorific Variations

The rules for these endings shift slightly depending on the tense:
- **Past Tense:** Regardless of whether the verb is action or descriptive, the past tense always uses **~나요**. For example, **닫다 (to close)** in the past tense becomes **닫았나요?**.
- **Future Tense:** This is typically formed using the **~ㄹ 것** structure combined with the verb **이다 (to be)**. Since **이다** conjugates as a descriptive verb, it becomes **~ㄹ 건가요?**. For example, **할 건가요? (Will you do it?)**.
- **Honorifics:** When using the honorific marker **~(으)시**, the verb is treated based on the resulting stem. **좋아하시다** becomes **좋아하시나요?**, and **아프시다** becomes **아프신가요?**.

---

### The Casual Ending: ~니

The ending **~니** is used exclusively in casual speech (**반말**) and can be attached to both action and descriptive verb stems in any tense. Like the other endings, **ㄹ** stems lose the **ㄹ** (e.g., **사니?**). 

While **~니** shares the "soft and curious" nuance of **~나**, it carries a specific social weight. It sounds like the speaker is "talking down" to the listener in a friendly or parental way. It is commonly used by parents speaking to children or older people speaking to those much younger. Because of this "talking down" nuance, you should not use **~니** with friends who are older than you, even if you are on casual terms, as it can sound sarcastic or condescending.

Examples of **~니** include:
- **더 먹을 거니?** / Are you going to eat more? (Future tense)
- **맛있니?** / Does it taste good?
- **지금 그게 중요하니?** / Is that important right now?
- **창문 닫았니?** / Did you close the window? (Past tense)
