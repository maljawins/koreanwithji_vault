## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces a secondary meaning for the grammar form (으)ㄹ까요?. While many learners first encounter this form as a way to make suggestions or ask "shall we?" / "shall I?", it also functions as a way to express wonder, doubt, or possibility. In this context, it translates to English as "could it be...?" or "would it be...?" when asking whether something is possible.

The lesson begins by reviewing the conjugation rules for (으)ㄹ까요? to ensure a solid foundation. It then moves into practical examples using both action and descriptive verbs, highlighting how the meaning shifts from a suggestion to a question of possibility depending on the context. Finally, the instructor provides a full conversation practice to demonstrate how both meanings of the form can appear naturally in the same dialogue.

---

### Conjugation Review: (으)ㄹ까요?
The instructor provides a visual chart to review how to attach this ending to various verb stems. For verb stems ending in a consonant, attach 을까요. For example, the verb 먹다 (to eat) becomes 먹을까요. For verb stems ending in a vowel, attach the consonant ㄹ followed by 까요. For example, 하다 (to do) becomes 할까요.

Specific irregular patterns are also noted on screen. For verb stems that already end with the consonant ㄹ, such as 살다 (to live), you simply attach 까요 to the end, resulting in 살까요. For verb stems ending in the consonant ㅂ, the ㅂ is removed and replaced with 우, followed by ㄹ까요. The instructor demonstrates this with 덥다 (to be hot), which transforms into 더울까요.

---

### Expressing Possibility: Could or Would
When used to ask about possibility, (으)ㄹ까요? can be used with both action verbs and descriptive verbs. This usage asks the listener for their opinion or speculation on whether an event or state is likely. The instructor provides several examples to illustrate this "could/would" meaning:

- **내일 비가 올까요?** / Could it rain tomorrow?
- **내일도 오늘처럼 더울까요?** / Could or would tomorrow also be hot like today?
- **빌리 씨는 지금 뭘 만들고 있을까요?** / What could Billy be making now?
- **제가 선인장을 만지면 많이 아플까요?** / Would it hurt a lot if I touch a cactus?
- **정말 그럴까요?** / Could it really be so?

A specific spelling note is shown regarding the verb 그렇다 (to be so) and other stems ending in ㅎ. When attaching this form, the ㅎ is removed before adding ㄹ까요, resulting in 그럴까요. This same rule applies to 어떻다 (to be how), which becomes 어떨까요, meaning "how would it be?" or "how could it be?".

### Conversation Practice
The instructor presents a dialogue between two characters, Suwon and Yerin, to show the grammar in action. Suwon starts by using the "suggestion" meaning: **우리 영화를 보러 갈까요?** (Shall we go to watch a movie?). Yerin agrees and then uses the "possibility" meaning: **그럼 무슨 영화가 좋을까요?** (Then, what movie would be good?).

The conversation continues with Suwon suggesting a horror movie using **어떨까요?** (How would it be?). Yerin explains she prefers action movies because horror is too scary, then asks **다른 장르는 없을까요?** (Would there not be another genre?). Since no action movies are available, Suwon suggests a popular romance movie. The dialogue concludes with Yerin agreeing to go see it together. This practice highlights how the form transitions smoothly between making a proposal and wondering about the best outcome.
