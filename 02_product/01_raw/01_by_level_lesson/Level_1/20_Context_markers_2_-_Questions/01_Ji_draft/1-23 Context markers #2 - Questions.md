**~나요? & ~은/ㄴ가요?**

Alright, let’s look at context markers used specifically for questions. **~나요?** and **~은/ㄴ가요?**.

The key concept to remember for both of these is **'curiosity'**. Just like this littler girl saying **'그런가요?'** 'Is that so?' or 'I wonder if that's true?'

When you use these forms, it sounds like you're genuinely curious about something. So, it gives off a very polite and kind nuance, much like 'Could it be that...?' or ‘I wonder if’ in English

**This 'curiosity' nuance only works when you use with '요', the polite speech.**

If you drop the '요' and just say **~나?** or **~가?**, it sounds like you are questioning yourself, not genuinely asking someone else. So, always use the ~요 form for a proper conversation.

The grammar for attaching **~나요?** and **~은/ㄴ가요?** differs between action verbs and descriptive verbs, so let's break that down

**Forming ~나요? & ~은/ㄴ가요?**

"The grammar for attaching **~나요?** and **~은/ㄴ가요?** differs between action verbs and descriptive verbs, so let's break that down."

**(Pointing to the "Action Verb" box on the right)**

**1. For Action Verbs:** "For action verbs, it's quite simple: you just add **~나요? (~nayo?)** after the stem. It's **not batchim-sensitive**."

* "So, **가다 (gada - to go)** becomes **가나요? (ganayo?)** – 'Do you go?' or 'Are you going, I wonder?'"
* "**먹다 (meokda - to eat)** becomes **먹나요? (meoknayo?)** – 'Do you eat?' or 'Are you eating, I wonder?'"

**2. For Descriptive Verbs:** "For descriptive verbs, you use **~은/ㄴ가요?** and it **is batchim-sensitive**."

* "After a **받침 (final consonant)**, you add **~은가요? (~eungayo?)**. So, **작다 (jakda - to be small)** becomes **작은가요? (jageun-gayo?)** – 'Is it small, I wonder?'"
* "After a **vowel**, you add **~ㄴ가요? (~n-gayo?)**. So, **크다 (keuda - to be big)** becomes **큰가요? (keun-gayo?)** – 'Is it big, I wonder?'"

**(Pointing to the "Irregular" boxes)**

**3. Irregular Verbs:** "As always, irregular verbs have their specific rules:

* **ㄹ (rieul) irregular verbs:** As always, you **remove the ㄹ batchim** from the stem first, and then 나요
  + For action verbs: **살다 (salda - to live)** becomes **사나요? (sanayo?)** – 'Do you live, I wonder?'
  + For descriptive verbs: **길다 (gilda - to be long)** becomes **긴가요? (gin-gayo?)** – 'Is it long, I wonder?'
* **ㅂ (bieup) irregular descriptive verbs:** You **replace ㅂ with 우 (u)** as usual, and then add **~ㄴ가요? (~n-gayo?)**.
  + So, **맵다 (maepda - to be spicy)** becomes **매운가요? (maeun-gayo?)** – 'Is it spicy, I wonder?'
  + And **덥다 (deopda - to be hot)** becomes **더운가요? (deoun-gayo?)** – 'Is it hot, I wonder?'"

4. Exceptions: 있다 and 없다

**Even though they are descriptive verbs, they take ~나요?** **So, just memorize them as they are: 있나요? and 없나요?**

**~나요? & ~은/ㄴ가요? – Comprehensible input 3 example dialogues**

“화장실이 어디예요?” is a perfectly fine. But this feels a bit “emotionless”, slightly cold, kind of like “WHERE IS THE BATHROOM?”.

But if you say “혹시 화장실이 어디 있나요?”, it feels like you’re genuinely curious, kind of like “Hey, I was wondering where the bathroom is”, which sounds way kinder than just “where is the bathroom?”

And this form is really commonly used together with “혹시” which means “By any chance”.

A is politely inquiring about Minsu's Korean skills, showing curiosity.

**~니? & ~냐?**

Now, let's look at two more question endings that are exclusively used in **반말 (banmal)**, or casual speech: **~니? (~ni?)** and **~냐? (~nya?)**

The usage is quite straightforward. Whether it's an action verb or a descriptive verb, and whether the verb stem has a **받침 (final consonant)** or not, it doesn't matter. You just attach **~니?** or **~냐?** directly after the stem.

There's one exception, as usual, with **ㄹ (rieul) irregular verbs**. For these, you **remove the ㄹ 받침** first, and then add **~니?** or **~냐?**

So, for example, **살다 would** become **사니?** or **사냐?**

Let's start with **~니? (~ni?)**. This ending is typically used more by **women** and has a **kinder and more feminine feeling** to it. You can think of it as the **반말 version of ~나요? and ~은/ㄴ가요?** that we just learned, carrying that sense of polite curiosity.

Because of its softer nuance, it can also be used by a person in a higher position (like a parent) when kindly asking a question to someone in a lower position (like their child), even if they aren't extremely close.

**너도 그렇니?'** This means, 'Could it be that you feel the same, too?' or 'Do you also think so, I wonder?' It expresses a gentle inquiry."

Now,**~냐?** is **very commonly used among male friends**. It's supposed to sound **cool and masculine**, but it is **super informal** – even a little 'thuggish' or blunt, depending on the context and tone.

**Most Korean women don't like it when guys use ~냐? to talk to them.** It can come across as rude or too aggressive. I personally only use **~냐?** with my very close guy friends, not girlfriends.

야, 거기 너! 돈 있냐?' Hey, you there! You got money, huh?'

This clearly demonstrates the very direct, almost challenging, and highly informal nature of **~냐?**

**을/ㄹ까요?**

The next one we’re going to learn is 을/ㄹ까(요). You're probably already familiar with this one. I remember you’ve used it in your sentences.

The core function of **~까 (~kka)** is to **ask for the listener's opinion**. It carries a nuance of **'mild suggestion' or 'soft wondering'**.

And it has a similar tone to all these expressions like Shall I? Should we? How about? Do you want me to? Do you think?

So, 제가 할까요? would mean Do you want me to do it?

The rule is, you add 을까 after 받침 like 먹을까요? and ㄹ까 after a vowel. 갈까요?

Couple of exceptions, though.

For verbs ending in ㄹ like 알다, you just add 까 and that’s it. So, you get 알까요?

For ㅂ irregular verbs, you replace ㅂ with 우 and then add ㄹ까, so 맵다 would be 매울까요?

For ㅎ irregular verbs, you remove ㅎ and attach ㄹ까, so 어떻다 would be 어떨까요?

**을/ㄹ The future branch**

을/ㄹ refers to something that hasn’t happened yet – something to be done, so to say, which is why you can see it being used all these forms like 할 거예요 / 할 수 있어요 / and 할 것.

So, it’s technically not a “future tense” but we’ll call it “the future branch” an easier understanding.
