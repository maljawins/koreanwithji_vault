**Intro**

Welcome to the last lesson of level 1 on negative sentences.

In Korean, there are 2 ways to make a negative sentence –[Stem + 지 않다] & [안 + Verb]

They both have exactly the same meaning just like “Do not” and “Don’t” in English.

지 않다 is slightly more formal and preferred in writing and it’s much easier to use than 안.

So let’s start with the easier one: 지 않다.

**Stem + 지 않다**

지 않다 is NOT 받침-sensitive, which means whether the stem ends in a 받침 or not, you can just attach 지 않다 after any verb stem. Then you can conjugate 않다 however you’d like as if it were a regular verb like 않아요, 않습니다 and so on.

[Show 저는 김치를 먹지 않아요]

[Show 저는 눈이 좋지 않아요]

And that’s pretty much

Now, what’s interesting about 지 않다 is that you can add

Now, you can do something interesting with 지 않다 to get across a slightly different

Negating SOV: 저는 김치를 먹지(를) 않아요

Negating TSV: 저는 눈이 좋지(가) 않아요

And after 지, you can optionally add the object marker 를 in a negative SOV sentence. And 지를 is often shortened to just 질.

And this puts extra emphasis to your sentence.

For example, 저는 김치를 먹지를(=질) 않아요 has a similar nuance to “I JUST don’t eat Kimchi.”

And you can add the subject marker 가 after 지 in a negative TSV sentence.

So, 저는 눈이 좋지가 않아요 has a similar nuance to “I JUST don’t have good eyes.”

You can also use the topic marker 는 in this place.

Let me use another example to show you what that does.

저는 케이팝이 싫지는(=진) 않아요.

저는 케이팝을 싫어하지는(=진) 않아요.

First off, you can shorten 지는 to 진. Let’s get that out of the way.

Now, these sentences don’t just mean “I don’t hate K-pop.” They’re actually more nuanced than that. In English, you’d say “I don’t HATE K-pop” with stress on “hate”, which clearly implies “I don’t particularly LIKE K-Pop. either.”

If you remember in lesson 13, I explained how the topic marker can imply contrast using the “Well, about” analogy. Back then, it was “Well, about noun” but now it’s “Well about verb”. Look at it this way.

[저는 케이팝이 싫지]는 않아요.

[저는 케이팝을 싫어하지]는 않아요.

Show “Well, ABOUT [whether I HATE K-pop], I don’t.”

The entire idea of “Whether I hate K-pop” is the topic [Highlight 는] for 않아요. [Highlight 않아요].

So, like “Whether I hate K-pop? (Highlight [whether I HATE K-pop]), ABOUT that, no, I don’t HATE it.”

In English, you can only express this message verbally by changing your tone. But in Korean, you can actually do this in writing using 는, which in my opinion, is super useful.

So, remember “Well, about verb” when you see 지는 or 진 before 않다.

**안 + Verb**

Let’s move on to 안 + Verb then.

Show

[저는 김치를 안 먹어요] – I don’t eat Kimchi

[저는 키가 안 작아요] – I’m not short.

안 means “not” and it’s an adverb. So, all you have to do is simply place it before the verb and you’re done. And it works with all descriptive verbs.

However, when it comes to action verbs, 안 has a restriction that 지 않다 doesn’t. And that is, it can’t be used directly with Sino-Korean action verbs.

To remind you, Sino-Korean means the word is derived from Chinese letters. And about 50% of Korean verbs are Sino-Korean, called 한자. And pure, or native Korean means the word doesn’t come from Chinese characters.

[Show 운동運動하다 - To (DO) exercise]

And 운동하다 is a Sino-Korean action verb meaning “to exercise”.

And here’s an INCORRECT way to use 안 with 운동하다.

Show [저는 오늘 안 운동했어요] – I didn’t work out today.

As you can see, you cannot place 안 in front of 운동하다 like this.

Then what about 지 않다? Like this?

Show [저는 오늘 운동하지 않았어요] – I didn’t work out today.

Well, this isn’t grammatically wrong but it still sounds a bit off.

What you should do instead is split 운동하다 into 운동을 하다 using the object marker – We learned this in the lesson Verb 101.

Now, since 하다 IS pure Korean, we can do THIS.

[Show 저는 오늘 운동을 안했어요]

And you can also do this using 지 않다.

[Show 저는 오늘 운동을 하지 않았어요.]

Now, this sounds much better than the one from earlier “저는 오늘 운동하지 않았어요”.

So, just remember it’s usually safe to split the Sino-Korean word from 하다 to make a negative sentence no matter which one you choose to use. [Show – 안 or 지 않다]

[Show 운동하다 – To (do) exercise]

Now, every single Sino-Korean verb is coupled together with a Sino-Korean noun (highlight 운동 / exercise) plus a main verb (highlight 하다 / do). And they all end in 하다.

To name a few, we have 공부하다 – To study, 운전하다 – To drive, and 설명하다 – To explain and many more.

However, that doesn’t mean all 하다 verbs are Sino-Korean though. For example, “말 in 말하다, which then means to say, talk and tell and 걱정 in “걱정하다 – to worry” are both pure Korean words. So don’t let yourself be fooled and always use a dictionary to be sure.

And here’s how. When you look up the word using Naver Dictionary, if you see Chinese next to it, that means it’s Sino-Korean. Now at this point, you’re probably wondering if you should learn Chinese, too and my answer is “No.” because most Koreans, including myself, don’t know how to write barely any of the thousands of Sino-Korean words we use every single day.

However, I definitely recommend learning a few common ones to help with your vocabulary once you’re through this course.

Now, here are some examples using 안.

저는 아파트에 안 살아요

I don’t live in an apartment.

살다 is a pure Korean verb and it means to live

So, you can just add 안 before 살다 and conjugate 살다.

교수님이 그 주제를 설명을 안 하셨습니다.

The professor didn’t explain that topic.

설명하다 is a Sino-Korean which means to explain.

So, if you were to use 안, you have to place it right before 하다.
