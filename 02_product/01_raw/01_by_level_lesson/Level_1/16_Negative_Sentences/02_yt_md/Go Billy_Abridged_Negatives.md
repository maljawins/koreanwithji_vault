### Overview
This lesson covers the fundamental ways to create negative sentences in Korean, focusing on the two primary methods: the long-form negative `~지 않다` and the short-form negative `안`. The instructor explains the grammatical structures for both, including how to handle different tenses and the specific placement of the negative marker in sentences. The instructor also addresses important exceptions and specialized negative verbs that replace standard negative constructions. A significant portion of the lesson is dedicated to common beginner mistakes, particularly regarding `하다` verbs and the distinction between "not doing" something (lack of action) versus "cannot do" something (lack of ability). By the end of the lesson, students should understand how to correctly negate actions and descriptions while choosing the most natural-sounding form for various contexts.

---

### Long-Form Negation: ~지 않다
The first method for creating a negative is the long-form construction. To use this, you take a verb stem (the verb minus the final `다`) and attach `지` directly to it, followed by the verb `않다`. The verb `않다` functions as a regular verb and must be conjugated to match the desired tense and politeness level. For example, in the present tense it becomes `않아요`, in the past tense `않았어요`, and in the future tense `않을 거예요`.

The instructor provides several examples of this transformation. The verb `좋아하다 (to like)` becomes `좋아하지 않아요 (I do not like it)`. For the future tense, `가다 (to go)` becomes `가지 않을 거예요 (I will not go)`. When expressing desire, `보고 싶다 (to want to see)` becomes `보고 싶지 않아요 (I do not want to see it)`. Crucially, for past tense, the tense marker is only applied to the final verb `않다`, not the main verb stem; thus, `공부하다 (to study)` becomes `공부하지 않았어요 (I did not study)`, rather than negating the conjugated form `공부했지`.

---

### Short-Form Negation: 안
The second method is the short-form negative using `안`. This is generally simpler as you just place `안` before the verb. For example, `좋아해요` becomes `안 좋아해요 (I don't like it)` and `추워요 (It is cold)` becomes `안 추워요 (It is not cold)`. While many verbs can use `안`, it is not universal. Generally, `안` sounds more natural and common with two-syllable verbs like `먹다 (to eat)`, `가다 (to go)`, `오다 (to come)`, `살다 (to live)`, `죽다 (to die)`, `놀다 (to play)`, and `사다 (to buy)`.

A critical rule for `안` involves `하다` verbs. If a verb consists of a noun plus the action verb `하다` (meaning "to do"), such as `공부하다 (to study)` or `요리하다 (to cook)`, the `안` must be placed between the noun and `하다`. Therefore, "I didn't study" is `공부를 안 했어요`, not `안 공부했어요`. However, this separation does not apply to descriptive verbs (adjectives) that end in `하다`, such as `피곤하다 (to be tired)`. For these, you cannot say `피곤 안 해요`; instead, you use the long form `피곤하지 않아요` or, in some cases, place `안` at the very beginning if it sounds natural.

---

### Comparing ~지 않다 vs. 안
While both forms mean "not," there are subtle differences in nuance. If a learner is in doubt, `~지 않다` is always a safe, grammatically correct choice. The long form `~지 않다` carries a bit more emphasis and contrast, similar to the difference between "do not" and "don't" in English. Because of this added weight, `~지 않다` is more common in formal writing or speech.

The long form is also the preferred choice for rhetorical questions. When asking "Is it not?" or "Does it not?", the contrast provided by `~지 않다` is more effective. For example, while `없다` is the standard negative for "to be/have," a rhetorical question would more likely use `있지 않다`.

---

### Negative Verbs and Exceptions
Some Korean verbs have unique negative counterparts that must be used instead of `안`. The most prominent is `있다 (to be/have)`, which becomes `없다 (to not be/not have)`. You should say `집에 없어요 (I am not at home)` rather than `안 있어요`. This extends to compound words like `재미있다 (to be fun/interesting)`, which becomes `재미없다 (to be boring/not fun)`.

Another unique pair is `알다 (to know)` and `모르다 (to not know)`. Using `안 알아요` is incorrect; one must use `몰라요`. For the verb `이다 (to be)`, the negative form is `아니다 (to not be)`. In a grammatically complete sentence, `아니다` is often preceded by the subject marker `이/가`, as in `우리 집이 아니에요 (It is not my house)`.

---

### Nuance and Particles with Negatives
The `지` in the long-form negative can be combined with various particles to change the nuance of the sentence. Adding the particle `도 (even/also)` creates the meaning of "not even." For example, `웃기지 않다` means "It's not funny," but `웃기지도 않다` means "It's not even funny."

The topic marker `는` can also be added for contrast. The instructor provides the example: `비싸지는 않지만 싸지도 않아요 (It's not expensive, but it's not cheap either)`. Here, the `는` emphasizes that while it might not be "expensive," it doesn't mean it's "cheap." Additionally, the object marker `를` can be attached to `지` (as `지를`) for a very slight added emphasis on the action itself.

---

### Action vs. Ability: 안 vs. 못
A common mistake for learners is using `안` or `~지 않다` when they actually mean they "cannot" do something. `안` and `~지 않다` refer to a simple lack of action or a choice. If you say `한국어를 잘 하지 않아요`, it implies you choose not to speak Korean well.

To express a lack of ability (cannot), you must use `못` or the long-form `~지 못하다`. `못` is placed before the verb (or before `하다` in action verbs), and `~지 못하다` is attached to the verb stem. For example, "I can't speak Korean well" is `한국어를 잘 못해요` or `한국어를 잘 하지 못해요`. Similarly, for "I can't understand," use `이해를 못해요` or `이해하지 못해요`.
