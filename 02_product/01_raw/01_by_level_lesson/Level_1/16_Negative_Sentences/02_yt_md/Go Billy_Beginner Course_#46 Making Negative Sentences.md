## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of negative sentences in Korean by comparing them to English. While English typically uses the word "not" before a verb to create a negative, Korean requires changing the verb itself. For the majority of verbs, this involves adding a specific piece of grammar, but for a few essential verbs, a completely separate negative counterpart must be used.

The lesson follows a logical progression: first covering the unique negative verbs that have no direct grammatical substitute, then moving to the two primary methods for turning any regular verb negative—the suffix `-지 않다` and the prefix `안`. The instructor also explains the nuances of answering negative questions, which can be counterintuitive for English speakers, and introduces negative adverbs that emphasize the degree of negation.

---

### Special Negative Verbs: 아니다, 모르다, and 없다
The instructor begins with verbs that have unique negative forms. The most important is `아니다 (to not be)`, which is the opposite of `이다 (to be)`. Unlike `이다`, which attaches directly to a noun, `아니다` requires the noun it describes to take a subject marker `이/가`. In the present tense informal polite form, it conjugates to `아니에요`, while in the past tense it becomes `아니었어요`.

> **저는 빌리가 아니에요.** / I am not Billy.
> **그건 우리 고양이가 아니었어요.** / That wasn't my cat.

Next is `모르다 (to not know)`, the opposite of `알다 (to know)`. This is an action verb used to express a lack of knowledge or acquaintance.

> **저는 빌리 씨를 몰라요.** / I do not know Billy.
> **우리 선생님도 빌리 씨를 모르세요.** / Our teacher also doesn't know Billy.

Finally, `없다 (to not exist/to not have)` is the opposite of `있다 (to exist/to have)`. Both are descriptive verbs, meaning they cannot be used with an object marker `을/를`.

> **빌리 씨는 지금 박물관에 없어요.** / Billy is not at the museum now.
> **우리 집에 피자가 없어요.** / There is no pizza at our house.

---

### Making Verbs Negative with -지 않다
For almost any other verb, the ending `-지 않다` can be used to create a negative. To apply this, the instructor demonstrates taking the verb stem and attaching `-지 않다`, which then conjugates like a regular verb. In the present tense informal polite form, this results in `-지 않아요`. This method is considered the "safest" because it works with virtually every verb and adjective in the language.

> **덥지 않아요.** / It's not hot (weather).
> **저도 우유를 좋아하지 않아요.** / I also do not like milk.
> **저는 지금 화장실에 가고 싶지 않아요.** / I do not want to go to the bathroom now.

---

### Making Verbs Negative with 안
A shorter way to make sentences negative is by adding the word `안` before the verb. This is very common in spoken Korean. The instructor provides three main tips for determining if `안` can be used. First, if the verb has only two syllables (like `가다`, `오다`, `먹다`), it can almost always use `안`. Second, if the verb does not end in `하다`, it is likely compatible with `안`. Third, for verbs ending in `하다`, `안` can only be used if the `하다` part can be separated from the noun part of the verb.

In the case of separable `하다` verbs like `공부하다 (to study)`, the `안` must be placed between the noun and the verb: `공부 안 하다`. It is incorrect to say `안 공부하다`. However, descriptive verbs (adjectives) that end in `하다`, such as `친절하다 (to be kind)`, cannot be separated this way, though they can still use `안` before the whole word or use the `-지 않다` form. When in doubt, the instructor recommends using `-지 않다` as it is always correct.

> **안 추워요.** / It's not cold.
> **내일 회사에 안 갈 거예요.** / I won't go to the company tomorrow.
> **저는 감자튀김을 안 좋아해요.** / I don't like French fries.

---

### Answering Negative Questions
The instructor highlights a major cultural and linguistic difference in how Koreans answer negative questions. In English, "Yes" or "No" refers to the facts of the situation. In Korean, `네 (yes)` and `아니요 (no)` refer to whether you agree with the speaker's statement. If someone asks "Don't you like kimchi?" and you agree that you do not like it, you must say `네 (Yes, I don't like it)`. To avoid confusion, the instructor suggests always answering with a full sentence rather than a single word.

---

### Negative Adverbs: 별로 and 전혀
To add nuance to negative sentences, certain adverbs are used exclusively with negative verbs. `별로` means "not very" or "not a lot." It must be paired with a negative verb form to be grammatically correct. `전혀` means "not at all" and provides a stronger level of negation.

> **별로 좋아하지 않아요.** / I don't like it very much.
> **저는 일본어를 전혀 몰라요.** / I don't know Japanese at all.
> **빌리 씨를 전혀 만나고 싶지 않아요.** / I don't want to meet Billy at all.
