## Teaching Points

*   **Honorifics in Future Tense:** Ji explains that the honorific suffix `-(으)시-` must come before the tense marker. For `가다 (to go)`, the honorific future becomes `가실 거예요`. He demonstrates this with the example:
    > **아빠가 집에 가실 거야.** / Dad will go home.
*   **Negation with Future Tense:** When using the long negation `-지 않다`, the future tense marker attaches to the auxiliary verb `않다`.
    > **나는 소파에 앉지 않을 거야.** / I will not sit on the sofa.
    > **아빠는 소파에 앉지 않으실 거야.** / Dad will not sit on the sofa (Honorific).
*   **ㄹ Irregular Verbs:** For verbs ending in `ㄹ` like `팔다 (to sell)` or `알다 (to know)`, the `ㄹ` of the stem is removed when followed by certain endings, or it merges with the future marker.
    > **할머니가 시장에서 김치를 파실 거야.** / Grandma will sell Kimchi at the market.
*   **-(을)ㄹ게요 (Informing/Promise):** Used only with the 1st person (I/We) and action verbs. It conveys a sense of making a promise or kindly informing the listener of a decision. It is often equivalent to "Let me..." in English.
    > **제가 설명할게요.** / Let me explain.
*   **-(을)ㄹ래요 (Intention):** Used for the speaker's intention (1st person) or to ask about the listener's intention (2nd person). It is more affirmative and less "kind" than `-(을)ㄹ게요`.
    > **오늘 뭐 할래?** / What do you want to do today?
    > **저는 여기 있을래요.** / I'm going to stay here.
*   **-겠다 (Conditional/Future):** A versatile ending with four main uses:
    1.  **Willingness/Determination:** (1st person) `제가 하겠습니다` (I will do it).
    2.  **Formal Notification:** Used in broadcasts or stations. `도착하겠습니다` (Will arrive).
    3.  **Conjecture/Guessing:** (3rd person) `피곤하시겠어요` (You must be tired).
    4.  **Question/Rhetorical:** `괜찮으시겠어요?` (Will you be okay?).

## Lesson Flow

Ji begins by finishing the honorific conjugation for `가다`, showing how `가다` becomes `가실 거예요`. He then moves to negation, using the verb `앉다 (to sit)`. He meticulously breaks down the order: Stem (`앉`) + Negation (`지 않`) + Honorific (`으시`) + Future (`ㄹ 거`) + Speech Level (`야/예요/입니다`). Amira follows along as Ji constructs the sentence "Dad will not sit on the sofa."

Next, Ji introduces the `ㄹ` irregular rule. He explains that when a stem ends in `ㄹ`, it is treated specially. He uses `팔다 (to sell)` to show that instead of adding another `ㄹ`, the existing one is used, resulting in `팔 거예요`. He then shows the honorific version `파실 거예요`, where the `ㄹ` drops out before the `시` suffix.

The middle of the lesson is dedicated to comparing `-(을)ㄹ게요` and `-(을)ㄹ래요`. Ji explains that `-(을)ㄹ게요` is like a "favor" or a "promise" to the listener, whereas `-(을)ㄹ래요` is a statement of pure intention. He provides a scenario: if someone asks "What are you doing today?", answering with `있을게` implies you are staying home for the other person's sake, while `있을래` simply states your plan. Amira listens intently, noting that she needs to revise these nuances to get used to them.

The final third of the recording covers `겠다`. Ji warns Amira that this is the most difficult ending because it isn't a true future tense but a "conditional" used in the present. He goes through the four usages: determination (I will quit drinking), formal announcements (The train will arrive), conjecture (That must be fun), and formal questions (Would you like to receive the prize?). He specifically highlights how `겠다` can be added to a past stem to mean "must have," such as `추웠겠어요` (It must have been cold). The recording ends just as Ji is about to start a new Zoom session to cover the final rhetorical usage of `겠다`.
