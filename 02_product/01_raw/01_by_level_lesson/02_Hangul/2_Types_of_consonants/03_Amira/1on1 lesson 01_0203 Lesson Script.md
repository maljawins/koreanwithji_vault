**1. Key Vowel: ㅡ (으 / eu)**

* **Importance**: Crucial for beginners
  important for spelling English loanwords (about 10,000 loanwords)
* **Explanation**:
  + Korean syllables require at least one consonant and one vowel.
  + Consonants can’t stand alone; **ㅡ** acts as a placeholder vowel just like ㅇ is a placeholder consonant.
  + Example: “Stress” becomes **스트레스** seu teu reu seu (4 syllables).
* **Pronunciation Tip**: [**WATCH THE VIDEO on ㅡ pronunciation**](https://youtube.com/shorts/SZzNxjOE5Uw)
  + The **ㅡ** sound doesn’t exist in English.
  + Think of the sound you make when disgusted: **으~ Brother 으~**
* **Practice**:
  + Say the ‘t’ sound without a vowel: **t-t-t... → elongate → 트**.

Practice with **아파트** (APT)

**1. Introduction to 받침 (Batchim)**

**What is 받침?**

* Definition: Bottom/final consonant in a syllable
* Analogy: Like a footstool (발받침 – bal batchim) supporting the syllable
* **Importance: Crucial for grammar and conjugations.**
* **Example:**
  + **어떻게 해? (eo tteo keh hae?) → 어떡해? eo tteok hae? (How do?)**
  + **The ㅎ 받침 in “떻” plays a key role.**

**1. Basic consonant ㅅ 시옷 (shi ot)**

All basic consonants are pretty much straight forward **except for ㅅ 시옷 – shi ot**

* Sound: Closer to the soft ‘s’ in “smooth” (not the strong ‘s’ in “sound”).
* Loanword Example: **스무스 seu moo seu (smooth)**

\*\* Tip: Ditch romanization; focus on Hangul. Romanization is like Safari/Edge; 한글 (Hangul) is Chrome.

**3-1. Brief Introduction to 받침 (batchim) to understand double consonants**

**Single Syllable 받침 Rule**

* **Only 7 받침 Sounds: ㄱ g(k) , ㄴ n, ㄷ d, ㄹ r(l), ㅁ m, ㅂ b, ㅇ ng**
* **Self-Explanatory: ㄴ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅇ**
* **Simplified Sounds: ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ**
  + **ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ → ㄱ
    (e.g., 낙, 낰, 낚 → 낙 nak)**
  + **ㄷ, ㅌ, ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ → ㄷ
    (e.g., 낟, 낫, 났, 낮, 낯, 낱 → 낟 nad)**
  + **ㅂ, ㅍ → ㅂ
    (e.g., 압, 앞 → 압 ab)**

**4. Introduction to 받침 Rule**

* **Key Point: To pronounce words with two or more syllables correctly, you need to understand 받침 (final consonants).**
* **Why 받침 is Tricky:**
  + **The sound of the 받침 changes depending on the initial consonant of the next syllable.**
  + **There are many rules (some even requiring advanced linguistic knowledge).**
    - **Example: The National Institute of the Korean Language has published a ~300-page guide on pronunciation rules.**
  + **While there are many exceptions, we’ll focus on the most essential, general rules.**

**받침 Rule 1. General Rule**

1. **When the Next Syllable Starts with ㅇ:**
   * **The 받침 sound moves to the second syllable.**
   * **Examples:**
     + **날아 → 나라 na ra**
     + **먹어 → 머거 meo geo**
     + **받아 → 바다 ba da**
     + **앞에 → 아페 a pe**
2. **Exceptions:**
   * **맛없다 → 마덥다 (ma dub da / not ma sub da)
     (Not tasty).**
   * **멋없다 → 머덥다 (mu dub da / not ma sub da)
     (Not cool, as in “no swag”).**
