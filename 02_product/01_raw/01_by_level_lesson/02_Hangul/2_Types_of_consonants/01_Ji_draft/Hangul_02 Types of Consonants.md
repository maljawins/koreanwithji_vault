**1. Types of Consonants**

To understand 받침, we first need to know about the three types of consonants:

First we have **Basic consonants** (ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅇ, ㅈ). They are pretty straight forward

Next we have **Aspirated consonants** (ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅍ, ㅊ, ㅎ). These have a puff of air coming out of your mouth when you say them.

And lastly, **Amplified (Double) consonants** (ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ). These have a strong, emphasized sound with no air coming out of your mouth.

**2. ‘ㅅ’ Is it really an “S”? – Think “Smooth”**

Most basic consonants are straightforward, but we need to talk about ‘ㅅ’ (시옷).

‘ㅅ’ is romanized as just ‘s,’ but there’s more to it.

I want you to listen closely to the difference between these two words

[Show 살 and 쌀] – [Show meaning below each word Flesh and Rice]

Notice how the ‘살’ is more “smooth” than ‘쌀’?

That’s right. The single 시옷 sound is closer to that hissing sound like in the word ‘**s**mooth’, or “style”, or “skin” where S is followed by another consonant, not a vowel.

So, whenever you see ‘ㅅ,’ think of the word ‘smooth.’ Because it has a more “Smooth” sound than the English letter ‘s’.

This is a classic example that shows how romanization doesn’t represent the real Korean sounds.

**The Aspirated Version of ㅅ**

Notice how letters that have double consonants also have aspirated counterparts like

* 가 카 and 까
* 다 타 and 따
* 바 파 and 빠
* 자 차 and 짜

But ‘What about ‘ㅅ’? Does this have an aspirated sound?’ Yes! It’s the ‘sh’ sound. But this ‘sh’ sound doesn’t have its own letter. Instead, the sound changes depending on the vowel it’s paired with:

* 사 (sa) vs. 샤 (sha).
* 서 (seo) vs. 셔 (shuh).
* 소 (so) vs. 쇼 (sho).
* 수 (su) vs. 슈 (shu).
* 스 (seu) vs. 시 (shi).

Basically, when ‘ㅅ’ is paired with vowels that have a y sound and ‘ㅣ,’ it makes the ‘sh’ sound.

Even the name of this letter, 시옷, is pronounced ‘shi-od.’

**4. Double Consonants**
"Now, let’s move on to double consonants.

Double consonants are the opposite of aspirated consonants—there’s no puff of air. Instead, you strengthen the sound as you say it.

Every consonant that has an aspirated version also has a double consonant, except for ‘ㅇ’ because ‘ㅇ’ is a silent consonant—you can’t strengthen ‘nothing.’

**4-1. Introduction to 받침 (Final Consonants)**
We need to briefly touch on 받침 before learning how to pronounce double consonants.

In principle, there can only be seven sounds for 받침 - ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, and ㅇ.

* Among these, ㄴ, ㄹ, ㅁ, and ㅇ are self-explanatory.
* And now we’re left with ㄱ ㄷ and ㅂ.

**4-2. Double Consonants Pronunciation**

Now when you say them, notice a tension formed in your mouth when you say them? Like 악 or 앋 or 압.

THIS is the key to pronouncing double consonants effectively: You have to maintain that tension and then move on to the next sound.

Let’s practice with some real words:

1. **악기** (musical instrument):
   * 악 (tension) + 기 = 악끼.
   * The ㄱ 받침 combines with the following 기 to create a ‘끼’ sound.
2. **닫다** (to close):
   * 닫 (tension) + 다 = 닫따.
   * The ㄷ 받침 combines with the following 다 to create a ‘따’ sound.
3. **압박** (pressure):
   * 압 (tension) + 박 = 압빡.
   * The ㅂ 받침 combines with the following 박 to create a ‘빡’ sound.
4. **숫소** (male cow):
   * 숫 (tension) + 소 = 숟쏘.
   * The ㅅ, like I mentioned is pronounced as ㄷ in 받침 position which creates the tension and then turns the following 소 into a ‘쏘’ sound.
5. **낮잠** (nap):
   * 낮 (tension) + 잠 = 낟짬.
   * The ㅈ 받침 is also pronounced as ㄷ. So the following 잠 is pronounced as ‘짬’.

So, it's important to remember that feeling, that tension forming up in your mouth.
