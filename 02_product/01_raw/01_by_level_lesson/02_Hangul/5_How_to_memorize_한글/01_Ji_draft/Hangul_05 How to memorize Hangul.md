**The Best Way to Retain Knowledge**

No matter what you're learning, the best way to retain that new knowledge is to USE it.

Right now, you're learning Korean online using technology, right?

Which means you'll probably be using a computer or mobile device more than handwriting.

So the first thing you need to learn is how to type in 한글.

Think about it: Dictionary searches. Pronunciation checks. Research. All of these require you to type in 한글.

And here's the huge advantage:

Once you learn Korean typing, your fingers will remember 한글 for you.

It's also excellent for memorizing vocabulary.

I used this method all the time when learning English vocabulary. I call this technique "air typing."

After you learn to type, whenever you encounter a new word, imagine the keyboard and type the word in the air with your fingers.

This creates muscle memory. Your brain and fingers link together, and you remember things much better.

**Use a Computer for This Course**

And I also recommend taking this course on a computer—laptop, desktop, doesn't matter.

I’m a strong advocate of learning with a computer, not mobile device. Here's why:

You can't take notes quickly on a phone. And how effectively can you study while constantly switching back and forth on that tiny screen trying to multitask?

Plus, phones are full of distractions.

So throw your phone in a corner somewhere and commit to learning seriously with a computer.

**Step-by-Step Practice Guide**

Here's exactly how to practice:

**Step 1:** Go to one of the websites.

**Step 2:** If you’re going to go on hancomtaja.com, temporarily switch the site language to English to understand the interface, then switch it back to Korean before starting.

**Step 3:** Begin with Location Practice to learn the keyboard layout.

* Left hand for consonants
* Right hand for vowels

**Step 4:** Move on to Word Typing Practice. Read each word aloud as you type.

* Don't worry about the meaning of the words for now.

**Step 5:** Go to papago.naver.com to check your pronunciation—but only after you've tried pronouncing the words on your own first.
