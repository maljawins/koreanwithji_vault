**Recap**

So far, we've learned how 한글 works, the sounds of consonants, vowels, and compound vowels.

We covered basic consonants, aspirated consonants, and double consonants—which are the amplified sounds of ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅈ. Right?

And then we learned consonant assimilation—how sounds change with amplifiers and nasal consonants.

**Now Compound 받침 is** the final part of learning Hangul. After this, you'll be done with Hangul!

**Compound 받침 인트로**

There are **11 compound 받침**, but don't worry—they're easier than they look.

ㄳ ㄵ ㄶ ㄺ ㄽ ㄾ ㅀ ㅄ ㄻ ㄼ ㄿ

First, you can ignore **ㄽ** because it’s only used in old Korean. I can't even think of any word that has this 받침, so you can forget about it. That leaves us with **10 compound 받침**:

ㄳ ㄵ ㄶ ㄼ ㄾ ㅀ ㅄ ㄺ ㄻ ㄿ

I’ll make something clear. These only appear in **받침** positions, meaning you’ll never see them at the beginning of a syllable.

**Dominant letter of compound 받침**

Every compound 받침 has one **dominant letter**.

Except for **ㄺ, ㄻ, and ㄿ**, the **first letter** is the dominant one. And when there’s only one syllable, you only pronounce the dominant letter.

Here are some examples:

**First Letter Dominant:**

* ㄳ → 넋 is pronounced 넉
* ㄵ → 앉 is pronounced 안
* ㄶ → 않 is pronounced 안
* ㄼ → 넓 is pronounced 널
* ㄾ → 핥 is pronounced 할
* ㅀ → 잃 is pronounced 일
* ㅄ → 값 is pronounced 갑

**Second Letter Dominant:**

For ㄺ, ㄻ, and ㄿ, the second letter is dominant:

 ㄺ → 읽 is pronounced 익

 ㄻ → 삶 is pronounced 삼

 ㄿ → 읊 is pronounced 읍

Remember? ㅍ 받침 makes a ㅂ sound.

**Consonant assimilation of compound 받침**

When these 받침 are followed by other consonants, the same consonant assimilation rules apply.

The only difference is that, no matter which letter is dominant, all compound 받침 act as either amplifiers or aspirators.

1. **When followed by ‘ㅇ’ silent letter**

Let’s first look at the easiest rule. If the next syllable starts with ‘ㅇ’, the second letter in the compound 받침 moves to the next syllable.

넋이 > 넉시
앉아 > 안자
않아 > 아나 – (ㅎ goes silent when followed by ㅇ, remember?)
넓어 > 널버
핥아 > 할타
잃어 > 이러 – Again, ‘ㅎ’ followed by ‘ㅇ’ turns silent
값이 > 갑시

1. **When followed by non-silent consonants**

When followed by other consonants, all you need to do is apply the usual consonant assimilation rules to the dominant letter.

However, compound 받침 always act as either amplifiers or aspirators.

**넋만 → 넉만 → 넝만**

The dominant letter here is ㄱ.

Remember how ㄱ 받침, when it meets a nasal consonant, becomes ㅇ (ng sound)?

So this is pronounced 넝만.

**앉다 → 안따**

The dominant letter of ㄵ 받침 is ㄴ.

But although ㄴ by itself is not an amplifier 받침, the ㄵ compound 받침 amplifies 다 to 따.

So this is pronounced 안따.

**않고 → 안코**

Remember ㅎ, the aspirator? The ㅎ in ㄶ aspirates 고 to 코.

**넓다 → 널따**

Again, ㄹ is the dominant letter, but it amplifies 다 to 따.

**넓혀 → 널펴**

The ㅂ sound combined with the aspirator ㅎ in the following consonant makes a ㅍ sound.

So this is pronounced 널펴.

**핥다 → 할따**

Again, 다 is amplified to 따.

**잃지 → 일치** The ㅎ in ㅀ aspirates 지 to 치. So this is pronounced 일치.

**값도 → 갑또** 도 is amplified to 또.

**값만 → 갑만 → 감만**

Remember when ㅂ 받침 is followed by ㅁ, it naturally turns into ㅁ? So this is pronounced 감만.

**읽는 → 익는 → 잉는**

Here, the dominant letter ㄱ is followed by ㄴ—a nasal consonant. So this is pronounced 잉는.

**Examples with Second Letter Dominant:**

Now let's look at the compound 받침 where the second letter is dominant:

**읽다 → 익따**

The dominant letter is ㄱ, and it amplifies 다 to 따.
**삶다 → 삼따**

The dominant letter is ㅁ, but again, 다 is amplified to 따.

**읊다 → 읍따**

ㅍ is the dominant letter, and ㅍ in 받침 position turns into ㅂ sound.

So this is pronounced 읍따. On a side note: This is the only word that has ㄿ 받침.

**The Exception: 닭 (Chicken)**

There's one exception you need to know. In the word 닭, the ㄹ sound is completely silent.
