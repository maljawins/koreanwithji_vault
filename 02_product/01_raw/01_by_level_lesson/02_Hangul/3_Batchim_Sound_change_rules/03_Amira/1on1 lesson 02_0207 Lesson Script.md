3. Consonant Assimilation

Consonant assimilation refers to the change in the 받침 sound depending on the consonant that follows.

Here’s a set of a few English words to help you understand what it does.

**Kindness → Kain nis**

**I don’t know → I don no**

**Did you → ‘D Jew**

If you look at these examples, you pronounce them in a way that is easier on your tongue instead of enunciating every single letter.

This is basically what consonant assimilation does. It’s there to make pronunciation easier not harder.

**Slide 18**

Remember from our previous lesson that there are only 7 받침 sounds? ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ ㄴ ㄹ ㅁ ㅇ.

And we split them into two groups.

**Slide 19**

And there was also ㅎ 히읗 – the aspirator consonant.

Consonant assimilation is basically about how these 8 consonants interact with each other to change pronunciation.

**Slide 20**

To understand this better, we’ll divide these sounds into three categories.

**Slide 21**

Types of consonants

1. Amplifier Consonants create tension in the mouth. We covered this in our previous lesson. So when they meet certain sounds, they amplify the next consonant.

ㄱㅋㄲ 는 ㄱ 받침 (악 앜 앆) 전부 '낙'으로 발음
ㄷㅌㅅㅆㅈㅊ ㄷ받침 (앋 앝 앗 았 앚 앛) 전부 '앋'으로 발음
ㅂㅍ받침은 ㅂ 소리 (압, 앞) 전부 '압'으로 발음

2. Nasal Consonants
ㄴ ㄹ ㅁ ㅇ these 4 consonants all have a nasal sound.
✅ When these meet other consonants, the sound often changes to flow smoother. You’ll see examples later!

3. ‘ㅎ’ is a wild card

Remember how we focused on ‘ㅅ’ last time? Yeah today we’re gonna be looking at this guy because

Sometimes, it becomes silent.
Other times, it aspirates other consonants

And in some cases, it even changes into a ㄷ (d) sound

So, there are **three main ways** sounds change due to consonant assimilation:

**1. When the following syllable becomes a double consonant**

* This happens with these 3 amplifier 받침 (ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ) forming that **tension** in the mouth which naturally amplies the sound of the next syllable causing them to be pronounced like double consonants.

**2. When pronunciation naturally shifts to an easier sound**
This isn’t really a "rule" per se and it’s just something that happens naturally. And in most cases, a nasal consonant (ㄴ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅇ) triggers this change.

**3. When ‘ㅎ’ causes a sound change in 3 ways -** We’ll take a closer look at this in detail today

Amplifier consonant 받침 + ㄱㄷㅂㅅㅈ = ㄲㄸㅃㅆㅉ

When an **Amplifier consonant** (ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ) is followed by one of these five consonants—**ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅅ, ㅈ**—the sound turns into a **double consonant (ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ, ㅉ)**.

Notice they all have their **double consonant counterpart.**

So when they come after ㄱ, ㄷ, or ㅂ 받침, the tension built up in the mouth naturally amplifies them into stronger sounds.

Now, let's look at the second case—when sounds shift naturally to make pronunciation smoother.

When an **Amplifier consonant** (ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ) meets a **Nasal consonant** (ㄴ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅇ), the tension created by the Amplifier consonant weakens due to the nasal sound. This makes the pronunciation **softer and more fluid**.

**Slide 32**

**When ㄱ Meets ㄴ, ㄹ, or ㅁ → It Becomes ㅇ (ng Sound)**

If you try to enunciate the final ㄱ sound before pronouncing ㄴ, ㄹ, or ㅁ, it feels unnatural and difficult to say smoothly.

**Slide 104**

And there’s one final thing before we move on to compound 받침. And it’s a quirky one. We call this palatalization

**Slide 105**

Think of it like this:

In English, D is often pronounced like J—like in "Did you" (D'jew).

And there are times when T is pronounced like CH, especially in British English—like "Tuesday" (Chewsday).

**Slide 106**

Here’s how it works.

**1. ㄷ + 이/여 → ㄷ becomes ㅈ**

If a syllable ends with ㄷ and the next one starts with 이 or 여, ㄷ becomes ㅈ.

굳이 > 구디 X 굳이 > 구지 O

**Slide 107~108**

**2. ㅌ + 이/여 → ㅌ becomes ㅊ**

If it's ㅌ (the aspirated version of ㄷ) followed by 이 or 여, it becomes ㅊ.

같이 > 가티 X 같이 > 가치 O
붙여 > 부텨 X 붙여 > 부쳐 O

**Slide 109~110**

**3. ㄷ + 히/혀 → becomes ㅊ (with ㅎ aspiration)**

When ㄷ meets 히 or 혀, where ㅎ acts as an aspirator, it becomes ㅊ.

묻히다 > 뭊히다 > 무치다

닫히다 > 닺히다 > 다치다
