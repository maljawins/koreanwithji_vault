## Teaching Points

Ji explains that consonant assimilation is the process where the sound of a final consonant (받침) changes depending on the consonant that follows it. He categorizes these into several key behaviors:

*   **The "ㅎ" (h) Rules:** Ji describes ㅎ as a "wild card" with three main behaviors:
    *   **Silent ㅎ:** When ㅎ is followed by the silent consonant ㅇ, it becomes silent. For example, **좋아** is pronounced as **조아** (jo-a). It also becomes silent or nearly silent when it follows nasal consonants (ㄴ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅇ), such as **안해** becoming **아내** (a-nae).
    *   **Aspiration:** When ㅎ meets the "amplifier" consonants (ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ, ㅈ), it combines with them to create their aspirated counterparts (ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅍ, ㅊ). This works both ways (받침 ㅎ + consonant, or 받침 consonant + ㅎ). Examples include **놓고** → **노코** (no-ko) and **입학** → **이팍** (i-pak).
    *   **Shift to ㄷ/ㄴ:** In specific cases, like when followed by ㄴ, the ㅎ sound shifts to a ㄷ sound and then assimilates further into a ㄴ sound. **좋네** is explained as shifting from **졷네** to **존네** (jon-ne).

*   **Nasal Assimilation:** Ji teaches that nasal consonants (ㄴ, ㅁ) often "take over" the preceding sound to make it flow better.
    *   **ㅇ (ng) + ㄹ (l) = ㄴ (n):** On screen, Ji shows **정리** (organize) and explains it is pronounced **정니** (jung-ni).
    *   **ㅁ (m) + ㄹ (l) = ㄴ (n):** Similarly, **금리** becomes **금니**. Ji notes that while some natives enunciate clearly, many follow this "natural flow" rule.

*   **Palatalization:** This is a "quirky" rule where ㄷ or ㅌ sounds shift when followed by the vowels 이 (i) or 여 (yuh).
    *   **ㄷ + 이 → 지:** **굳이** is pronounced **구지** (gu-ji).
    *   **ㅌ + 이 → 치:** **같이** (together) is pronounced **가치** (ga-chi).
    *   **ㄷ + 히 → 치:** Through a combination of palatalization and aspiration, **닫히다** becomes **다치다** (da-chi-da).

The lesson begins with a discussion on the optional nature of some pronunciation rules. Ji shows how **놓고** becomes **노코**. Amira struggles slightly with the distinction between the plain and aspirated sounds but corrects herself with Ji's guidance. When they reach the word **입학**, Amira successfully applies the rule to pronounce it as **이팍**.

The most challenging section for Amira is the shift of ㅎ to ㄷ/ㄴ. Ji explains **좋네** (good) step-by-step: first the ㅎ becomes a ㄷ sound (**졷네**), and because ㄷ is followed by ㄴ, it further changes to a ㄴ sound (**존네**). Amira finds this "quirky" but manages to replicate the sound.

The final major topic is palatalization. Ji uses the English "Tuesday" (chews-day) comparison. He introduces **같이** (together) and **붙여** (to stick), explaining how the ㅌ shifts to a ㅊ sound. Amira follows the logic well, noting that the "natural" feel of the pronunciation helps her remember the rule.

The lesson concludes with a recap of the day's points and a discussion about the pace of the lessons. Amira expresses satisfaction with the logical flow, and they schedule their next session for the following Wednesday.
