## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of vowel harmony, a phonetic principle in Korean where vowels are categorized into "bright" and "dark" groups. This classification is not based on the visual brightness or darkness of the ink on the page, but rather on the inherent feeling, connotation, and grammatical function of the sounds. While vowel harmony was a much stricter rule in Middle Korean, it remains a vital part of modern Korean, particularly in verb conjugation and the nuanced world of onomatopoeia.

The video explains that King Sejong the Great was well aware of these distinct "feelings" when he invented the Korean alphabet. He intentionally incorporated the concept of bright and dark sounds into the design of Hangul. Understanding this system helps learners predict how verbs will conjugate and allows them to grasp the subtle emotional differences between similar-sounding words.

---

### Categorizing Bright and Dark Vowels
The instructor visually categorizes the vowels on screen. A bright vowel is defined as any vowel that contains the basic components `ㅏ` or `ㅗ`. This includes all complex vowels and diphthongs derived from them. Conversely, dark vowels are those containing `ㅓ` or `ㅜ`.

- **Bright Vowels:** `ㅏ`, `ㅗ`, `ㅑ`, `ㅛ`, `ㅐ`, `ㅒ`, `ㅘ`, `ㅙ`, `ㅚ`
- **Dark Vowels:** `ㅓ`, `ㅜ`, `ㅕ`, `ㅠ`, `ㅔ`, `ㅖ`, `ㅝ`, `ㅞ`, `ㅟ`

The instructor notes that `ㅡ` and `ㅣ` are often considered neutral, though in many grammatical contexts, they behave more like dark vowels. A syllable is considered "bright" or "dark" based on the vowel it contains.

---

### Influence on Verb Conjugation
One of the most practical applications of vowel harmony is in the conjugation of the informal polite ending `~아요/어요`. The choice between these two endings depends entirely on the vowel in the verb stem. If the last vowel of a verb stem is a bright vowel (`ㅏ` or `ㅗ`), it takes the bright ending `~아요`. If the last vowel is anything else (dark or neutral), it takes the dark ending `~어요`.

The instructor demonstrates this with two common verbs:
- **좋다 (to be good):** The stem contains the bright vowel `ㅗ`, so it conjugates to `좋아요`.
- **먹다 (to eat):** The stem contains the dark vowel `ㅓ`, so it conjugates to `먹어요`.

This principle also explains why certain complex vowels exist while others do not. Within a single syllable, bright and dark sounds generally do not mix. This is why Korean has the sound `ㅘ` (bright `ㅗ` + bright `ㅏ`), but does not have a combination like `ㅗ` + `ㅓ` or `ㅜ` + `ㅏ`.

---

### Nuance in Onomatopoeia and Mimetic Words
Vowel harmony is most expressive in Korean onomatopoeia (sound-effect words) and mimetic words (words describing motion or feeling). By switching between bright and dark vowels, speakers can change the "size" or "weight" of a sound or action. Bright vowels generally indicate something small, light, quick, positive, or cute. Dark vowels indicate something large, heavy, slow, negative, or serious.

The instructor provides several comparative examples:
- **Splashing:** `퐁당` (bright) suggests a small, light splash, like a pebble in a pond. `풍덩` (dark) suggests a large, heavy splash, like a person jumping into a pool.
- **Hopping:** `깡총` (bright) describes the light, cute hopping of a small rabbit. `껑충` (dark) describes the large, heavy bounding of a kangaroo.
- **Knocking/Pounding:** `똑똑` (bright) is a light, polite knock on a door. `쿵쿵` (dark) is a heavy, loud pounding or thumping sound.

---

### Vowel Harmony in General Vocabulary
The concept extends even to descriptive verbs that are not strictly onomatopoeic. The instructor points out that the very words for "bright" and "dark" in Korean follow this logic. The verb `밝다 (to be bright)` contains the bright vowel `ㅏ`, giving it a positive and light feeling. In contrast, `어둡다 (to be dark)` contains the dark vowels `ㅓ` and `ㅜ`, reinforcing its heavy and negative connotation through its phonetic makeup.
