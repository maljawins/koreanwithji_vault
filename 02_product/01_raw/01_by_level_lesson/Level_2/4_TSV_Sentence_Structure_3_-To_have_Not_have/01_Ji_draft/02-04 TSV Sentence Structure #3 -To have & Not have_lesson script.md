**Intro**

Welcome. We’re going to learn how to say “I have something” in Korean.

In English, “I have a pen” is a simple SVO sentence where “Have” is the verb and “a pen” is the object of have.

**Don’t use 가지다**

[Show: 저는 펜을 (blank)]

So it looks like all we need is to find the verb and fill in the blank to make it an SOV sentence.

The word that pops up once you type in “have” on Google translate is “가지다”.

And “가지다” conjugates to “가져요.”

So you would think it’s “저는 펜을 가져요” But this is totally wrong.

And this is a classic example of why you can’t trust a translator.

The way you say “you have something” is actually in the TSV structure we’ve been learning for the past couple of lessons.

But let me briefly talk about 가지다 first.

가지다 can never be used on its own in a simple present tense. What I mean is that it can only be used in certain grammar forms like “저는 펜을 가지고 싶어요. - I WANT to have a pen”, or “저는 펜을 가지고 있어요 – which means “I’m having a pen” literally, but translates to “I have a pen ON ME.”

We’re going to learn about these forms in detail in level 2.

So, for now, just remember that **가지다** cannot be used by itself in the basic present tense to say "I have something." Again, nobody ever says “저는 펜을 가져요.”

I’m sure you can now see how you just cannot translate from English to Korean word for word just like I’ve always been saying. It just doesn’t work like that. So remember that your initial input should ALWAYS be in Korean, not the other way around.

Now, I want you to forget about the English word “have” and start thinking in terms of “WHAT exists in your possession?”

Let’s get right in.

**저는 펜이 있어요 – I have a pen**

This is how you say “I have a pen” in Korean. And it’s in the TSV structure.

있다 means “to be” as in “there is” or “To exist”

And we also have 없다, which is simply the opposite of 있다, namely “to NOT be” like “There isn’t” – or “To NOT exist”

So “I don’t have a pen” in Korean would be “저는 펜이 없어요”

**Show [“There is a pen / This is a pen”]**

A friendly reminder: 있다 is not the same as 이다 although in English, we use the same word “be” for both ideas.

Super important thing to note is that 있다 and 없다 are DESCRIPTIVE verbs, not action verbs.

But wait, “To exist” in English is an action verb. How come they’re descriptive verbs in Korean?

That’s because existence, by nature, is a state, not an action. You can’t “willingly” exist like you can’t force yourself into existence nor can you “exist” anything.

It just exists and it stays that way until it doesn’t. And that STATE of existence is what 있다 describes and 없다 describes its absence. That’s why they are descriptive verbs.

If it helps you, you can think of “있다” as “TO BE in existence”

So yes, 있다 doesn’t mean “to have” literally and can only be INTERPRETED as “have.”

Now, some people take “있다” literally as have and say “저는 펜을 있어요”. This is a super common mistake. But this is an incorrect sentence because descriptive verbs can never have an object.

And you can’t “exist” a pen. This makes no sense even in English.

Let me lay out the way it works.

Notice the subject marker 이, indicating that 펜 is the subject of the verb 있다.

Like I said, you should think of “What EXISTS in your possession”. And in this case, it’s 펜. Hence 펜이 with the subject marker.

So the technical meaning of 저는 펜이 있어요 is much closer to “To me, a pen exists” rather than “I have a pen.”

**있다 – “Have” or “There is?”**

Okay, now you know that 있다 literally means “To exist” and can only be INTERPRETED as “have”.

But how do you distinguish when it means “to have” and when it means “there is”?

It’s really easy to tell if you look at these three things: context, and the particle.

For example, 저는 펜이 있어요 this, without a doubt, means I have a pen.

But if you said, 여기 펜이 있어요, 여기 means here, and this obviously means here is a pen.

Let’s look at another example where you can distinguish the meaning by the particle.

나는 집**이** 있어요

나는 집**에** 있어요

집 is house or home. By the way, we have just one word for both house and home in Korean – 집.

Now these two sentences mean completely different things.

* **나는 집이 있어요**. This means "I have a house."
* **나는 집에 있어요.** But this one means "I’m AT home." The particle **에** is called the location marker. I briefly talked about this particle in the lesson “How Korean works”. This particle will be more relevant in the lesson It’s like “At” or “In” in English. So, this means you are physically present at home.
