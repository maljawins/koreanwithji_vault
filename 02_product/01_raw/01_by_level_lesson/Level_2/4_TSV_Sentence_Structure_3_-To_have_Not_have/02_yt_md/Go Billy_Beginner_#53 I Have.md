### Overview
The instructor introduces how to express possession in Korean, specifically focusing on the concept of "having" something. The lesson centers on two primary verbs: 있다, which learners may already know as "to exist," and 가지다, which specifically relates to holding or carrying an item. The instructor explains the grammatical nuances of each, particularly why certain possession sentences do not use object markers.

The learning arc moves from a review of existence to the functional use of these verbs for possession, followed by a critical distinction regarding what types of "things" can be "had" with each verb. The lesson concludes with a practice session where students apply these rules to various vocabulary words, including body parts, physical objects, and abstract concepts like questions.

### Using 있다 to Mean "To Have"
The verb 있다 literally means "to exist" or "to be at a location." The instructor provides review examples such as 교실에 학생들이 많이 있어요 (There are many students in the classroom) and 우리 집에 소금이랑 설탕이 있어요 (There is salt and sugar at my house). However, this same verb is the most common way to say "to have" in Korean.

A crucial grammatical rule is highlighted on screen: because 있다 is a descriptive verb meaning "to exist," it can never take an object marker (을/를). Instead, the item being "had" is treated as the subject that exists in relation to the speaker. For example, in the sentence 저는 시간이 많이 있어요 (I have a lot of time), the literal translation is "As for me, a lot of time exists." Using an object marker here, such as 저는 시간을 많이 있어요, is strictly incorrect.

---

### Possession with 가지고 있다
The instructor introduces 가지다 (or the shortened 갖다) as a verb meaning "to have" or "to carry with you." While learners may have seen this in the form 가지고 싶어요 (I want to have/possess something), it is frequently used in the present progressive-style form 가지고 있다 (or 갖고 있다) to indicate that one currently possesses or is carrying an item.

The instructor compares two ways to say "I have a hat." One might say 우리 집에 모자가 있어요 to mean "I have a hat at my house" (it exists there). Alternatively, saying 모자를 가지고 있어요 implies "I currently have a hat," likely meaning the speaker is carrying it or has it on their person. Unlike 있다, the verb 가지다 is an active verb and therefore uses the object marker (을/를).

---

### Constraints on 가지다
A vital distinction is made regarding the usage of 가지고 있다. This form is only used for physical objects that a person could reasonably carry. The instructor explains that you cannot use 가지고 있다 to say you have a brother, a sister, or body parts like eyes. For these situations, you must use the verb 있다 to indicate their existence. For example, you would say "I have a nose" using 있다, but never with 가지고 있다.

### Practice and Application
The lesson concludes with a "Conversation Practice" segment. The instructor presents six vocabulary words and asks the viewer to decide if they should use the 있다 pattern (Subject + 이/가 있어요) or the 가지고 있다 pattern (Object + 을/를 가지고 있어요).

- **코 (nose):** Only 있다 is possible because you cannot "carry" a nose.
  > **저는 코가 있어요.** / I have a nose.
- **컴퓨터 (computer):** Both are possible.
  > **저는 컴퓨터가 있어요.** / I have a computer (it exists).
  > **저는 컴퓨터를 가지고 있어요.** / I have a computer (possession/carrying).
- **여드름 (acne/pimple):** Only 있다 is possible as it is a physical condition, not a carried object.
  > **저는 여드름이 있어요.** / I have acne.
- **태극기 (Korean flag):** Both are possible.
  > **저는 태극기가 있어요.** / I have a Korean flag.
  > **저는 태극기를 가지고 있어요.** / I am carrying/possessing a Korean flag.
- **지갑 (wallet):** Both are possible.
  > **저는 지갑이 있어요.** / I have a wallet.
  > **저는 지갑을 가지고 있어요.** / I have a wallet (on me).
- **질문 (question):** Only 있다 is possible because a question is an abstract concept, not a physical item to be carried.
  > **저는 질문이 있어요.** / I have a question.
