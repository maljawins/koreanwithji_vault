## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the concept of "to become" in Korean, which is primarily expressed through the verb 되다. While 되다 is the direct translation, the lesson emphasizes that it is often used in contexts where English speakers might simply use the verb "to be," particularly when discussing future states or changes in status. The learning arc moves from using 되다 with nouns to a more complex structure used with descriptive verbs (adjectives) to show a change in state over time.

---

### Using 되다 with Nouns
The verb 되다 literally means "to become." It is used to describe a change into a certain state or profession, often where English uses "to be" for future intentions. For example, the English sentence "I want to be a teacher" is expressed in Korean as "I want to become a teacher" because it refers to a future transformation.

The instructor provides a specific grammar formula for this usage: **Noun + Subject Marker (이/가) + 되다**. If the noun ends in a consonant, use the marker 이; if it ends in a vowel, use 가. This noun represents the state or thing that the subject is becoming. The verb 되다 is then conjugated according to the desired tense or politeness level.

Visual examples shown on screen demonstrate this pattern:
- **저는 의사가 되고 싶어요.** / I want to be (become) a doctor. (의사 ends in a vowel, so 가 is used).
- **정말 큰 문제가 됐어요.** / It became a really big problem. (문제 ends in a vowel, so 가 is used; 되다 is conjugated in the past tense).
- **우리는 좋은 친구가 될 거예요.** / We will be (become) good friends. (친구 ends in a vowel, so 가 is used; 되다 is conjugated in the future tense).
- **제가 원하면 한국 시민이 될 수 있어요.** / If I want, I can be (become) a Korean citizen. (시민 ends in a consonant, so 이 is used).

---

### Becoming with Descriptive Verbs: -아/어/여지다
When the goal is to say "to become [adjective]" (e.g., "to become cold" or "to get pretty"), the verb 되다 cannot be used directly. Instead, Korean uses a specific conjugation pattern attached to descriptive verbs. This is necessary because the subject isn't becoming a "thing" (noun), but is rather transitioning into a different state described by an adjective.

The instructor explains the construction: take the stem of a descriptive verb and conjugate it as if adding the polite 요 ending (the 아/어/여 form), but instead of adding 요, attach **지다**. This combined form then functions as a regular active verb that can be further conjugated for tense.

The instructor provides several transformation examples:
- **멀다 (to be far)** becomes **멀어지다 (to become far / to get further)**.
- **예쁘다 (to be pretty)** becomes **예뻐지다 (to become pretty / to get prettier)**.
- **강하다 (to be strong)** becomes **강해지다 (to become strong / to get stronger)**.

This form is essential for expressing "getting" or "becoming" a certain way in a way that the simple future tense cannot. For instance, saying "The weather will be cold" (추울 거예요) is different from "The weather will get/become colder" (추워질 거예요).

Further examples illustrate this in context:
- **날씨가 더 좋아질 거예요.** / The weather will get (become) better.
- **저는 내일부터 정말 바빠질 거예요.** / I will be (become) really busy from tomorrow.
- **영희가 많이 예뻐졌어요.** / Young-hee got (became) very pretty.
- **최근에 제 노트북이 조금 느려졌어요.** / Recently, my laptop became a little slow.
