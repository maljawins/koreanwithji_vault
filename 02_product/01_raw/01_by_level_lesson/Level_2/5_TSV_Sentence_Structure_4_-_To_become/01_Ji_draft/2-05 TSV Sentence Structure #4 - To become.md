**이/가 되다 – To become**

Welcome. In this lesson, I’m going to introduce you to “되다” which is also one of the most important verbs in Korean, right up there with 하다.

It has many meanings and usages. But “to become” is its most standard dentition.

So we use 되다 to say "To become something” in the TSV structure.

Let me tell you this first. 을/를 되다 is always wrong no matter the usage because 되다 simply cannot take an object.

Now, a “become” phrase in English, for example, “become a doctor” makes “a doctor” look like the object of the sentence. But the action of becoming cannot be done to a doctor. You simply BECOME a doctor. So grammatically speaking, it’s actually a complement, not an object. A complement is simply the word that completes the meaning of a sentence.

So, again, “의사를 되다” is completely wrong because “a doctor” can never be the receiver of action of becoming. Rather, you, yourself BECOME a doctor.

And in Korean, you have to use the subject marker to mark **WHAT** it is you become therefore it should be “의사**가** 되다”

And here’s how 되다 conjugates.

되 doesn’t end in ㅏ or ㅗ, so what comes after is 어. So 되어 IS correct. But it’s almost always shortened to “돼”. ㅚ + ㅓ is ㅙ, so we get 돼(요).

Note that “돼” sounds exactly the same as the verb stem 되. It only LOOKS different in writing.

Fun fact : this is one of the most common mistakes among Koreans just like people get mixed up with “Your” and “You’re” in English.

**되다 meanings and usages**

Like I mentioned earlier, 되다 has a lot of meanings depending on the context, but they all come back to these four core ideas.

**First, 되다 means “to become.” Like I said, t**his is the most standard meaning.

**병아리는 닭이 돼요.** → *A chick becomes a chicken.*

Second, **되다 can also mean “to work” or “to function.”**

**컴퓨터가 안 돼요**
→ *The computer doesn’t work.*

**Next, 되다 is the passive form of 하다**, meaning *“to be done”.*

The idea behind this meaning is also an extension of the previous meaning.

It worked, so it’s done. Same idea. Different words.

But in Korean, we use ONE word but with different tenses.

If it works, it’s in the present tense

If it’s done, it’s in the past tense.

Here’s a really common phrase, which you’ve probably heard at least once.

“됐어(요)”

Literally, it means “it’s done”, but you can use it like “*Forget it”* ,*“No thanks.”* “I’m good” *or “Fine”.*

I mean, the meaning completely changes depending on context and delivery.

[Show short k-drama clips as examples comparing “됐어요 – It worked!” and “됐어요 – Forget it”]

And finally, **되다 is used to mean “to be okay” or “to be acceptable.”**

You’ve probably heard “**안 돼” It’s always translated to just “No” in English.**

**안 means “not” so** what it actually means is *“It’s not okay” or “it’s not acceptable”* **which explains why** we use 안 돼 in this type of situation [Show 엎어진 라면] where you would go [Insert dramatic No!!] in English.

앞으로 우리가 배울 많은 grammar form에서 지금 알려준 이 뜻으로 “되다”를 자주 볼 수 있을 것이다. 지금 단계의 너에겐 너무 advanced하기 때문에 간단한 예시만 보여주도록 하겠다.

“가도 돼요?” This is how we say “Can I go?” to ask for a permission to go.

However, it literally means “Is it okay even if I go?” because 가다 means to go and you can think of 도 as “even if” for the time being. And 되다, again means it’s okay.

So, “Is it okay for me to go?”가 조금은 더 정확한 해석이 되겠다.
