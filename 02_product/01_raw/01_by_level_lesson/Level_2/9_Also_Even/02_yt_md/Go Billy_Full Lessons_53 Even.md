### Basic Usage with Nouns: 도
The most fundamental way to say "even" is by using the particle **도**. Most learners first encounter 도 as meaning "also" or "too," but its literal meaning encompasses "even" as well. The specific translation depends entirely on the context of the sentence.

When attached to a noun, 도 functions as a marker that adds information to a subject or object. For example, **저도 (me too / even I)** can change meaning based on the surrounding conversation. The instructor provides the example sentence:
> **저도 할 수 있어요.** / I can do it too. (or) Even I can do it.

Another example demonstrates its use with objects:
> **사과도 맛있어요.** / Apples are delicious too. (or) Even apples are delicious.

---

### Even If: Using 도 with Verbs
To express "even if" an action occurs, the particle **도** is attached to a verb. The instructor explains a specific conjugation rule for this form: the verb must be conjugated as if it were in the polite 요 form, but the 요 is replaced with 도. The on-screen graphic illustrates this pattern: **verb ~아/어/etc + 도**.

This structure creates a conditional meaning. For instance, if you want to say "even if you don't go," you take the verb **가다 (to go)**, apply the negative **안**, conjugate it, and add the particle. The instructor shows this full sentence on screen:
> **학교에 안 가도 숙제는 해야 돼요.** / Even if you don't go to school, you have to do homework.

This verb form is also frequently combined with the verbs **되다 (to work/be okay)** or **괜찮다 (to be okay)** to ask for or grant permission. In these contexts, it translates to "it is okay if..." or "may I...".
- **컴퓨터를 써도 돼요.** / Can I use the computer? (Literally: Even if I use the computer, is it okay?)
- **가도 괜찮아요?** / Is it okay if I leave? (Literally: Even if I go, is it okay?)

---

### Emphatic "Even": 이라도 and 라도
When a speaker wants to add extra emphasis to "even"—often to express surprise or to highlight an extreme example—they use **이라도** or **라도**. The choice between the two depends on whether the preceding noun ends in a consonant or a vowel.

- Use **이라도** if the noun ends in a consonant.
- Use **라도** if the noun ends in a vowel.

The instructor provides two examples to illustrate this emphasis. The first uses **사람 (person)**, which ends in a consonant:
> **제가 미국 사람이라도 한국말을 잘 할 수 있어요.** / Even though I am an American, I can speak Korean well.

The second example uses **남자 (man)**, which ends in a vowel:
> **스코트랜드에서는 남자라도 치마를 입어요.** / In Scotland, even men wear skirts.

These forms are particularly useful when the speaker wants to point out that a certain condition or fact does not prevent the following action or state from being true.
