### Basic "Even If" with 도 and 그래도
The most fundamental way to say "even if" is by attaching the particle **도** to a conjugated verb. To use this, you first conjugate the verb into the 아/어/여 form and then add 도. For example, 하다 becomes 해도. This form is frequently used when asking for or giving permission, often paired with the verbs 되다 (to be okay/to work) or 괜찮다 (to be fine).

> **오늘 피자 먹어도 돼요?** / Can I eat pizza today?
> **저도 가도 괜찮아요?** / Can I go too?

The instructor notes that the verbs 이다 (to be) and 아니다 (to not be) are exceptions. 이다 becomes 이라도 after a consonant or 라도 after a vowel, while 아니다 becomes 아니라도. While 이라도/라도 can mean "even if it is," it has a common secondary meaning of "at least" or "or something," used when choosing a second-best option.

To connect two separate sentences with the meaning of "still" or "nevertheless," the word **그래도** is used at the start of the second sentence. It can also be attached to other connectors like 고, 지만, or 는데 to reinforce the contrast.

---

### Hypothetical and Emphatic "Even If": 더라도 and 지라도
The connector **더라도** is used to mean "even if" or "no matter if," but with a stronger emphasis on hypothetical or unlikely situations compared to the simple 도 form. It is attached directly to the verb stem.

> **실패하더라도 포기하지 마세요.** / Even if you fail, don't give up.
> **무슨 일이 있더라도 내가 도와줄게.** / No matter what happens, I'll help you.

A more formal and literary version of this is **지라도**. While 더라도 implies the speaker thinks the situation is unlikely, 지라도 can be used regardless of likelihood. It is attached as 을지라도 after a consonant or ㄹ지라도 after a vowel. To add even more emphasis to these "even if" expressions, the adverbs **비록** (although/even though) or **설사** (even supposing) can be added to the beginning of the clause. The instructor cautions that 설사 is typically used in sentences with negative implications or results.

---

### Quoted Concessions: 다고 해도
The form **다고 해도** (or 라고 해도 for 이다/아니다) is a combination of a quoting form and 해도 (short for 말해도). It translates to "even if one were to say" or "even supposing." It is often used when reacting to information heard from others or when making a hypothetical statement based on a claim.

> **이제 맞다고 해도 믿을 수가 없어요.** / Even if they say it's correct now, I can't believe it.

---

### Giving Examples with 만 해도 and 만 하더라도
When the speaker wants to provide a specific example to explain a broader point, they use **만 해도** or the more emphatic **만 하더라도**. These are attached directly to nouns and translate to "take for example," "just," or "even just [noun] alone."

> **1년 전만 해도 아무것도 없었어요.** / Just one year ago, there was nothing.
> **신발만 해도 백만 원이에요.** / Just shoes are a million won.

A less common but similar expression is **의 경우만 봐도**, which literally means "even if you only look at the case of..."

---

### Expressing Futility: 봤자, 봐야, and (으)나 마나
Several connectors are used to express that an action is useless or that the result will be the same regardless of the effort. **봤자** (conjugated verb + 봤자) and **봐야** (less common) mean "even if you try [action], it's no use."

> **더 해봤자 소용없을 거예요.** / Even if I try more, it'll be no use.
> **지금 가봤자 다 팔렸을 거예요.** / Even if I go now, they'll all be sold.

Similarly, **(으)나 마나** (verb stem + 나 마나/으나 마나) literally means "whether you do it or stop," implying the outcome is a foregone conclusion.

> **보나 마나 우리 팀이 이기겠지.** / It's obvious that our team will win (lit. whether you look or not...).
> **그 사람은 있으나 마나 도움이 안 돼요.** / No matter if that person is here or not, they're not helpful.

---

### Regardless of Choice: 든지 and 이고
The connector **든지** (often shortened to **든**) is used to show that any choice among multiple options is acceptable, translating to "regardless of whether" or "either... or." It can be repeated for emphasis (e.g., 하든지 안 하든지).

> **배추김치든 깍두기든 둘 다 좋아요.** / Whether it's Napa cabbage kimchi or radish kimchi, both are good.

In informal speech, **이고** (or **이고 뭐고**) is used to dismiss a topic as unimportant or to express lack of interest.

> **공부고 뭐고 일단 좀 쉬어요.** / Studying or whatever, first let's rest a bit.
> **돈이고 뭐고 다 필요 없어요.** / Money or whatever, I don't need any of it.
