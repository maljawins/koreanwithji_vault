## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Korean particle **도**, which is a fundamental grammar building block used to add the meanings of "also," "too," or "even" to a sentence. While learners have previously encountered the topic marker (은/는) and the object marker (을/를), **도** serves as a versatile alternative that attaches directly to nouns. The lesson focuses on the basic mechanics of attaching **도** to nouns and highlights critical rules regarding its interaction with other particles.

---

### Definition and Basic Usage
The particle **도** translates to "even," "also," or "too" depending on the English context. It is never used as a standalone word; it must be attached to a noun. The instructor demonstrates this through several simple transformations:
- **저** (me) + **도** = **저도** (even me, I also, me too)
- **피자** (pizza) + **도** = **피자도** (even pizza, also pizza, pizza too)
- **한국어** (Korean language) + **도** = **한국어도** (even Korean, also Korean, Korean too)
- **빌리** (Billy) + **도** = **빌리도** (even Billy, Billy also, Billy too)

While the instructor mentions that **도** can occasionally be added to other particles, this lesson specifically focuses on its placement after nouns to indicate that the noun is an addition to a previous statement or context.

---

### Conversation Practice
The instructor provides a dialogue between two characters, Alex and Jessica, to illustrate how **도** functions in a natural exchange. 

In the first part of the conversation, Alex expresses a desire:
> **알렉스: 저는 커피를 마시고 싶어요.** / Alex: As for me, I want to drink coffee.

Jessica uses **도** to show she has the same desire:
> **제시카: 저도 커피를 마시고 싶어요.** / Jessica: I also want to drink coffee.

In the second part, Alex lists multiple things he likes:
> **알렉스: 저는 커피를 좋아해요. 케이크도 좋아해요.** / Alex: I like coffee. I also like cake.

Jessica responds by confirming her shared preference:
> **제시카: 저도 케이크를 좋아해요.** / Jessica: I also like cake.

Through these examples, the instructor shows that **도** can replace the topic marker when referring to the subject (저도) or the object marker when referring to an additional item (케이크도).

---

### Important Usage Notes and Restrictions
A critical rule for using **도** is that it replaces the topic marker (**은/는**) and the object marker (**을/를**). It also replaces the subject marker, which is covered in later lessons. The instructor emphasizes that these markers cannot be combined with **도**. You must choose one or the other.

The following combinations are explicitly labeled as incorrect and should never be used:
- **Topic Marker combinations:** 은도, 는도, 도는
- **Object Marker combinations:** 을도, 를도, 도를

Instead of trying to combine them (e.g., "저는 도"), the speaker must simply use **저도**. The instructor advises learners to simply pick the particle that conveys the intended meaning—either the standard marker or the additive **도**—but never both at once.
