**Intro**

Welcome. In this lesson, we’re going to learn how to say “Also & Only”

**Let’s start with the particle “도” meaning “also”.**

**도 – Too, Also, Even**

In English, the sentence **“I also study math”** can have three different meanings.

1. **“I, too, study math”** – Someone else studies math, and I do, too
2. **“I study physics, and I study math too”** → I study other subjects, and math as well.
3. **“I do many things, and studying math is one of them”** – emphasis on *studying*.

So, in English, the **intonation and context** dictate the meaning
But in Korean, the meaning changes based on **where you place the particle**.

* **저도 수학을 공부해요** → *I, too, study math.*
* **저는 수학도 공부해요** → *I study math too (along with other subjects).*
* **저는 수학 공부도 해요** → *I also do math study (along with other activities).*

This rule also applies to **만** ("only"), which is what we’re learning next. But for now, let’s focus on **도**.

So, here’s another example sentence.

* **저도 강아지를 좋아해요**
  → *I, too, like dogs.*
  (Meaning: I’m not the only one who likes dogs, or *Even I* like dogs.)
* **저는 강아지도 좋아해요**
  → *I like dogs too.*
  (Meaning: I like other animals or things, and dogs are one of them. It could also mean *I even like dogs*, depending on context.)

In Korean, **도** covers both “also” and “even” — the difference comes from context.

If you think about it, the word “even” is just like a dramatic version of “also”.

Now there are more particles that mean “Even” but let’s just stick to “도” for now.

Also, **도** can attach to other particles to add more meaning:

* **월요일에도 미팅이 있어요**
  → *I have a meeting on Monday, too* / *Even on Monday.*
* **저는 집에서도 운동을 했어요**
  → *I worked out at home, too* / *Even at home.*

❗ **Note:** 도 cannot be added *after* topic (은/는), subject (이/가), or object (을/를) particles.

Instead, **도 replaces them**.

For example: 저**는도** / 저**가도** / 저**를도** are wrong.

It should always be 저**도.**
