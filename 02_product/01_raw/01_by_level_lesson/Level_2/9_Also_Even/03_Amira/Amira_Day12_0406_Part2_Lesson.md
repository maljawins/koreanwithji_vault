## Lesson Overview
Continuing from Part 1, where Ji finished explaining how the topic marker 는 functions as a contrast marker when attached to other particles, this part moves into the progressive tense and expressing desire. Ji explains the difference between 고 있다 and 는 중이다, then covers how to express "wanting" to do an action versus "wanting" an object. The lesson concludes with negative commands (지 말다) and a discussion of the upcoming homework and schedule.

## Teaching Points
- **Progressive Tense (~ing):**
    - **고 있다:** Used for a broader timeframe (e.g., "these days"). It is the only progressive form that functions in the future tense.
    - **는 중이다:** Literally "in the middle of." Used for actions happening right at this moment. It cannot be used for future plans.
    - Both forms apply only to action verbs, as descriptive verbs cannot be "ongoing."
- **Expressing Desire:**
    - **고 싶다:** Used for 1st person ("I want") and 2nd person ("Do you want?").
    - **Nuance of 가/이 with 싶다:** Since 싶다 is a descriptive verb meaning "to be desirable," using the subject marker (피자가 먹고 싶어요) is more natural than the object marker.
    - **기 싫다:** The preferred, more emotionally expressive way to say "I don't want to" or "I don't feel like it."
- **Wanting Objects:** Learners should avoid 원하다 in casual speech and instead use "Object + 가지고 싶어요" (want to have) or specific verbs like "Object + 먹고 싶어요."
- **Negative Commands:** Using the helping verb 말다 in the form 지 마세요 (Don't do it).

## Student Notes
- **Progressive Negation:** Never say "고 없어요." Use "안 ...고 있어요" or "...고 있지 않아요."
- **Natural "Want":** Use the subject marker (가/이) with 고 싶다 to sound more like a native speaker.
- **Emotional Expression:** Use 기 싫어요 when you feel tired or annoyed; it's more natural than 하고 싶지 않아요.
- **Object "Want":** Instead of "I want [Noun]," say "I want to have/eat/raise [Noun]."
