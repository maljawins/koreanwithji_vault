## Teaching Points

**1. Revisiting "하다" (to do) Verbs**
*   **Present Tense:** "하다" is unique because it takes the suffix "여" instead of "아/어". "하 + 여" contracts to "해", resulting in "해요".
*   **Past Tense:** Follows the same contraction: "하 + 였" becomes "했", resulting in "했어요".
*   **Future Tense:** Unlike the present/past, the "여" suffix is NOT used. You simply add "ㄹ/을 거예요" to the stem "하", resulting in "할 거예요". Ji corrected Amira's "요리헬 거예요" to "요리할 거예요".

**2. Natural Phrasing and Honorifics**
*   **Honorifics for Elders:** When speaking about a grandmother, "가다" (to go) should be honorified to "가시다". To make it negative in the future tense: "가시지 않을 거예요" or "안 가실 거예요".
*   **Humble Speech:** When doing something for a superior (like a father), "해 주다" (to do for someone) becomes "해 드리다". Example: "요리를 해 드릴 거예요" (I will cook for you).
*   **Possessive "네":** While "의" is the standard possessive, "네" is more natural when referring to someone's house or place. Example: "사라 씨네 집" (Sarah's house).

**3. Particle Nuance and Word Order**
*   **Adverb Placement:** Adverbs like "많이" (a lot) or "열심히" (hard/diligently) should typically be placed immediately before the verb for natural flow. Example: "책을 많이 읽을게" is more natural than "많이 책을 읽을게".
*   **Contrastive "은/는":** Using "오늘은" (as for today) implies a contrast with other days. If just asking a casual question, dropping the particle ("오늘 카페 갈래요?") is often better.
*   **Object Markers:** Ji emphasized that "먹다" (to eat) almost always requires an explicit object like "밥" (meal/rice) in Korean, unlike the English "I didn't eat."

**4. Irregular Verbs (Part 1)**
*   **"ㅡ" Irregular:** The "ㅡ" vowel is dropped when followed by a suffix starting with a vowel ("아/어"). The choice of "아" or "어" depends on the vowel in the preceding syllable. Example: "바쁘다" → "바빠요"; "크다" → "커요".
*   **"ㅂ" Irregular:** When followed by a vowel, the "ㅂ" 받침 changes to "우". Example: "춥다" (to be cold) → "추우 + 어요" → "추워요". Future tense: "추울 거예요".
    *   *Exceptions:* "좁다" (narrow) and "입다" (wear) are regular. "돕다" (help) and "곱다" (beautiful) take "아" instead of "어".
