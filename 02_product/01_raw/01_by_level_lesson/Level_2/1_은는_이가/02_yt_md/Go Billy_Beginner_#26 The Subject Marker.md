## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the Korean subject marker, a fundamental grammar point that identifies the "doer" of an action or the "target" of a description. While learners have already encountered this marker in fixed expressions like `맛이 있다 (to be delicious)` and `재미가 있다 (to be fun)`, this lesson formalizes its usage rules and provides a clear framework for distinguishing it from the topic marker.

The lesson follows a logical progression: first defining the grammatical function of a subject, then providing the mechanical conjugation rules based on final consonants or vowels, and finally engaging in a nuanced comparison with the topic marker `은/는`. The instructor emphasizes that while the two markers can be confusing, they often work together in a single sentence to maintain context while introducing new specific information.

---

### What Is the Subject Marker?
The subject marker's primary role is to mark the subject of a verb. The definition of a "subject" depends on the type of verb being used. For action verbs, the subject is the person or thing performing the action. For descriptive verbs (adjectives), the subject is the person or thing being described. The instructor illustrates this with the examples `맛이 있다` and `재미가 있다`, where the markers `이` and `가` specifically point to "taste" and "fun" as the subjects being described as "existing."

---

### Conjugation Rules: 이 vs 가
Choosing between the two forms of the subject marker depends entirely on whether the preceding noun ends in a consonant or a vowel. If the noun ends in a consonant (has a 받침), you attach `이`. If the noun ends in a vowel (no 받침), you attach `가`. The instructor provides four visual examples to demonstrate this:
- `한글` (ends in consonant `ㄹ`) + `이` = `한글이`
- `피자` (ends in vowel `ㅏ`) + `가` = `피자가`
- `운동` (ends in consonant `ㅇ`) + `이` = `운동이`
- `빌리` (ends in vowel `ㅣ`) + `가` = `빌리가`

---

### Subject Marker vs. Topic Marker
The instructor contrasts the subject marker with the topic marker `은/는`. While the topic marker sets the overall theme of the conversation (often translated as "as for..." or "when it comes to..."), the subject marker simply identifies the subject of a specific verb without shifting the broader topic. A key takeaway is that both can appear in the same sentence. For example, in the sentence `저는 피자가 맛있어요`, `저 (I)` is the topic marked by `는`, while `피자 (pizza)` is the specific subject marked by `가` that is being described as delicious. This prevents the sentence from accidentally meaning "I am delicious."

The instructor further illustrates the nuance using the sentences `빌리는 똑똑해요` and `빌리가 똑똑해요`. In the first version, Billy is the established topic, and subsequent sentences will likely continue to describe him. In the second version, Billy is merely the subject of that specific verb; the overall topic of the conversation remains unchanged from whatever was being discussed previously.

---

### Special Form: 제가
A critical irregular form occurs when the first-person pronoun `저 (I)` meets the subject marker `가`. Instead of becoming `저가`, it transforms into `제가`. The instructor provides a sample sentence to show this in context: `한국어는 재미있어요. 제가 많이 좋아해요.` In this example, "Korean" is the topic, and "I" is the specific subject performing the action of "liking."

---

### Adverb Usage: 더 (More)
During the conversation practice, the instructor introduces the adverb `더`, which means "more." It is placed directly before descriptive verbs to increase their intensity. For example, `더 맛있어요` means "is more delicious" or "tastes better." This is used to show preference or comparison between two subjects, such as pizza and hamburgers.
