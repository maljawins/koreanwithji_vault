**이/가 밉다 & 을/를 미워하다**

밉다 is an interesting word. It’s an irregular verb.

밉다 ends in ㅂ, so you replace the ㅂ 받침 with 우. So now you get 미우 and then after that you add 어요. That’s how you get 미워요 and 미워하다 from 밉다.

Now as for its meaning, if you look up 밉다, it will just tell you “hate”.

Well, it does mean “hate” but it’s not “hate-hate”. Let me explain.

“Hate” is a pretty strong word in English. But if you think about it, there are different levels of “hate”. And 밉다 would be the weakest level of hate.

For example, when a kid says “Mom, I hate you!”, he doesn’t **actually** hate his mom. THIS is exactly what 밉다 means. It has a "love-hate" feel to it. So ironically, you’d usually use 밉다 for someone you actually care about and not someone you ACTUALLY hate.

Therefore, 밉다 can only be used for **people or pets**, not for things.

Look at this kid.

[Show the book 선생님 미워!]

He’s saying “선생님 미워! – I hate you teacher!” while holding a flower in his hand. And this tells us that the poor boy has a crush on his teacher which is quite the opposite feeling of “hate”.

However, **싫다** straight up means "dislike" which can be used for both people and things. But it still has a weaker connotation than “hate”. For something you really hate, you can use adverbs like [너무 - too, 정말, 진짜 – really] in front of 싫다. Or 극혐하다 in slang.

The song “Dumb dumb” by “Red Velvet” has an interesting piece of lyric.

“미워도 싫지가 않잖아”

This translates to “Though I hate you, you know I don’t hate you.”

It makes no sense in English but now you know it makes perfect sense in Korean.

Take a look at these sentences.

“My leg hurts.” – 저는 다리가 아파요

“I hurt my leg.” – 저는 다리를 다쳤어요.

In English, we use the same word for both cases but in Korean we use 아프다 for the first and 다치다 for the second.

The difference is that 아프다 directly describes the feeling of pain, so it’s more like “painful” and is a descriptive verb whereas 다치다 means more like “to GET hurt” and is an action verb.

Therefore, the word we’re looking for is 아프다, not 다치다.

So we now have “저는 다리가 아파요” which literally means “To me, the leg is painful”.
