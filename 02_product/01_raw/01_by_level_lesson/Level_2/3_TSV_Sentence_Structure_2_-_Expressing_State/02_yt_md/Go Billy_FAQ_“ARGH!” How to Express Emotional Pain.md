### Overview

While a previous lesson focused on physical pain, this lesson explores the nuanced vocabulary used to express emotional pain in Korean. The instructor notes that there are dozens of specific words for emotions like regret, disappointment, anxiety, and guilt, but for the sake of practical communication, this lesson focuses on the most common variations of "sadness."

The instructor uses a "glass board" style to write out each expression in Hangul while explaining the specific situational nuances that distinguish one from another. The goal is to move beyond a simple dictionary definition of "sad" and understand which word fits a bad mood, a heartbreak, or the specific sadness of being lonely.

---

### General Mood and Basic Sadness

The most general way to express that you are not feeling well emotionally is **기분이 안 좋다 (to be in a bad mood)**. The instructor writes **기분(이) 안 좋다** on the screen, explaining that **기분** means "mood" and **안 좋다** means "not good." This is a useful, vague expression to use when you don't want to explicitly say "I am sad" but want to convey that you are feeling "meh" or just generally down. An example sentence provided is:
> **기분이 좀 안 좋아요.** / I'm in a bit of a bad mood. / I don't feel very good.

For the direct expression of sadness, the instructor introduces **슬프다 (to be sad)**. He writes **슬프다** on the board and explains that this is the standard, literal translation for when something sad happens or you experience a sad event.

---

### Deep Sadness and Heartache

When sadness is more intense, the word **우울하다 (to be depressed/gloomy)** is used. The instructor writes **우울하다** and clarifies that while it can translate to "depressed," it doesn't always imply a clinical condition. It is frequently used to describe feeling really down or "blue" on a particular day.
> **오늘 좀 우울해요.** / I feel a bit depressed today.

To express profound emotional pain, such as grief or extreme sadness, the instructor presents **가슴이 아프다 (to be heart-broken/sad)**. Literally, **가슴** means "chest," so the expression translates to "my chest hurts." This is used for situations that are deeply moving or tragic.

A similar but slightly different expression is **마음이 아프다 (to be heart-broken/hurt)**. Here, **마음** refers to the "heart" or "mind." The instructor notes that while this also means "sad," it carries a nuance of being disappointed, regretful, or emotionally hurt by a specific situation. It is slightly less common than **가슴이 아프다** for general sadness but is used when your "heart breaks" over something specific.

---

### Disappointment and Lonely Sadness

The word **섭섭하다 (to be disappointed/sorry)** is unique because it doesn't literally mean "sad," but it is often translated that way in context. The instructor writes **섭섭하다** and explains it describes a feeling of being sorry or disappointed that something is ending or that you missed out. For example, if you are sad to leave a party or sad that you weren't invited to one, **섭섭하다** is the appropriate word. It lacks the "upset" or "angry" edge of some types of disappointment; it is more of a wistful, "I wish it weren't so" feeling.

Finally, the instructor introduces **서글프다 (to be sad and lonely)**. He writes **서글프다** on the board and defines it as a specific combination of sadness and loneliness. This is the word to use when the source of your sadness is specifically your solitude or a feeling of being alone in the world.
