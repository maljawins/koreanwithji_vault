## Teaching Points

**1. Describing Others' Feelings with `~아/어 하다`**
*   **Logic:** In Korean, you can only state your own feelings directly. For others, you must describe their behavior.
*   **Structure:** Descriptive Verb Stem (D.V.S) + `~아/어 하다`. This transformation turns a descriptive verb (adjective) into an action verb.
    *   `슬프다` (to be sad) → `슬퍼하다` (to act/behave sadly).
*   **Usage by Person:**
    *   **1st Person:** Use the base D.V. form: `저는 슬퍼요` (I am sad).
    *   **2nd Person:** Can ask directly (`슬프세요?`), but commands/prohibitions require the action form: `슬퍼하지 마세요` (Don't be sad).
    *   **3rd Person:** Must use the `~아/어 하다` form: `얘가 슬퍼해요` (He acts sad).
*   **Desires:** The same logic applies to `~고 싶다` (want to). For others, it becomes `~고 싶어 하다`.
    *   `지수는 김밥을 먹고 싶어 해요.` (Jisoo acts like she wants to eat Gimbap).

**2. Beyond Feelings: Adoration and Hate**
*   Some `~아/어 하다` forms have evolved into specific transitive verbs:
    *   `예뻐하다` (to adore/cherish) from `예쁘다` (pretty).
    *   `귀여워하다` (to adore) from `귀엽다` (cute).
*   **Levels of Hate:**
    *   `밉다` / `미워하다`: A "weak" or "affectionate" hate, often used for people one cares about (e.g., a child to a mother).
    *   `싫다` / `싫어하다`: A more straightforward dislike or "hate," used for both things and people.

**3. Grammar Corrections from Homework**
*   **Future Tense + `~네(요)`:** You cannot use the "realization" ending `~네(요)` for the future because you cannot "realize" something that hasn't happened yet. Use `~군요` instead for figuring something out based on information.
    *   Incorrect: `미국에 살 거네요.`
    *   Correct: `미국에 사실 거군요.` (I see/figure you will live in the U.S.).
*   **`~(으)니까` vs. `~지 않습니까?`:**
    *   `~(으)니까` is used for a logical "because."
    *   `~지 않습니까?` (or the shortened `~지 않아요?` / `~잖아요?`) is a rhetorical "you know/right?" used when the listener already knows the fact.
*   **Multi-step Derivation:** Ji breaks down `정해져 있어요` (It is set/fixed):
    1.  `정하다` (to set/decide) + `~아/어지다` (passive) = `정해지다` (to be set).
    2.  `정해지다` + `~아/어 있다` (state of completed action) = `정해져 있다`.
    3.  Conjugate to present tense: `정해져 있어요`.
