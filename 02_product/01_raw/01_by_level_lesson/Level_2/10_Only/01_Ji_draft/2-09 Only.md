**Intro**

Welcome. In this lesson, we’re going to learn how to say “Only”

**Let’s start with the particle “만”.**

Just like **도**, **만** (meaning *only*) can be placed after any noun — and **where** you place it changes the meaning of the sentence.

It can also be attached to other particles like **에, 에서, 부터, 까지,** just like **도**.

But 만 CAN be used with subject, topic, and object marker. But it comes before the markers so it becomes 만이 만은 만을.

But you usually drop the 이 and 을 and just replace them using 만.

However, there is a slight difference between just 만 and 만은.

**So what’s the difference?**

* **난 이 순간만 기억해.**
  → *This is the only moment I remember.* (Plain and factual)
* **난 이 순간만은 기억해.**
  → *I might forget other moments, but not this one.*
  (은 implies contrast to other moments)

If you said 이 순간만 it just simply means this is the only moment I remember but if you said 만은 기억해, it would mean I could maybe forget other moments but at least not this moment.

So, the feeling and the nuance is completely different.

**밖에 + Negative**

Now, there’s **another way to say "only"** in Korean, and that’s **밖에**. “밖” means outside so 밖에 **literally means “outside of,”** and it can be translated as **“anything but.”**

**And 밖에 is always used with negative verbs.** It cannot be used in a positive sentence.

Think of it like this — It works similarly to the word **“any”** in English when used in a **negative sentence**. That’s why it always appears with **안** or **~지 않다** forms.

For example:

**저는 학교에서 밖에 공부를 안 해요**
⤷ I don’t study anywhere but at school.
⤷ In other words, **I only study at school.**

Another one:

**오늘 한 시간밖에 없어요**
⤷ I don’t have anything but one hour today.
⤷ Meaning, **I only have one hour.**

**It can also be used figuratively.**
For example:

**제 친구는 남자친구밖에 몰라요**
⤷ It doesn’t literally mean she only knows her boyfriend.
⤷ It means she’s so focused on him that she’s blind to anything else — like, **she’s all about her boyfriend.**

**And 밖에 can also be attached to other particles.**

For example: 에어컨이 사무실에 밖에 없어요

⤷ The air conditioner isn’t anywhere but in the office.

⤷ Meaning, I only have an air conditioner at the office — not at home.

Just make sure to put a space between the other particle and 밖에.

This is because 밖 is a separate word and **밖에** is treated as an adverbial phrase whereas particles like 에 and 도 are attached to the noun without the space

But in casual writing like on social media, many native speakers just ignore this spacing rule.

**밖에 VS 만**

Now let’s compare **만** and **밖에**.

First, **밖에** **cannot** be used with **‘아니다’**.
So,
❌ 여기서 나밖에 남자가 아니야 should be
✅ 여기서 나만 남자야 – *I’m the only man here.*

Also, **밖에** can’t be used in **propositive** or **imperative** sentences.
So,
❌ 이것밖에 하지 마 → ✅ 이것만 해 – *Only do this.*
❌ 오늘은 공부밖에 안 하자 → ✅ 오늘은 공부만 하자 – *Let’s just study today.*

Now, there are times when **밖에** sounds **more natural** than **만**.

Both mean *“I only eat vegetables,”* but **B** sounds more natural in many situations because **밖에** adds the nuance that *something is lacking or not enough*.
So, A might sound like: *“I only eat vegetables and I’m okay with that.”*
But B could sound like: *“I only eat vegetables... I wish I could eat other things too.”*

Also, **만** can be used in both **positive** and **negative** sentences, but **밖에** is used **only in negative sentences**.

For example:

* ✅ 집에 동생만 있어요 – *Only my brother is home.*
* ✅ 집에 동생밖에 없어요 – *No one else but my brother is home.*
* ✅ 집에 동생만 없어요 – *Everyone else is home except my brother.*
* ❌ 집에 동생밖에 있어요 – This is incorrect. **밖에** must be paired with a **negative verb**.
