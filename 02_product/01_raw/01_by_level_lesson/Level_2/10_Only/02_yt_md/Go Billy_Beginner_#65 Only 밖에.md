### The Meaning and Construction of 밖에
The word 박 originally means "outside," as seen in the example sentence 민우 씨는 밖에서 놀고 있어요 (Minwoo is playing outside). When combined with the particle 에, it creates the form 밖에, which can be used with a negative verb to mean "only." To use this correctly, the speaker must attach 밖에 directly to a noun without any space. The sentence must then conclude with a negative verb.

Literally, this construction translates to "outside of," "other than," or "nothing but." For example, saying "I only have 10,000 won" using this form literally suggests "Outside of 10,000 won, I have nothing." The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate this pattern:
- **저는 만 원밖에 없어요.** / I only have ten thousand won.
- **그 영화는 한글 자막밖에 없어요.** / That movie only has Korean subtitles.
- **한 명밖에 안 했어요.** / Only one person did it.
- **저는 영어밖에 할 수 없어요.** / I can only speak English.
- **저는 이거밖에 몰라요.** / I only know this.
- **오늘 한 시간밖에 없어요.** / I only have one hour today.
- **사과를 네 개밖에 안 샀어요.** / I only bought four apples.

---

### Comparing 밖에 and 만
While both 밖에 and 만 translate to "only" in English, they carry different nuances. The instructor compares two sentences:
- **A) 저는 채소만 먹어요.** / I only eat vegetables.
- **B) 저는 채소밖에 안 먹어요.** / I only eat vegetables.

Sentence B is described as sounding more natural in most cases. The key difference is that 밖에 emphasizes that something is lacking, missing, or insufficient. While 만 is a neutral statement of "only," 밖에 carries a feeling that the amount is "nothing but" or "not enough." In the vegetable example, sentence B implies that the speaker might wish they had more to eat or that eating only vegetables is a limited state. As a general rule, whenever a negative verb can be used, the 밖에 form will sound more natural than the particle 만.

---

### Usage Restrictions and Notes
The instructor highlights three critical rules for using 밖에. First, this form cannot be used with the descriptive verb 아니다 (to not be). Therefore, one cannot use it to say "I am only [something]." Second, 밖에 is strictly forbidden in commands or suggestions. It cannot be used to say "Only do this" or "Should we only do that?" In these imperative or suggestive contexts, the speaker must use the particle 만 instead. Finally, the instructor reiterates that despite these restrictions, 밖에 should be the preferred choice in standard declarative sentences for a more natural, native-like sound.
