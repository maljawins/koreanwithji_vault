## Teaching Points

### Overview
This lesson provides a comprehensive guide to expressing the concept of "only" in Korean, ranging from basic particles to advanced adverbs and complex grammar structures. The instructor emphasizes that while many learners default to the most basic form, choosing the correct expression depends heavily on the speaker's nuance, the grammatical context (such as positive vs. negative verbs), and whether the speaker intends to convey a sense of insufficiency or simple fact.

The learning arc begins with the standard particle 만, moves into the more natural but grammatically restrictive 밖에, explores the "just/nothing more" nuance of 뿐, and concludes with advanced adverbs like 오직 and 단지. The instructor also demonstrates how the concept of "only" is embedded in common structures like the "have to" (~야 되다) ending.

---

### The Standard Particle: 만
The most common and standard way to say "only" is by attaching the particle 만 to a noun. While it is versatile, it can sometimes sound slightly formal or "flat" because it lacks the emotional nuance found in other forms. When attached to a noun, it typically replaces the subject or object markers, though the markers can be added back after 만 for extra emphasis (e.g., 사과만을).

The placement of 만 is critical because it determines exactly what is being limited. The instructor illustrates this by comparing two sentences:
- **저는 사과만 먹어요.** / I only eat apples (and nothing else).
- **저만 사과를 먹어요.** / Only I eat apples (nobody else does).

---

### The Negative-Only Form: 밖에
The word 밖에 literally means "outside of" (from 박 meaning "outside" and 에 being the location particle). In the context of "only," it functions as "nothing outside of [noun]." Because of this literal meaning, it must always be used with a negative verb.

The instructor highlights that 밖에 is often more natural in conversation than 만, but it carries a specific connotation of insufficiency or lack. Using 밖에 implies that the amount is not enough or that the speaker is slightly complaining.
- **저는 사과만 먹어요.** / I only eat apples. (A neutral statement of fact).
- **저는 사과밖에 안 먹어요.** / I only eat apples. (Implies: "All I eat is apples, and it's not enough/I wish I had more").

There are two major restrictions for 밖에: it cannot be used in commands (imperatives) or in "if" statements (conditionals). For example, you cannot say "Give me only five dollars" using 밖에; you must use 만.

---

### The "Just" Nuance: 뿐이다
The form 뿐이다 (Noun + 뿐이다 or Verb Stem + ~을/ㄹ 뿐이다) translates to "it is only" or "it is just." It carries the nuance of "nothing more than" and is often used to downplay something.

The instructor explains a subtle but important distinction when using the optional 이다 (to be) in its future adjective form (~일) before 뿐:
- **너 뿐이야.** / It's only you. (Romantic/Positive: You are the only one for me).
- **너일 뿐이야.** / You're just you / You're nothing more than you. (Negative/Dismissive: Downplaying the person's importance).

Using the ~일 뿐이다 version adds a negative connotation of "nothing more than," which is appropriate for complaints like **그것은 돈의 낭비일 뿐이에요** (That is nothing more than a waste of money).

---

### Not Only... But Also: 뿐만 아니라
This is a more formal, "bookish" structure used to connect two ideas. It combines 뿐 and 만 with 아니라 (is not) to mean "not only [A] but also [B]."
- **그것 뿐만 아니라 이것도 그래요.** / Not only that, but this is also that way.
- **이 음식은 맛이 좋을 뿐만 아니라 건강에도 좋아요.** / This food is not only delicious but also good for your health.

---

### The Only One in Existence: 유일하다
When the speaker wants to emphasize that something is the "only" one in the sense of being unique or the sole thing in existence, the descriptive verb 유일하다 is used. It can be used as an adjective (유일한 + Noun) or an adverb (유일하게).
- **철수가 제 유일한 친구예요.** / Chul-soo is my only friend (my only friend in existence).
- **제가 유일하게 착한 사람이에요.** / I am the only nice person.

---

### Adverbs of Limitation: 단지, 단, 다만, and 오직
The instructor introduces several adverbs that refine the meaning of "only":
- **단지:** Means "simply." It is used to explain that a situation is not complex. Often paired with 뿐이다 (e.g., **단지 운이 좋을 뿐이에요** / It's simply that I'm lucky).
- **단:** An adjective used for emphasis, frequently appearing before numbers (e.g., **단 하나밖에 없어요** / There is only one single one).
- **다만:** Means "merely" or "it's just that." It is used at the beginning of a sentence, often to introduce a reason or a "but" clause.
- **오직:** An advanced adverb meaning "solely." It is used at the start of a sentence to provide heavy emphasis, often in conjunction with 만 or 뿐 (e.g., **오직 철수만 할 수 있는 거예요** / It is something that solely Chul-soo can do).

---

### "Only If" and the "Have To" Structure
The lesson concludes by deconstructing the common ~야 되다 (have to) ending. The 야 part actually means "only if." Therefore, **해야 돼요** literally means "Only if you do it, is it okay." This "only if" logic can be used in other sentences to show a prerequisite:
- **청소를 먼저 해야 같이 공원으로 갈 수 있어.** / Only if you clean first can you go to the park with us.
