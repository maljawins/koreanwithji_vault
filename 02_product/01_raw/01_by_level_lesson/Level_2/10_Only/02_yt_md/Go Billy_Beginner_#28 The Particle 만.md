## Teaching Points

### Overview
The instructor introduces the particle 만, which translates to "only" in English. As a particle, it is a functional piece of grammar that attaches directly to the end of a word to modify its meaning. The lesson focuses on the mechanics of attachment, the importance of placement within a sentence, and how 만 interacts with other common particles like the topic, subject, and object markers.

The learning arc begins with simple noun-particle pairings, moves into full sentence comparisons to show how meaning shifts based on placement, and concludes with advanced notes on particle stacking. Throughout the video, the instructor draws parallels between 만 and the previously learned particle 도 (also/too), as they share similar structural behaviors in Korean.

---

### Basic Usage and Meaning
The particle 만 means "only" and attaches directly after a noun without any spaces. Like the particle 도, it cannot stand alone and must have a preceding word to support it. The instructor provides several foundational examples to illustrate this: `저만 (only me / only I)`, `피자만 (only pizza)`, `고양이만 (only a cat / only cats)`, and `한국어만 (only the Korean language)`.

---

### The Importance of Placement
The meaning of a Korean sentence can change significantly depending on which noun the particle 만 attaches to. The instructor demonstrates this by comparing two sentences using the same vocabulary but different particle placement. In the first example, `저만 김치를 좋아해요`, the particle attaches to the subject, meaning "Only I like kimchi" (implying no one else likes it). In the second example, `저는 김치만 좋아해요`, the particle attaches to the object, meaning "I only like kimchi" (implying I don't like any other foods). This visual comparison highlights that 만 limits the specific word it follows.

---

### Using 만 with 하다 Verbs
Similar to the particle 도, 만 can be inserted between the noun and the verb in `하다` verb constructions. Normally, these verbs consist of a noun and the verb `하다 (to do)`, often separated by an object marker. By replacing the object marker with 만, the meaning changes to "only doing" that specific action. For example, `공부를 하다 (to study)` becomes `공부만 하다 (to only study and do nothing else)`. This is contrasted with `공부도 하다 (to also study)`, showing how these two particles function in the same grammatical slot.

---

### Particle Stacking and Replacement Rules
The instructor provides detailed notes on how 만 interacts with other markers. Most of the time, 만 simply replaces the topic marker `은/는`, the subject marker `이/가`, or the object marker `을/를`. However, it is grammatically possible to stack them, provided 만 comes first. The instructor lists correct combinations such as `만은/만는` (topic), `만을` (object), and `만이` (subject), though he notes these are much less common in everyday speech.

Crucially, the reverse order is strictly forbidden. The instructor displays a list of incorrect combinations on screen to warn learners: `은만/는만`, `을만/를만`, and `이만/가만` are all incorrect. While there are other ways to express "only" in Korean—such as using negative verb constructions which will be covered in future lessons—만 is the most versatile and useful form for beginners.
