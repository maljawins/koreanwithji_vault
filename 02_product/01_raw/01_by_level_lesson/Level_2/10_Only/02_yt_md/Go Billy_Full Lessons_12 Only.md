### Overview
The instructor introduces various ways to translate the English word "only" into Korean, emphasizing that the choice depends heavily on context and the specific grammatical function required. The lesson covers particles attached to nouns, markers used with nominalized verbs, and more complex structures used for emphasis or negative constructions.

The learning arc begins with the most common particle, 만, and its counterparts like 뿐 and 밖에. It then progresses to more advanced vocabulary and combined forms such as 뿐만 아니라. By the end of the lesson, students are introduced to literary or specific adjectival forms like 유일하다 and 따름이다, providing a comprehensive toolkit for expressing exclusivity in Korean.

---

### The Particle 만
The most common way to say "only" is by using the particle 만. It is attached directly after a noun. Visually, the instructor shows that 만 can replace topic markers (은/는) or subject markers (이/가) that would normally occupy that position in a sentence. While they can be added back alongside 만, the meaning remains the same.

> **저만 한국말을 합니다.** / Only I speak Korean.
> **그는 영화만 좋아해요.** / He likes only movies.

Beyond simple nouns, 만 can be used with verbs that have been nominalized using the ~기 form. This allows for sentences expressing that one "only does" a certain action.

> **매일 수업에 가기만 하면 졸업할 수 있을 거예요.** / If you only go to class, you'll be able to graduate.

---

### The Particle 뿐
The particle 뿐 is similar to 만 but is typically used with the verb 이다 (to be). A key grammatical distinction is that while 만 works with ~기 nominalization, 뿐 does not. Instead, to use 뿐 with a verb, one must use the ~ㄹ/을 뿐이다 construction.

> **내게 남은 것은 시간뿐이다.** / The only thing I have left is time.
> **나는 기도할 뿐이다.** / I only pray.

The instructor notes that `내게` is a contraction of `나에게`. To express the same "only pray" sentiment using 만, one would say **나는 기도하기만 한다**.

---

### Negative Exclusivity with 밖에
The word 밖에 literally means "outside" (e.g., **집 밖에** / outside the house), but when used in negative constructions, it functions as "nothing but," "other than," or "except." It must be followed by a negative verb like 못 하다 (cannot) or 없다 (to not be/have).

> **나는 한국말밖에 못 해.** / I can only speak Korean. (Lit: I don't speak anything other than Korean.)
> **그 사람밖에 없어.** / There's only that person. (Lit: There isn't anybody except that person.)

A specific advanced construction is ~ㄹ/을 수밖에 없다, which translates to "to have no choice but to [verb]" or "the only thing I can do is [verb]."

> **시험 결과가 아직 안 나왔는데, 기다릴 수밖에 없어.** / The test results haven't come out yet, so the only thing I can do is wait.
> **이제 한국에 있으니까, 한국말을 배울 수밖에 없다.** / Now that I'm in Korea, there's nothing I can do but learn Korean.

---

### Combined and Adverbial Forms
The instructor introduces several other ways to express "only" that vary in nuance:

**뿐만 아니라**
This is a combination of 뿐 and 만 used to mean "not only... but also."
> **저는 회사원일 뿐만 아니라 사장입니다.** / I'm not only an employee, but I'm the president.
> **그것뿐만 아니라 이것도 그래요.** / It's not only that, but this too.

**단지 and 다만**
These function similarly to "simply" or "merely."
- **단지**: Often translated as "simply." Example: **나는 단지 자고 싶다** (I only/simply want to sleep).
- **다만**: Often translated as "merely." Example: **나는 다만 키가 작을 뿐이다** (It's merely that I'm small).

**오직**
This is an emphatic "only" often used in combination with 만 or 뿐.
> **그건 오직 당신만이 할 수 있는 일이다.** / That is something that only you can do.
> **내겐 오직 너뿐이야.** / All I have is only you.

**단**
Used as an adjective to describe a noun as being the "only" one.
> **이 세상에 단 하나뿐인 책.** / The only (one) book in the world.

**유일하다**
This can be used as an adverb (유일하게) or an adjective (유일한) to mean "to be the only."
> **선생님이 유일하게 한국말을 할 수 있는 사람이세요.** / The teacher is the only person who can speak Korean.
> **선생님이 한국말을 할 수 있는 유일한 사람이세요.** / The teacher is the only person who can speak Korean.

**따름이다**
This is a more formal or literary way to say "I'm only" or "It's just that." It is frequently used with descriptive verbs.
> **그 친구의 성격이 부러울 따름이다.** / It's just that I'm jealous of his personality.
> **스테이크에 케첩을 뿌리는 것은 별로라고 생각할 따름입니다.** / I just think putting ketchup on steak isn't that great.
