---

### Basic "Only" with Nouns: 만 and 밖에
The simplest way to say "only" or "just" is by using the particle 만. This particle is attached directly after nouns and other particles but cannot be used directly with verbs. For example, to say "Only I have a car," one would say 저만 차가 있어요. If you want to say you only gave something to a specific person, you attach it after the dative particle: 철수에게만 줬어요 (I only gave it to 철수).

An alternative way to express "only" is using 밖에, which must be paired with a negative verb. While it can mean "besides" or "except," in this context it emphasizes that something is lacking, missing, or insufficient. For instance, 냉장고에 김치밖에 없어요 (There is only kimchi in the refrigerator) implies that the speaker feels there should be more than just kimchi. Because of this "insufficient" nuance, 밖에 cannot be used in commands; 만 should be used instead for imperative sentences. Additionally, 밖에 cannot be used with the verb 아니다. For extra emphasis, the topic marker can be added to form 밖에는.

---

### "Only" with Verbs: 기만 하다 and 기만 하면
To use the concept of "only" with a verb, the verb must first be turned into a noun using the ending 기. This is followed by the verb 하다 to create the form 기만 하다, meaning "to only do" or "to just do." The instructor provides several examples of this transformation: 오빠는 매일 공부하기만 해요 (My older brother only studies every day) and 우리 집 고양이는 먹기만 해요 (My family's cat only eats). This form can also be used in commands, such as 끝내기만 하세요 (Just finish it!).

When 기만 하다 is conjugated into the conditional 음연 form as 기만 하면, it translates literally to "if one only does." However, it more naturally functions as "whenever" or "all one has to do is." This describes a situation where one action inevitably leads to another. Examples include 영희는 슬픈 영화를 보기만 하면 울어요 (Whenever 영희 watches a sad movie, she cries) and 점심을 먹기만 하면 졸려요 (Whenever I eat lunch, I feel sleepy).

---

### Conditional "Only": 야(만)
The form 야 attaches to conjugated verbs to mean "only if." It is the root of common grammar structures like 해야 하다 (must/have to). In a sentence, it indicates that the first action is a necessary requirement for the second. Adding the particle 만 to create 야만 adds even more emphasis to the requirement. For example, 거기는 차가 있어야 갈 수 있어요 (You can only go there if you have a car) or 밥을 다 먹어야 나갈 수 있어요 (I can leave only if I finish eating).

---

### The Versatile 뿐: "Nothing More Than"
The particle 뿐 is used to mean "only and nothing more" or "just and nothing else." It can follow both nouns and verbs. When used with a noun at the end of a sentence, it becomes 뿐이다. It can also be used with the subject marker as 뿐이 followed by a negative verb, similar to 밖에. An example with a noun is 지금 할 수 있는 건 공부뿐이에요 (The only thing I can do now is studying).

To use 뿐 with verbs, you must use the adjective form 을/ㄹ 뿐. This often appears in the middle of a sentence as 을/ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 or at the end as 을/ㄹ 뿐이다. The instructor notes that all I did was... is a common translation: 저는 본대로 말했을 뿐이에요 (All I did was say what I saw). When used after a noun, there is a nuance difference between 뿐 and 일뿐 (from the verb 이다). 일뿐 often sounds negative or dismissive, as if the speaker is complaining that something is "just" a minor thing, whereas 뿐 is neutral. For example, 작은 실수일 뿐이었어요 (It was just a small mistake and nothing more).

---

### Expressing "Not Only": 뿐만 아니라 and 뿐더러
To say "not only A but also B," the most common form is 뿐만 아니라. This can be used with both nouns and verbs. For verbs, it uses the 을/ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 construction. Examples include 철수는 똑똑할 뿐만 아니라 인기도 많아요 (Not only is 철수 smart, but he's also very popular) and 이 식당은 한국 뿐만 아니라 해외에서도 유명해요 (This restaurant is famous not only in Korea, but also abroad).

A more formal version of this is 뿐더러. It is typically used in formal or official writing and is rarely heard in everyday speech. It attaches directly to verb stems as 을/ㄹ 뿐더러. For example, 아몬드는 단백질이 풍부할 뿐더러 몸에도 좋아요 (Not only are almonds rich in protein, but they're also good for your body).

---

### Emphatic and Archaic Forms: 이야말로 and 거니와
이야말로 (or 야말로 after a vowel) is used after a noun to emphasize it as being exactly or indeed the thing being discussed. It confirms the noun's importance. Examples include 저야말로 고맙습니다 (It's me who is [exactly the one who is] thankful) and 책임감이야말로 리더에게 가장 중요한 능력이에요 (Responsibility is [indeed] the most important ability to a leader).

거니와 is an archaic grammar form that is not common in modern speech but appears in older literature or formal contexts. It is used when the speaker agrees with the first statement and adds extra information, functioning like "not only is A true, but also B." For example, 이 차는 오래됐거니와 고장이 나서 탈 수 없어요 (Not only is this car old, but also it's broken so I can't ride it).

---

### Conditions: 한 and 이상
The instructor concludes with two forms that set conditions. 한 is used with verbs (conjugated as an adjective + 한) to mean "as long as" or "unless." For example, 학생들이 있는 한 계속 가르칠 거예요 (As long as there are students, I'll keep teaching).

이상 is used to mean "since" or "as long as," implying that because a certain condition is met, a specific result is natural or expected. It is often used with negative verbs to mean "unless." Examples include 걸을 수 있는 이상 계속 여행을 하고 싶어요 (As long as I can walk, I want to keep traveling) and 전문가의 도움이 없는 이상, 이건 불가능해요 (Unless I have the help of an expert, this is impossible).
