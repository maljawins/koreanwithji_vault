## Teaching Points

### Overview

This lesson covers three ways to express the concept of "only" or "just" in Korean, ranging from basic beginner particles to an intermediate grammar form. The instructor begins with the versatile particle 만, which can be used in almost any context. He then introduces the particle 밖에, which carries a specific nuance of insufficiency and must be paired with negative verb forms. Finally, the lesson concludes with the idiomatic grammar structure 을/ㄹ 수밖에 없다, which translates to "having no choice but to" do something. By comparing these forms, learners can understand not just the grammar, but the specific emotional weight each choice carries in a conversation.

---

### The Basic Particle 만

The particle 만 is the most fundamental way to say "only" or "just" in Korean. It is extremely flexible and can be attached to nouns, other particles, or even specific grammar forms. When used with common markers like the subject (이/가), object (을/를), or topic (은/는) markers, the markers themselves become optional. However, if you choose to include them, 만 must always come first, resulting in combinations like 만은, 만이, or 만을. Conversely, when 만 is used with other particles such as 에 (at/to), 에게/한테 (to a person), 까지 (until), or 에서 (from/at), 만 is placed at the end, creating forms like 에만, 에게만, or 까지만.

The instructor provides several examples to demonstrate its use in simple sentences. To say "I ate only one banana," you attach 만 to the counter: 바나나 한 개만 먹었어요. If you want to say "Only you didn't come to the party," you attach it to the pronoun: 너만 파티에 안 왔어. It is also used for time durations, such as 10분만 기다려 주세요 (Please wait for just 10 minutes), or for specific targets, as in 비밀번호는 철수에게만 알려줬어요 (I told the password only to Chul-soo). Beyond nouns, 만 can be embedded in verb forms like 하기만 하면 (if you only do...) or the polite sentence ending 니다만 (it is..., but...), which functions as a more formal version of 지만.

---

### The Negative Particle 밖에

The particle 밖에 also means "only" or "just," but it functions differently from 만. It must always be followed by a negative verb. This includes verbs preceded by 안 (not) or 못 (cannot), verbs ending in 지 않다, or inherently negative verbs like 없다 (to not exist/have). While the word 밖에 literally means "outside" (as in "the dog is outside"), as a particle it translates to "outside of" or "besides." Therefore, saying "I only ate one banana" using this form literally translates to "Outside of one banana, I didn't eat."

There is one notable exception to the negative verb rule: the verb 모르다 (to not know) cannot be used with 밖에, even though it is a negative concept. For all other negative situations, 밖에 is often preferred over 만 because of its specific nuance.

---

### Nuance: Insufficiency and Lacking

The primary difference between 만 and 밖에 lies in the speaker's perspective. While 만 is a neutral statement of fact, 밖에 emphasizes that something is insufficient, lacking, or that the amount is disappointingly small. For example, 지금 2000원만 있어요 simply states "I have only 2,000 won." However, 지금 2000원 밖에 없어요 conveys "I have nothing but 2,000 won," implying that this amount is not enough or that the speaker is broke.

This nuance of "lacking" makes 밖에 sound more natural in most negative contexts. If someone says 저는 한국어밖에 못해요, it implies a sense of regret or modesty, as if saying "I wish I spoke other languages, but unfortunately, I can only speak Korean." Similarly, saying 저는 라면밖에 못 끓여요 (I can't boil/cook anything but ramen) suggests that the speaker's cooking skills are limited. The instructor also uses the example of sleep: 잠을 세 시간밖에 못 잤어요 (I only got three hours of sleep) emphasizes the exhaustion and the lack of sufficient rest.

---

### Grammatical Restrictions on 밖에

Despite being more natural in many cases, 밖에 cannot be used in every situation. Specifically, it is forbidden in commands (imperatives) and suggestions. You cannot tell someone to "only eat vegetables" using 밖에 because you cannot "eat outside of vegetables." In these cases, you must use 만.

- **Correct:** 채소만 드세요. (Eat only vegetables.)
- **Incorrect:** 채소밖에 드시지 마세요. (This sounds unnatural/incorrect for a command.)

Similarly, for suggestions like "Shall we only meet for 10 minutes?", the neutral 만 is required: 10분만 만날까요?

---

### Intermediate Form: 을/ㄹ 수밖에 없다

The final grammar point is 을/ㄹ 수밖에 없다, which is an idiomatic extension of the "outside" logic. This form uses the bound noun 수 (meaning ability or way) and combines it with 밖에 없다 to mean "there is no way/ability outside of [verb]." In English, this is best translated as "cannot help but," "have no choice but to," or "there is nothing to do but."

To conjugate this, you attach 을 to verb stems ending in a consonant and ㄹ to those ending in a vowel. For example, 갈 수밖에 없어요 means "I have no choice but to go." The instructor provides the example 사진을 찍을 수밖에 없었어요 (I couldn't help but take a photo/I just had to take a photo). While the future-style 을/ㄹ ending is standard, in written Korean, you may occasionally see the present tense 는 used, such as 기다리는 수밖에 없어요 (There is nothing to do but wait), though the meaning remains the same.
