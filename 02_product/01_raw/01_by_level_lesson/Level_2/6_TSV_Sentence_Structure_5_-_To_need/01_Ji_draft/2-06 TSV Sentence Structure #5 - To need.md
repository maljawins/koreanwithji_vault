**Intro**

Welcome. In this lesson, we’re gonna talk about how to say **“I need something”** in Korean

In English, **“to need”** is an **action verb**, right? Like in: “I need help.”

But in Korean, there is **no action verb** that directly means “to need.”

Instead, we use this **descriptive verb**:
👉 **필요하다** — which literally means **“to be necessary”** or **“to be needed.”**

So instead of saying “I need help,” you actually have to say : **“Help is necessary or help is needed.”**

And there are two ways to say it. Let me show you what that looks like:

**저에게(=제게) / 저한테 도움이 필요해요**→ Literally: **To me, help is necessary.**
Here, “저에게” or 제게 means “to me”. “도움” is “help” and “필요해요” is “is necessary.”

*By the way,* “저에게” is often shortened to **“제게”.** And these are the only 3 pronouns that can be shortened when combined with 에게. Now 네게 is very very rare in conversations so just stick with 너한테.

Let’s look at another example using the topic marker this time.

📌 **저는 이 약이 필요해요.** → **As for me, this medicine is necessary.**

“저는” “As for me” 이 약이 this medicine 필요해요 is necessary.

And this is a very common mistake. 필요하다 is a descriptive verb, so you can’t use an object marker because you can’t “necessary” something.

Now, the sentence structure looks familiar, doesn’t it? Yes, it is T (Topic) S (Subject) V (Verb) structure we learned in level 1.

So “이 약” is what is necessary, namely the subject of 필요하다.

**필요하다 – 에게(한테) VS 은/는**

And here’s the **difference between 에게 and 은/는** when used with **필요하다.**

They carry a slightly different **tone and focus**.

**에게 / 한테 / 께** are **directional particles** that mean **“to someone.”**

So, you use these when you want to place more **emphasis on *who* the need is directed to.** It feels a little more **direct** and specific. It’s like saying “it’s necessary **for** him or her”

📍 **학생에게 / 한테 도움이 필요해요.**
→ **Help is necessary for the student.**

Here, we’re emphasizing that the **student is the one who needs help.**

You can also think of it as: 👉 “It is *to the student* that help is needed.”

**은/는**

The **topic marker** adds a different kind of nuance. Instead of pointing directly at the person, it **sets the person as the topic** of the sentence. That means it slightly **softens** the sentence and shifts the focus more toward **what the person needs.**

📍 **학생은 도움이 필요해요.**
→ **As for the student, help is needed.**

This version feels a little more casual and less forceful — almost like you're simply making an observation or giving context.

**필요(가) 있다 / 없다**

To say “I don’t need something”, you would think you can just negate 필요하다 and say [필요하지 않다 or 안 필요하다.]

Both are correct, but there’s a better way. It’s much more natural to say 필요 없다 – again this is a descriptive verb meaning unnecessary.

Now, let’s get technical. 필요, by itself as a noun, means “a need” or “necessity” hence, the optional subject marker. So 필요(가) 없다 means there is no need.

So, “I don’t need help” in Korean, would be “저는 도움이 필요 없어요.”.

Again, notice the same TSV sentence structure.

Then, can you say 필요(가) 있다? Yes, we do use it but to express a simple “I need coffee” sentences, you can stick to 필요하다.

When DO we use 필요(가) 있다 then? That would be in this form: ㄹ/을 필요(가) 있다. This means there is a need TO DO something.

We’ve established to call this verb branch – ㄹ/을 the future branch to keep things nice and easy but I also mentioned that this is not only used to indicate future events but also to imply possibility or uncertainty. Namely, things that are yet to happen.

Here’s an example. 걱정하실 필요가 없습니다 – There’s no need to worry.

Compared to the simple, plain 필요하다, this feels more neutral as 필요의 “주체”가 되는 것이 “너”가 아니라 현재 “상황”이기 때문에 그렇다.
