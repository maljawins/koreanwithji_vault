## Teaching Points

### Noun Connectors: "And" and "With"
Ji explains that Korean uses different particles for "and" depending on the level of formality and whether the noun ends in a consonant (받침) or a vowel.
*   **과/와 (Formal):** `과` is used after a consonant; `와` is used after a vowel. Commonly found in writing or news.
    *   `치킨과 맥주` (Chicken and beer)
    *   `남자와 여자` (Man and woman)
*   **하고 (Casual/Spoken):** Not sensitive to `받침`. The easiest and most common spoken form.
    *   `펜하고 종이` (Pen and paper)
*   **이랑/랑 (Casual/Spoken):** `이랑` follows a consonant; `랑` follows a vowel.
    *   `빵이랑 커피` (Bread and coffee)
    *   `너랑 나` (You and me)

These same particles are used to mean **"with."** To emphasize "together," the adverbs `같이` (casual) or `함께` (formal) can be added.
*   `엄마랑 같이` (Together with mom)
*   `친구와 함께` (Together with a friend)

### Similarity: 같이 and 처럼
When `같이` is used without a connecting particle, it means "like" or "as if," similar to `처럼`.
*   `천사같이 착해` (Kind like an angel)
*   `배우처럼 잘생겼어요` (Handsome like an actor)

### Sentence Connectors: ~고 (And)
The connector `~고` is attached directly to the verb stem to connect two actions.
*   **Tense Rule:** Usually, only the final verb is conjugated into the past or future tense.
    *   `밥을 먹고 커피를 마셨어요` (I ate and drank coffee) — `먹고` stays in the stem form.
*   **Past Tense Stem:** If emphasis on the first action's completion is needed, the past tense stem (`았/었/였`) can be used before `고`.
    *   `밥은 먹었고, 커피도 마실 거예요` (As for the meal, I ate it [already], and I'm going to drink coffee).

### Negation and Alternatives: 아니고 vs 말고
*   **아니고 (Not A, but B):** Used for noun-to-noun correction.
    *   `중국 사람이 아니고 한국 사람이에요` (I'm not Chinese, I'm Korean).
*   **말고 (Instead of/Except):** Used to exclude an option or suggest an alternative.
    *   `이거 말고 저거 주세요` (Not this, give me that instead).
    *   `자지 말고 빨리 준비해` (Don't sleep, get ready quickly).

### Noun Connector: 이나/나 (Or)
Used when the speaker doesn't have a strong preference ("this or that, either is fine").
*   `이나` follows a consonant; `나` follows a vowel.
*   **Nuance:** When used alone (without a second option), it means "or something" or "at least."
    *   `커피나 마시고 싶다` (I want to drink coffee or something).

5.  **Past Tense Stems:**
    *   Ji teaches how to form the past tense stem (`았/었/였`) and how it can be used with `~을 거예요` or `~겠다` to express assumptions or hypotheticals about the past (e.g., `배고팠겠어요` - "You must have been hungry").

6.  **Negation (아니고/말고):**
    *   Ji contrasts `아니고` (negating a noun) with `말고` (excluding/suggesting an alternative).

7.  **Noun Connector ("Or"):**
    *   Ji introduces `이나/나` and explains the nuance of having no strong preference.
